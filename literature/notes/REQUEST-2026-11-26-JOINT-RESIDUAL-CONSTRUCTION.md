## 2. Exact normalization yields the signed quadratic objective

This is an independent algebraic expansion of **Astra's already registered model**, not a new model. Fix the positive base predictor, the word group, a fitted static intercept and the amplitude \(\beta\). Absorb the fixed intercept into \(P_0\). Let

\[
y(w)\in\{-1,+1\},\qquad
\mu_t=\sum_wP_0(w\mid h_t)y(w),\qquad
z_t=x_tf(u_t)f(v_t),\qquad f\in\{-1,+1\}^{V}.
\]

Here \(u_t,v_t\) are the specified neighboring history windows and \(x_t\) is the observed sign in the registration. The normalized model is

\[
P_f(w\mid h_t)=
\frac{P_0(w\mid h_t)e^{\beta z_ty(w)}}{Z_t(z_t)}.
\]

Since \(y,z\) are signs,

\[
Z_t(z)=\cosh\beta+\mu_tz\sinh\beta,
\quad
\log Z_t(z)=a_t+b_tz,
\]

where all logarithms in this response are natural and

\[
a_t=\tfrac12\log\big(1+(1-\mu_t^2)\sinh^2\beta\big),
\qquad b_t=\operatorname{atanh}(\mu_t\tanh\beta).
\]

For the observed word \(w_t\), its exact log-likelihood gain is therefore

\[
\boxed{
g_t(f)=-a_t+\omega_tf(u_t)f(v_t),
\quad
\omega_t=x_t\big[\beta y(w_t)
-\operatorname{atanh}(\mu_t\tanh\beta)\big].
}
\tag{1}
\]

This identity holds on an arbitrary fixed data sequence. It needs no iid text assumption or planted synchronization model. Repeated occurrences of an edge can have different base distributions and still contribute additive \(\omega_t\) weights.

For small \(\beta\),

\[
\omega_t=\beta x_t(y(w_t)-\mu_t)+O(\beta^3),
\qquad a_t=\tfrac12(1-\mu_t^2)\beta^2+O(\beta^4).
\]

Thus the normalized prediction residual gives the first-order edge weight. At finite amplitude, the atanh term supplies the exact correction. Changing the intercept or amplitude changes these weights, so one fixed quadratic matrix does not solve the entire joint hyperparameter problem.

### A feasible solution, an upper bound and exact refinement

Aggregate non-loop undirected weights \(W_{ij}=\sum_{t:\{u_t,v_t\}=\{i,j\}}\omega_t\), and let \(\ell=\sum_{t:u_t=v_t}\omega_t\). Define \(C_{ij}=C_{ji}=W_{ij}/2\) and \(C_{ii}=0\). Then

\[
G(f)=-\sum_ta_t+\ell+f^\top Cf,
\qquad
G(f)\le-\sum_ta_t+\ell+V\lambda_{\max}(C).
\tag{2}
\]

The bound follows from \(\|f\|_2^2=V\). Loop terms are constants for Boolean labels. Removing them before spectral relaxation prevents irrelevant diagonal weights from changing its direction. Astra already removes loops in the synthetic table learner.

Taking the sign of a leading eigenvector produces a feasible joint proposal. It has no universal rounding guarantee for arbitrary signed costs. Its actual complete likelihood is available from (2). Flipping a single coordinate has exact gain

\[
\Delta_iG=-4f_i(Cf)_i.
\tag{3}
\]

Flipping a set \(T\) gives

\[
\Delta_TG=-2\sum_{i\in T,\,j\notin T}W_{ij}f_if_j.
\tag{4}
\]

Internal edges cancel under a joint flip. Equation (4) evaluates a proposed joint move, without asserting that searching all such sets is cheap. A gain above an installation price on training data is not yet a held-out improvement.

### An exact matched independent-edge comparator

For each directed history edge \(e=(u,v)\), aggregate \(\Omega_e=\sum_{t:e_t=e}\omega_t\). Allow an independent sign \(s_e\) in place of \(f_uf_v\), using exactly the same base, observations, group, intercept and amplitude. Then

\[
s_e^*=\operatorname{sign}(\Omega_e),\qquad
\boxed{G_{\rm edge}^*=-\sum_ta_t+\sum_e|\Omega_e|.}
\tag{5}
\]

Ties admit either sign. The vertex model imposes loop sign \(+1\), equal signs on reverse edges and product \(+1\) around every undirected cycle. The independent-edge model removes these sharing constraints and hence has at least as good a training optimum. Deployment/storage and generalization comparisons remain substantive, rather than consequences of this inclusion.

If preferred non-loop signs are cycle-consistent and loop weights are nonnegative, a spanning-tree propagation attains every preferred sign. This is an \(O(V+|E|)\) constructive special case. A directed-edge table, an undirected-edge table and a shared vertex table are different comparator classes and should be identified separately.

## 3. Synchronization: a joint relaxation and a usable certificate

McRae et al. study the exact Boolean problem in (2), its semidefinite relaxation

\[
\max_{X\succeq0,\,X_{ii}=1}\langle C,X\rangle,
\]

and its rank-\(r\) factorization

\[
\max_{Y\in\mathbb R^{V\times r},\,\|Y_i\|_2=1}
\langle C,YY^\top\rangle.
\tag{6}
\]

Every node receives a jointly optimized vector. Unit row norms are the relaxation constraint. They are not word-probability normalization or degree normalization of the graph.

### What the source theorem actually guarantees

Theorem1 assumes

\[
C=\operatorname{diag}(z)A\operatorname{diag}(z)+\Delta,
\]

with \(A\) a connected nonnegative weighted graph, Laplacian gap \(\lambda_2\), and

\[
\rho^\Delta=\max_i\left(-z_i\sum_{j\ne i}\Delta_{ij}z_j\right).
\]

For \(r\ge4\), if

\[
\rho^\Delta+\frac{r+11}{r-3}\|\Delta\|_{\rm op}
\le\frac{r-3}{r-1}\lambda_2,
\tag{7}
\]

every second-order critical point of (6) equals \(zu^\top\) for some unit vector \(u\). The noiseless case also allows \(r=3\). The proof uses random tangent perturbations, a Laplacian lower bound and a nuclear-norm estimate for quartic row distances. These are sufficient recovery assumptions. Equation (1) does not establish them for ordinary text.

### A deterministic certificate that does not require a planted model

The source's first/second-order analysis uses

\[
S(Y)=\operatorname{ddiag}(CYY^\top)-C.
\]

For an actual Boolean candidate \(f\), set

\[
S_f=\operatorname{diag}(f\odot Cf)-C.
\]

The following are direct consequences of this matrix, independently derived here:

1. If \(S_f\succeq0\), then \(f\) is globally optimal for both the Boolean problem and the SDP. For any feasible \(X\),
   \[
   \langle C,X\rangle=f^\top Cf-\langle S_f,X\rangle
   \le f^\top Cf.
   \]
2. If \(\lambda_{\min}(S_f)\ge-\tau\), then the additive optimization gap is at most \(V\tau\), by the same argument with \(S_f+\tau I\). Numerical eigenvalue uncertainty must be included before treating a floating-point computation as a certificate.
3. If \(v^\top S_fv<0\), the row-normalized joint perturbation
   \[
   Y_i(t)=\big(f_i\sqrt{1-t^2v_i^2},\,tv_i\big)
   \]
   satisfies, for sufficiently small \(t\),
   \[
   \langle C,Y(t)Y(t)^\top\rangle
   =f^\top Cf-t^2v^\top S_fv+O(t^4).
   \tag{8}
   \]
   It improves the lifted objective, even when no single sign flip improves the Boolean objective. A later rounding step is still necessary and is not guaranteed to preserve that improvement.

These give a concrete relaxation comparator and a training-optimization diagnostic on the exact matrix from (1). They do not require claiming a natural-text synchronization recovery threshold.

For \(m_C\) nonzero entries, forming \(CY\) and a projected gradient costs \(O(m_Cr)\) arithmetic operations, with \(O(m_C+Vr)\) numerical working storage. The full SDP has order \(V^2\) variables. Iteration count, tolerance and precision are additional costs. The landscape theorem is not an end-to-end finite-precision runtime theorem.

### A narrower random-initialization alternative

Li–Fan–Wei supply an explicit AMP iteration for a dense model

\[
M=\lambda v^\star v^{\star\top}+W,
\quad v_i^\star\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}\operatorname{Unif}\{\pm n^{-1/2}\},
\]

with GOE scaling \(\operatorname{Var}W_{ij}=1/n\) off diagonal and \(2/n\) on diagonal. Their update is

\[
x_{t+1}=M\eta_t(x_t)-\langle\eta_t'(x_t)\rangle\eta_{t-1}(x_{t-1}),
\]

\[
\eta_t(x)=\gamma_t\tanh(\pi_tx),\quad
\pi_t=\sqrt{\max\{n(\|x_t\|^2-1),1\}},\quad
\gamma_t=\|\tanh(\pi_tx_t)\|^{-1}.
\]

They start from \(x_1\sim N(0,I/n)\) independently of \(M\), with \(\eta_0=0\). The source analyzes emergence of useful global correlation, up to the inherent sign ambiguity, on an \(O(\log n/(\lambda-1))\) scale when \(n^{-1/9}\log n\lesssim\lambda-1\le0.2\). This is a concrete jointly updated proposal without an informative initial estimate. The proof was not read or independently certified. One dense iteration costs \(O(n^2)\), with \(O(n)\) extra vectors. Substituting sparse, dependent text residuals does not inherit the GOE theorem. No Bayes-risk calibration conclusion is imported.

## 4. GradMax: installing a dependent path from residuals

Take an existing layer input \(h\in\mathbb R^d\), a downstream preactivation \(z\in\mathbb R^q\), and add

\[
z^+=z+B\phi(Ah).
\tag{9}
\]

The source sets \(A=0\), with \(\phi(0)=0\) and \(\phi'(0)=1\). This preserves the current output while introducing a nonzero outgoing block \(B\). For the actual loss gradient \(\delta=\partial L/\partial z\), define

\[
G=\mathbb E[\delta h^\top].
\]

Then, at installation,

\[
\nabla_A L=B^\top G,\qquad \nabla_BL=0.
\]

AppendixA computes \(G\) by temporarily adding a zero dense edge \(Ch\) and using automatic differentiation on \(C\). The temporary dense object is search work. It can be removed after the factorized path is selected.

### An exact rank-one construction

For empirical \(G=U\Sigma V^\top\), choose \(B=c u_1\), \(A=0\), and then

\[
A_{\rm new}=-\eta c\sigma_1v_1^\top.
\]

For a smooth activation such as tanh and a locally twice differentiable finite empirical objective,

\[
L(A_{\rm new},B)-L(0,B)
=-\eta c^2\sigma_1^2+O(\eta^2).
\tag{10}
\]

Thus \(\sigma_1>0\) gives a strictly decreasing step for sufficiently small \(\eta>0\). From \(A=B=0\), changing either block alone leaves the output unchanged. The useful action installs the whole path, with one neutral initialization followed by a gradient-enabled update. This addresses a concrete dependence between two connection operations. It does not establish that every conceivable single added neuron is unhelpful, or that the finite gain pays an arbitrary fixed installation price.

The paper's equation(11) constrains only \(\|B\|_F\le c\) while describing a top-\(k\) initialization. Under this exact total-norm constraint,

\[
\max_{\|B\|_F\le c}\|B^\top G\|_F^2=c^2\sigma_1^2,
\]

attained by concentrating norm in a top singular direction. The rank-one construction above therefore has an exact normalization. A genuinely spread top-\(k\) solution follows instead from the extra constraint \(B^\top B=(c^2/k)I_k\), giving objective \((c^2/k)\sum_{j\le k}\sigma_j^2\). That is an explicitly different constraint, not the literal theorem of equation(11).

### A matched normalized-loss interface

At vocabulary logits, \(\delta_i=p_i-e_{w_i}\) is the exact softmax cross-entropy residual. A hidden-layer version backpropagates that residual normally. Consequently \(G\) can be computed using the same normalized prediction loss as the current model. A spectral score proposes a direction. Acceptance still evaluates the entire changed log-sum-exp, held-out performance and the agreed structure price.

The construction assumes \(h\) is already computable. Choosing an indicator for every history makes its potentially exponential dimension part of the search and installation costs. It is not a free solution for learning the unknown Boolean history value.

Direct dense formation of \(G\) from \(N\) samples costs \(O(Nqd)\) outer-product arithmetic, besides existing forward/backward passes. Dense SVD costs \(O(qd\min(q,d))\) and stores \(O(qd)\) numbers. Deploying \(k\) chosen units retains \(O(k(d+q))\) parameters and comparable linear work per evaluation, plus activations. These are explicit implementation counts, not a claimed universal complexity theorem.

## 5. Splitting: a pair can improve a stable single unit

For \(L(\theta)=\mathbb E[\Phi(\sigma(\theta,x))]\), the source defines

\[
S(\theta)=\mathbb E[\Phi'(\sigma(\theta,x))\nabla_\theta^2\sigma(\theta,x)].
\]

The ordinary Hessian has an additional term

\[
\nabla^2L=S+\mathbb E[\Phi''(\sigma)\nabla\sigma\nabla\sigma^\top].
\]

Under bounded third derivatives, Theorems2.2–2.3 replace a unit by two equally weighted children \(\theta\pm\varepsilon v\). Their first-order output changes cancel. For a minimum eigenvector of \(S\),

\[
L_{\rm split}-L=\tfrac12\varepsilon^2\lambda_{\min}(S)+O(\varepsilon^3).
\tag{11}
\]

It is possible that the original gradient is zero and its Hessian is positive definite, while \(S\) has a negative eigenvalue. Splitting then supplies a descent direction unavailable to small perturbations of that single unit. Algorithm1 selects negative splitting eigenvalues under a count budget. For multiple parents, Theorem2.4 makes the second-order splitting effects additive, so it does not assert hidden cross-parent synergy when every parent's splitting matrix is positive semidefinite.

### An exact small example, independently derived

Restrict the available single-unit family to

\[
\sigma(\theta,x)=\theta x+\theta^2,\qquad
\Pr(x=1)=\Pr(x=-1)=\tfrac12,
\quad L(\theta)=\tfrac12\mathbb E[(\sigma(\theta,x)-a)^2],
\]

with \(0<a<1/2\). Then

\[
L(\theta)=\tfrac12a^2+\tfrac12(1-2a)\theta^2+\tfrac12\theta^4.
\]

The single-unit optimum is globally \(\theta=0\). Yet the equal mixture of the two children \(\theta=\pm\varepsilon\) outputs the constant \(\varepsilon^2\), so

\[
L_{\rm split}=\tfrac12(\varepsilon^2-a)^2.
\]

At \(\varepsilon^2=a\), the loss is zero. The improvement \(a^2/2\) pays any additional total structure charge \(\kappa<a^2/2\) in the same average-loss units. Here \(S(0)=-2a\) and \(L''(0)=1-2a>0\), matching the mechanism in (11).

This is a mathematical example for the explicitly restricted family, which has no independent free bias. It is not a fair-task claim against a model allowed to output the target constant directly. It creates two interacting child representations while increasing the number of units by one. It does not learn arbitrary discrete operations from an empty architecture.

Directly checking \(K\) units with \(d\) parameters each stores \(O(Kd^2)\) splitting matrices and costs \(O(Kd^3)\) for dense eigendecompositions, plus the data/derivative passes. The selected children are deployed, not these search matrices. Exact smoothness and available second derivatives are material assumptions.

## 6. AdaNet: the candidate must already contain the dependencies

AdaNet Section5 proposes two complete candidate subnetworks at each round: one of current depth and one one-layer deeper, with a specified number of units per layer and access to existing representations. For a fixed candidate \(h\), its outer step minimizes

\[
F_t(w,h)=\frac1N\sum_i\Phi(1-y_iF_{t-1}(x_i)-y_iw^\top h(x_i))
+\Gamma_h\|w\|_1.
\]

This is convex in \(w\) for fixed \(h\). Candidate generation may train internal parameters jointly via equation(6), which the source explicitly identifies as generally nonconvex. Random candidates are another allowed weak learner. The theorem about the outer function class does not certify an efficient oracle for all useful subnetworks.

The reusable protocol is to train a **complete executable composite candidate**, then compare its complete output and complexity. Count every trained or discarded candidate in search work. Only retained candidates contribute to deployed work. The original paper uses a binary margin surrogate. Replacing it by full-vocabulary log loss defines a comparator but does not automatically transfer the paper's specific classification generalization bound.

A simple independent convexity check prevents an incorrect synergy claim. Let \(h_1,\ldots,h_k\) be fixed functions, let \(\ell(w)\) be the differentiable convex empirical loss of their linear combination around the current predictor, and use \(\sum_j\Gamma_j|w_j|\). If

\[
|\partial_j\ell(0)|\le\Gamma_j\quad\text{for every }j,
\]

then zero belongs to the full subgradient of the block objective at zero. Hence \(w=0\) is globally optimal over that entire block. Jointly optimizing only those same fixed linear coordinates cannot create a missed improvement. The possible new computation lies in constructing a different nonlinear candidate or representation, rather than merely grouping fixed coefficients.

AppendixC's restricted dual-norm step is useful as a residual-direction calculation over existing features. This response does not rely on its stronger wording about globally optimal finite steps, and does not interpret its restricted single-unit search as a joint internal-feature oracle.

## 7. Resource accounting and the exact meaning of joint acquisition

For the registered binary rolling histories, there are \(V=2^M\) possible vertices and at most two directed successors per vertex. The undirected graph has degree at most four. A sparse implementation can accumulate edge weights in \(O(N)\), store the graph in \(O(V)\), and multiply by \(C\) in \(O(V)\), **after paying for the base model and group masses**. The existing synthetic implementation uses dense matrices, so these potential sparse costs are not an implemented performance claim.

A complete sign table has a global sign symmetry. For a connected full deployment graph, \(V-1\) logical sign bits determine all products. A straightforward implementation may store \(V\) bits. Disconnected training components do not each get a free deployable gauge choice if future predictions can connect them. The window/group/amplitude/intercept selection, online history state, decoder and probability normalization must also be charged consistently.

In the Boolean table family every entry already has a value. A one-vertex flip changes that value. It is not automatically the act of adding a previously absent feature. The no-correction model is represented by \(\beta=0\), where the whole table is inactive. A claim that every single installation is unprofitable therefore needs an explicit partial-installation decoder and price. Astra's earlier parity-packet interface already supplies an example with such definitions. It should not be silently equated with local flips in the present family.

The mathematically supported distinctions are:

| Construction | What is selected jointly | Concrete useful guarantee | Remaining acquisition/price issue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exact likelihood graph plus synchronization | All vertex signs, or all lifted row vectors | Exact objective, flip gains, spectral upper bound, PSD certificate and explicit lifted escape direction | Arbitrary signed rounding and held-out gains remain unresolved by the source theorem |
| Rank-one GradMax path | Incoming and outgoing connection blocks | Neutral installation enables a nonzero residual gradient and a small loss-decreasing step | Current input features must exist, and finite gain must pay the complete path cost |
| Symmetric splitting | Two child parameter vectors and equal readout shares | Negative splitting eigenvalue gives local descent, with a concrete exact example | Requires a duplicable smooth unit, not arbitrary operation discovery |
| AdaNet composite weak learner | All internal parameters of a candidate subnetwork | An explicit outer candidate comparison/acceptance objective | Finding the useful composite candidate remains nonconvex search work |
| Random-initialization AMP | All coordinates of a discrete latent signal | An explicit jointly updated recovery algorithm in the specified dense model | GOE scaling and independence are absent from general text residuals |

## 8. Bounded response for Astra

The immediate source-grounded deliverables are equation(1)'s exact residual edge weight, equation(5)'s independent-edge optimum, and the synchronization certificate/lift in Section3. They apply to the fixed registered likelihood family and can distinguish poor optimization from a poor conditional model family on training data. They do not select the research route or replace held-out evaluation.

GradMax's precisely normalized rank-one path and the symmetric splitting example provide two concrete ways to make mutually dependent new operations available before demanding isolated immediate gains. AdaNet supplies an outer framework for accepting complete candidates while exposing the cost of their search. Each comparator must retain the same data access, normalization and installed-resource accounting to support a scientific comparison.
