## Main problem and exact resource model

For a known matrix T over a field K, C_K(T) counts gates in an acyclic linear circuit. A gate forms au+bv from two available values with arbitrary prescribed a,b in K. Intermediate results can be reused. An output selects and scales an available value, or returns zero. This count permits additional workspace and arbitrary coefficients. It is not the bounded-coefficient or in-place unitary model of several classical FFT lower bounds.

Theorem1.1 takes any J≥1 finite abelian groups of common order N≥2. N must be invertible in K, and K must contain a primitive root of unity for each group exponent. Each block can independently be a forward or inverse character transform. For their direct sum T,

\[
cJN\sqrt{\log_2N}\le C_K(T)
\le C_\epsilon JN(1+\log_2N)^{1/2+\epsilon}
\]

for every fixed ε>0. The lower constant is absolute. The upper constant depends only on ε. Gates may mix independent input blocks, so computing many transforms together does not remove the stated per-input lower cost. The result fixes the logarithmic exponent at1/2. It leaves the exact upper bound O(N√logN), without ε, open.

For G=(Z/2Z)^k the character table is the Walsh–Hadamard matrix on N=2^k coordinates. Its signs encode known binary inner products, but the coefficient field must have characteristic different from two. Binary address arithmetic used later in the construction does not change this field restriction.

Corollary8.2 gives the same exponent for J independent formal products of length-n polynomials over every field, in division-free binary arithmetic circuits. All2n−1 coefficients of every product are required as formal outputs. The statement is not just equality as a function on a finite input set. Prescribed scalar multiplications are charged in the arithmetic-operation formulation. Extracting the linear part of each gate also preserves the exponent when a formal circuit for a linear transform contains nonlinear intermediate products.

## Robust lower bound and what a state means here

Theorem1.2 allows an algebraic feedback system with s scalar variables. Each state is a weighted sum of at most two inputs or states, and the complete system has a unique solution for every input. With h=JN, it also permits corrections B and Z satisfying

\[
\operatorname{rank}B\le\beta h,\qquad
|\operatorname{supp}Z|\le\eta h\,2^{\sqrt{\log_2N}/8}.
\]

If T lies in the Zariski closure of the joint sums M+B+Z, where M is such an s-state transfer, then s≥ch√logN. The corrections can mix all batches, and all three terms can vary together before taking the closure. The support count is global over the full h-by-h matrix.

These scalar states are registers in a simultaneous linear system, not a finite set of possible physical memory values. Unique solvability does not assert convergence of an iterative recurrent implementation. Algebraic border robustness does not state tolerance to a specified norm error, random BSC observations or unknown data corruption. The separate numerical stability result below has its own error model.

### Rectangle prices combine rank with support

Section2 imports a compatible family of planar tensor prices from the companion *Euclidean geometry in the asymptotic spectrum of tensors*. At a finite placement S, the price is the logarithm of a restriction-monotone spectral point multiplied by the convex hull's semiperimeter δ(S). Tensor products add prices, while ordinary sums obey subadditivity in the unlogged spectral value. Positions have joint convexity, positive homogeneity and translation invariance. Price sublevels are Zariski closed. A common q-valued label shared on S costs δ(S)logq.

Put two copies of a matrix's row label on the left corners of a rectangle and two column copies on the right. For height L and width W, collapsing one direction gives

\[
P_M(0,W)=W\log\operatorname{rank}M,
\qquad P_M(L,0)=L\log|\operatorname{supp}M|.
\]

Theorem2.2 shows that every character table saturates the general upper bound

\[
P_{F_G}(L,W)=(L+\sqrt{L^2+W^2})\log N.
\]

A permutation matrix instead has price (L+W)logN. At W=1 their difference is L²logN/(1+√(1+L²)). This produces a constant-order distinction at height L of order1/√logN.

The proof implements a weighted swap by two character phases and separate changes of basis. The swap saturates the total transport price on two diameters. Lifting supporting forces through the contraction, then using rank-N three-leg bounds, places the two internal force vectors in one disk. Strict convexity forces both to equal their boundary midpoint. The copying inequality converts this force constraint into the four-distance lower bound. Figures1–2 display the limiting rank/support placements and the internal-force disk. The underlying spectral-price existence and closedness are explicit external inputs, not reproved in this reading.

### A global quotient preserves most independent lists

Section3 tests the list tensor of [I_N H], with one vector leg at the rectangle center and a duplicated column label on one side. At L=3/√logN, pairing two such lists exposes the Gram matrix with two Fourier off-diagonal blocks. Its price gives a list value exceeding1.82N asymptotically. A projection losing at most2^−100N dimensions retains at least1.8N for large N.

The crucial batch step does not assume a global low-rank correction respects block boundaries. An annihilating row quotient is put in echelon form. Scaling block i against block j by powers z^(j−i) removes the mixed upper blocks in the limit. The surviving block projections have total rank loss no larger than the original rank budget. A projective Grassmannian argument preserves the quotient inequality at border points. Figure3 depicts this removal of cross-block terms.

For a feedback transfer v=Cv+Ax, the paper repeats every variable on d time coordinates. Replacing a unipotent shift by a nilpotent shift changes rank by at most one per state. It truncates dependency paths after d−1 state expansions, leaving at most d2^(d−1) nonzeros per row. A scaling limit recovers d independent copies of the original transfer. Choosing d=ceil(√logN/8) makes the resulting rank-plus-support upper budget incompatible with the preserved list lower value. Finite small orders use two disjoint Vandermonde column minors. These steps give a field- and batch-uniform bound.

## The upper construction: prediction with supplied exact repair information

Theorem4.1 concerns commuting operators Q1,…,Qm with a known common diagonalizing transform S. Every eigenvalue is an ℓth root of unity for a fixed prime ℓ, and the exponent tuple at each frequency is supplied. The target F is their product. This is a construction for known operators.

A pencil stage contains row matrices tensored with I and all query powers Qj^a. Such a stage can contain m different queries, so its stage count alone is not a gate bound. A separate routing construction in Section5 pays for those queries.

### An exact predictor on most shifted rows

Every shift β in (Z/ℓZ)^m is assigned equally often across a fixed batch. At each frequency the shifted exponents are independent uniform digits when counted across this list of shifts. Their ordinary integer sum lies in a window of width O_ℓ(√(m logm)) except on at most a1/(2m³) fraction of rows. This is a deterministic counting guarantee for the complete set of assigned shifts, obtained from Hoeffding's inequality. It is not a distributional assumption about the input arrays.

An integral binomial interpolation polynomial predicts the product multiplier exactly within the window. Working over Z[1/ℓ,ζ] avoids illegal divisions after specialization to allowed positive characteristics. The polynomial's many monomials are evaluated using an affine matrix pencil L with L^b=pI for a suitable ℓ-power b. Quasi-commuting factors and the Gaussian-binomial identity yield that matrix power. Increasing batch width accommodates the pencil's independent coordinates. The width and fixed coefficients can be very large functions of the chosen parameters.

### Known-support checks and a double-zero correction

At a frequency ν, the set J(ν) of possible failed predictor rows is known from the exponents and shifts. Its size is at most s≤W/(2m³). A Vandermonde check matrix H recovers errors supported on that known set from HPX and HX after2s row conversions to spectral coordinates.

The repair uses

\[
g(y)=y-\ell^{-1}y(y^\ell-1),\qquad
g(z)=z,\quad g'(z)=0\quad(z^\ell=1).
\]

Thus g(y)−z=(y−z)²D_z(y). The spectral table computes a vector a whose lift Hᵀa agrees with D_z(p_i)e_i on J, and returns a and za. After inverse conversion these give Z and FZ in ordinary coordinates. The final output is

\[
g(P)X-PZ+FZ.
\]

Its error factors into one term zero on J and another zero off J. This handles the fact that J changes with frequency while the ordinary-coordinate lift is fixed. Figure4 shows exactly which2s rows cross the spectral boundary. The total is at most ℓ+2 predictor applications and one2s-row spectral table, hence O_ℓ(√(m logm)) pencil stages. A cyclotomic extension provides unit-separated check nodes, and expanding its basis returns independent base-ring rows.

The repair is exact. It receives the known error locations and exact linear checks in the common eigenbasis. It does not discover unknown parity support, locate arbitrary observational noise or estimate a spectrum from samples.

## Sharing query calls and obtaining the exponent

For prime-radix tensor powers, Section5 expresses a large Fourier transform as a product of m commuting quadratic-phase queries, up to permutations and scalings. Each query uses one distinguished address direction plus shared auxiliary axes. The auxiliary axes are occupied by data.

Labels use a matrix Γ(v,w)=1−Δ(v,w)^(ℓ−1) over F_ℓ to assign directions in a low-dimensional paired space. A second decomposition of the identity into weighted rectangles is over Z[1/ℓ]. Charged rectangles require separate common transforms. On the remaining rectangles, source and destination directions are orthogonal. The source partial transform and destination transposed transform then use only auxiliary axes and are shared across all m queries. Figure5 displays the common endpoints and query-specific reindexing, scaling and row mixing. The proof also covers a degenerate pairing restricted to the intermediate subspace by using dual bases for the full nondegenerate pairing.

The parameters make the cost of a pencil per row O(m^η) for any chosen positive η, while A, the number of address axes, is asymptotic to2m. Combined with the predictor stages this gives a fixed template with σ/W<A^θ for every θ>1/2. In a single large transform, inactive address digits already supply the independent rows needed to fill those batches. Padding is explicitly charged. Recursion on f=floor((k−r)/A) yields the bound O_ℓ,θ(ℓ^k(1+k)^θ).

Theorem5.1 also charges ring operations, scalar multiplications and indexing on O(k)-bit words under unit-cost integer arithmetic, lookup, comparison and digit extraction. It gives an effective fixed-radix construction, not a general machine-bit-cost bound for arbitrary field elements.

Proposition5.2 preserves selected bit slots in unnormalized Walsh computations. For even k, the normalized transform has a uniform dyadic circuit with a fixed coefficient list outside recursion. Under a per-gate multiplicative perturbation |δ_g|≤u≤c(1+k)^−B, its Euclidean operator-norm error is at most Cu(1+k)^B. The constants depend on the template. Odd widths can use Q(√2). This is a numerical circuit perturbation statement, separate from noisy observation or learning guarantees.

## Returning to arbitrary fields and all lengths

Sections6–8 prevent additional axes, workspace and extension coordinates from restoring the full logarithmic cost.

- **Synchronized shear layers.** Tensor powers of a unit shear reduce to bounded words built from small Fourier blocks and monomial operations. Their cost has the same exponent. Monomial and shear schedules can be synchronized across arbitrarily many tensor factors.
- **Geometric evaluation and restored workspace.** A geometric Vandermonde matrix factors as LΔ^−1Lᵀ, with L diagonally equivalent to a triangular Toeplitz matrix. Commutator updates implement a transform while returning every borrowed register to its initial value for all initial contents. Grouping a bounded number of axes lets fibers borrow occupied neighboring data without an exponential workspace multiplier.
- **Packing convolution.** The input is packed into extension, jet and cyclic coordinates with total base-field dimension erN between8n and9n. Reserving half of each radix prevents wraparound. Gathering by the original mixed-radix integer includes carries and recovers ordinary polynomial coefficients.
- **Short extension arithmetic.** A supply of primes with smooth p−1, using Bombieri–Vinogradov and Mertens as cited inputs, bounds loge. In positive characteristic, roots are supplied by finite-field extensions. Base-field multiplication recurses at length6er with logarithm O((logn)^(4/5)loglogn).
- **One deformation parameter in characteristic zero.** A prime cyclic algebra becomes a scalar factor plus a binomial algebra of degree p−1. All binomial relations are deformed together by one parameter t. A product wraps at most once per axis, so jets of length H=2r+d retain the full product polynomial. Only after reading that polynomial does the algorithm substitute at the other endpoint. Figure6 distinguishes this degree-bounded recovery from an unjustified evaluation of an arbitrary truncated series.
- **Arbitrary grids.** Large axes are handled individually, while small axes are grouped. The auxiliary field degree e is bounded by the number h of independent complementary fibers. Packing e fibers into an extension scalar charges all coordinates back to original data. Only auxiliary transform lengths must avoid the characteristic. Theorem8.1 covers tensor products of geometric evaluation, inverse evaluation and invertible triangular Toeplitz factors.

Finally a chirp specialization of one convolution operand realizes a cyclic Fourier transform over the algebraic closure. Taking homogeneous linear parts in all remaining variables simultaneously preserves the number of gates even when batches mix. This supplies the convolution lower bound and closes the all-field result.
