## 1. Research question and principal conclusions

A finite message selected using a Gaussian observation matrix can bias that matrix after conditioning on the message. The paper compares the remaining information about a spherical signal when the auxiliary observations use those actual rows versus independent replacement rows. The replacement preserves the signal/message marginal and the product signal/row marginal; it does **not** preserve their full joint distribution.

Write \(\sigma\) for uniform probability on \(S^{d-1}\). The principal comparison, **Theorem 4.1**, uses

\[
m=\lfloor d/10\rfloor,\qquad
\ell=\lfloor d/2\rfloor,\qquad r=\ell-m.
\]

Here \(S\sim\sigma\), \(A\) consists of \(m\) independent standard Gaussian rows and is independent of \(S\), while the countable message \(W\) may have any joint dependence on \((S,A)\). Append \(r\) fresh rows \(C\), independent of \((S,A,W)\). Separately draw an independent \(\ell\)-row matrix \(G\), independent of \((S,W)\). If the message entropy in **nats** satisfies \(H(W)\le d^2\), then, for sufficiently large \(d\),

\[
I(S;W\mid G,GS)
\le I(S;W\mid A,C,AS,CS)+Kd
\]

for an absolute \(K\). The two sides use their indicated different experiments.

The main streaming consequence, **Theorem 8.4**, concerns finite persistent memory and exact fresh Gaussian observations. If \(M(d)=o(d^2)\) bits, the deterministic finite sample horizon is \(T(d)\), and a learner achieves uniform-sphere angular success probability at least \(3/5\) at accuracy \(0<\epsilon(d)\le1/10\), then

\[
T(d)\ge c\,d\log(1/\epsilon(d))
\]

for an absolute positive \(c\) and all sufficiently large \(d\). The eventual dimension threshold can depend on the memory sequence. The other four proof routes use an original success premise of \(2/3\); their different row dimensions and intermediate hypotheses must remain attached to the corresponding results.

This is an information-comparison and sample-lower-bound paper. It supplies neither a finite-bit matching learner nor an empirical benchmark.

## 2. Exact learner and randomness model

**Definition 1.1, p. 3**, pre-generates iid \(X_t\sim N(0,I_d)\), independent of the signal, and exact labels \(Y_t=\langle X_t,S\rangle\). The stream is observed once in its prescribed order: the learner cannot select a query row or replay an earlier observation.

At each index, including index zero, the persistent state has at most \(2^M\) values. A transition can use the current state, index, entire current pair and fresh randomness carrying no additional signal/sample information. Computation within a transition is unrestricted; all information retained for later samples must enter the finite state. Nonuniform rules depending on \(d,\epsilon,t\) are allowed.

A shared rule seed \(\Xi\) and initial state \(U_0\) may depend on one another, but the **pair** must be jointly independent of the signal and complete row sequence. Joint measurability of the original randomized experiment is required on standard Borel spaces. Per-seed transition/stopping rules may be Borel or measurable in the completion of Gaussian-row measure times Lebesgue-label measure; this convention does not replace the original joint-measurability requirement.

The stopping index satisfies \(0\le\tau\le T\). The output kernel uses only the terminal state and \(\tau\), with fresh output randomness. In particular, the last real-valued sample may affect the output only through that state. The clock therefore contributes a finite terminal-label multiplicity, explicitly charged later.

Lemma 1.2 fixes a shared seed by averaging the **uniform-prior** success probability while retaining the conditional initialization law. It does not assert that the same fixed seed succeeds on every fixed signal. A worst-signal guarantee implies the required prior-average guarantee by integration; this is the direction used in the corollaries.

All auxiliary projections introduced in the proofs are analysis variables, never additional learner inputs.

## 3. Information identity and exact densities

### 3.1 Finite reference measures and density versions

The entropy convention in §2 permits a finite positive reference measure \(Q\), of mass \(c\):

\[
D_{\mathrm{fin}}(P\Vert Q)
=D(P\Vert Q/c)-\log c.
\]

It can be negative. Data processing is applied after normalizing by \(c\), and the same mass correction is subtracted after pushforward. Treating this quantity as an ordinary nonnegative KL divergence would misstate the finite-fiber argument.

For \(\nu\ll\sigma\) and a full-row-rank projection with fewer than \(d-1\) rows, the projected law has a Lebesgue density. The paper fixes a common jointly Borel version through shrinking cube averages, with a prescribed fallback where the finite limit fails. For the uniform sphere the explicit projection density uses the beta/gamma coefficient, the power \((1-\lVert y\rVert^2)^{(d-\ell-2)/2}\) after orthonormal reduction, and the Jacobian \(\det(gg^\top)^{-1/2}\).

Lemma 2.3 establishes the positive and negative log-integrability needed for the later entropy subtractions. For bounded densities \(\nu\le H\sigma\), the first projection-density moment is controlled by the inverse-distance energy with kernel \(\lVert s-u\rVert^{-\ell}\). Its local integrability uses \(d-1>\ell\). The corresponding actual conditional image law remains absolutely continuous when a finite-entropy message is correlated with the rows.

### 3.2 Why row information must be retained

Let \(P_1\) be the aligned experiment, \(P_0\) the independent-row experiment and \(J_i=I_i(S;W\mid G,Y)\). Proposition 2.4 gives

\[
J_0-J_1
=h_0(Y\mid G,W)-h_1(Y\mid G,W)
-I_1(S;G\mid W).
\]

The final negative term pays for the discrepancy between the actual conditional projection law and the projection of the message posterior \(\nu_W\). Contraction of that discrepancy yields

\[
J_0-J_1
\le h_0(Y\mid G,W)
 +\mathbb E_1\log p_{\nu_W}(G,Y).
\]

The argument first establishes finite quantities; it does not cancel unspecified infinite entropies. A deterministic refinement \(E=E(S,W)\) adds an \(H(E\mid W)\) comparison cost but does not change
\(I(A;W,E\mid S)=I(A;W\mid S)\).
This lets geometric labels assist the analysis without increasing the row-selection information charge.

## 4. Mixed shared/fresh moments and critical-radius refinement

### 4.1 The local geometric estimate

**Theorem 3.1** takes \(n=d-1\), \(m\) shared rows, \(r\) fresh rows, \(\ell=m+r<n\) and integer moment order \(q\ge1\), with
\(\ell-m-(q-1)\ge1\).
A finite nonnegative spherical measure \(\rho\) must satisfy, for every ambient center and radius,

\[
\rho(B(z,t))\le Bt^\ell,\qquad
\rho(B(z,t))\le Dt^n.
\]

For every fixed \(s\) and smoothing radius \(\delta>0\),

\[
\left[\mathbb E_X
 \left(\mathbb E_Z p_{\rho,\delta}((X;Z),(X;Z)s)\right)^q
\right]^{1/q}
\le e^{Cd}B\bigl(1+\log^+(D/B)\bigr).
\]

The fresh-row expectation is **inside** the power; the shared-row expectation is outside it. Under \(\rho\ll\sigma\), the exact cube-density version follows for \(\rho\)-almost every target \(s\), not automatically for every target.

The proof expands into \(q\) signal copies. Gaussian volume contributes products of inverse radial lengths and an inverse Gram determinant. Gram–Schmidt turns that determinant into inverse distances from successive differences to earlier spans. A net on a span of dimension at most \(q-1\), followed by the two ball-growth estimates, controls each tube integral. The positive transverse gap prevents angular divergence; the \(n-\ell\) gap controls small radial scales. Summing the scales between the two bounds produces the logarithm.

The separate actual-row-tilt result first applies the every-target **smoothed** bound under the product Gaussian law. An entropy variational inequality charges \(I(X;W\mid S)/q\le H(W)/q\). Actual conditional image densities then permit Lebesgue differentiation under the actual law; Fatou is applied to a nonnegative logarithmic expression. This order avoids transferring a \(\rho\)-null exceptional set to a different target distribution. Negative-log integrability remains an additional application obligation.

The brief localization-companion discussion substitutes \(m=r=q=\lfloor d/16\rfloor\) and total dimension \(p=2\lfloor d/16\rfloor\), with \(\rho(ds)=J_\nu(s)^{-1}\nu(ds)\). Its stated ball constants are \(2^p\) and \(2^{p+n}H\). The normalization and actual-law application in that companion are separate obligations, not full reading or certification of OAI271.

### 4.2 Critical radius: Theorem 4.1

For a message value \(w\), its posterior \(F_w\) is bounded by \(D_w\sigma\), \(D_w=1/\Pr(W=w)\). On dyadic radii \(R_j=2^{1-j}\), choose the first maximum of

\[
b_w(s)=\max_{j\ge0}R_j^{-\ell}F_w(B(s,R_j)),
\]

and retain that index together with a logarithmic mass level. The bounded posterior makes the small-radius tail vanish. The number of possible labels is at most \(C(d+1+\log D_w)^2\), so their conditional entropy is at most
\(C\log(d+1+H(W))\).

Inside a refined class of conditional mass \(\eta\), the normalized posterior has a density bound \(D_w/\eta\) and an \(\ell\)-dimensional ball bound proportional to \(4^\ell e^q/\eta\), where here \(q\) denotes the logarithmic **level**, not the moment order. A packing at the selected radius bounds the independent projection entropy by \(-q+Cd\): the covering count and Gaussian scale cancel the radius.

The mixed moment with moment order \(\lfloor d/10\rfloor\) bounds the aligned expected log density by the corresponding \(+q\), a \(Cd\) term, refinement entropy, a logarithmic posterior-bound term and the row-information charge \(H(W)/\lfloor d/10\rfloor\). The two level terms cancel. The final budget is of the form

\[
Cd+\frac{H(W)}{\lfloor d/10\rfloor}
 +2H(E\mid W)+\log(1+H(W,E)),
\]

which is \(O(d)\) when \(H(W)\le d^2\). The refined true signal has exactly the refined posterior law, so the almost-everywhere density version is applicable here.

## 5. Two other density-level comparisons

The p. 17 table explicitly changes the meanings of row-count symbols between the two arguments.

| Comparison | Total auxiliary rows | Actual rows | Moment order | Message condition and cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Critical radius, §4 | \(\lfloor d/2\rfloor\) | \(\lfloor d/10\rfloor\) | \(\lfloor d/10\rfloor\) | Countable \(W\), \(H(W)\le d^2\); \(Cd\) |
| One level, Proposition 5.3 | \(\lfloor d/4\rfloor\) | \(\lfloor d/32\rfloor\) | \(\lfloor d/16\rfloor\) | Finite \(U\); \(C[d+\log(1+d+H(U))+H(U)/d]\) |
| Two labels, Proposition 5.4 | \(\lfloor d/2\rfloor\) | \(\lfloor d/8\rfloor\) | \(\lfloor d/8\rfloor\) | Finite \(V\), \(H(V)\le d^2\); \(Cd\) |

In the **one-level** argument, only a dyadic level of the largest normalized ball mass is retained. A net partition and a bounded number of neighboring centers control a weighted entropy average. The scale index is not retained, so a logarithmic count of possible scales remains. On a level class of weight \(w_a\), the two growth constants both contain \(1/w_a\), whose cancellation in their ratio is essential. The expected logarithm of \(1/w_a\) costs the level-label entropy. The lower bound from the independent projection cancels the level's positive log-density contribution.

The **two-label** argument keeps the first maximizing radius and the dyadic mass level. Its covering bound directly cancels the radius, much as in §4. However, it also constructs a Gaussian-mollifier liminf density. The regularized Gram expression has \(K+\delta^2I\), and two Fatou steps give the needed moment bound for **every fixed target**. Actual image absolute continuity identifies this version where it is used. This is a different density-version route from simply declaring the cube-limit estimate valid everywhere.

These are complementary comparison statements with different quantitative interfaces; combining their strongest-looking parameters into one theorem would be unsupported.

## 6. Hybrid comparison through independent kernels

**Proposition 6.1** considers an actual block of \(m\le h_0=\lfloor d/8\rfloor\) observations, an incoming finite state \(U\), and outgoing finite state \(V\) with \(H(V)\le B\). The rows are independent of \((S,U)\), and the block transition is conditionally a kernel of the incoming state and actual data.

Let \(J_k(V)=I(S;V\mid D'_k)\) for \(k\) fresh auxiliary observations. Average over \(k_0=\lceil d/4\rceil\) through \(k_1=\lfloor d/2\rfloor\), write \(N_k=k_1-k_0+1\), and set \(q=\lfloor d/32\rfloor\). Then

\[
\overline J(V)-\overline J(U)
\le Cm+\frac m{N_k}\left(C+\frac B{h_0}\right)+\frac Bq.
\]

For \(B\le d^2\), this is \(O(d)\). The construction keeps the **actual outgoing state fixed** while replacing auxiliary rows one by one; it does not rerun the learner on a replacement stream.

The residual sphere after \(j\) independent observations has squared radius
\(R^2=G_{d-j}/(G_{d-j}+G_j)\), with independent chi-square variables. The inverse-radius moment and explicit spherical projection densities supply finite label entropies.

The proof introduces a hybrid label entropy plus the information in the actual prefix. One replacement step compares successive fresh-label slopes to an actual-row entropy together with its row information. Prior-actual-label and fresh-label conditioning costs both enter; neither may be silently discarded.

For the biased next row, the reference posterior excludes that row. Its reciprocal-distance integral \(A(s)\) is at least \(1/2\) and integrable in the required experiment. Repeated independent kernels select signal copies, each normalized by its own reciprocal-distance integral. Because these are **probability kernels**, the Gaussian matrices retain their original laws. An inverse-volume bound for adaptive selections from independent Gaussian kernels supplies a high moment. Tilting only the actual row charges \(K_i/q\), where the sum of the conditional row-selection informations \(K_i\) is at most \(H(V)\).

For the complementary fresh-label slope, a truncated reciprocal-distance coupling selects \(S^*\). Its marginal need not equal the original posterior. The argument controls the cost using KL relative to the product reference and a Cauchy density for a second fresh projected label. Removing \(S^*\) costs conditional information, which cancels the distance normalization. The truncation limit yields the desired bound involving \(-\mathbb E\log A\).

Fresh conditional entropy slopes are decreasing and lie between an absolute upper bound and \(-C-B/h_0\) in the range used. Averaging the replacement inequalities over \(k\) telescopes to two endpoint slopes. Summing \(K_i\) then gives Proposition 6.1. The lower-bound application permits a short final block.

## 7. The finite Gaussian-fiber route

**Proposition 7.1** instead uses actual block size \(n=\lfloor d/10\rfloor\), total auxiliary row count \(k=\lfloor d/3\rfloor\), and an outgoing alphabet of cardinality at most \(N\), with \(\log N\le d^2\). Its potential is
\(\Phi(V)=I(S;V\mid C,CS)\) for fresh \(k\)-row \(C\), and it proves an \(O(d)\) increase per block.

### 7.1 Likelihoods and a finite comparison law

State functions are likelihoods \(f_v(s)=\Pr(V=v\mid S=s)\). They are bounded by one and sum to one; they are **not each normalized posteriors**. With actual routing probabilities \(h_{vw}(A,As)\), define
\(g_w(A,s)=\sum_v f_v(s)h_{vw}(A,As)\) and \(f_w(s)=\mathbb E_Ag_w(A,s)\).

The real law \(P\) has density \(f_v(s)h_{vw}(A,As)\). A finite comparison measure \(Q\) has density

\[
p_C(Cs)\,\overline f_v(C,Cs)\,h_{vw}(A,As).
\]

It keeps the actual routing weights. Its mass is
\(m_Q=\mathbb E_C\int p_C(z)^2\,dz\), finite and positive by the inverse determinant moments of Gaussian rows. Cancellation gives

\[
D_{\mathrm{fin}}(P\Vert Q)
=\Phi(V)+\mathbb E[-\log p_C(CS)]
\le\Phi(V)+\frac k2\log(2\pi e).
\]

Here \(CS\) is Gaussian **unconditionally**; the argument does not say it is Gaussian conditional on \(C\). Normalize \(Q/m_Q\) for data processing and then subtract the same \(\log m_Q\).

### 7.2 Exact marginal and common local mass

Lemma 7.2 proves a two-point Borel measure identity for the equal-projection fiber. Its marginal density is

\[
q_w(s)=(2\pi)^{-k/2}
\int \lVert s-t\rVert^{-k}
\,\mathbb E_{A\sim\gamma_{n,\perp(s-t)}}g_w(A,t)\,d\sigma(t).
\]

The number of perpendicular Gaussian rows is the actual count \(n\). The remaining \(k-n\) rows have been integrated out. There is no further normalization in this formula.

The identity is first proved through Gaussian mollification and two dominated integration orders. Equality for continuous tests extends to all bounded Borel tests by uniqueness of finite Borel measures. This extension permits actual measurable routing, rather than an unjustified transfer of a real-observation null set to a perpendicular-row law.

The common local mass is
\(L_w(s)=\sup_{r>0}r^{-k}\int_{B(s,r)}f_w(t)\,d\sigma(t)\).
The paper proves

\[
\mathbb E\frac{L_W(S)}{\overline f_W(C',C'S)}\le e^{Kd},
\qquad
\mathbb E\frac{q_W(S)}{L_W(S)}\le e^{Kd}.
\]

For the first estimate, spherical projection bounds and finite-overlap nets handle dyadic scales. For the second, \(\nu_w=(f_w/L_w)\sigma\) is a finite measure with an ambient ball-growth estimate, not a probability measure. The cutoff

\[
J=1+\left\lceil\frac{\log_2N}{d-1-k}\right\rceil,\qquad r_*=2^{-J}
\]

makes \(Nr_*^{d-1-k}\le1\). The near part pays for the state count through this cutoff. On far annuli, an inverse-Gram moment controls mixtures of perpendicular Gaussian measures. A uniform \(L^\ell\) estimate for slab approximations and duality establish the needed Borel-test bound before absolute continuity is invoked. Applying it to \(0\le g_w\le1\) produces \(f_w^{1-1/\ell}\); summing over states costs \(N^{1/\ell}\).

Finally,
\(\log(f_W/\overline f_W)=\log(f_W/q_W)+\log(q_W/L_W)+\log(L_W/\overline f_W)\).
The finite-reference comparison controls the first term and Jensen controls the other two. Negative-part integrability of a finite-measure log-density ratio is bounded by the comparison mass divided by \(e\). Thus all terms in the split are well defined.

The cardinality assumption is substantive: it enters both the small-radius cutoff and the state sum. This route is not stated as an arbitrary finite-entropy substitution.

## 8. Measurability, stopping and terminal information

### 8.1 Borel extension under the correct experiment

Lemma 8.1 selects Borel versions of the fixed-seed rules. Finitely many states and indices give one common null set under Gaussian rows times Lebesgue labels. In the uniform-sphere experiment, each current exact observation pair has a density relative to that reference measure, so the coupled original and modified trajectories agree almost surely. The same unconditional pair-null bound covers whichever state is reached.

This preserves the fixed-seed **prior-average joint law**, not the rule on every individual signal. Global Borel versions are fixed before constructing the fiber comparison law. That ordering matters because its perpendicular Gaussian rows need not avoid arbitrary null sets from the real law.

### 8.2 A finite stopping clock is explicitly paid

Lemma 8.2 absorbs stopped states, storing the terminal index as well as the original state, and pads to full blocks. The augmented alphabet is at most

\[
N=(T+2)2^M.
\]

There are at most \((T+1)2^M\) terminal state/index labels. Spherical cap volume gives, including randomized terminal outputs,

\[
p_{\mathrm{success}}\le (T+1)2^M\epsilon^{d-1}.
\]

Put \(L=\log(1/\epsilon)\). On the only branch requiring proof, \(T\le dL\), success at least \(3/5\) implies

\[
dL=O(M+1),\quad L=o(d),\quad T=o(d^2),\quad
\log N=M\log2+\log(T+2)=o(d^2).
\]

These are consequences on the short-horizon branch, not a prior restriction on the accuracy sequence. If \(T\ge dL\), the desired lower bound already holds.

A separate unrestricted-data argument shows \(T>d/4\) for sufficiently large \(d\). Otherwise, even granting every pre-generated observation leaves a sufficiently large residual sphere with substantial probability. This excludes \(T=0\) and absorbs the additive final-block cost.

### 8.3 Conditional information required by an accurate output

Lemma 8.3 compares the true law with a reference that breaks signal/state dependence conditional on fresh auxiliary observations. If their residual radius \(R\) obeys \(\Pr(R<\rho)\le\eta<p\) and \(2\epsilon<\rho\), then

\[
I(S;V\mid G,GS)
\ge(p-\eta)(d-k-1)\log\frac{\rho}{2\epsilon}-\log2.
\]

The proof uses a cap bound on the residual sphere and binary relative entropy for the success event. The elementary radius-tail estimate is
\(\eta\le k/[d(1-\rho^2)]\). The critical-radius route uses a stronger chi-square concentration estimate.

| Streaming route | Original success premise | Terminal radius / bad-radius bound | Specific bookkeeping |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Critical radius, Theorem 8.4 | \(3/5\) | \(\rho=1/2\), bad probability \(O(1/d)\) | Terminal success mass at least \(1/2\); actual block \(\lfloor d/10\rfloor\) |
| One level, §8.4 | \(2/3\) | \(\rho=1/2,\ \eta\le1/3\) | Actual block \(\lfloor d/32\rfloor\); logarithm \(L-\log4\) |
| Two labels, §8.4 | \(2/3\) | \(\rho=1/4,\ \eta\le8/15\) | Actual block \(\lfloor d/8\rfloor\); remaining mass \(2/15\), logarithm \(L-\log8\) |
| Hybrid, §8.5 | \(2/3\) | \(\rho=2/5,\ \eta\le25/42\) | Remaining mass \(1/14\), logarithm \(L-\log5\), uniform over the averaged auxiliary counts |
| Fiber, Corollary 8.7 | \(2/3\), then \(3/5\) after fixation | \(\rho=1/2,\ \eta\le4/9\) | Fix all remaining random choices for a deterministic decoder; cardinality bound \(\log N\le d^2\) |

For the fiber route, the paper realizes initialization, transitions, stopping and output by a complete independent random-choice vector after the shared seed is fixed. Averaging selects a realization retaining success at least \(3/5\). The joint independence condition leaves the signal/row law unchanged; unused random coordinates do not become persistent learner state. This extra fixation is not required by all other routes.

Each route combines \(O(d)\) information increase per \(O(d)\)-sample block with terminal \(\Omega(dL)\) information. Initial potential is zero by joint data independence. The five applications derive the same order of sample lower bound while preserving their own interfaces.

## 9. Citation dependencies and scope of the claimed advance

All 12 source and printed references were read as bibliography entries, together with their roles in the manuscript. This does not count as independent reading of every cited paper.

- **Austin (2020)** supplies conditional-information chain-rule background.
- **Dagan–Kur–Shamir, COLT 2019**, is cited with Theorems 3 and 10 located in the published PMLR version. Its hard joint-data/empirical-regression setting is distinguished from fresh exact observations of one fixed signal.
- **Dupuis–Mao (2022)** supplies the entropy variational perspective used for selection charges.
- **Mattila (1975)** is an inverse-distance projection-energy precedent, not a citation for the paper's full mixed shared/fresh moment.
- **OpenAI, Localization costs and information growth for exact Gaussian observations (2026)** is the separate OAI271 companion. The local specialization is described above; that manuscript remains independently unread here.
- **Raz (2016)** and **Raz (2017)** provide memory/sample lower-bound context. The source fixes a v1 arXiv version for the former and gives FOCS/ECCC locators for the latter.
- **Russo–Zou (2016)** and **Xu–Raginsky (2017)** give information-theoretic adaptive-selection comparisons. Here the test is an exact projection density; the locally proved high moment yields a row-information coefficient \(1/q\).
- **Sharan–Sidford–Valiant, STOC 2019**, with full-paper arXiv1904.08544v2 (October 2020), provides a projection-moment antecedent. The source distinguishes its small uniform label noise, Euclidean accuracy \(d^{-r}\), stated range of \(r\), and \(\Omega(d\log r)\) consequence from the exact-observation/angular-accuracy statement here.
- **Steinhardt–Duchi, COLT 2015**, supplies streaming linear-prediction context.
- **van Erven–Harremoës, arXiv1206.2459v2 (2014)** is the specified divergence reference edition.

The paper's contribution is the actual-versus-fresh information comparison with finite-message selection and several complete proof routes, together with a precise persistent-bit application. It should not be summarized merely as the already familiar observation that a target vector contains \(d\log(1/\epsilon)\) bits: the proof bounds how quickly a restricted persistent learner can accumulate the relevant conditional information from fresh exact samples.

## 10. Limitations and useful follow-up boundaries

The finite persistent state does not charge unrestricted instantaneous arithmetic, the description of nonuniform rules or a data-independent shared seed. The observation model has exact real-valued Gaussian pairs. The lower bound is not automatically about noisy categorical data, chosen/repeated queries, recurrent neural activations stored as real numbers, or a predictor's cumulative log loss.

The horizon is deterministic and finite even though stopping can be adaptive below it. An expected-sample-time theorem would need additional reasoning. The prior-average success and fixed-signal implications are separated explicitly. Entropy-limited countable-message results and the cardinality-limited fiber argument are not interchangeable.

No learning of an unknown transition structure, randomized posterior-weight quantization, expert-mixture regret or physical gate implementation is derived. No FP transfer is proposed. Its resource-accounting distinctions are useful literature context, but a transfer would require a separate model mapping and proof.

Follow-up reading may compare OAI270's posterior-replica argument or OAI271's localization organization when their distinct tools become useful. Their unread status is retained. This note does not recursively certify their proofs or the historical citation tree. No numerical experiments, empirical uncertainty estimates or reproduction artifacts are claimed by this manuscript.
