## 1. The original problem and claimed theorem

Theorem 1.1 claims infinitely many primes \(p\) for which \(p-1\) is squarefree and has an even number of prime factors. Equivalently, \(\mu(p-1)=1\). Squarefreeness makes the conclusion agree for \(\Omega\), counting factors with multiplicity, and \(\omega\), counting distinct factors.

The proof writes \(p=2u+1\). It constructs nonnegative weights on odd \(u\) with \(\Omega(u)\) odd, detects prime values of \(2u+1\), then removes the nonsquarefree part at a scale smaller than the surviving prime mass. The stated theorem is infinitude. It does not assert a density of one half, an asymptotic for all primes with either Liouville sign, or an effective numerical threshold.

The paper explicitly distinguishes parity from smoothness. Its imported structural source is OAI-1006-021, *Weighted dilation graphs, smooth shifted primes and totient fibers*, denoted S. The new ingredients claimed here are a genuine prime in the designated long slot and a common group-supported Liouville sign at both endpoints of the graph correlation. The unsigned shifted-correlation statement from S does not by itself provide either extension.

The proof has four linked parts:

1. A long-prime polynomial estimate at frequencies through \(x^2\).
2. A common-sign, prime-slot shifted correlation with discrepancy required at only one endpoint.
3. Nonnegative parity-selecting candidate weights, with Type II and ordinary congruence distribution.
4. Initial sieving, bounded rough-integer proxies, subtraction of unbalanced composites, and a gap-uniform upper bound on balanced semiprimes.

## 2. Conventions and classical inputs — §2

Throughout,
\[
 L=\log x,\qquad T=\log L,\qquad W=\exp(\sqrt L).
\]
Every auxiliary exponent is fixed before \(x\to\infty\). Constants may depend on earlier fixed choices. No effective threshold is asserted. Dirichlet characters are zero on nonunits, and \(P^-(1)=\infty\).

The recorded classical inputs are the prime number theorem, Mertens estimates, Siegel–Walfisz for fixed log-power moduli, the primitive multiplicative large sieve, fixed divisor moments, and continuous and separated-point Dirichlet-polynomial mean squares. The last two have absolute constants independent of how the coefficients were formed. This is used with an explicit factorial estimate when a prime polynomial is raised to a growing power, rather than applying a fixed-order divisor theorem at a growing order.

The first-derivative test includes separation of the derivative from integers. A lower bound on the absolute derivative alone would not state the needed hypothesis. A second-derivative test is also recorded. Prime interval estimates use fixed logarithmic intervals or relative lengths that are fixed negative powers of \(L\), with the absolute prime number theorem error selected first.

Smooth separation in a fixed number of logarithmic coordinates has log-power Fourier integral mass. Its frequency cutoff exponent can be fixed before the final error target, increasing only the integration-by-parts order afterwards. The later allocation has \(O(T)\) one-dimensional transitions. It is treated as a product of fixed one-dimensional Fourier norms with a telescoped tail bound, not by invoking the fixed-dimension lemma in a growing dimension.

## 3. The long-prime polynomial estimate — §3

Lemma 3.1 states that, for fixed \(0<\tau<\eta<1\), \(C,A>0\), a sufficiently large fixed \(B_0\) gives
\[
 \left|\sum_{p\in I}\chi(p)p^{-1+it}\right|\ll L^{-A}
\]
uniformly for
\[
 x^\tau/2\le N\le x^\eta,\quad I\subset[N,2N],\quad
 q\le L^C,\quad L^{B_0}\le |t|\le x^2,
\]
for every character modulo \(q\), including principal and imprimitive characters.

### Quantitative power sums

For \(K=k(k+1)/2\), the power-sum moment is bounded by
\[
 J_{s,k}(M)\le \exp(C_1k^{C_2})M^{2s-K+1/100}
\]
for some \(k\le s\le C_1k^4\). The argument uses the classical \(p\)-adic Vinogradov method. Wooley is cited for its exposition, without invoking the modern efficient-congruencing theorem.

The iteration starts with error exponent \(K\) at \(s=k\). Degenerate tuples with fewer than \(k\) distinct coordinates contribute a square-root moment. For nondegenerate tuples, a list of \(2k^3+5\) primes near \(M^{1/k}\) contains one avoiding both Vandermonde products. Congruences of power sums modulo \(r^j\), Newton identities, and an invertible Vandermonde Jacobian bound the second initial list by \(k!r^{K-k}\) possibilities. Remaining coordinates divide by \(r\). Their prescribed power-sum differences are bounded by the zero Fourier coefficient of a nonnegative moment.

The recurrence replaces \(\epsilon_s\) by \((1-1/k)\epsilon_s\). Small \(M\), coordinate permutations, prime selection and rounding have explicit degree-dependent costs. After \(O(k\log k)\) iterations, the error is at most \(1/100\), with logarithmic constant polynomial in \(k\).

### Logarithmic phases and the high-height strip

The resulting progression estimate covers
\[
 e^{L/T^2}\le N'\le2x^5,\quad q\le L^C,\quad
 e^{L/(2T^2)}\le |v|\le4x^3,
\]
and gives cancellation
\[
 \sum_{\substack{n\in I\\n\equiv a\pmod q}}n^{iv}
 \ll (N'/q)\exp(-L/T^{C_3})
\]
on arbitrary subintervals and all residue classes.

After setting \(H=N'/q\), \(y=\log|v|/\log H\), low \(y\) uses the monotone derivative test. Otherwise average shifts through \(H^{3/4}\) and Taylor expand at degree \(\lceil4y+8\rceil\). One coefficient bounds occupancy of a torus box by \(\exp(O(k))H^{4/5}\). Iterated fundamental-theorem-of-calculus bounds on box suprema, combined with the moment, give \(\exp(-cL/T^{10})\) saving while \(k\ll T^2\), \(s\ll T^8\).

Write \(D(s,\chi)\) for the Dirichlet \(L\)-function. Periodicity and truncation first establish \(\log|D|\ll_C T^2\) near real part one, within width \(10r_*\), \(r_*=T^4/L\), at heights from \(1/2\) to \(3x^3\). Jensen bounds the local zero count. Disk Blaschke factors give a local logarithmic-derivative formula. The classical nonnegative polynomial \(3+4\cos\theta+\cos2\theta\) then excludes zeros in
\[
 \operatorname{Re}s\ge1-cT^2/L,\qquad
 1\le|\operatorname{Im}s|\le x^3
\]
and bounds \(D'/D\) by \(O_C(L)\) on a narrower strip. The argument is deliberately away from height zero. It does not remove a possible real exceptional zero, which remains compatible with the later use of ineffective Siegel–Walfisz.

### Mellin inversion

Smooth the interval at width \(L^{-A-4}\) and first insert the von Mangoldt weight. The Mellin integral begins on real part \(1/L\). A fixed logarithmic height cutoff is chosen before \(B_0\), so after a short leftward contour shift all relevant heights remain away from zero. The principal-character pole lies outside the rectangle. The factor \(N^z\) contributes \(\exp(-c\tau T^2/2)\), which beats every fixed log power. Endpoint smoothing, horizontal edges and full tails are retained. Removing higher prime powers and partial-summing away \(\log p\) proves the stated estimate uniformly for every subinterval.

## 4. Prime-slot shifted correlation with a common sign — §4

### Exact hypotheses and measures

There are \(\asymp T\) small and big groups, with harmonic masses \(V_g\) bounded above and below. Small groups lie in \([\exp(L^a),\exp(L^b)]\), big groups in \([\exp(L^c),\exp(L^d)]\), where
\[
 0<a<b<c<d<.47.
\]
Every big group is a full prime interval. The integer \(n_*\) removes the entire group prime-power part. For ordered distinct marks,
\[
 W_{\mathbf t}(n)=
 q_0^{\omega_P(n)-\sum t_g}
 \prod_g\frac{(\omega_g(n))_{t_g}}{V_g^{t_g}}.
\]
Fixed mark multiplicities give log-power bounds, even with arbitrary bounded tests of the lists.

The common endpoint sign is either identically one or
\[
 \varepsilon(n)=(-1)^{\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}v_p(n)}.
\]
It is real, completely multiplicative and of modulus one. The same choice is used at both endpoints. This is a sign based on total multiplicity, while the marked weight uses distinct divisor counts.

Each invariant core has the form
\[
 F(n)=\sum_{mpr=n_*}
 \alpha_m\chi_0(m)m^{i\sigma_0}
 \mathbf1_{p\in I_p,\ p\ {\rm prime}}p^{i\sigma_1}c_r,
\]
with log-power bounded coefficients, characters and frequencies, \(\alpha\) supported on \(W\)-rough integers, and a prime slot inside \([x^\tau,x^\eta]\), where \(0<\tau<\eta<1/4\). Theorem 4.1 assumes arbitrary log-power reciprocal character–Mellin discrepancy for the actual \(\alpha\) at the first endpoint, on every interval below \(x^2\). No discrepancy is required at the second endpoint.

It concludes arbitrary log-power cancellation for the harmonic correlation of \(\overline F(z)G(z+k)\), marked weights at both ends, common signs, smooth size cutoff and bounded log-power twists, with \(Z=xL^{O(1)}\) and \(0<|k|\le L^C\).

The imported graph contracts specify counting measure in physical position and mass \(\prod_gV_g^{-M}\) on ordered distinct lists, \(M=J+\ell\), with sufficiently large fixed \(J\). Free labels are independently harmonic and forbidden from both endpoint lists. The ideal space permits repetitions. Symmetrization occurs at both ends. The residual ideal family contains one raw pattern and signed comparison patterns, with two global cell-and-character kernels rather than one expansion per group.

### Endpoint bounds and the sign cancellation

A core has divisor bound \(L^{C'}\tau(n_*)^2\). For a physical list product \(P\), \(n_*\mid n/P\), and the normalized reciprocal list mass is at most one. Fixed divisor moments therefore give the required endpoint \(L^2\) norm with exponent independent of \(J\). Both the rough \(m\) and the positive-power prime slot use primes above \(W\), so the number of their factor allocations is at most \(3^{O(\sqrt L)}\). This supplies the \(\exp(O(\sqrt L))\) endpoint supremum required by transference.

After lifting, write the common shared product as \(D_R\), free product as \(D_C\), and \(n=D_Rw\), \(n'=D_Rw'\). The shift becomes \(w'=w+kD_C\). The factor \(1/D_R\) converts the shared lists to harmonic probability laws, leaving the two unshared marked weights with their exact normalizations.

The decisive identity is
\[
 \varepsilon(D_Rw)\varepsilon(D_Rw')
 =\varepsilon(D_R)^2\varepsilon(w)\varepsilon(w')
 =\varepsilon(w)\varepsilon(w').
\]
This holds even on overlap configurations. It uses the common real sign at both endpoints. Shared collisions and free-label exclusions are separately bounded by exponentially small harmonic costs and divisor moments. The raw pattern is exactly the desired correlation.

The Fourier cutoff exponent is fixed before the ideal family and \(J\). Later improvements in precision increase only differentiation orders. Endpoint norms, kernel accuracy, \(J\), arc estimates, prime-polynomial accuracy and finally the discrepancy exponent have a stated noncircular order.

### Comparison terms and the three frequency regimes

Each comparison contains two nonempty independent free products. Unique factorization bounds their product atoms by \(L^C/n\). A bilinear exponential estimate handles minor arcs, including the log-power shift \(k\). Major arcs require only local Fourier energy at the discrepancy-bearing endpoint.

Extract one small-prime mark. Square exceptions have negligible coefficient-square mass. The core becomes a four-factor convolution of the original rough \(\alpha_m\), the extracted small prime, the designated long prime, and one bounded residual sequence containing all remaining marks. The residual need not be a unit modulo the arc denominator. Splitting by its gcd and then applying exact character orthogonality covers that case, including imprimitive characters.

The local Mellin inequality has first-term factor \(1/Y\) and error \((H/Y)^2\sum|a_w|^2\). Smooth separation and translated-interval mean squares control full Fourier tails. Only four convolution factors enter fixed divisor moments. The remaining target is an integrated square of
\[
 P_s(t)P_l(t)\mathcal R(t)
\]
up to time \(2LY/H\), not a pointwise bound multiplied by an uncontrolled length.

When \(P_s\) is small, the other factors have scale \(Y/P\) larger than the time range because \(b<c\). Exceptional unit intervals number at most
\[
 \exp\bigl(O(L^b+L^{1-a}\log L)\bigr)=Y^{o(1)}.
\]
The growing-power coefficient estimate is explicitly \(j_0!(C/P)^{j_0}\).

The residual scale is at least \(Y^{1-\eta-o(1)}>Y^{.6}\). First- and second-derivative tests bound its sparse Gram matrix by entries
\[
 O\!\left(\frac{U}{1+|t_i-t_j|}+Y^{1/2}\right),
\]
and the subpower number of exceptional intervals gives row norm \(O(U\log Y)\). This yields a sparse residual energy bound. On high exceptional times, the long-prime lemma saves logarithms. On low times, the actual \(\alpha\) discrepancy does so. After paying all fixed costs, subtraction from the residual graph pairing leaves the raw correlation.

## 5. Nonnegative candidate weights, mass and squarefree loss — §5

### Prime geometry and parity

Choose a fixed sufficiently large even \(r_0\) and
\[
 c_1=1,\quad c_2=6/5,\quad c_3=3/2,\quad c_4=9/5,\qquad
 s'=\frac1{r_0(c_1+c_2)},\quad s_j=s'2^{-j}L.
\]
The ordinary bands \(Q_j\) have \(\log p\in[c_1s_j,c_2s_j]\), ending when \(s_j\) reaches the scale \(20T\). Additional group bands use \([c_3s_j,c_4s_j]\) inside exponent ranges \([L^{1/10},L^{1/5}]\) and \([L^{3/10},L^{2/5}]\). These intervals are mutually disjoint, including ordinary versus group bands. All primes are at least \(L^{20}\), and harmonic masses approach \(\log(6/5)\).

There are \(r_0\) labelled prime slots in each ordinary band. Divide their ordered tuple count by \((r_0!)^{j_x+1}\), allowing repetitions, to define \(a(v)\). On its support,
\[
 a(v)=\prod_{j,p\in Q_j}\frac1{e_p!}\le1.
\]
With \(q_0=1/2\), one mark per group and
\(\mathcal E(u)=(-1)^{\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}v_p(u)}\), define
\[
 A_0(u)=W_1(u)a(u_*),\qquad
 A(u)=A_0(u)\frac{1-\mathcal E(u)}2,\qquad
 X_A=\sum_uA(u)\Psi(u/x).
\]
Then \(0\le A\le A_0\le L^{C_0}\). The ordinary part has exactly \(r_0(j_x+1)\) factors with multiplicity, an even number. The group projection makes its factor count odd. Every supported \(u\) is odd, so \(\Omega(u)\) is odd and \(\Omega(2u)\) is even.

### Harmonic localization and tail

The ordinary harmonic mass is
\[
 H_Q=\prod_j\frac{V_{Q,j}^{r_0}}{r_0!}=L^{O(1)}
\]
with both upper and lower fixed log-power bounds. For one group,
\[
 Z_g(q)=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_g}\left(1+\frac q{p-1}\right),\qquad
 H_g=\frac{Z_g'(q_0)}{V_g}
 =\frac{Z_g(q_0)}{V_g}\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_g}\frac1{p-1+q_0}.
\]
Thus \(1\le Z_g(q_0)\le H_g\le C\), and \(1\le H_P=\prod_gH_g\le L^C\). The distinction between an occurring prime's harmonic mass \(1/(p-1)\) and a one-slot mass \(1/p\) is retained.

Under normalized independent harmonic sampling, each group has both even and odd total multiplicity with probability bounded below. Conditioning on all but one group gives a uniform positive lower bound on odd total group parity. An exponential moment with exponent \(L^{-2/5}\) gives
\[
 \sum_{\substack{r>x^\sigma\\r_*=1}}\frac{W_1(r)}r
 \le L^C\exp(-\sigma L^{3/5}).
\]

The exact identity
\[
 X_A=H_QH_P\,
 \mathbb E[U\Psi(U/x)\mathbf1_{\mathcal E(R)=-1}]
\]
localizes the mass. Leave one \(Q_0\) slot free. Its normalized harmonic mass in a logarithmic interval of fixed length is \(O(1/L)\), giving the upper bound. For the lower bound, constrain only finitely many initial ordinary slots near their midpoints, control the group part by its tail, and keep odd group parity. The free prime can then place \(U/x\) in a fixed interval where \(\Psi>0\), with harmonic probability \(\gg1/L\). Therefore
\[
 X_A\asymp (x/L)H_QH_P,\qquad X_A\gg xL^{-C_A}.
\]

### The loss is measured against detected prime mass

Coincident ordinary slots have harmonic probability \(O(TL^{-20})\). In a group, the probability a given prime occurs is \(O(1/p)\), and conditional on occurrence the probability of exponent at least two is exactly \(1/p\). Group squares therefore cost \(O(T e^{-L^{1/10}})\). Bands are disjoint and all primes odd.

Multiplying the unrestricted harmonic loss by the size cutoff gives
\[
 \sum_{2u\ {\rm not\ squarefree}}A(u)\Psi(u/x)
 =o(X_A/L).
\]
The comparison is with \(X_A/L\), the eventual prime-detection scale, rather than merely with \(X_A\).

## 6. Type II distribution and exact change of variables — §6

Theorem 6.1 concerns \(UV\asymp x\), with both factor scales between \(x^{b_*}\) and \(x^{1-b_*}\), arbitrary intervals \(K,J\) in their dyads, and log-power bounded \(\alpha,\beta\). Only \(\alpha\) must have rough support and the prescribed reciprocal character–Mellin discrepancy. The discrepancy is required for the actual sequence \(\alpha_m\mathbf1_{m\in K}\). No smoothness, discrepancy or rough-support assumption is imposed on \(\beta\). The conclusion is
\[
 \sum_{\substack{m\in K,n\in J\\mn=2u+1}}
 \alpha_m\beta_n A(u)\Psi(u/x)\ll xL^{-D_*}.
\]

### Smooth allocation with constants fixed before the diagonal choice

Split \(A\) into its unsigned and signed components, using the same sign at both later endpoints. Designate an existing ordinary prime slot in a fixed band small enough that its exponent upper bound is below \(\min(b_*/4,1/4)\). Delete a group part larger than \(x^{b_*/4}\). Its tail \(\exp(-cL^{3/5})\) absorbs the \(\exp(O(\sqrt L))\) possible rough divisors of \(2u+1\).

Set \(E=UL^{-K_0}\), put the entire group part and designated prime into \(h\), and allocate the remaining ordinary slots between \(e,h\). The smooth take/skip rule maintains
\[
 0\le R_i\le\sum_{t\ge i}\log p_t+(c_2+1)s_{j_x}.
\]
The inequality \((r_0-1)c_1/2\ge c_2+1\) ensures enough remaining logarithmic mass after a skipped slot. At termination,
\[
 EL^{-C_s}\le e\le E
\]
with \(C_s\) independent of \(K_0\).

There are \(N=O(T)\) one-dimensional transitions. A branch has Fourier norm at most \(C_F^N\), and all branches cost \((2C_F)^N=L^{C_f}\), independent of \(K_0\). Tail errors involve the high-derivative constant only once per term, so improving precision does not enlarge \(C_f\). The dependence on \(E\) lies in a scalar phase of modulus one. Frequencies are bounded by \(O(L/T)\) before the fixed cutoff separation.

The resulting coefficients \(a_e,c_r\) are bounded by fixed log powers from the original factorial normalization, independently of \(K_0\) and Fourier accuracy. All marked primes and the sign remain on \(h\).

### Diagonal, reversible determinant and endpoint identification

Cauchy in \(e,n\) gives
\[
 |B'|^2\ll EVL^{C_p}\mathcal N.
\]
The diagonal contributes \(\mathcal N_{\rm diag}\ll xL^{C_d'}\), by fixed second and sixth divisor moments. Enlarging the divisor set makes \(C_d'\) independent of \(K_0\). Since \(EV\asymp xL^{-K_0}\), \(K_0\) can now be fixed large enough for the requested saving.

For an off-diagonal pair,
\[
 mn=2eh+1,\qquad m'n=2eh'+1,
\]
there is a unique nonzero integer \(k\) with
\[
 m'-m=2ek,\quad h'-h=kn,\quad z=m'h,\quad z+k=mh'.
\]
The bound is \(|k|\ll L^{K_0+C_s}\). The converse works for either sign of \(k\): impose \(m'\equiv m\pmod{2|k|}\), set
\[
 e=\frac{m'-m}{2k}>0,\qquad n=\frac{2eh+1}{m},
\]
and use roughness of \(m\) to obtain \((m,k)=1\). The determinant identity then makes \(n\) an integer and recovers both original equations uniquely. This is an exact bijection, not merely a forward implication.

The congruence is represented by a normalized character sum of total coefficient mass one. Pulled-back cutoffs are smooth in three fixed logarithmic coordinates, including the exact \(1/m\) term in \(n\), with log-power derivative bounds once \(K_0\) is fixed. Buffers equal one on the original dyads, so they do not alter the discrepancy-bearing sequence. Absolute divisor-moment correlations control the full Fourier tails at a fixed cutoff exponent.

Both reconstructed invariant cores contain the original restricted \(\alpha\). The first is conjugated outside the core, giving the exact original square coefficient. Because all group primes are below \(W\), the group weights and signs of \(z,z+k\) are exactly those of \(h,h'\). A separated sum is \(Z\) times a harmonic correlation from Theorem 4.1, with \(Z=xL^{O(1)}\). Choose its precision after the shift count and all separation costs, and choose discrepancy exponent \(B\) last.

This formulation permits a second proxy replacement after exchanging factor names, with an already nonsmooth bounded proxy as the companion coefficient. If proxy coefficients are bounded by one, the Type II constants are fixed with that bound before the later cell precision is chosen.

## 7. Ordinary congruence distribution — §7

For odd squarefree \(d\), define
\[
 r(d)=\sum_{d\mid2u+1}A(u)\Psi(u/x)-X_A/\varphi(d).
\]
For every fixed \(0<\vartheta<1/2\) and \(D>0\), Theorem 7.1 gives
\[
 \sum_{\substack{d\le x^\vartheta\\d\ {\rm odd,\ squarefree}}}|r(d)|
 \ll xL^{-D}+X_A L^{-18}.
\]
This is below level one half. It is proved for the actual candidate weight, rather than being included in the generic sieve import.

Character orthogonality leaves both nonprincipal sums and a principal correction for \((u,d)>1\). Every prime dividing a supported \(u\) is at least \(L^{20}\), and the reciprocal totient sum is \(O(L)\), so the principal correction is \(O(X_A L^{-18})\).

Deleting one labelled \(Q_0\) slot while retaining the original factorial denominator gives
\[
 a^-(v)=\frac1{r_0}\prod_{j,p}1/e_p!,
 \qquad A(u)=\sum_{pr=u,\ p\in Q_0}b(r),\quad |b(r)|\le L^{B_0}.
\]
This remains exact when primes repeat. The two factor scales are both positive powers of \(x\).

For squarefree \(d=fh\), each character is induced by a unique primitive character of conductor \(f\mid d\). The coprimality masks are retained. Large conductors use smooth separation with its full Fourier integral and the primitive large sieve, yielding
\[
 xL^{B_2}\bigl(L^{-K}+x^{-c/2}+x^{\vartheta-1/2}\bigr).
\]
The \(h\)-sum costs a logarithm, not a factor \(x^\vartheta\). Choose \(K\) after the fixed exponent.

Small conductors use Siegel–Walfisz in the prime slot. The mask \((p,h)=1\) removes only \(O(1)\) prime terms because each such prime has positive-power size. Potentially exceptional real characters are included through the ineffective classical estimate. Choosing its accuracy after \(K\) finishes the claimed summed remainder.

## 8. Prime extraction and the constant independent of the gap — §8

### Exact imported sieve and proxy contracts

The block sieve from S has coefficient bound one, support \(d\le z^{4h+2}\), relative error \(O(e^{-h})\) uniform in growing even \(h\), and a nonnegative fixed-depth upper bound. These are distinct uses. Its hypotheses do not themselves give distribution for \(A\), which comes from §7 or an explicit lattice count.

Define
\[
 V(y)=\prod_{p\le y}(1-1/p),\quad
 V_1(y)=\prod_{3\le p\le y}(1-1/(p-1)),\quad
 \mathfrak S=2\prod_{p\ge3}\left(1-\frac1{(p-1)^2}\right).
\]
Then \(V_1(y)\sim\mathfrak S V(y)\).

The prime and \(x^\gamma\)-rough proxies are normalized on full logarithmic cells of width \(L^{-S_1}\):
\[
 B_I(m)=
 \frac{\#\{n\in C_Y:I(n)=1\}}
      {\#\{n\in C_Y:P^-(n)>W\}}
 \mathbf1_{P^-(m)>W}.
\]
Later factor restrictions may cut a cell, but do not redefine its ratio. The exact subset relation gives \(0\le B_I\le1\). For \(S_1>2B+3\), the interval-restricted difference \(I-B_I\) has the required discrepancy. These are ordinary integer-cell estimates, independent of the candidate weight.

The prime density in the balanced range is at most \(C_{\rm pr}/(LV(W))\), with \(C_{\rm pr}\) independent of both the gap \(\kappa\) and cell precision. The imported nonnegative rough density \(D_\gamma(w)\) is a finite factorization integral with the factorial denominators for unordered prime factors. Its essential identity and upper estimate are
\[
 \int_b^{1/2}D_\alpha(1-\alpha)\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha}

 D_b(1)\le\frac{e^{-\gamma_E}}b(1+O(e^{-c/b})).

Continuity is used only on fixed compact ranges with \(w>\gamma\). The unbalanced factor ranges have a fixed \(2\kappa\) margin from that boundary.

### A positive pair bound fixed before \(\kappa\)

Choose a fixed micro/macro division of ordinary bands, and a fixed exponent \(\theta\) in a gap between them. An assignment consists of all micro slots and one mark per group. Its product \(D_1\) retains multiplicities, with
\[
 D_1\le x^{1/20},\quad
 \sum_a\lambda_a/D_1\ll H_Q,\quad
 \sum_a\lambda_a\ll x^{1/20}H_Q.
\]
Ban every unassigned small non-group prime, and independently ban an unmarked group prime with probability \(1-q_0\). Compatible assignments recover the exact damping probability \(q_0^{\omega_P(u)-s_P}\), even with higher powers. Dropping the macro factorial contribution gives a nonnegative majorant of \(A_0\), hence of \(A\).

On a rough pair box \(M_1M_2\asymp x\), \(M_i\ll x^{.54}\), the condition \(D_1\mid u\) becomes \(mn\equiv1\pmod{D_1}\). This has exactly \(\varphi(D_1)\) residue pairs even when \(D_1\) is not squarefree. At an odd sieve prime \(l\nmid D_1\), the two disjoint bad conditions have local count
\[
 \nu(l)=(2l-1)\mathbf1_{l\le W}
 +(l-1)\mathbf1_{l\ {\rm banned}},\qquad

A fixed-depth upper sieve has level \(D=(x^\theta)^{10}\le x^{1/20}\). Its product is compared with \(V(W)^2V(x^\theta)\), correcting for primes in \(D_1\) and averaging the independent mask. The mask product is at most \(H_P\), because \(H_g\ge Z_g(q_0)\). Summing the main terms over assignments gives \(O(X_A V(W)^2)\).

The errors must also be summed over all assignments. Using \(\sum_a\lambda_aD_1\ll x^{1/10}H_Q\) gives

Thus the positive dyadic pair constant depends only on the original weight geometry and the already fixed micro-band choice.

There are \(O(\kappa L+1)\) balanced dyads, yielding

The resulting \(C_{\rm bal}=C_{\rm pr}^2C_1\) is fixed independently of \(\kappa,b_1,S_1\). This order is what permits the final narrow-gap argument.

### Presieve and subtraction of unbalanced composites

Choose \(\kappa\) so small that \(C_{\rm bal}\kappa<\mathfrak S/4\), then choose sufficiently small \(b_1\), a fixed finite mesh in \((b_1,1/2-\kappa]\), distribution accuracies and discrepancy exponent \(B\). Only then choose \(S_1>2B+3\). Type II is applied with coefficient bound one, so increasing proxy precision does not reopen its constants.

All required factor ranges are positive-power ranges. A test can be replaced on either factor with total error \(o(X_A/L)\), including when the other factor already carries a proxy. The actual interval restriction remains in the discrepancy sequence.

Initial sieving of \(N_u=2u+1\) at \(x^{b_1}\), with density \(1/(p-1)\) for odd primes, gives

 \frac{\mathfrak S X_A}{L}
 \left(\frac{e^{-\gamma_E}}{b_1}
 (1-O(e^{-c/b_1}))+o(1)\right)
\]
as a lower bound. Its level is below the available \(x^\vartheta\).

For a prime \(n\) in a fixed unbalanced bin, conditional sieving of \(n\mid N_u\) at \(W\) uses growing depth \(2\lceil T^2\rceil\). The map \((n,d)\mapsto nd\) is injective because \(n\) is the unique prime factor above \(W\). Hence the sum of all remainders is bounded by the original single summed remainder without a divisor multiplicity. The main mass is
\[
 X_A V_1(W)\bigl(\log(\gamma'/\gamma)+o(1)\bigr).
\]

For a presieved composite with least prime factor \(n\), the complement is \(x^\gamma\)-rough. Repeated occurrences of \(n\) are allowed. Replacing only that roughness test, then applying the conditional sieve, bounds the removed mass by a Riemann sum for the rough-density integral. The identity \(D_{b_1}(1)-1\) leaves a positive constant after subtraction. With \(b_1\) sufficiently small, the remaining weight of primes together with composites whose least prime factor exceeds \(x^{1/2-\kappa}\) is at least \(\mathfrak S X_A/(2L)\).

### Balanced composites and the final theorem

Such a remaining composite has exactly two prime factors with multiplicity. Both lie in the balanced interval \([x^{1/2-2\kappa},x^{1/2+2\kappa}]\). Ordered pairs cover distinct-prime products and squares. The exact telescoping identity replaces both prime indicators in succession. The second replacement uses the first bounded proxy as its companion.

The positive pair estimate and prime-density bound give
\[
 \text{balanced composite weight}
 \le C_{\rm bal}(\kappa+1/L)X_A/L+o(X_A/L).
\]
Subtracting it leaves
\[
 \sum_{2u+1\ {\rm prime}}A(u)\Psi(u/x)
 \ge(\mathfrak S/2-C_{\rm bal}\kappa-o(1))X_A/L
 \gg X_A/L.
\]
The squarefree-loss lemma removes only \(o(X_A/L)\). A surviving supported odd \(u\) has \(\Omega(u)\) odd, \(2u\) squarefree and \(\Omega(2u)\) even. Since \(u\in[x,2x]\) and \(x\) can be arbitrarily large, the claimed infinitude follows.
