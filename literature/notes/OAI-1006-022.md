## Main question and stated scope — §§1–2

For a uniformly chosen prime \(3\le p\le x\), list prime factors of \(p-1\) with multiplicity in decreasing order, pad with ones, and divide their logarithms by \(\log(p-1)\). The paper claims convergence of every fixed finite-dimensional joint distribution against bounded continuous test functions to the ranked Poisson–Dirichlet law PD(1). It uses ordinary equal prime weighting and all real cutoffs \(x\). Uniform stick breaking has pieces \(B_1=1-U_1\), \(B_j=(\prod_{i<j}U_i)(1-U_j)\). The introduction also states the corresponding fixed-\(u\) shifted Dickman density.

This claim goes beyond the companion 021 lower bound with the full power exponent. It does not establish recursive independence of descendants in a Pratt tree. The stated route first reaches compact regions of the simplex with coordinate sum below one, then uses a separate probability argument to exclude boundary escape. The size-biased boundary and sorting argument is followed below.

Here \(L=\log x\) and \(W=\exp(L^{.24})\). Siegel–Walfisz and rough-number estimates may carry ineffective constants. Mean squares for continuous and separated frequencies have constants independent of the coefficient representation. This matters when a prime polynomial is raised to a growing power. Fixed-order divisor estimates are not silently extended to growing order. The block sieve is imported from 021 with coefficient bound one and level \(z^{4h+2}\). A constant-factor upper bound alone does not detect primes.

Two analytic inputs are proved in Appendix A, whose complete argument is recorded below: cancellation for reciprocal long-prime polynomials with polynomial-size moduli and frequencies between a logarithmic threshold and \(x^2\), and logarithmic-phase cancellation in progressions for long integer intervals. Rough counts use Bonferroni truncation of depth \(O(\log L)\), with level \(x^{o(1)}\) and errors on the enclosing interval scale.

## Extraction of the ordinary prime statistic — §7

### Progressions, repeated arrays and the sieve error

Theorem 7.1 asserts, for the same interior slots and every nonnegative smooth compactly supported \(\Phi\),
\[
 \sum_{p\ {\rm prime}}\Phi((p-1)/x)
 \bigl(f_x(p-1)-C_x\bigr)=o(x/\log x)
\]
through all real \(x\), with \(f_x(u)=\sum_{\mathbf l}\mathbf1_{l\mid u}\).

For fixed disjoint inert marking arrays, the independent divisor model replaces each divisibility indicator by a Bernoulli variable of mean \(1/p\). The mean \(m_{\mathcal W}\) is bounded above and below by positive constants depending on \(K,q\), uniformly in the fixed band exponents. An exactly-one-hit event gives the lower bound.

The summed progression lemma reaches \(r\le x^{c_0}\), where \(c_0<\varepsilon/2\). It covers an array weight, products of two weights including a repeated array, constants and bounded linear combinations. Repeated arrays require expanding \(\omega_g^2\) with coincident represented primes and using \(q^2\) on the unrepresented primes. This is needed later for deweighting. Truncated divisor support is \(x^{o(1)}\), reciprocal coefficient mass is bounded, and ordinary absolute coefficient mass is \(x^{o(1)}\). CRT counts give \(x/(\ell r d')\) when \((d',r)=1\). Band primes dividing \(r\) are forced absent, whose model discrepancy is exponentially small in \(L^{.1}\). Summed rounding errors have a power saving because the long tuple product stays below \(x^{1-\varepsilon}\).

Set \(\gamma=c_0/(4h+3)\), \(z=x^\gamma\) for a large fixed even \(h\). The block-sieve level \(z^{4h+2}<x^{c_0}\) fits the progression result. Apply it to the two nonnegative weights \(\mathcal W f_x\) and \(\mathcal W\), then subtract. The surviving normalized presieve error is
\[
 \limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{L}{x m_{\mathcal W}}
 \left|\sum_{P^-(u+1)>z}\Phi(u/x)\mathcal W(u)(f_x(u)-C_x)\right|
 \ll e^{-h}/\gamma.
\]
Its surviving constant is independent of \(K,h\). In particular, the error does not contain the reciprocal of a rare-mark mean.

A presifted composite has a bounded number \(2\le j\le2/\gamma\) of prime factors. Repeated large prime factors have negligible mass, so ordered factorizations with weight \(1/j!\) suffice. Telescope each prime slot to

The companion convolution has fixed divisor-power growth and can be truncated to a log-power bound using a higher fixed divisor moment. Smooth separation leaves the exact interval-restricted discrepancy from Theorem 3.1. Clipping the centered long-prime statistic gives a globally bounded invariant core without altering the actual range. Choose the Type II target and then sufficient fixed \(K\) after \(h,\gamma\) and all finitely many composite lengths.

Candidate small groups use exponents .28–.35 and candidate big groups .40–.45, all above \(W\). For each factor \(m\), remove its entire candidate prime-power part to obtain \(\bar m\), then replace the coefficient by
\[
 \widetilde a(m)=

This makes the threshold and logarithm invariant under reallocation of candidate primes. The change is bounded absolutely before any cancellation. The mean removed logarithm is \(O(L^{.45})\). ### Positive selection before a signed expansion

For \(v=m_1\cdots m_j\), \(u=v-1\), independently toss candidate-group coins with probabilities
\[
 s_g(u,v)=c_1

The constant \(c_1\) ensures values at most one. Full rough product dyads have uniform residue estimates for any fixed number of candidate primes. In the independent residue model, the disjoint events \(p\mid u\) and \(p\mid v\) have probabilities

Their group sums approach \(V_g\) and \(jV_g\). Fixed joint factorial moments, followed by fixed-count truncation and Bonferroni, identify bounded one- and two-group tests. No growing-moment assumption is inserted.

### Exact allocation, two-sided correlation and deweighting

Off candidate square exceptions, every active prime in \(v\) can be assigned independently to any of the \(j\) factor slots. Because the threshold and coefficient depend on bars, all \(j^{\omega_P(v)}\) allocations have the same coefficient. The identity

recovers exactly the active marked weight. The residual core \(G_P(v)\) depends only on \(v_*\) and is bounded by a fixed log power times \(\tau(v_*)^{C_j}\). Its bars continue removing all candidate primes, including inactive ones.

Each expanded term is therefore a fixed log-power multiple of
\[
 \sum_u\Phi(u/x)\mathcal W(u)(f_x(u)-C_x)

\]
Taking \(\Psi(y)=y\Phi(y)\) converts this to \(x\) times Theorem 6.1. The exponent in that theorem is chosen after all fixed expansion costs. Composite proxy contributions vanish, leaving a marked prime statistic.

To remove the remaining inert marks, fix \(h\) and its sufficient \(K\), then choose any finite number \(b\) of disjoint arrays. In the independent model,
\[
 Z_b(u)=

 C\,e^{-h}/\gamma+C\sqrt{C_K/b}.

Take real \(x\to\infty\) with every array fixed, then \(b\to\infty\) with \(h,K\) fixed, and finally \(h\to\infty\), choosing its new sufficient fixed \(K\) each time. No array count grows with \(x\) in an application of the correlation or progression theorem.

## Interior factorial measures, boundary control and the full law — §8

Choose \(p\) uniformly in \(x<p\le2x\), and regard prime factors of \(p-1\), with multiplicity, as labelled balls of masses \(\log q/\log(p-1)\). Their total mass is one. The expected ordered factorial measure \(\mu_{x,d}\) converges locally on
\[
 \mathcal D_d=\{t_i>0,\ \sum_i t_i<1\}
\]
to \(\prod_i dt_i/t_i\). This is established first on rectangles with positive lower endpoints and upper endpoints summing below one. The reciprocal long-prime tuple sum tends to the product of logarithmic interval lengths. Smoothing is removed using bounded tuple counts and the prime number theorem. The exact denominator differs from \(\log x\) by a relative \(O(1/\log x)\), handled by fixed endpoint enlargement before a limit. Coincident labelled balls at positive mass require a large squared prime divisor and have vanishing normalized count. Compact local masses are bounded by \(a^{-d}\), so finite grids extend rectangle convergence to continuous compactly supported tests. No bound on total factorial mass near zero is assumed.

Now sample balls successively without replacement with probabilities proportional to remaining mass. The ordered tuple is tilted by
\[
 w_d(t)=\prod_{i=1}^d
 \frac{t_i}{1-t_1-\cdots-t_{i-1}},
\]
so its interior limiting density is
\[
 h_d(t)=\prod_{i=1}^d(1-t_1-\cdots-t_{i-1})^{-1}.
\]
The triangular change
\[
 A_i=\frac{t_i}{1-t_1-\cdots-t_{i-1}},\qquad
 t_i=A_i\prod_{r<i}(1-A_r)
\]
has the reciprocal Jacobian required to turn \(h_d(t)\,dt\) into uniform measure on \((0,1)^d\). Thus the interior density has total probability one. Compact cutoff functions then exclude every boundary face, exhaustion event and padded zero. This supplies convergence of the first \(d\) size-biased draws on the entire compact cube.

The expected remaining mass converges to \(2^{-d}\). For any selected \(d\) members of a mass-one collection, their sorted padded values \(S_{d,i}\) and the full ranked values \(V_i\) satisfy
\[
 0\le V_i-S_{d,i}\le r
\]
where \(r\) is the unselected mass. Hence any bounded continuous \(k\)-coordinate test has error at most
\[
 \omega_F(\eta)+2\|F\|_\infty\,\mathbb E r/\eta.
\]
Finite sorting is continuous. First take real \(x\to\infty\), then \(d\to\infty\), then \(\eta\downarrow0\). This yields the ranked PD(1) law in each prime dyad. Finally use finitely many dyads below a real \(X\) and discard a proportion \(2^{-J_0}+o(1)\), taking \(X\to\infty\) before \(J_0\to\infty\). The result is ordinary equal weighting over all \(3\le p\le X\), not a logarithmic average or a subsequence statement.

## The analytic input is proved in the appendix — Appendix A

The appendix is included in this full reading. It does not leave the two main analytic estimates as unexamined black boxes.

### Quantitative power sums with growing degree

For \(K=k(k+1)/2\), the power-sum moment \(J_{s,k}(M)\) is shown to satisfy
\[
 J_{s,k}(M)\le
 \exp(C_1k^{C_2})M^{2s-K+1/100}
\]
for some \(k\le s\le C_1k^4\). The proof follows the classical Vinogradov method in its \(p\)-adic form, with quantitative dependence on \(k\). It cites Wooley for an exposition of the classical method, not as an invocation of the modern efficient-congruencing theorem.

At the \(s+k\) stage, tuples with fewer than \(k\) distinct coordinates are separated and bounded by a square-root moment. For a nondegenerate tuple, a fixed list of \(2k^3+5\) primes of size \(M^{1/k}\) contains one avoiding the two Vandermonde products. Congruences of the first \(k\) powers and the invertible Jacobian \(k!\prod_{i<j}(z_j-z_i)\) yield at most \(k!r^{K-k}\) possibilities for the second initial list. Dividing the remaining variables by \(r\) gives a shorter moment with prescribed power-sum differences. Positivity of its Fourier moment bounds these differences by the zero coefficient.

The recurrence sends the exponent error \(\epsilon_s\) to \((1-1/k)\epsilon_s\). Small \(M\), degeneracies, permutations, rounding and prime choices are explicitly included in the constants. After \(O(k\log k)\) iterations, \(\epsilon_s\le1/100\) and the total logarithmic constant is polynomial in \(k\).

### Logarithmic phases on progressions
