## Main question and stated scope — §§1–2

For a uniformly chosen prime \(3\le p\le x\), list prime factors of \(p-1\) with multiplicity in decreasing order, pad with ones, and divide their logarithms by \(\log(p-1)\). The paper claims convergence of every fixed finite-dimensional joint distribution against bounded continuous test functions to the ranked Poisson–Dirichlet law PD(1). It uses ordinary equal prime weighting and all real cutoffs \(x\). Uniform stick breaking has pieces \(B_1=1-U_1\), \(B_j=(\prod_{i<j}U_i)(1-U_j)\). The introduction also states the corresponding fixed-\(u\) shifted Dickman density.

This claim goes beyond the companion 021 lower bound with the full power exponent. It does not establish recursive independence of descendants in a Pratt tree. The stated route first reaches compact regions of the simplex with coordinate sum below one, then uses a separate probability argument to exclude boundary escape. That last argument remains unread at this checkpoint.

Here \(L=\log x\) and \(W=\exp(L^{.24})\). Siegel–Walfisz and rough-number estimates may carry ineffective constants. Mean squares for continuous and separated frequencies have constants independent of the coefficient representation. This matters when a prime polynomial is raised to a growing power. Fixed-order divisor estimates are not silently extended to growing order. The block sieve is imported from 021 with coefficient bound one and level \(z^{4h+2}\). A constant-factor upper bound alone does not detect primes.

Two stated analytic inputs await their Appendix A proofs: cancellation for reciprocal long-prime polynomials with polynomial-size moduli and frequencies between a logarithmic threshold and \(x^2\), and logarithmic-phase cancellation in progressions for long integer intervals. Rough counts use Bonferroni truncation of depth \(O(\log L)\), with level \(x^{o(1)}\) and errors on the enclosing interval scale.
