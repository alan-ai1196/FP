## 1. Exact result and analytic starting point

For every primitive nonprincipal real Dirichlet character of conductor \(q\ge3\), and every real zero \(\beta\in(0,1)\), the theorem claims
\[
(1-\beta)\log q\ge c
\]
for one absolute \(c>0\).

This is a uniform logarithmic exclusion near 1 for real zeros. It is not a fixed zero-free half-plane for all complex zeros, and it does not assert RH. The paper states existence of an absolute constant; it does not supply a numerical value or an implemented procedure for computing one.

Write \(\ell=\log q\) and \(\delta=(1-\beta)\ell\). The logarithmic derivative of the completed L-function has a sum of nonnegative real zero contributions for real \(s>1\). Retaining the contribution of \(\beta\), and adding the zeta logarithmic derivative, gives
\[
-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)-\frac{L'}L(s,\chi)
\le C\ell+\frac1{s-1}-\frac1{s-\beta}.
\]
At \(s=1+1/\log X\), the Euler-product series has nonnegative coefficients \(1+\chi(n)\). Restricting it to primes with \(\chi(p)=1\) yields
\[
\sum_{\substack{p\le X\\\chi(p)=1}}\frac{\log p}{p}
\ll \ell+\frac{\delta(\log X)^2}{\ell}.
\]
Mertens' estimate and the bound for primes dividing \(q\) then imply
\[
\sum_{\substack{H<p\le X\\p\nmid2q,\ \chi(p)=-1}}
\frac{\log p}{p}
\ge \log X-C\ell-C\frac{\delta(\log X)^2}{\ell}-C_H.
\]
A very small \(\delta\) therefore forces almost all the relevant logarithmic prime mass into the negative-character class at a suitable fixed-power scale. This is the only substantial analytic estimate newly developed in the proof; the rest is an algebraic determinant comparison. [§§1–2.]

## 2. A uniform interpolation lemma

Let \(A:\mathbb C^4\to\mathbb C^3\) be surjective with one-dimensional kernel \(\mathbb Cc\). The four coordinates of \(c\) must be linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\). For the integer box \(E_N=\{0,\ldots,N-1\}^4\), the lemma claims that polynomials with separate degrees bounded by \(t_1,t_2,t_3\) can interpolate arbitrary values on \(A(E_N)\) if
\[
t_j\ge3(N-1),\qquad
\prod_{j=1}^3(t_j-3(N-1)+1)>(4N-3)^4.
\]
The degree bound is uniform in the coefficients of \(A\) and in the coordinates chosen on its image. It does not bound the coefficients or numerical conditioning of an interpolating polynomial.

The proof assumes a nonzero annihilating functional \(\sum v_nB(An)=0\). Let \(P\) be the convex hull of its nonzero support. A dimension count supplies a nonzero polynomial \(R\), with separate degrees \(t_j-3(N-1)\), vanishing on the projected lattice points in \(4P\).

Choose an integer point \(m\) where \(R(Am)\ne0\) with minimum distance to \(4P\), and a nearest point \(y\in4P\). The vector \(h=m-y\) exposes a face \(G\) of \(P\) of dimension at most three. Expressing \(y/4\) with at most four vertices selects a vertex \(u\) for which \(y-u\in3P\).

For a support point \(n\notin G\), the translated point \(m+n-u\) is strictly closer to \(4P\): a short move from \(y+n-u\) toward \(y\) reduces the squared distance because \(h\cdot(u-n)>0\). Minimality forces the translated \(R\) to vanish at all those off-face points. The p. 4 figure illustrates this strict distance decrease; it is schematic, while the argument takes place in four real dimensions.

The integer vertices of \(G\) lie in a rational hyperplane \(r\cdot v=k\). Rational independence gives \(r\cdot c\ne0\), so the restriction of \(A\) to that hyperplane is an affine bijection. Three inverse coordinate functions provide a Lagrange polynomial \(Q\) of total degree at most \(3(N-1)\), selecting \(u\) among the face points. The product
\[
B(z)=Q(z)R(z+A(m-u))
\]
stays inside the separate-degree budget and isolates the nonzero term \(v_uR(Am)\), a contradiction.

A rectangular corollary uses
\[
U=N^{4/3},\quad
T_1=32H^{2/3}U,\quad T_2=T_3=32H^{-1/3}U,\quad 1\le H\le N.
\]
The corresponding monomial rows span all \(N^4\) evaluations. The constant 32 is independent of \(A,H\). The paper also gives a torus-derivation interpretation as a rectangular multiplicity estimate, without invoking a general algebraicity criterion as a black box. [§3.]

## 3. Building a determinant with a favored coordinate

Associate the character to a quadratic field \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)\), with \(d\) squarefree and \(|d|\le q\). Apart from the single fixed field \(d=2\), adjoining \(\sqrt2\) gives a biquadratic field
\[
K=\mathbb Q(a,b),\qquad a^2=d,\quad b^2=2.
\]
The automorphisms \(\sigma,\tau\) change the signs of \(a,b\), respectively. Work in the integral subring \(\mathcal R=\mathbb Z[a,b]\); maximality of this order is not required.

For \(n\in E_N\), set
\[
\theta_n=n_1+n_2a+n_3b+n_4ab.
\]
The map with coordinates \(\theta_n,\sigma(\theta_n),\sigma\tau(\theta_n)\) has rank three and kernel generated by \((ab,b,-a,-1)\). These coordinates are rationally independent because \(1,a,b,ab\) form a basis of \(K/\mathbb Q\).

Order every monomial evaluation row by weight
\[
w(\alpha)=\alpha_1+H\alpha_2+H\alpha_3.
\]
Greedily retain a row whenever it enlarges the preceding span over \(K\). The interpolation result guarantees \(M=N^4=U^3\) retained rows, hence a nonzero determinant \(\Delta\in\mathcal R\).

Every retained weight is at most \(96H^{2/3}U\). If
\[
S_1=\sum\alpha_1,\qquad S_2=\sum(\alpha_2+\alpha_3),
\]
the paper proves, for fixed \(H\) and sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
S_1\ge c_0MH^{2/3}U,\qquad
S_2\le C_0MH^{-1/3}U,
\]
with \(c_0=(4\cdot97^2)^{-1}\), \(C_0=192\). Bounding the number of retained indices with each fixed first exponent proves the lower bound. Thus \(S_2/S_1\le(C_0/c_0)/H\).

Only the existence of a spanning interpolation family is used. Its potentially large interpolation coefficients never enter \(\Delta\), which consists of the original monomial evaluations. [§4.]

## 4. Size versus divisibility

The norm \(\operatorname{Nm}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\Delta)\) is a nonzero integer. At every embedding, \(|\theta_n|\le8N\sqrt q\). Hadamard's inequality gives
\[
\frac14\log|\operatorname{Nm}(\Delta)|
\le \frac M2\log M+
(S_1+S_2)(\log N+\tfrac12\ell+\log8).
\]

For an admissible prime \(p>H\), \(p\nmid2q\), \(\chi(p)=-1\), Euler's criterion gives
\[
\theta^p\equiv g_p(\theta)\pmod{p\mathcal R},
\]
where \(g_p=\sigma\) or \(\sigma\tau\), according to \((2/p)\).

For a retained row with \(\alpha_1=pk+r\), replace its first-coordinate factor by
\[
\theta_n^r(\theta_n^p-g_p(\theta_n))^k.
\]
The expanded row equals the original plus rows of strictly lower weight, because each replacement reduces weight by \(p-H>0\). Greedy selection ensures those earlier rows lie in the span of earlier retained rows. The row transformation is triangular over \(K\) with diagonal 1, preserving the determinant.

Each replacement row itself is divisible by \(p^k\) in \(\mathcal R\). Consequently
\[
\Delta\in p^{E_p}\mathcal R,\qquad
E_p=\sum_{\alpha}\lfloor\alpha_1/p\rfloor,
\]
and \(p^{4E_p}\mid\operatorname{Nm}(\Delta)\). Integrality of the triangular transformation matrix is unnecessary: determinant equality is over the field, while divisibility follows from the replacement entries.

Combining distinct primes, using the floor error and Chebyshev's bound, gives
\[
\frac14\log|\operatorname{Nm}(\Delta)|
\ge S_1\left(\log U-C\ell
-C\frac{\delta(\log U)^2}{\ell}-C_H\right)-CMU.
\]
The crucial comparison is \(\log N=\tfrac34\log U\). The weighted interpolation construction makes the other exponents too small to consume that gap. [§5.]

## 5. Closing the contradiction and limits

If the theorem fails, there is a sequence with \(\delta\to0\). Its conductors can be taken to infinity: finitely many bounded-conductor characters, analyticity, and \(L(1,\chi)\ne0\) prevent zeros from accumulating at 1 within that finite family. The exceptional fixed field \(d=2\) is therefore eventually absent.

Divide the size/divisibility comparison by \(S_1\log U\). The leading terms are
\[
1\le
\frac34(1+S_2/S_1)
+\left(C+\tfrac12(1+S_2/S_1)\right)\frac{\ell}{\log U}
+C\delta\frac{\log U}{\ell}
+\text{vanishing errors}.
\]
Choose \(H\) first so that \(S_2/S_1\le1/12\), making the first term at most \(13/16\). Next choose a sufficiently large fixed \(\gamma\) and put \(N=\lceil q^\gamma\rceil\); then \(\log U/\ell\to4\gamma/3\), making the second term's limiting bound less than \(1/16\). Finally take the sequence limit. The third term vanishes because \(\gamma\) is fixed and \(\delta\to0\); the floor and determinant-size errors vanish by the lower bound for \(S_1\). This yields the contradiction \(1\le7/8\). [§6.]

The order \(H\), then \(\gamma\), then the conductor limit is part of the proof. Allowing those parameters to drift freely with the limiting sequence would not be the argument presented.
