## 1. The exact reconstruction problem

Let \(K/k\) and \(L/l\) be finitely generated function fields over arbitrary algebraically closed constants. Fix a prime \(\ell\) different from both field characteristics. The relative transcendence degrees are at least two. Neither equality of dimensions nor equality of characteristics is supplied in advance.

For the maximal pro-\(\ell\) Galois group \(G_F\), the paper uses the abelian and class-two quotients
\[
 \Pi_F^a=G_F/[G_F,G_F],\qquad
 \Pi_F^c=G_F/[[G_F,G_F],G_F],
\]
with closed commutator subgroups. Put \(\Delta_F=\ker(\Pi_F^c\to\Pi_F^a)\). The central commutator gives a continuous alternating \(\mathbb Z_\ell\)-bilinear map into \(\Delta_F\).

The input is a continuous \(\mathbb Z_\ell\)-isomorphism
\[
 \varphi:\Pi_L^a\longrightarrow\Pi_K^a
\]
for which some isomorphism \(\Delta_L\to\Delta_K\) preserves the full commutator map. Merely preserving the relation “these two elements commute” is a weaker specification than the stated input. Multiplication of \(\varphi\) by a global unit \(u\in\mathbb Z_\ell^\times\) changes the compatible central map by \(u^2\).

Theorem 1.1 identifies these bracket-compatible isomorphisms, modulo one global unit, with constant-preserving isomorphisms of the perfect closures \(K^i\to L^i\). The direction on Galois groups is contravariant. In positive characteristic the field side is taken modulo postcomposition with any integer power of Frobenius on the perfect closure. There is no such quotient in characteristic zero. The theorem includes surfaces and \(\ell=2\). It does not require valuations, inertia groups or rational quotient maps as extra marked data.

The proof first recovers local valuation data, then bounds supports of transported pencils, recovers completed curve subfields, and identifies genuine divisors and rational quotients. Its last step imports Pop's global reconstruction theorem with the recovered data. This dependence is part of the proof, rather than a separate field-reconstruction argument internal to the manuscript.

## 2. Topology and lifting the group input

The dual objects are
\[
 W_F=\operatorname{Hom}(F^\times,\mathbb Z_\ell),\qquad
 \widehat F=\varprojlim_e F^\times/F^{\times\ell^e}.
\]
The first carries the compact pointwise topology. The second has its \(\ell\)-adic topology. They should not both be treated as unrestricted compact products. Since \(F^\times/k^\times\) embeds in a free divisor group, it is free abelian. A continuous dual of a product of copies of \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) is described by coefficient families having only finitely many entries nonzero modulo each \(\ell^e\). This gives the precise completed multiplicative group used in evaluation.

Lemma 2.1 records torsion freeness of \(\widehat F\) and the finite-level quotient identity. Kummer theory identifies \(W_F\) with \(\Pi_F^a\) after choosing compatible Tate roots. A change of that choice accounts for a global scalar.

Two kinds of inclusion have different justifications in Lemma 2.2. If \(E\) is relatively algebraically closed in \(F\), a relative projective model shows that \(F^\times/E^\times\) is free abelian. The associated splitting proves that \(\widehat E\) is closed and \(\ell\)-saturated in \(\widehat F\), and that \(W_F\to W_E\) is surjective. If \(F/E\) is finite, the field norm composed with inclusion is multiplication by \([F:E]\). Torsion freeness therefore makes \(\widehat E\to\widehat F\) injective, including for inseparable extensions. This conclusion differs from the possible kernel at a single finite Kummer level.

Lemma 2.3 lifts the stipulated bracket-compatible map to an isomorphism of class-two groups, also at \(\ell=2\). With the bases and central kernels identified, form the fiber product of the two extensions and quotient by the diagonal central kernel. The resulting extension is abelian. Its base is a product of copies of \(\mathbb Z_\ell\), projective in the category of abelian pro-\(\ell\) groups. Pontryagin duality reduces that assertion to injectivity of a direct sum of divisible groups \(\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell\). A continuous section gives a graph projecting isomorphically onto each original extension. No division by two or additional power datum enters this argument.

On a smooth projective curve, the kernel of all point orders at finite level is \(\operatorname{Pic}(C)[\ell^e]\), and in the inverse limit it is \(T_\ell\operatorname{Pic}(C)\). When the characteristic differs from \(\ell\), its rank is \(2g(C)\). Replacing individual normalized point generators by units preserves their finite-level vanishing tests.

## 3. Recovering local data from commutators

Section 3 relates commuting characters to the alternating-pair identity
\[
 f(x)g(1-x)-f(1-x)g(x)=0.
\]
The cohomological comparison is made at every \(\mathbb Z/\ell^e\) level. Alternating commutator forms determine the relevant central extensions because abelian extensions of the torsion-free abelian pro-\(\ell\) base split. Continuous forms involve finitely many coordinates. In this setting the \(\ell=2\) case does not require a separate diagonal power parameter.

Inflation from the maximal pro-\(\ell\) quotient is injective in degree two here. The paper proves the required divisibility of the multiplicative group of the maximal pro-\(\ell\) extension by adjoining roots and all finite Galois conjugates. The five-term sequence then applies. The imported Merkurjev–Suslin degree-two norm-residue theorem identifies the kernel through Steinberg relations. Transgression against the central commutator quotient and separation by all finite characters give the commuting/alternating equivalence.

The rational character results cited from Topaz are used for arbitrary residue characteristics. A maximal alternating subspace has dimension equal to the relative transcendence degree. Its valuative part satisfies equality in the relative valuation inequality. Intersections of two maximal subspaces recognize the rank-one relative inertia lines of quasi-prime divisors, and their alternating centralizers give decomposition spaces. Approximation distinguishes comparable and incomparable valuations. The actual integral inertia and decomposition groups are recovered by intersecting these rational spaces with \(W_F\), using their saturated annihilator descriptions.

Iterating actual restriction to units and quotient by inertia recovers flags and residue groups. At this stage quasi-divisors can be nontrivial on constants, and their residue characteristic can equal \(\ell\). They must not already be called genuine divisors.

The finite-constant recognition test uses Pop's minimized-inertia criterion and Topaz's appendix. The relevant set is the closure of the **union** of quasi-divisorial inertia groups, not their span. Its image in a residue quotient is tested for the complete curve-like structure: point generators converge to zero outside finite sets, their summation map has one diagonal relation, and the cokernel is finitely generated.

The manuscript checks the required valuation hypotheses. Minimal relative rank excludes an \(\ell\)-divisible convex subgroup. For flags the final convex step supplies the relevant reduction. Independent relative values produce an auxiliary field \(k(x_1,\ldots,x_{d-1})\) with the same constant residue field by the unique least-term argument. Thus the criterion recognizes algebraic closures of finite fields and their point inertia data. The later use of genus additionally requires exclusion of residue characteristic \(\ell\).

## 4. Finite curve tests preserve relations and support

Section 4 fixes one embedded normal projective model \(X\) of \(L\). For any finite list \(g_j\) and any \(q=\ell^e\), it constructs a terminal residual curve of genus at most a constant \(G_X\), independent of that list and of \(e\).

The preservation claim concerns the entire relation kernel in \((\mathbb Z/q)^r\): a product of the chosen functions is a \(q\)-th power before reduction exactly when the corresponding residual product is one. The functions need not be independent. Simultaneous root covers may be disconnected. Their connected components and degrees encode the size of the generated finite Kummer subgroup.

The construction spreads the model, divisors, cover and component data over a finite coefficient ring with \(\ell\) inverted. It preserves integral and normal fibers, divisor degrees and orders, and the necessary finite étale splittings. Constants are specialized to finite-field closure residue using a valuation construction that also accommodates arbitrary cardinality of the original constants. A Gauss extension and the fundamental inequality control the subsequent algebraic extension.

General hyperplane sections preserve the selected cover components, meet divisors transversely and avoid the specified bad sets. The argument only needs finitely many open conditions, not an uncountable-field choice principle. The genus bound comes from the fixed Hilbert polynomial after \(d-1\) hyperplane differences, with associated points handled in the construction.

Old Kummer relations survive because a root of a valuation unit is again a unit. Preservation of each cover component's degree fixes the size of the residual subgroup, so no new relations appear. In addition, for \(m=\ell\) and \(m=q\), and in fact each divisor of \(q\), the test preserves
\[
 \sum_{\operatorname{ord}_D(g_j)\not\equiv0\pmod m}\deg D
\]
as the number of residual points with order nonzero modulo \(m\). This weighted support identity connects high-dimensional divisors to the next curve estimate.

## 5. Why a finite-level argument bounds exact completed supports

Write \(\Theta:\widehat K\to\widehat L\) for the dual of \(\varphi\). For a pencil \(t-a\), put \(h_a=\Theta[t-a]\). Its support at level \(\ell^e\) is finite because that level has an ordinary rational representative. Its exact support in the completion is not assumed finite.

Choose five fixed anchors. Their support counts \(N_i\) modulo \(\ell\) are finite before any exact-support theorem is available. In characteristic zero the anchors include \(0,1,\ell\). Define
\[
 c_\ell=5(1-1/\ell)-2=3-5/\ell>0,\qquad
 B=\max\left\{1,\frac{2G_X-2+\sum_iN_i}{c_\ell}\right\}.
\]
In particular \(c_2=1/2\). For one additional \(a\), take a sufficiently large \(e\) so the chosen source classes are nonzero, distinct and generate a noncyclic finite subgroup. Their integral independence gives such thresholds. The threshold may depend on \(a\). The eventual bound does not.

The finite curve test transports all those relations. If the corresponding quasi-valuation had nonzero relative value on \(k(t)\), the residual evaluations would be cyclic, contradicting the preserved subgroup. Distinctness also forces the anchor differences to have the same relevant constant value. Rescaling gives a nonconstant residual pencil with distinct constants. The characteristic-zero anchors force the values of \(1\) and \(\ell\) to agree, excluding residue characteristic \(\ell\). The finite-constant test identifies the point orders on both residual curves up to units, and the Picard kernel identifies their genus.

After removing a purely inseparable \(p\)-power, the residual parameter defines a separable map to \(\mathbb P^1\) of degree \(n\). This removal changes no vanishing modulo \(\ell^e\), since \(p\ne\ell\). Over anchor \(i\), at most \(N_i\) points have ramification index not divisible by \(\ell\). Hence the fiber has at most \(N_i+n/\ell\) points. Riemann–Hurwitz, using the different bound even for wild ramification, yields
\[
 c_\ell n\le 2G_X-2+\sum_iN_i.
\]
The sixth pencil member has zeros and poles in two fibers, giving at most \(2B\) points with nonzero order modulo \(q\). Transferring back,
\[
 \sum_{D\in S_e(h_a)}\deg D\le 2B.
\]

The increasing union of the sets \(S_e(h_a)\) is the exact support. Positive integral divisor degrees turn the uniform finite-level bound into a finite exact support with the same bound. This is the decisive passage to the completion. Corollary 5.2 gives finite exact support for every transported **ordinary** element \(\Theta[z]\), \(z\in K^\times\). It does not assert finite support for arbitrary elements of \(\widehat K\).

## 6. Curve recovery, fixed incidence and norm descent

For a relatively algebraically closed curve subfield \(E\subset K\), the proof first collects infinitely many bounded-degree divisors detecting its transported pencils. Infinitely many mod-\(\ell\) independent pencil classes survive the finite Kummer kernel. If only finitely many divisors occurred, the divisor image and the zero-order kernel would both be finite-dimensional. The latter finiteness uses Deligne's étale cohomology finiteness theorem on a smooth open model. This is a specific imported input.

For a detecting divisor, the corresponding quasi-valuation has constant residue on the curve field. When its exact order on \(\Theta[z]\) vanishes, constant rescaling makes \(z\) a principal unit. Consequently finite-level representatives become powers on all but finitely many of the chosen divisors. The exceptional divisors are finite by the exact-support result.

The incidence lemma applies to an arbitrary collection of tests \((g,q)\). Bounded degrees give finitely many relevant Hilbert polynomials and an integral family of geometrically integral divisors. A parameter curve \(T'\) is fixed **before** the tests. Its incidence variety \(Z'\) dominates \(X\) generically finitely, producing
\[
 L\subset M=l(Z'),\qquad P_0=l(T')\subset M,
\]
with \(M/P_0\) regular. Figure 1 on p. 20 displays the Cartesian base-change square and these two maps.

For each test the degree-\(q\) finite étale root cover splits into \(q\) components on densely many tested fibers. Constancy of the generic component number therefore gives complete generic splitting. The argument counts all \(q\) components and does not rely on \(q\) being prime. A test-dependent finite base change followed by normalization makes the cover split everywhere on the resulting normal integral locus. Restriction above the already fixed \(T'\) gives a root in \(M\overline{P_0}\). There is no intersection of infinitely many open subsets. Only the auxiliary splitting extension depends on the test, while \(M\) and \(P_0\) remain fixed.

Lemma 6.4 uses Hilbert 90 to descend a \(q\)-power class over \(M\overline{P_0}\) to a unique class over \(P_0\). Regularity supplies the uniqueness. These classes are compatible as \(e\) varies, giving the required containment in a completed curve field over a finite extension of \(L\).

The remaining descent is genuinely \(\ell\)-adic. In a finite normal extension \(N/L\), the relative curve closure \(P_N\) has completed group containing \(H=\Theta(\widehat E)\), which has infinite \(\mathbb Z_\ell\)-rank. Every automorphism over \(L\) fixes \(H\). Distinct curve subfields have completed intersection of finite rank by Lemma 6.5: vertical point valuations on their product force vanishing of all exact point orders, leaving only a finite-rank Tate module. Multiplication by a ramification index remains injective in \(\mathbb Z_\ell\), even when that index is divisible by \(\ell\).

Thus all conjugates of \(P_N\) coincide. Its intersection \(P_L=P_N\cap L\) is again a curve field, with purely inseparable invariants handled explicitly. The norm carries \(P_N^\times\) into \(P_L^\times\), so
\[
 [N:L]H\subset\widehat{P_L}.
\]
Closed \(\ell\)-saturation cancels the \(\ell\)-part of the degree, and the remaining factor is an \(\ell\)-adic unit. Hence \(H\subset\widehat{P_L}\). Applying the inverse correspondence and the finite-rank intersection result proves equality and uniqueness. This is norm plus saturation descent, different from the exact compositum descent in OAI-1006-015.

## 7. Genuine divisors, rational quotients and the final imported theorem

Proposition 6.6 recognizes a quasi-prime divisor \(v\) as trivial on constants exactly when
\[
 D_v^\perp\cap\widehat E=0
\]
for some curve subfield \(E\). In one direction choose a transcendental residue and use injectivity of the induced completed field inclusion. In the other, a constant of positive valuation gives a nonconstant principal unit \(1+cx\) in every suitable curve field, violating the criterion. This zero-intersection test is distinct from the finite-dimensional mod-\(\ell\) criterion of OAI-1006-015.

The point inertia lines of \(E\) are the saturations of nonzero images of genuine divisorial inertia under \(W_K\to W_E\). Every point can be extended by a Gauss valuation and finite prolongation. An image \(e\mathbb Z_\ell\operatorname{ord}_P\) is nonzero even when \(\ell\mid e\), and saturation recovers its point line. Picard Tate rank recovers genus, so genus zero identifies the relatively algebraically closed rational subfields.

The recovered quotient diagrams are actual commuting squares
\[
 \begin{array}{ccc}
 W_L&\xrightarrow{\varphi}&W_K\\
 \downarrow\rho_P&&\downarrow\rho_E\\
 W_P&\xrightarrow{\varphi_E}&W_E .
 \end{array}
\]
The top map is always the original fixed \(\varphi\). Targets are not independently rescaled. Point decomposition equals inertia because the curve constants are algebraically closed. Relative algebraic closedness makes the rational inclusions regular, including in positive characteristic.

Section 7 imports two precisely separated results. Pop 2018, Proposition 2.4, recovers the total decomposition graph from the class-two datum together with genuine divisorial inertia in relative dimension greater than one. Pop's birational Isom theorem, cited as the 2012 author's version Theorem 2.1(2), restates the decomposition-graph Main Theorem and reconstructs the perfect field from that graph and the required geometric rational quotient diagrams. The paper supplies all rational quotients, including the Bertini-type families specified there.

This is **not** an application of Pop 2018, Theorem 1.1, which recovers rational quotients under a dimension-greater-than-two hypothesis. Those quotients were recovered directly in §6, so that stronger dimension restriction is unnecessary here.

Compatibility is checked along each flag by restricting the actual decomposition group and then quotienting inertia. A flag trivial on the curve field kills its inertia in the quotient map. Otherwise its nonzero inertia image lies in the unique corresponding point line, with trivial final residue group. Saturation identifies that line, but the actual finite-index images and restriction maps are retained. This preserves the precise data needed by Pop's theorem, rather than replacing them with abstractly isomorphic linear diagrams.

The imported theorem then gives the field isomorphism and its uniqueness up to Frobenius and one global scalar. Perfect closure does not change the absolute Galois group. Abelianization removes the inner-automorphism ambiguity. Equality of characteristics follows from the reconstructed field isomorphism.
