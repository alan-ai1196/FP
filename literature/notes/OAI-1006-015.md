## Main question and exact result

The paper asks whether a function field over algebraically closed constants can be recovered from the first two Milnor K-groups modulo a single prime, including their full multiplication, without separately supplying rational subfields or divisorial inertia.

Fix a prime \(\ell\), with \(\Lambda=\mathbb F_\ell\). For a field \(F\),

\[
V_F=F^\times/(F^\times)^\ell,\qquad
R_F=\operatorname{Span}_\Lambda
\{[x]\otimes[1-x]:x\ne0,1\},
\]

\[
W_F=(V_F\otimes_\Lambda V_F)/R_F.
\]

The datum consists of \(V_F=K_1^{\mathrm M}(F)/\ell\), \(W_F=K_2^{\mathrm M}(F)/\ell\), and the quotient product \(m_F\). A compatible linear isomorphism \(\Theta:V_K\to V_L\) satisfies
\((\Theta\otimes\Theta)(R_K)=R_L\). It then induces the unique compatible degree-two isomorphism. Multiplying \(\Theta\) by \(a\in\Lambda^\times\) multiplies that degree-two map by \(a^2\).

Theorem 1.1 assumes that \(K/k\) and \(L/l\) are finitely generated, that the constants are arbitrary algebraically closed fields of characteristic different from \(\ell\), and that both relative transcendence degrees are at least two. It claims a canonical bijection

\[
\operatorname{Isom}^{i}_{\mathrm F}(K,L)
\;\longrightarrow\;
\operatorname{Isom}_{\mathrm M}(V_K,V_L)/\Lambda^\times.
\]

Here \(K^i\) denotes the perfect closure, and field isomorphisms must carry \(k\) onto \(l\). In characteristic zero there is no field-side quotient. In characteristic \(p>0\), field isomorphisms are identified under postcomposition with any integral power of Frobenius on the perfect closure.

Equivalently, every compatible \(\Theta\) is \(a\alpha_1\) for one global \(a\in\mathbb F_\ell^\times\) and a constant-preserving isomorphism \(\alpha:K^i\to L^i\). Equality of characteristics and relative transcendence degrees is part of the conclusion. The scalar ambiguity is trivial when \(\ell=2\). Frobenius ambiguity still has its stated role in positive characteristic.

The perfection qualification is necessary for these invariants: purely inseparable inclusion and the \(p^r\)-power map compose to multiplication by \(p^r\) in degree one and \(p^{2r}\) in degree two, both invertible modulo \(\ell\). This theorem is not a statement that two bare vector-space dimensions determine a field, nor does it assume that the given map already lifts to pro-\(\ell\) Galois data.

## Position relative to the cited literature

The introduction distinguishes several earlier reconstruction settings. Bogomolov–Tschinkel and Pop treated function fields over algebraic closures of finite fields using small pro-\(\ell\) Galois invariants. Pop's later divisorial-inertia result supplies that extra inertia structure and uses a larger dimension threshold. Topaz's cited finite-coefficient theorem requires rational subgroups as additional input and relative transcendence degree at least five. The paper claims to recover those subgroups from the product and to reach every relative dimension at least two.

The integral, rational and infinitely-divisible-quotient Milnor invariants in the Bogomolov–Tschinkel, Cadoret–Pirutka and later Topaz papers are different data. They are historical comparisons rather than substitutes for the single-prime theorem here.

The explicitly imported local inputs are Topaz's alternating-character valuation theory and Pop's minimized-inertia density theorem. The bounded-support and incidence method is adapted from the separately cited OpenAI Bogomolov–Pop manuscript. The finite-coefficient support, descent and point-incidence arguments are supplied in this paper. It expressly does not take a pro-\(\ell\) field-reconstruction theorem as a premise.

## 1. Alternating characters recover dimension and quasi-divisors

Section 2 passes to the compact dual

\[
A_F=\operatorname{Hom}(F^\times,\Lambda)
=\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(V_F,\Lambda)
\]

with the topology of pointwise convergence. Continuous linear forms on \(A_F\) recover \(V_F\). The dual \(\Phi=\Theta^*:A_L\to A_K\) preserves alternating pairs, defined by

\[
f(x)g(1-x)=f(1-x)g(x).
\]

This condition says exactly that the determinant tensor functional annihilates the Steinberg-relation subspace.

For a valuation \(v\), minimized inertia and decomposition are
\(I_v=U_v^\perp\) and \(D_v=(U_v^1)^\perp\). A quasi-divisorial valuation is minimal under coarsening among valuations with relative value group \(vF/v\kappa\cong\mathbb Z\) and residual relative transcendence degree one less. It can act nontrivially on the constants. A true prime divisor is a quasi-divisor trivial on the constants.

The stated local theorem gives a unique coarsest valuation attached to a valuative character subspace, a valuative part of codimension at most one in an alternating subspace, and recognition of decomposition by alternation with valuative characters. Its finite-coefficient form includes \(\ell=2\). All relevant characters annihilate \(-1\), because the constants are algebraically closed.

The paper supplies the accompanying valuation facts. Residue constants remain algebraically closed. The relative value group is torsion free and satisfies

\[
\operatorname{rrank}(vF/v\kappa)
+\operatorname{trdeg}(Fv/\kappa v)
\le\operatorname{trdeg}(F/\kappa).
\]

At equality the relative value group is finite free and the residue field is finitely generated. Equality passes through coarsenings and compositions. For the finite-free cases, splitting the relative value-group sequence gives
\(D_v/I_v\simeq A_{Fv}\), including its topology and alternating relation. Incomparable valuations yield the approximation identity
\(D_{v_1}\cap D_{v_2}=I_u\) for their finest common coarsening \(u\).

The maximal alternating dimension is exactly the relative transcendence degree \(d\). A quasi-divisorial inertia line is precisely a one-dimensional intersection of two \(d\)-dimensional alternating subspaces. Its decomposition consists of all characters alternating with a nonzero generator of that line. The proof handles comparable and incomparable valuation cases separately.

Successive residue quotients recover quasi-prime flags down to residual curves. Their inertia determines the composite valuation, because the value group has no nonzero \(\ell\)-divisible convex subgroup. These local constructions remain multiplicative statements in residue characteristic \(\ell\). No Galois-cohomological interpretation in that characteristic is silently imposed.

## 2. Residual point lines and the finite-constant-field test

Section 3 defines \(S_F^{\mathrm{in}}\) as the closure of the union of quasi-prime inertia spaces. This is not the closed subgroup generated by them. For a terminal flag \(v\), its image
\(J_v=\rho_v(S_F^{\mathrm{in}}\cap D_v)\) is a set of characters, not generally a vector subspace. The family of lines contained in this set is the intrinsic object used next.

Pop's density input is first stated for \(\mathbb Z_\ell\)-valued characters on an individual field. Its hypotheses include no nonzero \(\ell\)-divisible convex subgroup and an auxiliary subfield \(\kappa_1\) with \(\kappa_1v=\kappa v\) and both relevant residual/ambient relative dimensions equal to one. The auxiliary field need not be algebraically closed. Independent relative values along the terminal flag construct it.

Reduction of characters gives an inclusion from the hatted closed union into the finite-coefficient closed union. The inertia of each constant-reduction quasi-prime divisor has relative value group \(\mathbb Z\), so its character reduction is surjective. This transfers the required density consequence. The argument does not assert that reduction commutes with intersection with decomposition, and never lifts the given \(\Theta\).

A compactness argument with valuation rings in the patch topology shows that closure of the union introduces no nonvaluative characters. Residual images are also valuative, using approximation in the incomparable case.

For a smooth projective curve \(C/b\), the point orders
\(\delta_x=\operatorname{ord}_x\bmod\ell\) are nonzero and have distinct lines. They tend to zero outside finite subsets. The continuous sum

\[
\prod_{x\in C(b)}\Lambda\longrightarrow A_{b(C)}
\]

has exactly the constant coefficient families as kernel. The proof uses divisibility of \(\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)(b)\). This relation remains valid in characteristic \(\ell\). The further assertion that the common point-order kernel in \(V_{b(C)}\) has dimension \(2g(C)\) requires characteristic different from \(\ell\).

For the line family inside \(J_v\), convergence together with a one-dimensional relation kernel generated by a family with every coordinate nonzero characterizes residue constants algebraic over a finite field. In that case all its lines are actual point lines. Otherwise a nontrivial valuation of the constants produces an extra constant-reduction point line. The relation among the true points, extended by zero at that extra line, violates the required full-support kernel property.

## 3. Finite curve tests preserve mod-ℓ information

Section 4 fixes a normal projective model \(X\) of \(L/l\). The support of \(h\in V_L\) consists of prime divisors where its order is nonzero modulo \(\ell\). The quantity \(s_X(h)\) is the sum of their projective degrees, without multiplying by their order multiplicities.

A finitely generated extension has a finite-dimensional Kummer kernel. The relative algebraic closure of the base inside it is finite, and \(r\) independent classes becoming powers would force an extension of degree \(\ell^r\). This bound is used repeatedly to retain infinite-dimensional families after finite extensions.

The finite curve test takes any finite independent list \(g_1,\ldots,g_m\) in \(V_L\). It constructs a terminal quasi-prime flag with residual constants algebraic over a finite field of characteristic different from \(\ell\), and a smooth residual curve of genus at most \(G_X\). The classes remain independent, and the number of nonzero point orders of each residue is exactly \(s_X([g_j])\).

The genus bound depends only on the embedded model, not on the list or the degree \(\ell^m\) of its simultaneous root cover. The construction:

1. Removes codimension-two singular/intersection loci, then cuts by general hyperplanes while preserving the integral finite étale Kummer cover. The paper gives an incidence-square argument for geometric integrality of the hyperplane pullback.
2. Meets the supporting divisors transversely, preserving their integer orders at exactly their degrees' worth of points. Hilbert-polynomial differences bound the final genus without assuming that \(X\) is Cohen–Macaulay.
3. Spreads all finite data over a finitely generated coefficient ring with \(\ell\) inverted, preserving the smooth curve, root-cover independence and parameter–unit expressions.
4. Specializes to finite-field constants and realizes the result by a constant-reduction valuation followed by the smooth flag. A monic primitive-element model and its irreducible reduction identify the desired residue field exactly.

The construction of the constant valuation allows a transcendence basis of any cardinality. The later proof therefore does not depend on countability or on intersecting infinitely many open conditions.

## 4. Five fixed pencil members give a uniform support bound

For \(t\in K\setminus k\), put \(h_a=\Theta([t-a])\). The claim is

\[
\sup_{a\in k}s_X(h_a)<\infty.
\]

The classes \([t-a]\) are independent in \(V_{k(t)}\). Removing finitely many indices eliminates the finite Kummer kernel on passing to \(K\). Choose five fixed anchors from the remaining set, then apply the finite curve test to them and one additional parameter.

Independent evaluations on the matched decomposition space force the six functions \(t-a_j\) to have equal values, with every anchor difference having that same value. After rescaling by a constant, their residues form one pencil \(\tau_0-\beta_j\) with distinct \(\beta_j\). In original characteristic zero the five anchors are chosen to include \(b_0,b_0+1,b_0+\ell\). Their equal valuation forces residue characteristic different from \(\ell\). In positive original characteristic this exclusion is automatic.

The intrinsic point matching preserves the number of nonzero orders of each tested class and the common point-order kernel. Since both residues now have characteristic different from \(\ell\), their genera agree and are at most \(G_X\).

Remove the maximal inseparable power \(\tau_0=\tau^{p^r}\). Orders remain nonzero modulo \(\ell\) at exactly the same points. If the separable map defined by \(\tau\) has degree \(n'\), an anchor fiber has at most \(N_i+n'/\ell\) points, where \(N_i=s_X(h_{a_i})\). Its different contribution is at least
\((1-1/\ell)n'-N_i\). The five disjoint fibers and Riemann–Hurwitz give

\[
\bigl(5(1-1/\ell)-2\bigr)n'
\le 2G_X-2+\sum_{i=1}^{5}N_i.
\]

The coefficient is positive even at \(\ell=2\). The sixth member has zeros and poles in two fibers, hence at most \(2n'\) nonzero point orders. Restoring the finitely many omitted parameters proves the bound. The test curve can vary with the sixth class, while its genus bound and the five anchors stay fixed.

## 5. A single incidence curve and exact descent

A curve subfield is relatively algebraically closed, contains the constants and has relative transcendence degree one. Its mod-\(\ell\) group embeds in the ambient one. Distinct curve subfields have only a finite-dimensional intersection of these groups, whereas each group is infinite dimensional. The proof first compares the two fields in the function field of their product of curves, where the common classes lie in a point-order kernel, then controls the finite Kummer kernel on passing to the ambient field.

For \(H=\Theta(V_E)\), the union of the pencil supports is an infinite family \(\mathcal S\) of bounded-degree divisors. It is infinite because the common divisor-order kernel on \(X\) has dimension at most \(2G_X\), by the finite curve test. At all but finitely many divisors of \(\mathcal S\), each fixed representative \(g\) of a class in \(H\) is a unit with an \(\ell\)-th-power residue. Matching inertia and decomposition proves this: their restriction images on \(E\) are the same one-dimensional line whenever the corresponding inertia detects \(E\).

Bounded degree gives finitely many Hilbert polynomials and a finite-type parameter family. A fixed integral parameter curve \(T'\) inside that family supplies an incidence variety \(Z'\), with

\[
L\subset M=l(Z'),\qquad P_0=l(T')\subset M,\qquad
[M:L]<\infty,\qquad M=LP_0,
\]

and \(M/P_0\) regular.

The proof must make this same \(T'\) work for every \(g\in H\). Split root covers on the dense tested fibers force geometric generic splitting. Shrinking the base separately for each class would be inadequate, since the resulting open might miss \(T'\). Instead, for each class the generic root is descended to a finite parameter extension. After normalizing that finite cover of the base, the smooth unit locus is normal. A generically split finite étale cover of this normal locus splits everywhere, so it splits over the chosen curve's generic point. Thus \(g\) has an \(\ell\)-th root in \(M\overline{P_0}\), while \(T',M,P_0\) stay fixed.

Hilbert 90 then identifies its class with one from \(P_0\). To descend to \(L\), take a finite normal extension containing \(M\). Infinite-dimensional intersection forces automorphisms over \(L\) to preserve the relative closure of \(P_0\). Intersecting with \(L\), with uniform \(p\)-powers if necessary, gives a curve subfield \(P_L\). Regularity and the compositum identity yield

\[
M\overline{P_0}=L\overline{P_L}.
\]

A second Hilbert-90 descent gives \(\Theta(V_E)\subset V_{P_L}\) already in \(V_L\). Applying the inverse map and finite-intersection lemma upgrades containment to equality and gives a bijection of curve subfields.

This exact compositum descent is important with finite coefficients. The proof does not discard the class by using a norm whose extension degree could annihilate it modulo \(\ell\).

## 6. Recognizing true divisors and enough rational functions

Section 6 recognizes a true divisor among quasi-divisors by the existence of a curve subfield \(E\) for which
\(\dim_\Lambda(V_E\cap D_v^\perp)<\infty\). A valuation trivial on constants admits such an \(E\) through a transcendental residue and the finite Kummer-kernel bound. If the valuation is nontrivial on constants, infinitely many \(1+cx\) are principal units whose classes retain infinite dimension. The criterion therefore fails for every curve subfield.

A good curve subfield is rational and every one of its point lines is detected by a nonzero restriction of true divisorial inertia. Inertia restrictions divisible by \(\ell\) are zero and are deliberately excluded. The unique full-support relation detects whether every point is present. Once all are present, the zero common point-order kernel detects genus zero.

Ratios \(t=a/b\) of two transverse local parameters at a smooth point give good functions. The exceptional divisor of the blowup has a rational-coordinate residue, forcing \(k(t)\) to be relatively algebraically closed. The divisors \(a-cb\) and \(b\) detect all finite points and infinity with multiplicity one.

For every nonzero \(f\), choose the smooth point where it is a unit. Then both \(t=a/b\) and \(ft\) are good. Thus good functions generate the field and their classes span \(V_F\). A common-partner construction supplies, for any finite list of good \(t_i\), one good \(s\) independent of each, such that every \(t_i+cs\), \(c\ne0\), is good. These local constructions need dimension two and impose no higher threshold.

## 7. Simultaneous values recover constants and algebraic relations

The induced point maps \(B_t:\mathbb P^1(k)\to\mathbb P^1(l)\) initially are only set bijections. Coordinates are chosen so that they fix infinity. No initial Zariski continuity is assumed.

For any finite tuple of good functions, blow up a smooth point where all differentials are nonzero. Its exceptional divisor has order one on every \(t_i-t_i(P)\) simultaneously. Matching that divisor transfers the entire value tuple to the target relation variety. Applying the inverse map gives the reverse dense-open inclusion.

For \(z_c=t+cs\), these inclusions force the target relation variety to be two-dimensional, with a generically one-point projection to the first two coordinates. Its function-field extension is purely inseparable. Thus the transported addition is a perfect rational function \(R_c(X,Y)\), allowing \(p\)-power roots.

A cofinitely injective perfect rational function in one variable is a projective linear map composed with an integral Frobenius power. One-variable slices of the \(R_c\)'s align the \(B_t\)'s modulo finite disagreement and embed the transported affine group in this transformation group.

The Frobenius exponent of the affine image is zero, since the multiplicative group of an algebraically closed field is divisible and the additive group is divisible or torsion. The image therefore lies in \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(l)\). The translation subgroup cannot be contained in a torus with only identity/inversion normalizer action. It is conjugate to a group of translations, producing an injective additive map \(\chi:k\to l\). Conjugation by dilations makes it multiplicative, identifying the characteristics.

Surjectivity requires the two-variable identity again. The proof obtains
\(F(X,B(b))=X+\chi(b)\) for cofinitely many \(b\), with a finite exceptional set of \(X\) that may depend on \(b\). It first promotes each fixed-\(b\) equality to an identity of perfect rational functions. Only then does it choose one \(X_0\) and evaluate, giving
\(\chi(b)=h(B(b))\) outside a finite set. There is no selection of a point avoiding infinitely many unrelated exceptional sets. Cofinite injectivity puts \(h\) in the projective/Frobenius group, so \(\chi(k)\) is cofinite in \(l\), hence equals \(l\).

A common partner aligns every good function with the same constant-field isomorphism \(\sigma\). Write \(B_t=H_t\sigma\) outside finite sets and set \(u_t=H_t^{-1}(t')\in L^i\). For each finite tuple, delete its finitely many exceptional coordinate fibers and take closures. The \(H_t\)'s are Zariski homeomorphisms, including their coordinatewise Frobenius powers, so the two simultaneous-value inclusions prove equality of the relation varieties. Every polynomial relation is preserved in both directions. Good-function generation now constructs \(\alpha:K^i\to L^i\), and matched good subfields give surjectivity.

## 8. One global scalar and only Frobenius ambiguity

Section 8 first separates true divisors by their restrictions to good rational subfields. Decomposition has infinite-dimensional restriction when the valuation is trivial on a curve subfield, and dimension at most one otherwise. Using decomposition rather than only inertia retains this distinction when the ramification multiplicity is divisible by \(\ell\).

A Chinese-remainder construction supplies a good \(a/b\) whose restriction is nonconstant along one divisor and positively valued along another. The table on p. 33 records the value prescriptions, and independent first-order terms at a third smooth point guarantee goodness.

A compatible automorphism preserving every good rational subspace must therefore fix all true-divisor pairs. On a fixed good rational subfield it fixes each point line. The single relation among point orders forces the individual point scalars to agree. A blowup detecting two good functions simultaneously identifies their two subfield scalars. Since their classes span \(V_K\), the scalar is global. Applied to \(\alpha_1^{-1}\Theta\), this proves \(\Theta=a\alpha_1\).

Finally, a field automorphism acting by a scalar preserves the perfection of each good rational subfield. Its action there is a projective transformation and Frobenius power. Classes with divisors \([a]-[b]\) show that the induced point permutation fixes every unordered pair, hence every point. The transformation agrees with the action on constants and fixes \(0,1,\infty\). It is therefore identity in characteristic zero or one Frobenius power in positive characteristic. The exponent is common to every good function because it is determined on the constants. Generation proves the asserted uniqueness on the whole perfect closure.

## Evidence, limits and follow-up scope

This is a theoretical reconstruction proof, with no experiments, benchmark or computational implementation. It gives an isomorphism classification from infinite algebraic data. It does not provide a finite-sample, noisy, efficient or algorithmic reconstruction guarantee.

The theorem excludes constant characteristic \(\ell\), non-algebraically-closed constants and relative dimension one. Local multiplicative arguments may include residue characteristic \(\ell\), but the genus and Kummer steps explicitly arrange or require its exclusion. Positive-characteristic reconstruction is of perfect closures, with the stated scalar and Frobenius identifications.

The principal imported results are the precise finite-coefficient alternating-pair statement from Topaz's 2019 v1 source, Pop's 2015 minimized density result with its Topaz appendix, and standard algebraic-geometric results cited by Stacks tags.
