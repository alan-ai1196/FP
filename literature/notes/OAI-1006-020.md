## 1. Statement, scope and normalizations

Theorem 1.1 asserts that a continuous irreducible odd two-dimensional representation of \(G_{\mathbb Q}\) over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_2\), finitely ramified and de Rham at two with distinct Hodge–Tate weights, is a Tate twist of the representation of a classical cuspidal eigenform. Scalar and reducible semisimple residual representations are included.

The introduction separates three stages: potential completed-form occurrence for the entire target family, actual classical vectors at the target, then cyclic solvable descent of an already classical packet. The locally reducible branch keeps a chosen line character, uses dense specially normalized ordinary points, and proposes a finite joint-support and polynomial-orbit passage to the target. Those later proofs remain unread at this checkpoint.

The conventions are geometric Frobenius and reciprocity sending a uniformizer to geometric Frobenius. The cyclotomic character has exponent one, Hodge–Tate weight \(-1\), and local expression \(\varepsilon=x|x|\). With \(\chi=\det r\), the completed-form central character is \(\psi=\chi\varepsilon\), and the local block parameter is \(r\otimes\varepsilon\), of determinant \(\chi\varepsilon^2\).

At a locally reducible target the selected line character \(\eta_1\) has larger exponent \(a>b\), with \(\eta_2=\chi/\eta_1\). The favorable upper-Borel unnormalized inducing order is \((\eta_2\varepsilon,\eta_1)\), and the algebraic factor is \(\operatorname{Sym}^{a-b-1}\det^{b+1}\). This describes the target and normalized dense points, not every continuous family member. Group duals are contragredient, while the commuting scalar Hecke, torus and \(U\)-actions are transposed without inversion.

Evaluating one representation on geometric rather than arithmetic Frobenius inverts eigenvalues but does not replace the representation by its dual. A genuinely dual modular representation is converted with the wedge pairing and a nebentype twist, preserving the conclusion up to integer Tate twist.

## 2. Initial reductions actually proved

A continuous representation over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_2\) has finite coefficients: the closed subgroups defined over the countably many finite extensions cover the profinite source, so Baire gives one open subgroup. Adding finitely many coset matrix entries gives a finite field for the whole image. Compactness then supplies a stable lattice.

“Virtually solvable” refers to the identity component of the reduced algebraic monodromy over an algebraic closure. A nontrivial connected unipotent radical would supply a globally invariant line. A scalar connected component gives finite projective image and equal weights after finite restriction, contradicting regularity. The remaining case is quadratic induction from a character.

The rank-one de Rham character is made into an algebraic Hecke character using local algebraicity, the global unit relation and finitely many ray-class representatives. This does not assume initial rationality over a number field. A real quadratic inducing field forces equal algebraic exponents by the unit theorem, again excluded. An imaginary quadratic field gives the required theta series. Hence the remaining target has nonsolvable connected monodromy and stays absolutely irreducible after every finite restriction.

Auxiliary fields are totally real, solvable, of even degree and completely split at two. For degree \(d\), with \(u\) allowed nondyadic finite places, the total number of allowed real, dyadic and other places is \(s=2d+u\). Even degree permits the definite quaternion algebra ramified at every real place and no finite place. Trace-image closures and pro-modular containments are set-theoretic. Traces determine semisimplification at a residual point, not its extension class.

## 3. Representation spaces, dimensions and curve topology

Proposition 3.1 uses determinant deformation rings and the finite universal Cayley–Hamilton algebra to build actual matrix representation schemes, including scalar residue. Fixed determinant is a base change. Closed incidence in projective lines records invariant lines and specified characters. Proper incidence descends over the globally absolutely irreducible locus. Each recorded character value satisfies its quadratic characteristic polynomial, so adjoining finitely many character generators gives a finite completed extension of the trace image.

At a globally absolutely irreducible parameter, the framed conjugacy orbit has dimension three. A recorded line costs at most one more dimension, and costs zero if its characters are generically distinct. Lemma 3.2 contracts a formal prime before applying the finite-type dimension formula. It does not assert that arbitrary formal primes algebraize. Lemma 3.3 proves density of normalized characteristic-two curves and integral characteristic-zero coefficient points by formal Noether normalization and suitable specializations.

For a curve representation over \(K=k((t))\), every local and global problem is enhanced by the **same** coefficient variable \(z\mapsto t\), since a local restriction need not generate all of \(K\). The coefficient DVR is
\[
 \mathcal L=\widehat{\mathcal O[[z]]_{(\varpi)}},
 \qquad\mathcal L/\varpi^a=(\mathcal O/\varpi^a)((z)).
\]
Residue and finite-length coefficients keep their Laurent topology, rather than a discrete topology.

Lemma 3.4 identifies enhanced completed localization with continuous deformation functors. A bounded set with integral residual image lies in a finite order
\[
 B=A_0+U+U^2+\cdots+U^{N-1},
\]
where \(U\) contains bounded nilpotent increments and the maximal ideal has nilpotence exponent \(N\). Negative Laurent powers in nilpotent increments are permitted, with no uniform valuation bound across all Artinian targets. A residual element \(z^{-1}\) is excluded: integral residual image is essential. Finite-jet expansion supplies the converse continuity. Frames, determinant, lines and restriction maps are preserved.

A component of integral dimension \(e\) has enhanced curve completion dimension \(e\): adjoining \(z\) adds one and the curve quotient subtracts one. At a characteristic-zero coefficient point, no \(z\) is added, giving dimension \(e-1\).

The curve-field cohomology lemma passes from finite \(k[[t]]/t^n\)-coefficients through inverse limits and then inverts \(t\). Compact Nakayama gives finite cohomology modules. Duality at real places uses Tate cohomology, while the displayed \(h^0_v\) remains ordinary invariants. These real contributions are retained at characteristic two.

For \(D=\operatorname{ad}^0\rho\), the dual in characteristic two is \(\operatorname{ad}\rho/\text{scalars}\), not automatically \(D\). The one-global-frame relative relation estimate over all real places and \(m\) dyadic local factors is
\[
 g-r\ge3-6m-h^0(D^\vee(1)).
\]
The proof bounds actual minimal relations using two obstruction stages: the kernel on \(H^2\), then the cokernel on \(Z^1\). Minimal relative coordinates cannot remove an undetected surviving relation. Real Euler terms cancel with the corresponding global terms. Extra local conditions are base changes of this presentation. With independent local frames the forgetful fiber has dimension \(4|S|-1\) on the absolutely irreducible locus.

## 4. Bad loci and uniform local inertia

In characteristic two, irreducible dihedral curves lie in a closed trace locus of dimension at most \(d\), by the minus-rank bound for characters over finitely many quadratic extensions. Fixing their product is an isogeny constraint even though two is not a unit. Finite projective image gives constant traces and only the residual pseudorepresentation. For abelian totally real \(F\), Brumer–Leopoldt gives dimension at most one for the reducible locus.

Lemma 3.9 gives a uniform finite local extension for a finite list of source families at nondyadic places. The finite Cayley–Hamilton algebra first kills the residual group, leaving pro-two congruence image. Wild and non-two tame inertia disappear. The arithmetic relation \(\phi\tau\phi^{-1}=\tau^q\) bounds semisimple tame eigenvalue order by \(q^2-1\). A fixed further extension leaves unipotent inertia at every field-valued point.

On a characteristic-two curve, unipotent two-by-two inertia has exponent at most two. Prescribing the original Frobenius trace to its residual value, with finite determinant, also gives finite Frobenius order, hence finite local image. Required finite local extensions are built through cyclic prime-degree Grunwald–Wang steps with dyadic and real splitting. Only the prime-degree statement is used. New base-field ramification does not create new ramification of the restricted representation.

## 5. Raw tame rings and the different marked-line base

The unrestricted real–dyadic product is a flat complete-intersection normal domain over \(\mathcal L\), dimension \(1+8d\), with normal integral special fiber of dimension \(8d\). Real scalar residue has model
\[
 \mathcal O[[a,b,c]]/(a^2+2a+bc),
\]
with geometrically normal special fiber. The dyadic input is the all-residue fixed-determinant deformation theorem of Böckle–Iyengar–Paškūnas. Tensor-product normality, complete intersections and excellence carry these properties to the common enhanced curve.

For \(u\) nondyadic places with \(q_j\equiv1\pmod4\), the raw matrix model prescribes inertia eigenvalues of type \(\eta=1\) or \(i\). Its expected dimension, after \(g\) further variables, is \(e=1+8d+4u+g\). Frobenius determinants remain unrestricted.

For \(i\)-type, a proper invariant-eigenline incidence has chart equations \(x_jn_j=2ih_j\). Weighted convergent normal forms prove integrality of the simultaneous chart, without requiring uniform denominator bounds. Connected closed fibers and properness prove the incidence integral and its image all of the raw spectrum set-theoretically. This gives one horizontal irreducible component, not reducedness of the raw ring.

For unipotent type, each place has an unramified pattern and the closure of a nontrivial-unipotent pattern. Proper incidences yield horizontal components indexed by these choices. Their common special fiber has two reduced components at each place:
\[
 U:a=b=c=0,\qquad
 R:g'=d,\quad a^2+bc=bf+ce'=0.
\]
The homogeneous polynomial argument precedes completion. On generic ramified pieces \(\tau\ne1\), while generic unramified pieces have nonzero Frobenius trace. Thus every special-fiber generic point lies on exactly its corresponding horizontal unipotent component. The raw special fiber may have nilpotents, such as \(a(d+g')\). The incidence is not a normalization, since a scalar pair can have a projective-line fiber.

The marked scalar dyadic condition serves connectedness instead. After killing nilpotent diagonal character torsion, its reduced character ring is \(k[[x,y]]\). The dyadic Demuškin presentation gives a linear cocycle relation on three upper-entry values. An explicit triangular change turns it into
\[
 x^2v+y^2u_2=0.
\]
Its coefficient ideal is \((x^2,y^2)\), with radical \((x,y)\), and is not radical itself.

For \(m\) marks, a stratum with \(r\) vanished coefficient rows loses \(2r\) base dimensions but gains only \(r\) fiber dimensions. No component can lie entirely on a rank-drop stratum. The remaining vector bundle has one generic component. The equations form a regular sequence, so absence of embedded primes and generic reducedness give an integral polynomial product. Homogeneous completion embeds in a formal power-series ring over the fraction field. The full reduced real-plus-marked product is therefore an integral complete intersection of dimension \(2d+5m\), also after finite constant extension. No normality claim is made for this marked product.
