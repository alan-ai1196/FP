## 1. Statement, scope and normalizations

Theorem 1.1 asserts that a continuous irreducible odd two-dimensional representation of \(G_{\mathbb Q}\) over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_2\), finitely ramified and de Rham at two with distinct Hodge–Tate weights, is a Tate twist of the representation of a classical cuspidal eigenform. Scalar and reducible semisimple residual representations are included.

The proof separates three stages: potential completed-form occurrence for the entire target family, actual classical vectors at the target, then cyclic solvable descent of an already classical packet. The locally reducible branch keeps a chosen line character, uses dense specially normalized ordinary points, and proves a finite joint-support and polynomial-orbit passage to the target. The ordinary dense points do not make the entire continuous family de Rham.

The conventions are geometric Frobenius and reciprocity sending a uniformizer to geometric Frobenius. The cyclotomic character has exponent one, Hodge–Tate weight \(-1\), and local expression \(\varepsilon=x|x|\). With \(\chi=\det r\), the completed-form central character is \(\psi=\chi\varepsilon\), and the local block parameter is \(r\otimes\varepsilon\), of determinant \(\chi\varepsilon^2\).

At a locally reducible target the selected line character \(\eta_1\) has larger exponent \(a>b\), with \(\eta_2=\chi/\eta_1\). The favorable upper-Borel unnormalized inducing order is \((\eta_2\varepsilon,\eta_1)\), and the algebraic factor is \(\operatorname{Sym}^{a-b-1}\det^{b+1}\). This describes the target and normalized dense points, not every continuous family member. Group duals are contragredient, while the commuting scalar Hecke, torus and \(U\)-actions are transposed without inversion.

Evaluating one representation on geometric rather than arithmetic Frobenius inverts eigenvalues but does not replace the representation by its dual. A genuinely dual modular representation is converted with the wedge pairing and a nebentype twist, preserving the conclusion up to integer Tate twist.

## 2. Initial reductions actually proved

A continuous representation over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_2\) has finite coefficients: the closed subgroups defined over the countably many finite extensions cover the profinite source, so Baire gives one open subgroup. Adding finitely many coset matrix entries gives a finite field for the whole image. Compactness then supplies a stable lattice.

“Virtually solvable” refers to the identity component of the reduced algebraic monodromy over an algebraic closure. A nontrivial connected unipotent radical would supply a globally invariant line. A scalar connected component gives finite projective image and equal weights after finite restriction, contradicting regularity. The remaining case is quadratic induction from a character.

The rank-one de Rham character is made into an algebraic Hecke character using local algebraicity, the global unit relation and finitely many ray-class representatives. This does not assume initial rationality over a number field. A real quadratic inducing field forces equal algebraic exponents by the unit theorem, again excluded. An imaginary quadratic field gives the required theta series. Hence the remaining target has nonsolvable connected monodromy and stays absolutely irreducible after every finite restriction.

Auxiliary fields are totally real, solvable, of even degree and completely split at two. For degree \(d\), with \(u\) allowed nondyadic finite places, the total number of allowed real, dyadic and other places is \(s=2d+u\). Even degree permits the definite quaternion algebra ramified at every real place and no finite place. Trace-image closures and pro-modular containments are set-theoretic. Traces determine semisimplification at a residual point, not its extension class.

## 3. Representation spaces, dimensions and curve topology

Proposition 3.1 uses determinant deformation rings and the finite universal Cayley–Hamilton algebra to build actual matrix representation schemes, including scalar residue. Fixed determinant is a base change. Closed incidence in projective lines records invariant lines and specified characters. Proper incidence descends over the globally absolutely irreducible locus. Each recorded character value satisfies its quadratic characteristic polynomial, so adjoining finitely many character generators gives a finite completed extension of the trace image.

At a globally absolutely irreducible parameter, the framed conjugacy orbit has dimension three. A recorded line costs at most one more dimension, and costs zero if its characters are generically distinct. Lemma 3.2 contracts a formal prime before applying the finite-type dimension formula. It does not assert that arbitrary formal primes algebraize. Lemma 3.3 proves density of normalized characteristic-two curves and integral characteristic-zero coefficient points by formal Noether normalization and suitable specializations.

For a curve representation over \(K=k((t))\), every local and global problem is enhanced by the **same** coefficient variable \(z\mapsto t\), since a local restriction need not generate all of \(K\). The coefficient DVR is
\[
 \mathcal L=\widehat{\mathcal O[[z]]_{(\varpi)}},
 \qquad\mathcal L/\varpi^a=(\mathcal O/\varpi^a)((z)).
\]
Residue and finite-length coefficients keep their Laurent topology, rather than a discrete topology.

Lemma 3.4 identifies enhanced completed localization with continuous deformation functors. A bounded set with integral residual image lies in a finite order
\[
 B=A_0+U+U^2+\cdots+U^{N-1},
\]
where \(U\) contains bounded nilpotent increments and the maximal ideal has nilpotence exponent \(N\). Negative Laurent powers in nilpotent increments are permitted, with no uniform valuation bound across all Artinian targets. A residual element \(z^{-1}\) is excluded: integral residual image is essential. Finite-jet expansion supplies the converse continuity. Frames, determinant, lines and restriction maps are preserved.

A component of integral dimension \(e\) has enhanced curve completion dimension \(e\): adjoining \(z\) adds one and the curve quotient subtracts one. At a characteristic-zero coefficient point, no \(z\) is added, giving dimension \(e-1\).

The curve-field cohomology lemma passes from finite \(k[[t]]/t^n\)-coefficients through inverse limits and then inverts \(t\). Compact Nakayama gives finite cohomology modules. Duality at real places uses Tate cohomology, while the displayed \(h^0_v\) remains ordinary invariants. These real contributions are retained at characteristic two.

For \(D=\operatorname{ad}^0\rho\), the dual in characteristic two is \(\operatorname{ad}\rho/\text{scalars}\), not automatically \(D\). The one-global-frame relative relation estimate over all real places and \(m\) dyadic local factors is
\[
 g-r\ge3-6m-h^0(D^\vee(1)).
\]
The proof bounds actual minimal relations using two obstruction stages: the kernel on \(H^2\), then the cokernel on \(Z^1\). Minimal relative coordinates cannot remove an undetected surviving relation. Real Euler terms cancel with the corresponding global terms. Extra local conditions are base changes of this presentation. With independent local frames the forgetful fiber has dimension \(4|S|-1\) on the absolutely irreducible locus.

## 4. Bad loci and uniform local inertia

In characteristic two, irreducible dihedral curves lie in a closed trace locus of dimension at most \(d\), by the minus-rank bound for characters over finitely many quadratic extensions. Fixing their product is an isogeny constraint even though two is not a unit. Finite projective image gives constant traces and only the residual pseudorepresentation. For abelian totally real \(F\), Brumer–Leopoldt gives dimension at most one for the reducible locus.

Lemma 3.9 gives a uniform finite local extension for a finite list of source families at nondyadic places. The finite Cayley–Hamilton algebra first kills the residual group, leaving pro-two congruence image. Wild and non-two tame inertia disappear. The arithmetic relation \(\phi\tau\phi^{-1}=\tau^q\) bounds semisimple tame eigenvalue order by \(q^2-1\). A fixed further extension leaves unipotent inertia at every field-valued point.

On a characteristic-two curve, unipotent two-by-two inertia has exponent at most two. Prescribing the original Frobenius trace to its residual value, with finite determinant, also gives finite Frobenius order, hence finite local image. Required finite local extensions are built through cyclic prime-degree Grunwald–Wang steps with dyadic and real splitting. Only the prime-degree statement is used. New base-field ramification does not create new ramification of the restricted representation.

## 5. Raw tame rings and the different marked-line base

The unrestricted real–dyadic product is a flat complete-intersection normal domain over \(\mathcal L\), dimension \(1+8d\), with normal integral special fiber of dimension \(8d\). Real scalar residue has model
\[
 \mathcal O[[a,b,c]]/(a^2+2a+bc),
\]
with geometrically normal special fiber. The dyadic input is the all-residue fixed-determinant deformation theorem of Böckle–Iyengar–Paškūnas. Tensor-product normality, complete intersections and excellence carry these properties to the common enhanced curve.

For \(u\) nondyadic places with \(q_j\equiv1\pmod4\), the raw matrix model prescribes inertia eigenvalues of type \(\eta=1\) or \(i\). Its expected dimension, after \(g\) further variables, is \(e=1+8d+4u+g\). Frobenius determinants remain unrestricted.

For \(i\)-type, a proper invariant-eigenline incidence has chart equations \(x_jn_j=2ih_j\). Weighted convergent normal forms prove integrality of the simultaneous chart, without requiring uniform denominator bounds. Connected closed fibers and properness prove the incidence integral and its image all of the raw spectrum set-theoretically. This gives one horizontal irreducible component, not reducedness of the raw ring.

For unipotent type, each place has an unramified pattern and the closure of a nontrivial-unipotent pattern. Proper incidences yield horizontal components indexed by these choices. Their common special fiber has two reduced components at each place:
\[
 U:a=b=c=0,\qquad
 R:g'=d,\quad a^2+bc=bf+ce'=0.
\]
The homogeneous polynomial argument precedes completion. On generic ramified pieces \(\tau\ne1\), while generic unramified pieces have nonzero Frobenius trace. Thus every special-fiber generic point lies on exactly its corresponding horizontal unipotent component. The raw special fiber may have nilpotents, such as \(a(d+g')\). The incidence is not a normalization, since a scalar pair can have a projective-line fiber.

The marked scalar dyadic condition serves connectedness instead. After killing nilpotent diagonal character torsion, its reduced character ring is \(k[[x,y]]\). The dyadic Demuškin presentation gives a linear cocycle relation on three upper-entry values. An explicit triangular change turns it into
\[
 x^2v+y^2u_2=0.
\]
Its coefficient ideal is \((x^2,y^2)\), with radical \((x,y)\), and is not radical itself.

For \(m\) marks, a stratum with \(r\) vanished coefficient rows loses \(2r\) base dimensions but gains only \(r\) fiber dimensions. No component can lie entirely on a rank-drop stratum. The remaining vector bundle has one generic component. The equations form a regular sequence, so absence of embedded primes and generic reducedness give an integral polynomial product. Homogeneous completion embeds in a formal power-series ring over the fraction field. The full reduced real-plus-marked product is therefore an integral complete intersection of dimension \(2d+5m\), also after finite constant extension. No normality claim is made for this marked product.

## 6. Completed forms and actual local actions — paper §4

For sufficiently small determinant-one compact groups, the full definite completed-form module is finite free over the Iwasawa algebra. Finite projective stabilizers are removed by shrinking the level. The residual summand embeds in this free module. Integral and torsion-coefficient reduction is exact.

Fixed smooth principal types and algebraic weights of a fixed central degree give a dense classical subspace. Algebraic matrix coefficients separate points modulo the center, and finite stabilizer averaging involves only a fixed finite denominator. Distinct sufficiently ramified finite inertial characters exclude character representations and the exceptional ordinary degeneracy. Classical definite forms transfer to Hilbert cusp forms with the stated local potentially semistable parameters.

The Hecke algebra is the closure in the endomorphisms of the actual integral compact module. Galois traces are interpolated using each finite operator test, rather than assuming a uniform denominator for all classical vectors. For a fixed test, finitely many vectors are approximated and their denominators cleared once. Integral Hecke operators are contractions. Chebotarev then gives a Cauchy trace at every finite test, hence a continuous integral trace. The determinant identity holds on the dense classical subspace. The compact pseudodeformation map is surjective, and the algebra is reduced, torsion-free and the closure of classical support.

The product-block equivalence uses finite-length cyclic spans and successive one-factor equivalences. The Paškūnas–Tung input includes scalar residue at two and finiteness over the integral pseudodeformation center. It does not require integral surjectivity of the center map, whose comparison may have a cokernel killed by two. For the product projective generator \(P\), the multiplicity module \(L=\operatorname{Hom}(P,M)\) is finite over the block endomorphism ring and the local pseudodeformation ring, faithful over the Hecke algebra, and finite over that algebra. Faithfulness embeds the Hecke algebra in the finite local-linear endomorphism ring of \(L\), proving its local finiteness.

Actual local center actions are compared with global Galois restriction on the dense distinct-principal-type classical subspace. The irreducible local case uses the unitary completion and locally algebraic comparison. The ordinary case uses the precise inducing order. If \(n=a-b-1\) and \(\delta=\eta_l\varepsilon/\eta_h\), twisting by \(\eta_h^{-1}\circ\det\) gives the locally algebraic source characters \(|\cdot|x^{-n}\) and \(\delta|\cdot|^{-1}x^n\), with valuations \(-n-1\) and \(n+1\) at two. Its unitary completion reverses the order to \((\delta,1)\). Twisting back gives \((\eta_l\varepsilon,\eta_h)\). This is a statement about the locally algebraic subspace and its completion, not an identification of a whole locally analytic induction. Bounded maps and naturality of the center extend equality to the Banach space and then the torsion-free lattice.

At a characteristic-zero coefficient point the eigenspace contains a tensor product of irreducible unitary Banach representations with the prescribed local parameters. Absolutely irreducible regular de Rham local parameters supply locally algebraic vectors. In the generic reducible case, the possible continuous upper-Borel orders are \((\gamma_i\varepsilon,\gamma_j)\), with ratios excluding \(1,\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{-1}\).

Lemma 4.3 bounds the compact-dual growth degree of each irreducible smooth representation by one. The supersingular estimate explicitly covers two and bounds congruence invariants by \(2\cdot2^n+2\cdot2^{n+1}+8\). Products of \(d\) finite-length factors have growth at most \(d\). On one Hecke component \(C\), a nonzero module supported on that component embeds in the free Iwasawa module and has growth \(1+3d\). Filtering by \(r=\dim C\) parameters and bounding the block-finite quotient gives growth at most \(r+d\), hence
\[
 \dim C\ge1+2d.
\]

At auxiliary ratio level, exact function-space reduction and the projective block functor give flatness over the diamond group ring. Two old-to-Iwahori degeneracy maps \(\alpha\) and their trace maps \(\beta\) have \(C=\beta\alpha\) and one actual scalar operator
\[
 D=\det C\sim(q+1)^2-q\chi(\phi)^{-1}T(\phi)^2.
\]
At the selected characteristic-two curve, regular Frobenius makes \(D\) a unit. The integral identity \(\alpha\operatorname{adj}(C)\beta=D\operatorname{id}\) is checked on the principal and special classical local alternatives, then extended by density and torsion-freeness. Thus diamond augmentation gives \(2^h\) copies of the old module with its actual old unramified deformation action, not merely the same multiplicity. The operator relation is \(U^2-T(\tau)U+1=0\).

Quadratic characters trivial at dyadic and real places give actual semilinear intertwiners by multiplication by the character of the reduced norm. The ratio level and central character are preserved. Reduction fixes the characteristic-two point. These identities survive the enhanced curve coefficient change, localization, completion and smooth framing. Forward solvable base change transfers dense classical points to a common bounded level, using the uniform inertia bound. This proves potential pro-modular occurrence, without descending an arbitrary pro-modular family.

## 7. Patching and propagation — paper §5

Theorem 5.1 propagates potential pro-modularity from a characteristic-two curve to every irreducible closed locus containing it, when the generic representation is not virtually solvable and its allowed nondyadic local images are finite. Dyadic deformations remain unrestricted. Initial solvable base changes trivialize the bad curve images, make the determinant unramified there and arrange unipotent source inertia and \(q\equiv1\pmod4\).

The auxiliary problem allows variable global determinant while keeping its dyadic, real and bad-inertia restrictions. The tangent calculation uses the full adjoint module, whose trace pairing is perfect even in characteristic two. Auxiliary primes split the cyclotomic–Kummer tower and have regular split Frobenius. An undetected cocycle has zero first-order trace on the tower kernel. Four matrix-basis elements, their pair and triple traces identify the unchanged matrix algebra, so the cocycle there is inner. The kernel invariants are scalars, and the remaining tower characters have dimension at most two. Finite evaluations and Chebotarev then choose a fixed number \(h\) of primes at arbitrarily deep levels.

The real local Euler terms are retained. With independent frames the relative generator number is \(g=2h+1\). Representatives of old cotangent directions are fixed before the level varies. Lifts are corrected by multiples of the uniformizer so that every presentation has exactly the same composite to the old ring. This does not assume flatness of the old global ring.

The ray-class character construction gives \(b=h-2d+2>0\) auxiliary twist parameters. Dyadic units and uniformizers are killed, and real places split. The dyadic \(S\)-unit group has rank \(2d-1\). The direction of two is primitive, and the imposed cyclotomic–Kummer splitting kills both two and minus one in the cyclic auxiliary residue-unit quotients. The other \(2d-2\) generators leave a quotient of rank \(b\) and unbounded exponent. The ambient raw local ring with \(g\) variables has dimension
\[
 e=1+8d+4u+g=8d+4u+2h+2.
\]

The finite-level modules are flat over the diamond group ring, and augmentation is the actual old module repeated \(2^h\) times. The two tame types \(1\) and \(i\) agree modulo the uniformizer, including their deformation actions. The common special old support has dimension at least \(2d+4s-1\), using the component bound and the \(4s-1\) independent-frame directions.

The patching construction takes a nonprincipal ultraproduct separately at every finite maximal-ideal jet, then its inverse limit. For a fixed ring \(B\), the residue field becomes the ultrapower \(K^*\). This scalar extension need not be finite and is distinct from the finite curve-coefficient extensions. The map \(B\to B^*\) is faithfully flat, fixed finite modules extend exactly, and dimensions are preserved. Integrality follows from a centered discrete valuation and Chevalley cofinality, which give a uniform product-nonvanishing bound at finite jets.

For varying modules the proven operations are bounded generation, surjections and cokernels of fixed finite lists of operators. Arbitrary varying submodules are not asserted to patch exactly. The needed uniform Artin–Rees estimate concerns extensions with one **fixed quotient**: a fixed finite free presentation of that quotient controls the intersection filtration uniformly. A fixed-length filtration with fixed successive quotients then patches exactly.

Let \(J=(\varpi,V_1,\ldots,V_h)\). At any fixed truncation length, sufficiently deep diamond relations vanish to higher \(J\)-order. Flatness gives polynomial graded layers over the fixed old special module \(C=\overline N^{\oplus2^h}\). Because the old action was fixed in the presentations, these are fixed ambient modules, not only abstractly isomorphic vector spaces. The fixed-quotient Artin–Rees lemma patches this finite filtration. Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{gr}_J N_{\eta,\infty}
 =C^*\otimes_{K^*}K^*[T_0,\ldots,T_h].
\]
Initial forms and separatedness prove that \(\varpi,V_1,\ldots,V_h\) is a regular sequence. In particular,
\[
 \dim\operatorname{Supp}N_{\eta,\infty}\ge2d+4s+h,
 \qquad
 N_{\eta,\infty}/(V_1,\ldots,V_h)
 \cong(N_\eta^{\oplus2^h})^*
\]
with the original deformation action and before reduction modulo \(\varpi\).

Formal auxiliary character twisting supplies a finite map to the fixed-determinant patched ring with \(b\) formal variables. Determinants detect their squares. Its closed fiber has \(l_j^2=0\), hence finite length, and complete Nakayama proves finiteness. The twisted module has support dimension at least \(2d+4s+h+b=e\), while \(\varpi\) remains regular. For \(i\)-type the single horizontal component therefore has full support. The common special fiber transfers all its generic points to the unipotent type. Each such point has a unique horizontal component below it and height one there. A horizontal support component through it must be that unique component, giving full unipotent support.

Twists are removed using the actual finite-level quadratic intertwiners and their inverses, which patch compatibly. At a fixed-determinant point, \((1+l_j)^2=1\) forces \(l_j=0\) or \(-2\) in its residue field. Evaluation at the corresponding sign character leaves a nonzero module by Nakayama, and sign invariance returns the original point to the untwisted support. In characteristic two the two signs coincide.

Finally old auxiliary inertia is trivial, so the operator relation becomes \(V_j^2=0\). Every prime over an old point therefore contains the diamond augmentation ideal. Nakayama and the exact old-action augmentation show full support of the old module after faithful scalar extension, then before it. Passing from the framed enhanced germ to a reduced irreducible trace locus descends nilpotence by faithful flatness. Reducedness makes each Hecke-support equation zero in the localization, and injectivity of localization of a domain makes it zero on the entire locus. This is the asserted potential pro-modularity propagation.

## 8. Large fields with a bounded allowed set — paper §6

The allowed rational primes, including those of the modular seed, are fixed first. Proposition 6.1 constructs abelian totally real two-extensions \(\mathbb Q\subset A\subset F\), completely split at two, with a uniform bound \(u_A,u_F\le U\) on allowed nondyadic places while both \([A:\mathbb Q]\) and \([F:A]\) grow. For reducible residue with character ratio \(\alpha\), marks are needed only when \(\alpha_2=1\). They are all lifts of one dyadic place of \(A\), so \(m=[F:A]\) and \(m/d=1/d_A\). Otherwise \(m=0\).

For each \(\beta=\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\), the dual Selmer group with zero localization at every allowed unmarked place, including infinity, and unrestricted marked localization is zero. Restriction from \(A\) to \(F\) is injective on the residual extension classes. Poitou–Tate, with ordinary invariant dimensions at the real places, then gives the strict Selmer bounds
\[
 \dim H^1_{\rm str}(F,k(\beta))\le
 \begin{cases}
 d+u_F,&\alpha_2\ne1,\\
 2d-3m+u_F,&\alpha_2=1.
 \end{cases}
\]
The strict condition is zero at the marks and full at the other allowed places. The contribution of each marked dyadic place is minus one, compared with two at an unmarked scalar dyadic place.

For absolutely irreducible residue, a degree-\(2^n\) cyclic subfield of a prime cyclotomic field, ramified away from the residual splitting field, preserves the entire residual image. The conductor is chosen split in the cyclotomic–Kummer field for two, with every prescribed odd prime nonsquare modulo it. Each prescribed prime then has full Frobenius order and only one place above it in each cyclic layer. The conductor itself need not be allowed: base-field ramification does not ramify an originally unramified representation.

For scalar residue, the fixed quadratic base is \(B=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{17})\). Its narrow class number is one, proved using Minkowski's bound and explicit units with all signatures. A cyclic conductor inert in \(B\) gives a tower in which exactly one finite prime ramifies at each quadratic step. The one-prime genus lemma preserves odd narrow class number, and the signature map shows that totally positive units are squares.

Write \(\pi=(5+\sqrt{17})/2\), generating one dyadic prime of \(B\). It is positive at both real embeddings but nonsquare at the other dyadic completion, where its unit value is seven modulo eight. For totally positive squareclasses with even valuations away from the first set of dyadic lifts, the valuation-parity map is injective. A nonzero Selmer space would have an invariant vector under the cyclic two-group. Transitivity forces all parity entries to be one, so that vector is uniquely \([\pi]\), contrary to its required local squareness at the other dyadic places. This proves a stronger vanishing than needed. The only scalar restriction-kernel character ramifies at the excluded conductor, establishing injectivity as well.

For distinct characters, the paper proves its own rank-one characteristic-two version of auxiliary-prime switching. It draws on Ramakrishna's strategy but does not apply his lifting theorem unchanged. At a conductor with trivial coefficient and residue cardinality one modulo four, the unramified line and a transverse quadratic ramified line are self-orthogonal. If the opposite Selmer group's unramified evaluations surject onto all conductor coordinates, Poitou–Tate says that relaxing those local conditions creates no new classes. Switching to the ramified lines therefore replaces the original Selmer group by the kernel of its evaluations.

Evaluation freedom is proved over \(k_0=\mathbb F_2(\alpha)\), of size \(q\ge4\). The odd-order coefficient action kills cohomology of the auxiliary quotient, so restriction to the joint splitting kernel is injective. Its additive evaluation image is a \(k_0\)-subspace, and independence of classes forces surjectivity. Distinct coefficient Frobenius isotypes are independent. If the inverse characters are Frobenius-conjugate, semilinear coefficient Frobenius preserves all zero, rank and quadratic-line conditions.

When \(\alpha_2\ne1\), the base is \(\mathbb Q\) and the relevant Selmer dimension difference is minus one. Each switch has enough nonzero opposite-dual evaluation to decrease every nonzero original space. When \(\alpha_2=1\), use a quadratic base ramified at a prime where \(\alpha\) is nontrivial. First kill rational auxiliary spaces \(X_0\), with full real and zero dyadic conditions. This guarantees \(W\cap\sigma W=0\) for the two quadratic-base spaces \(W=X_B,Y_B\). At split conductors the paired evaluations are independent. A union bound
\[
 (q^{-1}+q^{-2})+q^{-2}+q^{-1}<1
\]
simultaneously secures rank two on the opposite dual, a nonzero original evaluation and preservation of \(X_0=0\). No independence between those three failure events is assumed.

If only one original space survives, it and its conjugate dual have dimension one. A final conductor inert in the quadratic base removes it. Inert evaluations have an affine offset, but evaluations of a class and its conjugate are exactly equal, including that offset. Pre-step \(X_0=0\) supplies injectivity of \(1+\sigma\). Its preservation after this final inert switch is neither needed nor asserted.

Conductors are combined through a diagonal cyclic quotient with every coefficient odd. The first conductor makes the prescribed old primes have full Frobenius order. Later conductors are quadratic residues at those primes and at earlier conductors, preserving their local quadratic lines. Each loop strictly decreases a finite initial cohomological dimension. An explicit bound on the number of conductors is independent of both cyclic exponents, although their numerical sizes need not be bounded. Each allowed old or conductor prime has at most \([B:\mathbb Q]\le2\) lifts. This is what makes the uniform \(U\) possible and permits
\[
 d+u_F+3<2d-1,\quad
 2d-2m+u_F+3<2d-m-1,\quad
 2d-2m-4-u_F>d.
\]
In the marked case, \(m>U+4\) and \(m(d_A-2)>U+4\) suffice. These degree choices occur after the allowed set and its uniform bound are fixed.

## 9. Connectedness and one common potential level — paper §7

Theorem 7.1 proves potential pro-modularity of the target and, more generally, of the closed trace image of an entire integral framed family containing it, over one common solvable totally real Galois field split at two and at one fixed tame level.

A classical cuspidal residual seed is chosen before preparing the fields. Absolutely irreducible residue uses Serre modularity at two. Reducible residue uses Billerey–Menares and also receives an explicit Eisenstein construction: \(f(\tau)-f(\ell\tau)\) has integral positive coefficients and zero constant term at infinity. Good reduction and the q-expansion principle exclude a vertical pole. At other cusps the constant-term factor is \(1-(\ell/e)^{-k'}\), with \(e=1\) or \(\ell\). Choosing \(\ell\equiv1\) to a sufficiently large fixed power of two clears all finitely many cusp denominators. The residue is nonzero and cuspidal. For even \(k'\ge4\), Kodaira–Spencer, Serre duality, positivity and Nakayama give surjective reduction of cusp sections, and the finite torsion-free cuspidal Hecke algebra supplies a characteristic-zero eigenpacket. The scalar case is included.

In the characteristic-two representation scheme, mark a local invariant projective line at each chosen place. A closed residual point is transverse if no marked line is globally invariant, geometrically after residue extension. At a marked transverse point the local representation is scalar: the different global and marked lines split its restriction, and its two local characters coincide.

The reduced real-plus-marked local base has dimension \(2d+5m\). The relative presentation has generator-minus-relation count at least \(-6m\). Thus every component of a completed transverse chart has dimension at least \(2d-m\), and Grothendieck connectedness joins components through intersections of dimension at least \(2d-m-1\).

The globally reducible locus is smaller. After adjoining a global invariant line, its movement costs one parameter, the fixed-product diagonal character base costs at most one by abelian Leopoldt, and a coboundary costs at most one. With diagonal characters frozen, an upper extension preserving a transverse second line must vanish at each mark, hence lies in the strict Selmer group. The marked lines cost at most \(m\) additional parameters. The resulting bounds are \(d+u_F+3\) or \(2d-2m+u_F+3\), strictly below the component-intersection dimension. This is a relative-generator estimate for the whole formal reducible chart, not just a tangent estimate at one character.

In a maximal-dimensional component intersection, generic global irreducibility permits forgetting the frame and marked lines at cost at most \(3+m\). Recording marked characters first keeps a finite trace extension, so subsequently forgetting them loses no further dimension. The trace image has dimension at least \(2d-2m-4\). Fixing \(u_F\) bad Frobenius traces leaves dimension greater than \(d\), allowing a non-virtually-solvable characteristic-two curve outside the bad loci, with finite bad local images. The unrestricted propagation theorem crosses each successive intersection.

The transverse residual fiber is geometrically connected. Scalar residue gives nonzero additive cocycles with the origin removed and affine charts of complementary marked lines. Distinct characters give two upper-triangular orientations meeting at the split representation, with marked lines chosen to avoid both invariant lines. Absolutely irreducible residue gives one conjugacy orbit. The finite component graph then has a finite spanning tree. Only finitely many chart chains and field extensions are used, so their Galois compositum and a common deeper level work simultaneously.

Over the intermediate field \(A\), the seed component has dimension at least \(1+2d_A\). Its central character only needs to match the prescribed one residually, since exact integral function-space reduction transfers residual occurrence. An unrestricted fixed-determinant target component has trace-image dimension at least \(2d_A-2\), even if the originally prescribed family itself is small. Cutting each special fiber by bad Frobenius traces and, when needed, one entire constant scalar local pseudorepresentation costs a fixed number independent of \(d_A\). This leaves seed and target curves outside the bad locus.

To enter transverse charts, choose a vector on the local curve-field line at the distinguished dyadic place and take the lattice generated by its global orbit. Compactness bounds it, irreducibility makes it rank two, and Nakayama makes the chosen vector primitive. Its residue lies in no globally invariant line because its orbit spans the residual lattice. Restriction to \(F\) preserves nonzero residual extensions by the field-preparation injectivity. Galois transport of the chosen local line to all marks therefore preserves transversality.

Connectedness transfers potential pro-modularity from the seed curve to the target curve over \(F\). This makes the original curve over \(A\) potentially pro-modular. Applying unrestricted propagation over \(A\) then reaches its entire containing target locus. Marks serve connectedness only. They do not impose an ordinary or potentially crystalline condition on the target family.

## 10. Character orientation, genuine vectors and descent — paper §8

### The family and the weight map

For a locally reducible regular de Rham target, the higher-exponent character can be chosen as a subcharacter. If the chosen line has smaller exponent, its extension ratio has negative exponent. The rank-one Bloch–Kato formula gives zero de Rham extension space, so that extension splits and the other summand provides the required line.

The global line chart through this target has characteristic-zero completed dimension at least \(5+2-3=4\). Passing to the original horizontal component adds one, then forgetting conjugacy loses three. Its joint trace-and-subcharacter domain \(\mathscr D\) therefore has dimension at least two. It retains the chosen \(\eta_1\), with \(\eta_2=\chi\eta_1^{-1}\), and the whole trace family becomes pro-modular over a common \(F\).

Finiteness includes the character. For a finite-index normal restriction, each old trace is integral over the restricted trace by the monic trace-of-powers polynomial. Each \(\eta_1(g)\) satisfies its quadratic characteristic polynomial. Finitely many trace generators and the character values on a uniformizer, minus one and five generate a finite complete algebra. Consequently \(\mathscr D\) is finite over the restricted trace image, and also over the product of local pseudodeformation images. Exceptional ratio equations \(\eta_1/\eta_2\in\{1,\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{-1}\}\) have image dimension at most one, so they are proper testing loci. They need not exclude the final target.

In the torsion completed forms define the lower-unipotent-invariant periodic part
\[
 U=\sum_{a\bmod2}n^-(a)\mathbf p,\qquad
 W=\{f:U^Nf=f\text{ for some }N>0\},\qquad M=W^\vee.
\]
The sum is a product over all dyadic places and \(\mathbf p=\operatorname{diag}(1,2)\) in each. Torus and tame Hecke actions commute with \(U\), and \(U\) is bijective on \(W\). A matrix identity improves upper-unipotent invariance by one congruence level. Iterating a multiple of each vector's period proves that one fixed compact open subgroup fixes \(W[\varpi]^{T_1}\). Admissibility and compact Nakayama make \(M\) finite over \(\Omega=\mathcal O[[T_1]]\). The joint action algebra, including \(U^{\pm1}\), is finite over \(\Omega\).

Let \(\Lambda=\mathcal O[[1+4\mathbb Z_2]]\), with \([5]\mapsto\eta_1(5)\). At each dyadic place the generic Banach factors permit either order \((\eta_1\varepsilon,\eta_2)\) or \((\eta_2\varepsilon,\eta_1)\). Lower-disk vectors in these continuous inductions have unit \(U\)-eigenvalue and, after integral scaling and reduction, belong to \(W\). Thus a dense set of coefficient points lies in the joint trace-and-weight support with one of finitely many assignments.

For each assignment the joint image is a finite \(\Lambda\)-algebra. Their kernel-vanishing loci form a finite closed cover of the irreducible \(\mathscr D\), so one kernel vanishes identically. The trace-plus-weight image is finite over \(\Lambda\), and the existing finiteness of \(\mathscr D\) over the smaller trace image passes through it. Since \(\dim\mathscr D\ge2=\dim\Lambda\), the weight map is injective and finite, and \(\dim\mathscr D=2\). Unramified character values remain in the trace algebra, rather than being silently omitted from the family.

### Dense modular points determine the favorable order

Choose \(c^2=5^{w+1}\), where \(w=\operatorname{exp}\chi\), and specialize \(\eta_1(5)=c\zeta\) for two-power roots of unity \(\zeta\). These translated torsion weights are Zariski dense by Weierstrass preparation. Finite dominance lifts a dense set to \(\mathscr D\), avoiding any fixed proper closed subset. The value \(c\zeta\) specifies a continuous character. The paper does not treat \((w+1)/2\) as an integral Hodge–Tate weight.

The one continuous normalizing family character is
\[
 \lambda(g)=\eta_{2,\mathrm u}(\varepsilon(g))^{-1}.
\]
At the selected points, after discarding finitely many trivial inertial ratios, \(r'=r_x\otimes\lambda_x\) has line and quotient \(u_h\xi\varepsilon\) and \(u_l\), with \(\xi\) nontrivial finite inertial. Rank-one Euler and Bloch–Kato formulas give \(H^1=H^1_g\) of dimension one, so every such extension is de Rham. Potential semistability and distinct inertial characters force monodromy zero. The **dual** of \(r'\) has Hodge–Tate weights \(\{0,1\}\) and the ordinary matrix orientation required by Thorne's Theorem D. That cited theorem makes the dual modular, and hence \(r'\) modular. It is not applied directly to every point of the continuous family.

A uniform finite bad-inertia quotient bounds tame conductors, while \(\lambda\) adds no tame ramification. Thus all these points occur at one common tame level after solvable base change and Jacquet–Langlands. Local compatibility, with geometric reciprocity \(\varepsilon=x|\cdot|\), identifies the smooth inducing characters. The weight-two algebraic factor \(\det\) converts their order to \((\eta'_2\varepsilon,\eta'_1)\). The unnormalized intertwiner carries the necessary modulus factors. Untwisting definite functions by \(\lambda_x^{-1}\) of the reduced norm restores the fixed central character and gives
\[
 (\eta_2\varepsilon,\eta_1)
\]
at every dyadic place. This is one favorable assignment on a dense set, not a freely chosen order in a semisimple target fiber.

The kernel of this single joint-image assignment vanishes on the dense points and hence on the domain \(\mathscr D\). It therefore vanishes at the original target, including exceptional character ratios. Finite lying over and faithful support of the ordinary module place the target in the favorable joint trace-and-weight support.

### Specialization and the polynomial orbit

The finite faithful action algebra containing both \(U\) and \(U^{-1}\) provides a scalar quotient of \(M[1/2]\) above the target. Continuity and compactness give a lattice-valued quotient after rescaling. Both operators preserve this lattice, so the scalar \(U\)-eigenvalue is a unit. Pontryagin duality inserts a copy of \(E'/\mathcal O'\) inside \(W\). Its compatible elements of exact order \(\varpi'^n\) give a nonzero continuous integral completed-form vector upon taking the Tate module. The proof needs a divisible copy inside \(W\), not divisibility of all of \(W\). It does not assume that ordinary vectors or algebraic vectors commute with specialization.

The resulting vector \(f\) is lower-invariant, has unit \(U\)-eigenvalue \(\alpha\), and its torus exponents satisfy \(\delta_v=e_{2,v}-e_{1,v}\ge0\). With \(x\) in a small upper-unipotent polydisk, the matrix identity in Lemma 8.7 defines polynomials of bounded multidegree
\[
 P_j(x)=\alpha^{-j}\sum_{a\bmod2^j}
 \prod_v(1-x_va_v)^{\delta_v}n^-(a)\mathbf p^j f.
\]
Unitarity, the unit eigenvalue and the ultrametric triangle inequality give
\[
 \sup_x\|n^+(x)f-P_j(x)\|
 \le\sup_{y\in(2^{j+m}\mathbb Z_2)^d}\|n^+(y)f-f\|\longrightarrow0.
\]
There is no factor for the growing number of residue classes. Fixed-grid Vandermonde inversion proves closedness of the space of polynomials of bounded multidegree. Thus the actual upper orbit is polynomial. Gauss decomposition gives its algebraic matrix coefficients, including negative determinant powers. Mapping the binomial monomial basis of
\[
 \bigotimes_{v\mid2}\det^{e_{1,v}}\operatorname{Sym}^{\delta_v}(E'^2)
\]
to the orbit coefficients is equivariant on an open compact group. Zariski density and irreducibility make the map injective. The target vector is therefore locally algebraic, of type \(\operatorname{Sym}^{a-b-1}\det^{b+1}\) in each factor.

### Descent only after actual classicality

If the target is locally absolutely irreducible, the all-prime local correspondence already gives locally algebraic vectors from regular de Rham parameters, including scalar residual behavior at two. In either case, the resulting definite eigenpacket is regular algebraic and cannot be a reduced-norm character because its Galois representation is absolutely irreducible. Jacquet–Langlands gives a Hilbert cusp form whose Galois representation is the restricted target by Chebotarev.

Only this actual classical packet is descended. Along a prime-degree cyclic tower, the extending Galois representation makes its conjugate Hecke systems equal, so strong multiplicity one gives invariance. Cyclic descent preserves the regular real type because all real places split. The descended Galois representation differs from the given one by a character of the cyclic quotient, since their common restriction is absolutely irreducible. The inverse finite Hecke-character twist corrects this at every step. Over \(\mathbb Q\) one obtains the claimed classical cusp form up to the integer Tate twist fixed by the original weights.
