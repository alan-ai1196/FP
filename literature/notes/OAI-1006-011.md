## 1. Original question and asserted results

For an elliptic curve \(E/\mathbb Q\), write

\[
a(E)=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s),\qquad
r(E)=\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb Q),\qquad
c_2(E)=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb Z_2}
       \operatorname{Sel}_{2^\infty}(E/\mathbb Q).
\]

The main pointwise assertion is

\[
c_2(E)\in\{0,1\}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a(E)=r(E)=c_2(E),\qquad \Sha(E/\mathbb Q)\text{ finite}.
\]

The hypothesis concerns the **full infinite two-primary Selmer corank with the actual local Kummer conditions**. It is not an assumption that the Mordell–Weil rank is small. The theorem imposes no rational-torsion, residual-irreducibility, reduction-at-two, CM or rational-isogeny restriction. The proof first establishes analytic rank zero or one, then invokes the forward Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin results for rank equality and finiteness of the whole Sha. [§§1–2, Theorem 1.1, §14.]

For every fixed \(E\), among signed squarefree \(d\), ordered by \(|d|\), the paper asserts densities \(1/2\) for \(a(E^{(d)})=0\) and for \(a(E^{(d)})=1\). The complement of analytic rank at least two has density zero. The rank equality and Sha finiteness therefore hold on a density-one set. The algebraic input is Smith's full \(2^\infty\)-Selmer distribution, not a BSD-dependent analytic corollary of that distribution. [Theorem 1.2 and §14.]

A finite-descent corollary uses

\[
d_2(E)=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\operatorname{Sel}_2(E/\mathbb Q)
       -\dim_{\mathbb F_2}E(\mathbb Q)[2].
\]

If \(d_2(E)\le1\), then \(a=r=c_2=d_2\), whole Sha is finite and \(\Sha[2^\infty]=0\). The preliminary relation is \(d_2=c_2+\dim_{\mathbb F_2}F[2]\), where \(F\) is the finite quotient of the infinite Selmer group by its divisible subgroup. Its alternating pairing makes the extra dimension even. Before the converse, this only leaves a potentially divisible two-primary Sha. Finiteness then kills that divisible group. [§2 and Corollary 1.3.]

**The density theorem does not by itself give mean analytic rank \(1/2\).** A sparse large-rank tail could still contribute to the mean. That issue belongs to the next paper, OAI-1006-012. This manuscript also does not prove the exact BSD leading coefficient. Its two-primary and all-prime companion results are distinguished from the present converse. The all-prime converse uses the two-converse, rather than supplying a circular input here. [§1.]

## 2. Normalizations and the missing-vertex strategy

For a good odd prime, let \(t_q=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}E[2]^{\operatorname{Fr}_q}\). The three residual types are split, simple and root, with \(t_q=2,1,0\). The weight is

\[
w(h)=\frac12\sum_{q\mid h,\ q\nmid2N}t_q,\qquad
\mathcal L(h)=\frac{\sqrt{|h|}\,L(E^{(h)},1)}
                   {\Omega_E^{\operatorname{sign}(h)}}.
\]

The notation \(s(h)\) records the two-primary finite Sha length in the rank-zero or rank-one situations where finiteness is known. A root prime has zero weight, so bounded weight does not bound the number of prime factors. Quadratic twists preserve \(E[2]\). Reducibility of that two-dimensional \(\mathbb F_2\)-representation is equivalent to rational two-torsion. In the other case its image is \(C_3\) or \(S_3\). Irreducibility over \(\mathbb F_2\) is not silently strengthened to absolute irreducibility. [§§2–3.]

The families have the form

\[
h_x=h_0\prod_q(q^*)^{\lambda_q(x)},\qquad
q^*=(-1/q)q,\qquad x\in\mathbb F_2^b,
\]

with linear activations, distinct new primes, fixed old factors and prescribed local classes. All new primes are absent at \(x=0\). Coefficient symbols construct families whose nonzero vertices have analytic rank zero or one and bounded normalized arithmetic valuations. A determinant interpolation argument transfers this information to the missing vertex. The constants must be fixed before \(b\) grows. There is no prime-size or computational-efficiency assertion. [§3.]

The two transfers have different hypotheses.

| Transfer | Nonzero-vertex input | Extra uniformity |
|---|---|---|
| Cyclotomic | \(L(E^{(h_x)},1)\ne0\) and \(v_2\mathcal L(h_x)-2w(h_x)-s(h_x)\le B\) | Base Selmer corank zero. In the irreducible case, \(v_2(q^2-1)\) is bounded for added primes. |
| Heegner | The product \(L(E^{(h_x)},s)L(E^{(h_xk)},s)\) has a simple zero and \(2j(P_{|h_x|}(h_x))-4w(h_x)-s(h_x)-s(h_xk)\le B\) | Base Selmer corank over \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{k})\) is one with bounded finite quotient, \(\omega(k)\) is bounded, and each new prime splits in \(K\) or has \(t_q=0\). |

Genus points use a fixed complementary twist but a varying genus field, usually conductor one. Ring points use \(K\) fixed within each cube, with conductor \(|h|\). The class sums are unaveraged. Dividing them by the class number would change the integrality problem. In the irreducible odd construction \(K\) may vary between cubes. In the rational-torsion odd construction one \(K\) is fixed before \(b\). [§3.]

## 3. Coefficient sources and finite precision

The even source is a holomorphic weight-\(3/2\) form orthogonal to unary theta series, on a fixed local filter. Waldspurger comparison supplies exact square ratios

\[
2v_2\!\left(\frac{a(D)}{a(D_0)}\right)
 =v_2\!\left(\frac{\mathcal L(h)}{\mathcal L(h_0)}\right).
\]

The argument applies to the whole specified Hecke eigenspace, including old vectors, and retains the local condition at two. Squarefree coefficients are rational up to a two-power root of unity after normalization. Their valuations are integral, even if the coefficient field is ramified. The good-prime recurrence retains its two scalar characters and the quotient character. These facts matter for the subsequent divisions by two. [§4.1.]

The odd source is a sequence of finite-precision Kummer detectors. A prime \(\ell\) is chosen with Tate Frobenius congruent to complex conjugation to high precision. Reduction of a nontorsion genus point is tested in \(E(\mathbb F_{\ell^2})\) by a compatible row of \(E[2^M]\). Openness of the Kummer translation image supplies detections of bounded valuation. This does not assume supersingular reduction of the target \(E\). Supersingular points occur in the auxiliary quaternionic modular construction. [§4.2.]

Weighted ternary theta series encode those detections. The source checks the scalar Weil transformation, local signs at two and bad primes, oriented optimal embeddings, stabilizers and the Brandt recurrence. A fixed multiplier removes cuspidal and torsion terms. The resulting coefficient congruence is a fixed factor times the Kummer evaluation of the **unaveraged** genus point. Visibility detects a simple central zero and bounds the point index. The local weights and reciprocal character conventions are part of the argument, not decorative normalizations. [§4.2.]

The odd normalization is defined through a fixed nonprincipal ultrafilter of coefficient precisions and bounded-weight sequences of indices. For every fixed weight cutoff, the sharp lower bound holds simultaneously for all indices below that cutoff on an ultrafilter-large set. A coarse bound handles larger weights. The proof does not assert that one finite precision works at all weights. A finite graph or cube is chosen first, then its finite test list and weight bound, then a sufficiently advanced actual stage. [§4.3.]

A normalized unit coefficient gives analytic nonvanishing or a simple zero, a uniform upper bound for the corresponding central value or Heegner index, and a bound on finite Selmer data. The source separates the lower bounds used to define the symbols from these upper bounds detected by units. The reducible odd lower bound is established using the even construction before the odd symbol is normalized. [§§3–4, 7.]

Contraction deletes the whole rational prime from ramification support. In a quadratic label field this includes both oriented places. Root probes combine through their total rational label. In the irreducible case the root character can take values in the units of a nonreduced algebra, with square one. Simple-pair derivatives depend on whether the two transposition orientations match. Transfer of a minimal-weight unit uses matched actual probes and rational Frobenius lifts. [§5.4–5.7.]

[§6.1.]

Forest differences turn trees into contractions, kill cycles and forbidden double-simple contractions, and allow primary networks to implement addressing. Proper restrictions break the cycle, while the path connects its endpoints only if all groups are present. [§6.2–6.3 and p. 43.]

The simultaneous-unit step is stronger than separate nonvanishing. The highest homogeneous parts of the terminal polynomials are multiplied as ordinary polynomials **before** Boolean reduction. Exponents zero, one and two are implemented differently. A squared address variable uses the singular program that differentiates both tests. ## 6. Local Selmer matrices and three-state linearity

For irreducible \(V=E[2]\), put \(F=\mathbb Q(V)\). At a split or simple prime \(p\), an oriented degree-one prime in a suitable fixed subfield supplies a generator \(\pi_p\) and classes

\[
C_p(x)=\operatorname{cor}_{F'_p/\mathbb Q}(\chi_{\pi_p}x),
\qquad x\in V^{H_p}.
\]

Their inertia coordinates make distinct-prime classes independent. Root primes contribute no coordinates. Subtracting the actual local Kummer lift gives the active-prime matrix

\[
B_{qp}(y,x)=
 \langle C_p(x)(\phi_q),y\rangle
 -{\bf1}_{p=q}\langle K_p(x)(\phi_p),y\rangle.
\]

Its reciprocity defect is a pairing of finitely many support types. Finer neighborhoods that fix self entries are realization data and do not enlarge the type alphabet. The moving odd detector prime contributes a bounded extra local cost. [§8.1–8.3, p. 59.]

Double-coset residue prescriptions span all \(2\times2\) binary matrices for split pairs in the \(S_3\) case and \(J\mathbb F_4\) in the cyclic case. The relation \(1+R+R^2=0\) changes the rational norm bit without changing the matrix. Analogous relations give independence for split-simple and simple-simple pairs. Self entries are controlled by a Hilbert-symbol identity and fixed support neighborhoods, not assumed freely assignable. [Lemma 8.1.]

A type correction \(L\) makes \(M=B-L\) symmetric. If cross-block \(M\)-entries vanish, then

\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\operatorname{Sel}_2(E^{(h)})
 \ge \sum_i\dim\ker M_i-C.
\]

Type-sum constraints kill the correction, then stationary and fixed local conditions cost bounded codimension. The independent inertia coordinates inject the remaining kernel into Selmer. A unit symbol therefore bounds the number of singular blocks uniformly. [Proposition 8.2.]

A site has two coordinates: one split prime or a pair of simple primes. Its states are absent and two present alternatives differing by \(J\). The three symbol values sum to zero. The three determinants also sum to zero, since reversing longer permutation cycles cancels them in characteristic two and the toggle isolates the deleted pair. This gives two multilinear functionals on tensor products of binary planes. It does **not** identify the symbol with the determinant. [Lemma 8.3, p. 63.]

The binary tensor lemma says that pure zeros of a nonzero functional span its kernel. Applied to the determinant, an invertible state is the empty state plus singular pure states. Addressing uses invertible primary path blocks whose Schur complement supplies a chosen toggle everywhere except the designated nonzero address. All activations remain linear and vanish at zero. [Lemmas 8.4–8.5.]

## 7. Irreducible saturation and the bounded companion

Finite Ramsey colorings and a diagonal construction stabilize **ordered** word evaluations on a countable library of site slots. The library is closed under all finite addressing operations. It asserts neither permutation symmetry nor one infinite array of arithmetic primes. In the odd case, every finite restriction occurs at arbitrarily high actual admissible precisions. The extracted sequence need not itself be ultrafilter-large. [§9.1.]

A unit word can be expanded into singular blocks. Their number is bounded by the Selmer nullity inequality, so choose one actual maximal singular word \(W\), with its finite sites fixed before \(b\). Place an addressed copy at each nonzero cube vertex. At that vertex its designated copy remains \(W\), while every other copy is nonsingular. Expanding the others into empty plus singular states leaves the unit term. Every other term has more singular blocks than the maximum and vanishes. This saturates the punctured cube. Merely bounding the number of blocks without fixing one finite word would not control their sizes. [Lemmas 9.2–9.3.]

Root primes calibrate all support labels and split diagonal choices coefficient by coefficient in the cube variables. Paired simple sites remove the residual discriminant obstruction. Added roots have \(t_q=0\). In the even case the finitely many two-adic templates can also bound \(v_2(q^2-1)\), giving the required cyclotomic family. [§9.2.]

The odd case needs a companion \(k\) with bounded support and bounded \(s(k)\), while also keeping \(kh_x\) nonvanishing. An even symbol is first conditioned by a fixed maximal singular prefix and possibly one simple environment coordinate. Its remaining functional then equals the determinant. This equality is a consequence of maximality and the tensor lemma, not a general equality between symbols and Selmer determinants. [Lemma 9.6.]

Balanced activation leaves one singular word matrix \(M\) and an odd number of invertible \(N=M+D\) copies at every nonzero address. A fixed collection of split interface sites couples identically to each copy. Paired \(N\)-copies cancel their Schur contributions in characteristic two. The interface lemma chooses

\[
H=\sum_i b_i^tJb_i
\quad\text{so that}\quad
\begin{pmatrix}D&N\\N&N+H\end{pmatrix}
\text{ is invertible}.
\]

At most \(\dim U\) maps are required. The proof lowers the nullity by two when possible. Its one-dimensional-kernel case uses the diagonal/rank identity for a binary symmetric matrix. The construction respects \(\mathbb F_4\)-linear spans in the cyclic case. [Lemmas 9.7–9.8, pp. 71–72.]

The companion support consists of the fixed prefix and environment, the bounded interface and one root prime. It can vary with the finite realization, but \(\omega(k)\) and \(s(k)\) stay bounded. Independent norm prescriptions make \(k\) locally square at nonroot address primes and \(h_x\) locally square at nonroot companion primes. The simultaneous units at \(k\), \(h_x\) and \(kh_x\) supply the varying-field Heegner hypotheses. The base Selmer bound uses isogenies with composite multiplication by two and bounded generator dimensions, without assuming Sha finiteness of the original corank-one curve. [§9.3–9.4.]

## 8. Auxiliary nonvanishing in prescribed local classes

The even assertion first chooses one prime discriminant \(D_0\) in the required filter and absorbs it into the curve. Hoffstein–Luo then supplies a nonvanishing twist with at most four prime factors and splitting at a fixed finite set. Multiplication gives a discriminant with **at most five prime factors**, independent of the curve and the finite local restrictions. Congruence mod eight preserves the two-adic class. The argument is existential and permits arbitrarily large primes. [§10, first proof.]

The odd assertion retains a fixed admissible progression throughout a derivative first moment. With \(L\)-functions normalized to center \(1/2\),

\[
\sum_{\substack{n>0\text{ squarefree}\\n\equiv a\pmod H}}
 L'(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{\sigma n})\Phi(n/X)
 =cX\log X+c_1X+O(X^{1-\eta}),\qquad c>0.
\]

The approximate functional equation has a double-pole kernel. A quadratic-large-sieve second moment controls the discarded square-divisor tail by \(X^{1+\rho}/Y\). Poisson summation retains the progression modulus. Its zero frequency factors through the symmetric-square \(L\)-function and gives a positive Euler-product residue. Nonzero quadratic Gauss sums factor into a twisted cusp-form \(L\)-function and a controlled correction. After smooth cutoffs and a contour shift the nonzero-frequency contribution is \(O(J^BX^{7/8})\). Choosing \(Y=X^{\delta_0}\) leaves a power saving. This proves simple-zero existence in the fixed progression. It claims no uniform asymptotic as the progression varies and no five-prime bound for the odd witness. [Lemma 10.1 and its proof.]

## 9. Binary interpolation and denominator control

Let \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z_2[[t]]\), \(O=\widehat{\Lambda_{(2)}}\) and \(G=(\mathbb Z/2)^b\). Modulo \(2^M\),

\[
O/2^M\simeq(\mathbb Z/2^M)((t)).
\]

Negative Laurent support is finite at each precision, but elements of \(O\) need not admit central evaluation at \(t=0\). Central values are taken on \(\Lambda[1/2]\) coordinates. [§11.]

If a growing block is a direct sum of augmentation blocks with bounded residual determinant order, its inverse at any sign character has bounded negative Laurent support modulo \(2^M\), independent of the number of blocks. The geometric inverse expansion has only \(M\) terms. Schur elimination then leaves a bounded old complex. Boundary pivots at its central fiber produce a clearing identity \(d_x=f_xu_x\). The degree of \(f\), \(v_2(f_0(0))\) and its negative support are bounded. Arbitrary eliminated determinant factors go into \(d\), not \(f\). No bound on all numerator coefficients is required. [Lemmas 11.1–11.3.]

For one group-ring coefficient,

\[
A(x)=\sum_S c_Sx_S,\qquad 2^{|S|}\mid c_S.
\]

Its \(j\)-th binary digit has Boolean degree at most \(2^{j-1}\). Chevalley–Warning therefore gives a nonzero vertex matching \(q\) specified coefficients modulo \(2^M\) whenever \(b>q(2^{M-1}-1)\). Applying this to the bounded negative and constant coefficients of \(f\), and just the constant coefficient of \(d\), yields

\[
b>(L(M)+2)(2^{M-1}-1)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_2(u_x(0)-u_0(0))\ge M-2c_0,
\quad c_0=v_2(f_0(0)).
\]

The base may vary with the family. Pivot valuations affect the precision and dimension required, not the eventual transferred upper bound. [Lemmas 11.4–11.5, p. 83.]

Finite cochain models are constructed by a terminating homological perturbation of residual contractible disks. Their maps to the original cochains, inverses and homotopies are retained. This is stronger than selecting abstract quasi-isomorphic modules and is used throughout the arithmetic evaluation arguments. [Lemma 11.6.]

## 10. Cyclotomic determinant: integral construction and specialization

The positive complex is the fiber of ordinary global cochains to ordinary real cochains. It removes the high real cohomology while retaining the connecting real invariant line. Complete real Tate cochains would lose that rational line. “Ordinary global cohomology” here means unmodified global cohomology, not an ordinary-reduction assumption at two. [§12.1.]

Kato classes are projected to the lattice of one fixed modular form, then pushed along an integral group-ring coefficient map. The map does not divide by \(|G|\) or require a surjective cohomology specialization. Smoothing factors are chosen to be units at \((2)\) and to have bounded nonzero central valuations. Their integers can vary with the conductor while these bounds stay fixed. The central reciprocity formula contains the omitted Euler factors at unused good primes and the fixed period vector. [§12.2.]

The rational divisibility used is Kato's height-one statement over \(\Lambda[1/2]\), with a separate CM input from Burungale–Tian. The stronger integral theorem is not invoked. A fixed coefficient-prime correction has nonzero central value. At a fixed bad odd prime the appropriate local object is \(H^1(I_q,V_h)\), not an indiscriminate replacement by \(V_h^{I_q}(-1)\). The split-multiplicative example would otherwise introduce the wrong factor \(1-\gamma_q\). Reciprocal Frobenius conventions are reconciled by integral group-ring units with no loss per new prime. [§12.3.]

Wedge the lifted zeta class with the real invariant line. Rational divisibility and normality place each character determinant coordinate in \(\Lambda[1/2]\). At Selmer corank zero,

\[
L(E^{(h)},1)=0\Rightarrow u_h(0)=0,\qquad
\left|v_2(u_h(0))-
 [v_2\mathcal L(h)-2w(h)-s(h)]\right|\le C_E.
\]

Integral Poitou–Tate and the dual-exponential lattice give this formula. The global-line index cancels. Unused local torsion lengths cancel the unused Euler factors exactly. Active primes contribute \(t_q\). The sign of the degree-two torsion contribution is negative in the coordinate of the inverse determinant. All bad-place conditions remain Kummer. [Proposition 12.3, p. 91.]

For residual extensions of trivial modules, the positive complex becomes exactly a free rank-two module in degree one over \(O[G]\). The residual calculation uses bounded class-group ranks in the real cyclotomic tower, Ferrero–Washington and surjectivity of unit signatures. It gives the reducible cyclotomic lower bound and direct family integrality. [§12.5.]

For irreducible residual representations, new local blocks are eliminated instead. The identity \(v_2(\alpha_q)=v_2(q^2-1)-3\) bounds the residual Euler polynomial's \(t\)-order when \(v_2(q^2-1)\le L\). The bounded clearing lemma then applies. In both cases binary transfer forces central nonvanishing at the base. Only the central specialization error determines the final additive valuation loss. Old pivots and pole bounds determine how large a cube is needed. [§12.6.]

## 11. Heegner determinant: exact maps and local conditions

Explicit Gross–Zagier gives the height of the unaveraged point as a constant times \(c\sqrt{|k|}\) times the derivative of the two-twist product. Comparing ring and genus points cancels the regulator on the common rank-one line. Period and twist-lattice factors have bounded two-adic valuation. Restriction and conjugate addition/subtraction give the base Selmer comparison over \(K\), with composites multiplication by two. Only rationalized corank addition is used, not an integral direct-sum decomposition. [§13.1.]

The finite Artin models retain the original global and local cochains and their comparison maps through a fixed inner ultrafilter. Transfers from growing ring-class fields are completed first. Only the resulting bounded target cycles, local lifts and boundary witnesses enter the limiting diagram. No uniform model rank is asserted for the growing source fields. Adding a switch extends the same stage diagrams rather than reconstructing unrelated old complexes. [§13.2.]

At every bad finite place the local condition is the derived two-completion of the local point group shifted to degree one. Its derived reduction contains both local torsion in degree zero and the Kummer image in degree one. Exact orthogonality is proved integrally. Full and strict conditions, and the truncated finite/transverse conditions at derivative primes, are carefully distinguished. [§13.2.2.]

Moving ring-class characters are trivial at \(2N\), have odd Frobenius exponent at prescribed split primes, and have increasing inertia order at a fresh \(r_i\). Their construction imposes class-group relations on the primitive inertia character without dividing by a class number. A Tate-image kernel argument gives bounds for \(2\pm a_{r_i}\) and \(\det(1\pm\operatorname{Fr}_{r_i})\) independent of \(K\) and the number of prescribed primes. The CM case uses a fixed power on the norm-one Cartan. [§13.3.]

At characteristic-zero height-one tests the relaxed active local complexes are acyclic. At \((2)\), odd deformation exponent makes a split-prime scalar nonconstant over \(\mathbb F_2((t))\). A root prime also gives acyclicity, including at an inert place where the Tate Frobenius is squared. These are the precise alternatives used in interpolation. [Lemma 13.4.]

## 12. Central Heegner valuation, evaluation and switches

The center of the transferred class is the primitive Kummer class times a bounded nonzero factor \(a_{r_i}-2\chi_e(r_i)\) and a unit, with an explicit retained boundary witness. The determinant pairs the point with its dual functional. Constant rational cohomological rank justifies its central specialization. In a simple-zero instance,

\[
v_2(u(0))
 =2j(P_c(e))-\operatorname{length}\Sha(E^{(e)}/K)[2^\infty]
