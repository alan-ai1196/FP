## 1. The question and the three distinct conclusions

For a fixed elliptic curve \(E/\mathbb Q\), write
\[
a_d=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E^{(d)},s),\qquad
r_d=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}E^{(d)}(\mathbb Q),
\]
and let \(\mathcal D(Y)\) be the nonzero, signed, squarefree integers of absolute value at most \(Y\).

Theorem 1.1 claims, for every \(E/\mathbb Q\),
\[
\lim_{Y\to\infty}\frac1{\#\mathcal D(Y)}
\sum_{d\in\mathcal D(Y)}a_d=\frac12.
\]
There is no rational-two-torsion, CM, isogeny or reduction restriction. The normalization is by signed squarefree integers, not by a weighted discriminant family or a fixed root-number subfamily.

The companion paper OAI-1006-011 supplies analytic-rank-zero and analytic-rank-one densities of one half. Those densities alone do not control the contribution of the remaining ranks. The new ingredient is Theorem 1.2: constants \(C_E\) and an integer \(R_E\) exist such that, for every fixed integer \(R\ge R_E\),
\[
\limsup_{Y\to\infty}\frac1Y
\sum_{\substack{d\in\mathcal D(Y)\\a_d>R}}a_d
\le \frac{C_E}{R}.
\]
The height limit is taken first, with \(R\) fixed. Letting \(R\) tend to infinity afterward supplies the needed uniform integrability. The tail theorem does not itself use the companion density theorem.

Corollary 1.3 concerns **algebraic** rank. For every fixed real \(t\), and every fixed positive integer \(m\), it claims
\[
\frac1{\#\mathcal D(Y)}\sum_d e^{t r_d}\longrightarrow
\frac{1+e^t}{2},
\qquad
\frac1{\#\mathcal D(Y)}\sum_d r_d^m\longrightarrow\frac12.
\]
Its proof uses the companion density and rank-equality result together with a Koymans–Smith exponential-moment bound. It does not use the analytic tail theorem. The paper does not claim these higher moments for analytic rank or uniformity when \(t\) or \(m\) grows with \(Y\).

The historical comparison in §1 preserves family and hypothesis differences. Heath-Brown's conditional average bound uses RH for the relevant twists and a weighted discriminant setup. Miller–Wong's conditional higher moments use an integer family that can repeat squareclasses. Fiorilli's exact conditional mean includes an averaged cancellation hypothesis on nonreal zeros. Smith's earlier Selmer distribution requires BSD for its stated analytic consequence. The present proof claims to remove these analytic assumptions using the companion result and a separate tail argument. The discussion of Hanners's claimed full BSD proof identifies an unestablished passage from a finite set of curve tests to all curves. That manuscript is not used, and this reading does not independently adjudicate it.

## 2. Finite reduction and the exact probability space

In §2 the unitary normalization is
\[
L_F(s)=L(F,s+\tfrac12).
\]
Its center is \(1/2\). The local inverse factor is
\[
1-\lambda_F(p)p^{-s}+\xi_p p^{-2s},
\]
where \(\xi_p=1\) at good primes and zero at bad primes. Modularity, the local parameter bounds and \(|\lambda_F(n)|\le\tau(n)\) supply the basic analytic input. The completed function has factor \(C_F^s\Gamma(s+1/2)\), where \(C_F=\sqrt{q_F}/(2\pi)\).

Let \(S\) contain the primes dividing \(2q_E\). The proof works simultaneously with the finite family of signed \(S\)-supported twists of \(E\), and with admissible squarefree \(d\equiv1\pmod4\), coprime to \(S\). Every signed squarefree original parameter can be moved into one of these families with bounded multiplicity and without increasing its absolute height. Explicitly, split off its positive \(S\)-part and choose a sign to make the remaining parameter congruent to one modulo four. The nonincreasing height is needed in the induction on dual twists, as well as in the final transfer back to \(E\).

For an admissible \(d\), the conductor is exactly \(q_Fd^2\). The Dirichlet coefficients are the quadratic character twist of the fixed coefficients, including the treatment of the fixed bad primes.

The probability model is an **all-integer residue model**. Fix the value \(c=\pm1\) at two. At odd primes use independent variables
\[
\Pr(Z(p)=0)=\frac1p,\qquad
\Pr(Z(p)=1)=\Pr(Z(p)=-1)=\frac{1-1/p}{2},
\]
extended completely multiplicatively. The corresponding integer character is \(z_m(p)=(m/p)\), with \(z_m(2)=c\). CRT identifies finite-prime model averages with complete integer residue averages.

This is not the distribution of characters sampled directly from squarefree integers. The proof first controls nonnegative averages over all integers and then restricts to the admissible squarefree family with the chosen value at two. Confusing these probability spaces would change the local moments used by the comparison.

## 3. Second moments with arbitrary fixed bad-prime factors

Section 3 establishes a logarithmic second-moment bound uniformly over the finite family. For fixed exponents \(A,B\),
\[
\frac1M\sum_{\substack{d\text{ admissible}\\M\le|d|<2M}}
|L_{F^{(d)}}(\sigma+it)|^2
\ll (\log(3M))^B(1+|t|)^A,
\qquad \tfrac12\le\sigma\le2.
\]
There are corresponding prefix bounds and localized Dirichlet-polynomial bounds with an additional fixed power of \(1+N/M\). The proof adapts Fourier cutoff and prime-square inflation. It does not import a full-level asymptotic as though it applied without change to arbitrary elliptic curves.

### Gauss factors and Poisson summation

The normalized odd Gauss sums are evaluated at every prime power. Writing a nonzero dual frequency as \(h=h_1h_2^2\), the two-variable series factors as two \(L\)-functions of the twist by the signed squarefree part \(h_1\), times a correction holomorphic for both real parts greater than \(1/2\).

The fixed arithmetic exponent in the correction bound is independent of how many derivatives of a smooth weight are later requested. A crucial local distinction is that primes dividing the squarefree part \(h_1\) do not each create a growing arithmetic cost. Their local correction starts in degree two. The polynomial losses are assigned to the fixed bad primes, the auxiliary multiplier and the square part \(h_2\). The argument never divides by a Gauss coefficient that might vanish or by a global \(L\)-value.

The nonzero Poisson frequency has a kernel factor of the form
\[
\left(1+\frac{|h|U}{rN_1N_2}\right)^{-J}
\]
at averaging height \(U\). A compactly supported Fourier transform gives an exact cutoff \(|h|\ll rN_1N_2/U\). Integrations by parts increase the smoothness requirement without changing the fixed arithmetic exponent. Powers of two are separated using a convergent weighted sum.

### Prime-square inflation and the simultaneous induction

Inflate \(d\) to \(dp^2\), with \(p\) in a large prime interval of scale \(P\), so the integer averaging height becomes \(U=MP^2\). A nonnegative Schwartz weight with compact Fourier support dominates the needed interval. Choosing \(P\) sufficiently large makes the dual squarefree twist have height at most \(M/2\). The finite family from §2 closes the induction under this reduction.

The original squarefree polynomial is recovered through its exact local Euler expansion at \(p\). The expansion still works when \(p\mid d\), because the relevant character terms then vanish. The map \((d,p)\mapsto dp^2\) is injective in the recovery argument, including the case where the exponent of \(p\) becomes three. Averaging over primes and using Minkowski gives the recursive saving.

The paper fixes its constants in an explicit order: arithmetic exponents, moment exponents, smoothness orders, a small absorption constant, the inflation scale, and finally the induction constant. The arithmetic exponent does not silently depend on a later growing derivative order. A Gaussian approximate functional equation handles the full \(L\)-function. A vector-valued strip argument is implemented through unit-vector scalar projections, avoiding a loss proportional to the number of twists.

### Comparison with the independent model

Put \(\ell=\log X\). Proposition 3.7 compares a short character polynomial \(S\), multiplied by another short polynomial \(H\), against the independent model:
\[
\frac1X\sum_m\Psi(m/X)|H_{z_m}S_{z_m}(m/X)-A(m/X)|^2
=
\int\Psi(x)\,\mathbb E|H_ZS_Z(x)-A(x)|^2\,dx
+O(e^{-\Delta\ell/2}).
\]
Here \(S\) has length at most \(X^{1-\Delta}\),
\[
c\ell^{-1/10}\le\Delta\le\tfrac12,
\qquad R_0\le X^{\eta\Delta},
\qquad \sum_{r\le R_0}|h(r)|\ll R_0^2,
\]
with fixed derivative bounds for the weights. The zero frequency is exactly the integer residue model. The nonzero frequencies are summed with their actual decay before their logarithms are bounded in terms of \(X\). In particular the small-frequency-scale regime uses its \(Q^J\) bound, rather than an inappropriate large-\(Q\) estimate.

The auxiliary multiplier budget accounts for both coefficient indices and the arithmetic losses. This permits a short mollifier later. It does not justify an arbitrary long Euler product inside the comparison.

## 4. An exact derivative identity and a growing uniform range

Section 4 fixes all finite differentiation orders first, then a sufficiently large integer \(k_0\), then one lower bound on \(X\) that works simultaneously for
\[
k_0\le k\le\lfloor(\log X)^{3/5}\rfloor.
\]
This growing range is necessary to control the final rank mass.

For \(x=d/X\), define
\[
W_{k,x}(u)=\int_0^\infty e^{-v}
\left(1-u+\frac{\log(C_F|x|v)}{\ell}\right)_+^k\,dv.
\]
If
\[
g_d(z)=L_{F^{(d)}}(\tfrac12+z)(C_F|d|)^z\Gamma(1+z),
\]
then \(g_d(-z)=\varepsilon_dg_d(z)\). The weighted, absolutely convergent Dirichlet series satisfies the exact identity
\[
(1+\varepsilon_d(-1)^k)
\sum_n\frac{\lambda_F(n)\chi_d(n)}{\sqrt n}
W_{k,d/X}(\ell^{-1}\log n)
=\ell^{-k}g_d^{(k)}(0).
\]
Thus rank greater than \(k\), together with \(\varepsilon_d=(-1)^k\), forces this sum to vanish. The matching parity is essential. Section 6 later uses \(k\) and \(k-1\) to cover both functional signs.

The weight is divided into an initial piece, dyadic transition pieces with \(\delta_j=2^{-j}\), and an infinite terminal piece. The last scale is \(\delta_J\asymp\ell^{-1/10}\). A transition piece is supported at logarithmic indices corresponding to length at most \(X^{1-\delta_j/4}\). Its fixed-order derivatives cost powers of \(k/\delta_j\), while its size gains \(\delta_j^k\).

The terminal weight has an additional exponential decay in \(n/X\). Its second moment is at most a fixed logarithmic power times \(\delta_J^{2k}\). A sufficiently large fixed \(k_0\) absorbs that logarithmic factor uniformly in the entire range. The inequalities use \(k/(\ell\delta_j)\ll\ell^{-3/10}\). They do not hide a constant raised to the \(k\)-th power in a bound that is supposed to be uniform.

## 5. Block mollifiers and control on the whole model space

For a character value \(V(p)\in\{-1,0,1\}\), put
\[
D_p(b,V)=1-\lambda(p)V(p)p^{-1/2-b}
+\xi_p V(p)^2p^{-1-2b}.
\]
For real \(b\ge0\), this quantity is positive. Uniform local upper and lower bounds also hold for complex \(b\) with nonnegative real part.

Set \(s=k/\ell\), and use Euler cutoffs
\[
Y_j=X^{\delta_j^2/\sqrt{k}},\qquad
P_j^{\rm eu}(V)=\prod_{p\le Y_j}D_p(s,V)^{-1}.
\]
Partition the primes into blocks with bounded reciprocal-prime mass. In block \(i\), introduce a degree variable, truncate the inverse-factor polynomial at total degree
\[
m_{ji}=\lceil k^{1/4}+j+i\rceil,
\]
and multiply the block truncations to obtain \(H_j\).

The coefficient bound is \(|h_j(r)|\le\tau(r)r^{-1/2}\). Its length is small enough for Proposition 3.7 with \(\Delta=\delta_j/4\), once \(k_0\) is fixed sufficiently large. The length proof uses the decay in \(j\) and \(i\), not just a count of blocks.

### The simultaneous good event for integer characters

A 64th-moment estimate controls the relevant prime sums over all integers. The direct CRT error is bounded by \(Y_0^{128}/X\), which is small after the fixed choice of \(k_0\). It does not require a new prime-in-progressions theorem.

Outside \(O(X/k^2)\) integer parameters, all of the following hold simultaneously:
\[
\tfrac12\le H_jP_j^{\rm eu}\le2,\qquad
P_0^{\rm eu}/P_j^{\rm eu}\ge2^{-jk/8},\qquad
P_0^{\rm eu}\ge2^{-Jk/8}.
\]
Cauchy's coefficient estimate on a circle of radius two yields a relative block-truncation error. Good prime-sum bounds make those relative errors summable. Positivity of the actual real Euler factors is used when converting logarithmic prime-sum estimates to product inequalities.

### Full \(L^2\) estimates do not discard the bad event

The separate probabilistic norm estimates are over the **entire independent probability space**. They are not obtained by removing exceptional samples from an otherwise unbounded random Euler product.

Use a finite Euler product \(L_Q(b)\), with \(Q\) beyond all the finite polynomial lengths needed. Bounds are uniform in \(Q\). Exact cancellation below \(Y_0\) gives a residual product whose first and second moments are both \(1+O(e^{-2\sqrt{k}}/\sqrt{k})\). Controlling both moments is what bounds its distance from one.

Lemma 5.5 proves a relative estimate
\[
\|(H_j-(P_j^{\rm eu})^{-1})L_Q(b)\|_2
\le
\left(\exp\!\left(C\sum_i2^{-m_{ji}}\right)-1\right)
\|(P_j^{\rm eu})^{-1}L_Q(b)\|_2.
\]
Each block has a positive \(L^2\) lower bound and a bounded circle norm. Independence and telescoping multiply relative errors. There is no extra factor equal to the number of prime blocks. Small primes are handled by direct local bounds, and no nonvanishing on the coefficient-extraction circle is assumed.

The resulting shifted-product estimates are polynomial in the shift and in \(k2^j\), while the first product at \(s\) is exponentially close to one in \(k^{1/4}\).

### Returning to the actual weights

The proof needs an estimate for \((1-z/k)_+^k-e^{-z}\) that holds for every real \(z\), including arbitrarily negative \(z\). That range matters because the gamma integral contains an unbounded logarithm. Fixed derivatives have an error bounded by \(C_r k^{-1}(1+|z|)^2e^{-z}\).

A Fourier representation of the first-weight error therefore has a \(k^{-1}\) norm gain. The later weights retain their \(\delta_j^k\) gain. Exact finite-Euler-product coefficient identities then give
\[
\|H_0S_0-A_x\|_2\ll k^{-1},\qquad
\|H_jS_j\|_2\ll(k2^j)^{C_5}\delta_j^k,
\]
where
\[
A_x=(C_F|x|)^{k/\ell}\Gamma(1+k/\ell)
\]
is uniformly bounded above and below. The argument estimates the actual completed-derivative weights. It does not merely replace them heuristically by an exponential or interchange an uncontrolled infinite Euler limit.

## 6. From nonvanishing counts to rank mass and the mean

Proposition 3.7 transfers the model bounds to integer characters. Chebyshev's inequality makes the first mollified piece close to \(A_x\) outside \(O(X/k^2)\) integers. The later pieces and the terminal tail are smaller at their respective geometric scales. Intersecting these estimates with the simultaneous positive-product event shows that the first piece dominates the sum of all later pieces.

The total weighted derivative series is consequently nonzero outside \(O(X/k^2)\) parameters. Its exact identity excludes rank greater than \(k\) for the matching functional sign. Applying the neighboring derivative order for the other sign, and splitting the value at two, yields
\[
\#\{d\text{ admissible}:X\le|d|<2X,\ a(F^{(d)})>k\}
\ll_E X/k^2
\]
uniformly from a fixed lower bound up to \(k\le(\log X)^{3/5}\).

The remaining tail beyond this moving cutoff uses a separate pointwise bound
\[
a(F^{(d)})\ll_E\log(3X).
\]
The paper proves it through the functional equation, fixed-strip bounds and Jensen's formula. The disk is centered at two, with outer radius \(5/2\) and inner radius two. The Euler product provides a positive uniform lower bound for \(|L(2)|\). This prevents a small central value from invalidating the zero count.

Let \(T_X(k)\) be the rank-tail count and \(K=\lfloor(\log X)^{3/5}\rfloor\). The integer-tail identity gives
\[
\sum_{\substack{X\le|d|<2X\\a_d>R}}a_d
\le R\,T_X(R)+\sum_{k=R}^{K-1}T_X(k)
+C_E\log X\,T_X(K)
\ll_E \frac XR+X(\log X)^{-1/5}.
\]
The last exponent comes from \(\log X/K^2\). A bound available only for fixed \(k\) would not justify this step. Conversely this count estimate does not by itself provide a uniformly bounded second moment of analytic rank.

For fixed \(R\), dyadic summation up to \(Y\), together with a finite contribution from small blocks, proves the \(C_E/R\) limsup estimate. The finite-family reduction transfers it back to all signed squarefree parameters of the original curve.

Finally,
\[
\#\mathcal D(Y)=2Y/\zeta(2)+O(\sqrt Y).
\]
The companion density theorem makes the count of ranks at least two \(o(Y)\). The part between two and a fixed \(R\) then contributes \(o(Y)\), and the new tail bound controls the rest by \(C_E/R\) after the height limit. Letting \(R\) grow shows that all ranks at least two contribute \(o(Y)\). The rank-one density supplies the claimed mean of one half.

## 7. Why the algebraic moments use a different input

Section 7 uses the density-one agreement of analytic and algebraic rank from the companion result. Thus algebraic ranks zero and one each have density one half, and the set of algebraic ranks at least two has density zero.

Koymans–Smith, Theorem 1.4 in arXiv:2606.31649v1, is specialized to the base field \(\mathbb Q\), the abelian variety \(E\), one variable, and the polynomial \(P(u)=u\). It bounds the mean of \(\exp(\kappa r(E^{(v)}))\) over all nonzero signed integers by \(\exp(\exp(C_E\kappa))\), for each fixed positive \(\kappa\) and a height threshold allowed to depend on \(\kappa\). This integer family can repeat squareclasses.

Nonnegativity permits restriction to signed squarefree integers. Dividing by their asymptotic count gives a finite exponential-moment limsup, with the factor \(\zeta(2)\). For \(t>0\), Cauchy–Schwarz at parameter \(2t\) makes the exceptional density-zero set contribute zero in the limit. For \(t<0\), boundedness by one suffices. At \(t=0\), the average is identically one.

For a fixed integer \(m>0\), use \(x^{2m}\ll_m e^x\) and Cauchy–Schwarz. The proof does not differentiate the limit of exponential moments. Neither the cited input nor this argument supplies a limit with a growing moment parameter, or an analytic-rank moment theorem.
