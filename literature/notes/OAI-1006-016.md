## Main theorem and the two scalar fields

For a field \(F\) of characteristic \(p\), set

\[
V_F=F^\times/(F^\times)^p,\qquad
R_F=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb F_p}
\{[f]\otimes[1-f]:f\ne0,1\}.
\]

The degree-two quotient is
\(W_F=(V_F\otimes_{\mathbb F_p}V_F)/R_F=K_2^{\mathrm M}(F)/p\).
A compatible \(\mathbb F_p\)-linear isomorphism \(\Theta:V_K\to V_L\) carries \(R_K\) onto \(R_L\) under its tensor square, equivalently respecting the full degree-two product.

Theorem 1.1 assumes finitely generated extensions \(K/k\) and \(L/l\) of algebraically closed characteristic-\(p\) fields, each of relative transcendence degree at least two. It claims that

\[
\operatorname{Isom}(K,k;L,l)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Isom}_{\mathrm M}(V_K,V_L)/\mathbb F_p^\times
\]

is bijective. Thus a compatible map is a nonzero scalar multiple of a unique constant-preserving field isomorphism's induced map. The conclusion recovers the original fields, with no further Frobenius quotient.

The proof uses a second scalar field \(C_F=F^p\). As a set,

\[
V_F=F^\times/C_F^\times=\mathbb P_{C_F}(F).
\]

The additive law of the \(\mathbb F_p\)-vector space \(V_F\) is multiplication of representatives, so \([f]+[g]=[fg]\), and its zero is the projective point \([1]\). Projective lines instead arise from two-dimensional \(C_F\)-linear subspaces of \(F\). These two structures must be kept distinct. The projective coefficient field is \(F^p\), rather than the original algebraically closed constant field.

For \(d=\operatorname{trdeg}(F/\kappa)\), the source proves \([F:F^p]=p^d\) by computing the degree in two towers over a rational transcendence field and its \(p\)-th-power subfield. This calculation does not assume that the chosen finite extension of the rational field is separable. Since \(d\ge2\), the vector-space dimension used in the projective lifting is at least \(p^2\ge4\).

## 1. The symbol detects degree-p subfields

Section 2 develops the required \(p\)-basis facts directly. If
\(F^p\subset M\subset F\) and \(s\notin M\), then \(s\) has degree \(p\) over \(M\). A smaller degree factor of \((T-s)^p\) would have a coefficient forcing \(s\in M\). When \([F:F^p]\) is finite, successive adjunctions extend any \(p\)-independent list to a \(p\)-basis.

Its monomials carry commuting Euler derivations. For a basis element \(s_j\), \(D_j\) multiplies a monomial by its \(s_j\)-exponent in \(\mathbb F_p\). On \(C(s_j)\), the kernel is \(C\), and eigenvectors with eigenvalue in \(C\) are exactly scalar multiples of \(s_j^i\), \(0\le i<p\). For distinct basis entries,
\(C(s_j)\cap C(s_k)=C\).

The needed vanishing-symbol implication is

\[
s\notin C,\qquad \{s,t\}_F=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t\in C(s).
\]

For a \(p\)-independent pair \(s,t\), the proof defines the logarithmic-derivative determinant

\[
\beta([f],[g])
=\frac{D_sf}{f}\frac{D_tg}{g}
-\frac{D_tf}{f}\frac{D_sg}{g}.
\]

It is \(\mathbb F_p\)-bilinear, kills \(C^\times\) and every Steinberg pair, and takes value one on \(\{s,t\}_F\). Hence that symbol cannot vanish. This is the exact implication used subsequently. The stronger Bloch–Gabber–Kato injectivity theorem is cited for context but is not invoked to prove it.

The complementary affine fact follows from the defining relation itself:
\(\{t,c+dt\}=0\) for nonzero \(c,d\in C\) and nonzero arguments. Rescaling \(q=-dt/c\) turns it into \(\{q,1-q\}=0\).

Membership in the degree-\(p\) subfield \(C(s)\) alone is too large to identify the two-dimensional span \(C+Cs\). Section 3 supplies the additional intersection constraint.

## 2. An intersection forces one unique power correction

Proposition 3.1 assumes \(p\)-independent \(X,Y\), elements
\(U\in C(X)^\times\), \(V\in C(Y)^\times\), and the nonmonomial conditions
\([U]\notin\mathbb F_p[X]\), \([V]\notin\mathbb F_p[Y]\). If

\[
X C(Y/X)^\times\cap U C(V/U)^\times\ne\varnothing,
\]

then one unique integer \(1\le n<p\) satisfies

\[
U^n\in C+CX^n,\qquad V^n\in C+CY^n.
\]

Both coefficients in each inclusion are nonzero. Either inclusion by itself uniquely determines this exponent.

For a common witness \(Z\), define
\(B_X=U/D_XU\), \(B_Y=V/D_YV\), and \(A_X=B_X-1\), \(A_Y=B_Y-1\).
The nonmonomial assumptions ensure that the denominators are nonzero and that \(A_X,A_Y\) are nonconstant in their respective degree-\(p\) fields.

The derivations
\(\delta=D_X+D_Y\) and
\(\delta'=B_XD_X+B_YD_Y\)
both send \(Z\) to \(Z\). With \(q_X=D_XZ/Z\), \(q_Y=D_YZ/Z\),

\[
q_X+q_Y=1,\qquad A_Xq_X+A_Yq_Y=0.
\]

The \(q\)'s are nonzero and killed by \(\delta\). Consequently

\[
\frac{D_XA_X}{A_X}=\frac{D_YA_Y}{A_Y}\in C.
\]

The intersection \(C(X)\cap C(Y)=C\) and the Euler eigenbasis give
\(A_X=c_XX^m\), \(A_Y=c_YY^m\), with one common \(1\le m<p\).

Set \(n=p-m\). Comparing logarithmic derivatives shows that

\[
\frac{U^n}{X^n+c_XX^p}\in C^\times.
\]

Since \(X^p\in C\), this gives the first affine inclusion, and the second follows identically. If \(U^r=h+jX^r\) for another \(1\le r<p\), the same derivative computes
\(A_X=h/(jX^r)\), a scalar multiple of \(X^{p-r}\). Independence of the \(p\) monomials forces \(r=n\).

The conclusion is invariant under changing all representatives by \(C^\times\). This representative independence and the one-sided uniqueness are what permit a global correction in the next section.

## 3. A connected graph propagates the correction to every line

Section 4 forms a graph on the nonzero elements of \(V_K\), excluding its zero point \([1]\). Join \([x]\) to \([y]\) when their image representatives \(X,Y\) generate distinct degree-\(p\) fields over \(C=L^p\). This means \(p\)-independence. The graph is complete between the parts indexed by \(C(X)\). Since \([L:C]\ge p^2\), every vertex has a neighbor and any two vertices have a path of length at most two.

For an edge, \(1,x,y\) are linearly independent over \(H=K^p\). An affine dependence would give a vanishing symbol in the source, then put \(Y\) in \(C(X)\) by the previous implication.

Choose arbitrary nonzero \(a,b\in H\) and set

\[
u=x+a,\qquad v=y+b,\qquad z=bx-ay=bu-av.
\]

The actual figure on p. 7 displays the common point \([z]\) on the lines joining \([x],[y]\) and \([u],[v]\). It expresses only incidence, with no order or metric assumption in characteristic \(p\).

Four Steinberg consequences give

\[
\{x,u\}=\{y,v\}=\{y/x,z/x\}=\{v/u,z/u\}=0.
\]

After applying \(\Theta\), the first two put \(U,V\) into \(C(X),C(Y)\), and the last two put \(Z\) in the intersection of Proposition 3.1. Injectivity preserves the nonmonomial hypotheses.

Fixing the neighbor and \(b\) while varying \(a\), uniqueness of the \(Y,V\) inclusion keeps \(n\) constant. Setting \(a=1\) then compares different neighbors and choices of \(b\) through the fixed \(X,U\) inclusion. Adjacent vertices have the same exponent. Connectivity therefore gives one global \(n\in\mathbb F_p^\times\).

The map \(\Psi=n\Theta\) sends every line through \([1]\) into a line. Every projective line is a multiplicative translate of one through \([1]\), so the multiplicative group law extends this to all lines.

At this point the argument has containment, not yet equality. A separate lemma says that no nonidentity scalar on \(V_F\) preserves all projective lines: for \(1<r<p\), the binomial coefficient of \(x^{r-1}\) prevents \((x+1)^r\) from lying in \(F^p+F^px^r\). Apply the line-containment construction to \(\Psi^{-1}\). Its possible new scalar \(r\), composed with \(\Psi\), would preserve lines as a scalar map, so \(r=1\). Both directions now give containment and hence equality of lines. The same scalar lemma proves uniqueness of the original \(n\).

## 4. Semilinearity becomes the field law

Section 5 gives the coordinate proof of the projective lifting theorem it needs. A line-preserving bijection between projective spaces of vector dimension at least three preserves spans, so the dimensions agree. Normalize basis-point images and the images of \(e_0+e_i\). On the affine chart, parallelism forces coordinatewise bijections. Diagonal lines identify these coordinate maps with a single \(\sigma\). Parallel diagonals give additivity, and lines of varying slope give multiplicativity. Thus \(\sigma\) is an isomorphism of coefficient fields and the projective map has an additive \(\sigma\)-semilinear lift \(S\).

Comparing two lifts on independent vectors and their sum makes their pointwise scalar factors agree. Comparing dependent vectors through a third vector gives global uniqueness up to one target scalar, including uniqueness of \(\sigma\).

Apply this to \(\Psi\) over \(H=K^p\) and \(C=L^p\), and normalize \(S(1)=1\). For each nonzero \(f\), the two semilinear maps

\[
w\longmapsto S(fw),\qquad
w\longmapsto S(f)S(w)
\]

induce the same projective map because \(\Psi\) preserves multiplication of representatives. They agree at one, so uniqueness of the lift makes them equal everywhere. The additive lift is therefore multiplicative and is a field isomorphism \(K\to L\).

The source then recovers the named constants through the intrinsic identity

\[
\bigcap_{m\ge1}F^{p^m}=\kappa.
\]

For a nonconstant \(f\), complete it to a transcendence basis and let \(E=B(f)\) be the resulting rational field. If \(f\) had a \(p^m\)-th root in the fixed finite extension \(F/E\), Eisenstein irreducibility of \(T^{p^m}-f\) would require \(p^m\le[F:E]\). This fails for large \(m\). Perfectness gives the opposite inclusion for constants.

Finally, two field isomorphisms whose induced maps differ by an \(\mathbb F_p^\times\)-scalar both preserve lines. The scalar lemma forces that scalar to be one, and normalized semilinear uniqueness forces the field maps themselves to coincide.

## Frobenius, scope and comparison with OAI-1006-015

The concluding remark makes the target quotient explicit. For these nonperfect function fields, the endomorphism \(F\to F\), \(f\mapsto f^p\), is not surjective and induces zero on \(V_F=F^\times/(F^\times)^p\). It is not an extra automorphism ambiguity.

The map \(F\to F^p\) is instead a field isomorphism with a different actual target. Its induced map lands in

\[
V_{F^p}=(F^p)^\times/(F^{p^2})^\times,
\]

and is already covered by the theorem. The two target groups cannot be silently identified through the inclusion \(F^p\subset F\), which kills these classes.

This distinguishes the paper from [OAI-1006-015](OAI-1006-015.md), where the coefficient prime differs from the characteristic and the result concerns perfect closures modulo Frobenius. The present modulo-characteristic proof uses finite \(p\)-degree, elementary derivations and projective geometry. Its full theorem assumes the same fixed characteristic \(p\), algebraically closed constants and relative dimension at least two. It does not establish a relative-dimension-one version, reconstruction from partial/noisy products or an efficient algorithm.
