## 1. Problem and precise result

For an elliptic curve over the rationals, distinguish

\[
r(E)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}E(\mathbb Q),\qquad
a(E)=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s),\qquad
s_2(E)=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb Z_2}\operatorname{Sel}_{2^\infty}(E/\mathbb Q).
\]

The main theorem asserts that if **\(E(\mathbb Q)[2]\ne0\)** and **\(s_2(E)\in\{0,1\}\)**, then

\[
a(E)=r(E)=s_2(E),\qquad \Sha(E/\mathbb Q)\text{ is finite}.
\]

It is pointwise at the prescribed curve. No exceptional density-zero set is discarded. The hypotheses allow CM, one rational two-torsion line or full rational two-torsion, and arbitrary reduction at 2 and the bad primes. The conclusion includes finiteness of the **whole** Shafarevich–Tate group, obtained after analytic rank zero or one has been established by invoking the forward Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin results. It does not prove the leading-coefficient BSD formula.

The exact Kummer sequence gives

\[
s_2(E)=r(E)+\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb Z_2}\Sha(E)[2^\infty].
\]

Thus a Mordell–Weil rank assumption alone cannot replace the Selmer-corank assumption. Neither can the dimension of the finite two-Selmer group. In particular, the corank-one hypothesis does not initially supply a nontorsion rational point or finite Sha. The proof preserves this distinction at its unknown base vertex.

The introduction cites the broader OpenAI Goldfeld/2-converse manuscript as context, not as a premise of this rational-two-torsion proof. Classical modularity, two-parity, rational isogeny invariance, Euler systems, duality, Heegner formulas and half-integral-weight forms are substantive imported inputs. [Original, §§1–2 and §9.]

## 2. Notation and the order of construction

Choose a fixed support containing \(2N_E\). A filter fixes the real sign and unit squareclasses there, with additional finite exclusions. For new good odd primes, put \(q^*=(-1/q)q\), and form squarefree products \(h\) of these signed primes. The empty product is 1. Write

\[
t_q=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}E[2]^{\mathrm{Fr}_q},\qquad
w(h)=\frac12\sum_{q\mid h}t_q,
\quad s(h)=\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb Z_2}\Sha(E^{(h)})[2^\infty]
\]

when that group is finite, and normalize the central value as
\(L_*(h)=L(E^{(h)},1)\sqrt{|h|}/\Omega\) with the fixed sign-appropriate period. Valuations have \(v_2(2)=1\). These weights count residual fixed-space dimensions, not uniformly one per prime.

A binary family has addresses \(x\in\mathbb F_2^b\), with a new prime present when a nonzero linear activation \(\lambda_q(x)\) is 1. At \(x=0\), every new prime is absent. The method constructs analytically nonvanishing twists at all nonzero addresses, with valuation bounds whose constants are independent of \(b\), then interpolates back to the unknown zero address. Prime sizes, realization precision and the number of auxiliary primes may grow.

The roadmap separates three negative companions in the odd case:

| Companion | What is fixed or allowed to vary | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| \(h_*\) | Fixed before odd parameters vary; \(L(E^{(h_*)},1)\ne0\) | Defines a genus-sum point \(P_h\) over the varying field \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{hh_*})\), and hence the odd detector. |
| \(k'(h)\) | May depend on \(h\); its number of prime factors is uniformly bounded | Proves the lower divisibility bound needed before the odd coefficients can be normalized. |
| \(k\) | Fixed before cube dimension grows; \(L(E^{(k)},1)\ne0\) | Defines the fixed ring-class field base \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt k)\) for final transfer. |

The logical sequence is: cyclotomic lower bound → even coefficients and finite networks → uniform even upper estimate and corank-zero conclusion → bounded-factor companion plus ring-class lower bound → odd lower bound → odd normalization → simultaneous odd/even networks over fixed \(K\) → corank-one transfer. The even estimate, rather than an already-normalized odd symbol, supplies the missing premise of odd normalization. [Original, Figure 1, §§7.4 and 9.]

## 3. Cyclotomic interpolation: rational divisibility and integral control are separate

Use \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z_2[[t]]\), \(\mathcal O=\widehat{\Lambda_{(2)}}\), and residue field \(\mathbb F_2((t))\). There is no central-evaluation homomorphism from \(\mathcal O\) to \(\mathbb Q_2\); central values must be taken from coordinates first known to lie in \(\Lambda[1/2]\).

For nonzero central values the relevant normalized defect is

\[
D(h)=v_2(L_*(h))-2w(h)-s(h).
\]

The section proves a lower bound \(D(h)\ge-C_E\). Its missing-vertex statement says that a corank-zero base \(h_0\) has nonzero central value if arbitrarily large cubes retaining all its old primes have nonzero central values and \(D(h_x)\le B\) at all other vertices. The returned upper bound differs by a constant independent of the base's number of prime factors and of the cube. The necessary dimension can depend on the base.

### Positive global complex and fixed Kato lattice

The positive complex removes ordinary real-place cohomology by a cone. Here “ordinary” describes the unmodified cohomology convention; it imposes no ordinary-reduction hypothesis at 2. Finite Artin models and derived specialization retain the real contribution and its fixed integral index.

After localization at \((2)\), the positive global complex becomes free of rank two in degree one. The residual proof uses the extension of trivial modules supplied by rational two-torsion, explicit unit signatures in cyclotomic layers and Ferrero–Washington's vanishing of the abelian \(\mu\)-invariant. Bounds in finite ramification supports are used to control growth slopes; this is not a uniform bound on the number of all finite-stage generators as support grows.

Kato classes are transferred into a **single fixed modular-form lattice**. The coefficient map is an integral weighted coset sum, without division by the order of a quadratic character group. Smoothing parameters can satisfy the growing tame congruences while their 2-adic central loss remains fixed. This is the source of uniform 2-integrality.

Rational height-one divisibility uses Kato's rational theorem, or the cited CM rank-zero input, together with explicit local comparison complexes. Characterwise comparison scalars can have uncontrolled 2-valuations: they are units after inverting 2, so this comparison proves rational regularity only. The proof does not use them to establish the uniform integral bound.

At a bad prime the local object is \(H^1(I_q,V)\), not automatically \(V^{I_q}(-1)\). The split multiplicative example distinguishes the resulting factors \(1-\gamma_q/q\) and \(1-\gamma_q\). At unused good primes the inverse-Frobenius correction is built in the group ring using a ratio congruent to 1 modulo 2; the apparent half-projector cancels inside each factor. No loss accumulates per unused prime.

### Determinant, central length and binary congruence

The wedge of the real class and the positive lift of the Kato class defines a scalar determinant coordinate \(u\). Rational divisibility gives its character values in \(\Lambda[1/2]\). Integral construction gives \(2^{C}u\in\mathcal O[G]\). Fourier inversion, followed by the intersection \(\mathcal O\cap\Lambda[1/2]=\Lambda\), returns \(2^{C}u\in\Lambda[G]\) with the same fixed exponent; it does not charge a new \(|G|\) denominator.

At a corank-zero character the central determinant comparison reads

\[
v_2(u_h(0))=v_2(L_*(h))-s(h)-\sum_{q\mid h}t_q+O_E(1).
\]

The global-to-local image index cancels against the corresponding Poitou–Tate length. Unused-prime local torsion cancels its reciprocity Euler factor. Active primes contribute their exact \(t_q\) rather than a bounded error repeated once per prime. At 2, an integral Kummer quotient and a bounded formal-logarithm comparison cover every reduction type. Zero central value makes the rational finite Selmer class zero under the corank-zero hypothesis.

The binary lemma supplies a nonzero address agreeing with the zero address on \(r\) integral group-ring tests modulo \(2^M\) whenever

\[
b>r(2^M-1).
\]

The proof controls the degree of each binary digit of a sign evaluation and applies Chevalley–Warning parity to the common-zero count. Using sufficiently high precision contradicts uniformly bounded nonzero-vertex valuations if the base coordinate vanishes. [Original, §3.]

## 4. Ring-class deformation: retain arithmetic maps, not just limiting matrices

Fix an imaginary quadratic \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt k)\) split at \(2N\), excluding the extra-unit fields and, for a CM curve, its CM field. Every active prime of \(h\) splits in \(K\). The primitive, **unaveraged** ring-class character sum of conductor \(|h|\) lies on the twist over \(K\). At a product simple zero its free-line index is \(j_h\).

The two outputs have different uniformity:

\[
2j_h\ge4w(h)+s(h)+s(hk)-C(E,A),\qquad \omega(k)\le A,
\]

and, for **fixed** \(K\) with \(s_2(E/K)=1\), a missing-vertex implication from product simple zeros and an upper bound on
\(2j_{h_x}-4w(h_x)-s(h_x)-s(h_xk)\) at every nonzero vertex to a product simple zero at the base. The second assertion initially assumes neither rank one nor finite Sha at that base.

### Finite models and the two limits

The finite coefficient rings are \(A_i=(\mathbb Z/2^i)[t]/(t^i)\), also with a fixed elementary 2-group algebra. A perturbation construction compresses flat cochain complexes to bounded free complexes while retaining maps into the original cochains, contraction homotopies, dualities and boundary witnesses. Arithmetic descent, Shapiro transfer, character weights and growing coset sums are completed **before** compression on the target field. The unbounded-degree source ring-class complexes are not retained as bounded objects.

For a fixed field and active support, residual global/local cohomology bounds give inner bounded ranks. Finite matrix identities pass through one fixed ultrafilter and compatible cofinal precision. Later switches extend the same finite stages and contractions; arbitrary replacement by an equivalent abstract complex would not preserve all previously defined evaluations.

The local Kummer condition is the perfect complex \(B(F_v)^\wedge_2[-1]\). Derived reduction includes \(B(F_v)[2^n]\) in degree zero as well as the usual quotient in degree one. Its exact self-orthogonality is checked using local Tate duality and derived Nakayama. Discarding these degree-zero terms would spoil the later injectivity arguments.

### Moving characters and actual cocycle evaluations

A moving split conductor prime \(r_i\) provides scalar characters trivial at \(2N\), with odd exponents at selected oriented active primes. Kummer prescriptions respect class-group relations by valuation tests. Tate-image arguments leave room for trace bounds at \(r_i\); the CM case uses a fixed-power construction in the norm-one Cartan. Finite Chebotarev then realizes all conditions. No single prime is required to split in an infinite extension.

Active local complexes disappear at characteristic-zero tests by ramified quadratic inertia, and at the residual test by the nonconstant scalar Frobenius. The moving-prime complex is also acyclic on the relevant tests. Removing these contributions yields residual sizes controlled by the number of companion ramified primes and retained derivative slots, not by the number of active primes. A pro-2 generator/Schreier argument through the fixed two-division field supplies this bound. It is a bound on ranks, not on torsion exponents.

Evaluation on **every sequence** of Galois elements is retained in degrees zero, one and two. In particular,

\[
e^1_gd=(\rho(g)-1)e^0,\qquad
e^2_{g,h}d=\rho(g)e^1_h-e^1_{gh}+e^1_g.
\]

These identities make limiting cycles actual abstract crossed cocycles even if finite representatives are only approximate cycles. A finite detecting list proves injectivity after arbitrary field extension, but does not replace the all-sequence identities. Restriction to the **exact** common kernel of Tate, ring-class and quadratic actions remains injective: a fixed nontrivial scalar proves the characteristic-zero assertion; moving inertia proves the residual assertion.

The outer construction quotients an ultraproduct of completed DVRs by elements whose valuations tend to infinity. Every surviving nonzero element is a finite power of 2 times a unit, so the quotient is again a DVR with characteristic-zero fraction field. Integral degree-two evaluation survives on its new cycles. Uniform finite Tate-matrix witnesses retain irreducibility. Derivative primes realizing \(\tau u\) give local evaluations of \((\tau u)^2\); on the exact kernel this is \((1+\tau)b(u)\). ## 5. Integral switches, determinant bounds and fixed-field transfer

### The switch at 2

For inert derivative primes whose Frobenius tends to complex conjugation \(J\), the local complex splits into degrees 0, 1 and 2, with degree-one finite and singular planes \(F\oplus S\). Their coordinates are cocycle values at Frobenius squared and at tame inertia. Extra congruence precision makes both pure planes isotropic **integrally**. The proof uses the tame Fox resolution and a Hilbert-symbol calculation; characteristic-two skew symmetry alone would not suffice.

Kolyvagin derivative operators satisfy
\((\sigma_\ell-1)D_\ell=(\ell+1)-\sum\sigma_\ell^i\).
One fixed multiplier clears torsion descent, cuspidal ambiguities and fixed bad-place component obstructions. Descent is performed once for the complete conductor, so its loss is not repeated for each derivative prime. Weighted transfer again uses no class-number division.

The finite and singular local formulas are compared using the exact Frobenius polynomial. In the limit they give

\[
f(P_{I\cup\{\ell\}})=0,\qquad s(P_{I\cup\{\ell\}})=Jf(P_I).
\]

The reduction of the derivative point contains \(\ell(\ell+1)/2\). Its extra factor of 2 is paid by choosing \(v_2(\ell+1)\ge a+1\) at retained precision \(2^a\), not by inserting another class denominator.

The determinant uses the class and its degree-two functional produced by the **same** integral duality diagram. Switching finite to singular conditions preserves its valuation exactly even when localization is nonprimitive. A fractional complement to \(2^de_1\) can have unit exterior volume; forcing an integral complement would introduce a spurious loss. Torsion elementary divisors remain in the determinant comparison.

### Rank reduction and the central formula

Away from \((2)\), exact-kernel evaluation and Chebotarev supply rank-two localizations that reduce specialized Selmer dimension while preserving generic rank one and determinant valuation. When the scalar character squares to one, two inequivalent sign components require an intermediate switch before the next reduction. This gives \(u_h\in\Lambda[1/2]\).

For the uniform residual bound, a hypothetical sequence of valuations tending to minus infinity passes to the outer DVR. Extra outer cohomology is detected even when it has no exact inner-cycle lift. A bounded number of switches reduces it to rank one. A surviving maximal boundary minor then has bounded valuation. For a two-term complex, the coordinate is \(\pm\alpha\beta/\Delta\), yielding a lower bound from integral class/functionals and that minor. Bounded matrix size by itself is explicitly insufficient: the one-by-one matrices \([2^i]\) show why.

Central specialization uses the entire derived complex and its Tor terms. The scalar at the moving auxiliary conductor is \(a_r-2\chi_h(r)\), with bounded nonzero valuation. Each active split prime contributes twice \(t_p\), once at each place of \(K\). Thus

\[
v_2(u_h(0))=2j_h-\operatorname{length}\Sha(E^{(h)}/K)[2^\infty]-2\sum_{p\mid h}t_p+O_E(1).
\]

The Cassels–Tate **isogeny identity**, applied only after the product simple zero establishes finiteness, compares restriction of scalars with the two rational twists. Split-prime factors cancel, including every active prime. It yields

\[
\operatorname{length}\Sha(E^{(h)}/K)[2^\infty]
=s(h)+s(hk)+\sum_{p\mid k}t_p+O_E(1).
\]

The last sum is bounded by \(2A\). A mere statement that isogeny kernels/cokernels are killed by 2 would not bound their lengths and is not the argument used. These identities give the claimed ring-class lower bound.

### Why the family needs more than individual bounds

For final transfer the trace class and dual functional are constructed over \(\Lambda[G]\) before evaluating characters. At an unused split prime the trace multiplier is \(e_q=a_q-z-z^{-1}\), while the local determinant contributes \(l_q\). The relaxed coordinate differs from the primitive one by \(e_q^2/l_q\). Since \(l_q/e_q^2\equiv1\pmod2\), the correction

\[
\prod_q\left(1+\frac{l_q/e_q^2-1}{2}(1+g_q)\right)
\]

is integral over \(\mathcal O[G]\) without a denominator per prime.

The quotient \(\mathcal O/2^M\mathcal O\) is a Laurent-series ring with finite negative support. Simultaneous Schur elimination of all new local blocks gives a pole bound independent of their number: a perturbation of a block-diagonal matrix has inverse pole at most \(Mh\) modulo \(2^M\), where \(h\) bounds each residual determinant order. Here order four suffices for the two-place local blocks. The remaining complex has the fixed old size.

A bounded-complex argument then produces \(f_xu_x=d_x\), with \(f,d\in\mathcal O[G]\), \(f_0\in\Lambda\), bounded finite \(c=v_2(f_0(0))\), and uniform negative-support bounds for \(f_x\). No comparable support bound is needed for \(d_x\). The finite quotient of the **fixed** base Selmer group controls central torsion even without known finite Sha; no uniformity in varying \(K\) is claimed here.

Testing coefficients of \(f_x\) in degrees \(-L(M),\ldots,0\), plus the constant coefficient of \(d_x\), gives

\[
v_2(u_x(0)-u_0(0))\ge M-2c
\]

at a nonzero address once \(b>(L(M)+2)(2^M-1)\) and \(M>c\). If the base derivative vanished, Gross–Zagier would make its Heegner sum torsion and its specialized coordinate zero. The congruence would contradict the fixed nonzero-vertex upper bound. This proves fixed-\(K\) missing-vertex transfer. [Original, §5.]

## 6. Auxiliary twists and independent coefficient detectors

### Nonvanishing with controlled prime count

Its eventual size threshold may depend on the curve and the growing congruence conditions.

The proof uses approximate functional equations, a quadratic-large-sieve estimate for the square-divisor tail, and Poisson summation in the progression. The zero frequency factors through the symmetric-square L-function; its residue is positive, including CM. For an imposed squarefree divisor \(b\), the central first moment has an error \(O(b^B X^{1-\eta})\) with absolute exponents. The sieve density is
\(\beta_p=1/(1+pF_p^{\rm ev})=p^{-1}+O(p^{-2})\).
Nonzero frequencies are bounded through quadratic Gauss-sum local factors and a shifted twisted-GL2 Dirichlet series, keeping polynomial dependence on the imposed divisor. Truncation gives a fixed distribution exponent. Positivity and a lower linear sieve produce rough bounded-factor central witnesses. ### Even and odd coefficients are different constructions

An even filter admits a holomorphic cuspidal weight-3/2 coefficient system orthogonal to unary theta series. A selected nonzero Fourier functional, sufficiently deep local vectors and fixed-support sieves permit arbitrary bad-prime depth. Waldspurger's comparison on the full good-Hecke eigenspace yields the exact same-filter ratio

\[
2v_2\!\left(\frac{a(D)}{a(D_0)}\right)
=v_2\!\left(\frac{L_*(\sigma D)}{L_*(\sigma D_0)}\right).
\]

Galois comparison makes normalized squarefree coefficients rational up to a 2-power root of unity. There is no new-prime product hidden in the ratio. The cyclotomic lower bound therefore normalizes these coefficients uniformly.

For the odd source, \(P_D\) is an unaveraged **conductor-one genus sum** over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{Dh_*})\), distinct from the conductor-\(|h|\) sums over fixed \(K\). Its free part lies in the rational \(D\)-twist component. A Kummer-translation argument, using irreducibility over \(\mathbb Q\) also for CM and a central scalar, chooses good primes \(\ell\) whose Frobenius approaches conjugation and whose finite-field functional detects one fixed simple-zero witness with bounded valuation.

The paper explicitly constructs a weighted ternary theta lift on the quaternion algebra ramified at \(\ell\) and infinity. Its local Schwartz functions include selected roots at level primes, the integral generator condition \((1+x)/2\) at 2, a determinant-character weight on nonzero nilpotent reductions at primes of \(h_*\), and a quadratic weight on the trace-zero residue line at \(\ell\). Weil transformation formulas establish the scalar modular law at every required depth. Integer lifts of finite coefficient values need not satisfy characteristic-zero eigenrelations.

Optimal-embedding orbit weights and orientations give

\[
a_M(D)=c_0\,2^{\omega(N)}\zeta_M(D)\,
\lambda_M(\operatorname{red}_\ell(LP_D))\pmod{2^M},
\]

with fixed \(c_0,L\), a unit phase, and \((D/\ell)=1\). Stabilizer denominators are cleared uniformly; there is no class-number average. The two orientations at each level prime and at \(\ell\), together with the embedding stabilizer, account for the displayed multiplicity.

A visible odd coefficient detects a simple zero. Conversely point divisibility supplies a lower bound on its coefficient valuation, with fixed losses. This detector is constructed before its uniform arithmetic lower bound is established. [Original, §§6–7.3.]

B_U=2^{-\lceil\delta+w(U)\rceil}\prod_{p\in U}(U_p-\eta(p)V_p)g

are integral by the half-integral-weight Hecke recurrence. In matching parity, subtraction of the surviving unary theta series makes a further division by 2 integral. Odd-stage global integrality uses a truncated bound at every Fourier index: stabilized bounded weights handle the low range, while a coarser arithmetic lower bound handles high weights. A statement only about finitely many visible coefficients would be insufficient.

Multiplying by ordinary theta places the forms in a finite weight-two Hecke span. An integral Fourier module, including diamond transforms and undivided unary corrections, is closed under continuous trace and determinant operators. After reduction, fresh traces kill the unary corrections, and the orbit satisfies

d(u)t(v)=t(v),\qquad t(u)t(v)=t(uv)+t(uv^{-1}),\qquad t(1)=0.

A level-prime identity is proved on the ramified principal-series constituent, with old constituents contributing zero trace differences. First-shell coefficient arithmetic turns an inertia toggle into contraction of two vertices with product labels. The entire rational prime is removed from the new coefficient system, including both oriented places when it splits. Simple–simple contraction is zero. A trivial primary label gives zero, and a pair of good simple primes with matching relevant labels preserves the symbol when \(\#E(\mathbb F_q)/2\) is odd. These are coefficient statements, before prime realization.

A tree with two simple vertices also gives zero. Auxiliary-primary networks act by sums of such terminal finite-difference operators. For grounded Laplacian \(Q\), the constant operator coefficient is \(\det Q\) and the pair coefficient is \(c_a^T\operatorname{adj}(Q)c_b\), including singular \(Q\).

One has zero constant and the selected first derivative at its target; the other is a two-choice family whose first-degree difference is supported at that target. ## 8. Closing the proof at the prescribed curve

If the isogeny class contains a full-two-torsion curve, use it. Otherwise the two rational two-isogenous curves have quadratic two-division fields \(F,F'\). A prime inert in both is a good simple prime, with group order 2 modulo 4. A prime split in \(F\) and inert in \(F'\) can change a bad simple label into a good one. These supply operations are justified through the finite label alphabets, rather than assumed for arbitrary unconstrained residue patterns.

Minimum seeds make edge bits irrelevant at the minimum split count. Good twins stabilize that count at sufficiently large permitted simple counts. Finite reserves then contract sufficiently rich lists to seeds and twin pairs. Duplicating full labels constructs neutral packets with zero total rational label and, when needed, a donor surplus. Distinct primes realize duplicated labels; no actual prime factor is repeated.

For corank zero, assign a neutral packet to each nonzero linear form. Every nonzero address has a nonzero terminal polynomial. Addressing makes it a unit, so its central value is nonzero with \(v_2(L_*(h_x))\le2w(h_x)+C\). Cyclotomic transfer reaches the base. The same stationary construction proves a uniform even estimate at any partitionable corank-zero parameter.

For an already analytically rank-one \(h\), choose a varying negative companion \(k'(h)\), square at \(2Nh\), with bounded prime count. Finite dummy reserves and the rough nonvanishing theorem make \(hk'\) analytically nonvanishing and partitionable. Switching isogeny orientation when necessary avoids paying for an unbounded initial bad-simple imbalance with new primes. The even estimate applies there.

The explicit Gross–Zagier height ratio compares the conductor-\(|h|\) ring sum to the conductor-one genus sum:

\[
2j_h(k')-2j(P_h)=v_2(L_*(hk'))+O(1).
\]

The derivative and rank-one regulator cancel. The remaining square-root factor is exactly the normalization of \(L_*\); fixed parametrization, projections and local classes account for the bounded error. Combining the ring-class lower bound and even upper estimate yields

\[
2j(P_h)\ge2w(h)+s(h)-C.
\]

This discharges the odd normalization premise. The odd symbol's finite graph tables, label operations and witness type are then stabilized with the same ultrafilter. The moving detection prime adds only boundedly many label coordinates. The actual stage is selected after the finite cube and networks, so there is no unjustified intersection of infinitely many precision conditions.

### The full-two-torsion exception is treated separately

After restricting all new primes to split in the final field \(K\), an even minimum might occur only at the empty configuration. The proof does not assume a nonempty seed in that situation. Failure of an ordinary nonempty unit gives one extra valuation at all nonempty split parameters. Trace arithmetic then forces a mod-8 congruence at split primes and makes a further division by 2 of the unary-subtracted form integral. Fresh ramification ensures \(K\not\subseteq\mathbb Q(E[8])\); a prime inert in \(K\) but trivial on \(E[8]\) produces a nonzero squared trace. Its square lies over \(K\), giving a nonempty split witness for the deeper test. The even valuation upper bound changes by a fixed amount only.

An orientation-correction lemma identifies an oriented total with zero rational projection as lying in \((1+c)V\). Reserved labels allow conjugation choices in contraction forests to correct that total without changing the common rational labels. ### Final fixed companion and simultaneous return

Choose once a negative even unit \(k\), with fresh ramification independent of the intrinsic old label extension, and set \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt k)\). In either residual case construct common terminal packets whose odd test at \(h\) and even test at \(hk\) are separately nonzero. The simultaneous address lemma supplies a common finite realization where both are units at every nonzero vertex. All new primes split in \(K\), and the valuation constants stay fixed as dimension grows.

At these vertices, the height comparison and unit bounds give \(2j_h(k)\le4w(h)+C\). At the base, forward analytic nonvanishing of \(E^{(k)}\) gives \(s_2(E^{(k)})=0\), hence

\[
s_2(E/K)=s_2(E)+s_2(E^{(k)})=1.
\]

This is a corank identity after rationalizing the quadratic projections, not an integral direct-sum decomposition. Fixed-field transfer proves that \(L(E,s)L(E^{(k)},s)\) has a simple zero. Its second factor is nonzero, so \(a(E)=1\). The forward theorem then supplies rational rank one and finite whole Sha. [Original, §9.]
