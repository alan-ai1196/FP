## 1. What the theorem actually asserts

For odd \(N\), the paper considers the algebra generated by \(T_\ell\) and \(\ell S_\ell\), \(\ell\nmid2N\), on all holomorphic forms of positive weights at tame level \(\Gamma_1(N)\), including Eisenstein forms. Its completed varying-weight algebra is
\[
 \mathbb T_2(N)=\varprojlim_k
 \left(\mathbb Z_2\otimes_{\mathbb Z}T_{\le k}^{(2)}(N)\right).
\]
Each finite weight range has its \(2\)-adic topology. The Frobenius polynomial convention is
\[
 Z^2-T_\ell Z+\ell S_\ell,\qquad
 S_\ell=\ell^{i-2}\langle\ell\rangle\quad\text{in weight }i.
\]

Theorem 1.1 states that every continuous, odd, absolutely irreducible
\[
 r:G_{\mathbb Q}\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_2(E),
 \qquad E/\mathbb Q_2\text{ finite},
\]
unramified outside finitely many finite primes, has a continuous coefficient point
\[
 \lambda:\mathbb T_2(N)\longrightarrow\mathcal O_E
\]
with the prescribed Frobenius traces and determinants at every \(\ell\nmid2N\), for some odd \(N\) containing all odd ramified primes of \(r\).

The target algebra allows auxiliary tame primes. Thus the theorem does not establish occurrence at an arbitrarily prescribed set of tame primes in Emerton's stronger formulation. No residual irreducibility, residual nonscalarity or de Rham condition on \(r\) is assumed. The conclusion is completed-Hecke occurrence, called pro-modularity here. It is not classical modularity of the original representation, nor a statement that it lies in one fixed algebraic weight.

A stable integral lattice gives the integrality of the required traces and determinants. All temporary coefficient extensions in the proof are finite. At characteristic two, a pseudorepresentation means a two-dimensional determinant law containing both trace and determinant, so the integral deformation arguments make no division by two.

## 2. A large family and two different modular seeds

### Determinant normalization

Global class field theory writes the determinant as an odd-conductor finite-order character times a continuous character of \(\mathbb Z_2^\times\) through the cyclotomic character \(\varepsilon\). Using
\[
 \mathbb Z_2^\times=\{\pm1\}\times5^{\mathbb Z_2},
\]
a square root after finite coefficient extension produces a continuous cyclotomic twist making
\[
 \det r=\chi=\delta\varepsilon^w.
\]
The parity of \(w\) is prescribed, but \(w\) may be arbitrarily large in that parity class. The twisting character is one at \(-1\), preserving oddness. The local central character is \(\psi=\chi\varepsilon\), with scalar-unit action \(z^{w+1}\). The local block Galois parameter is normalized as \(r|_{G_{\mathbb Q_2}}\otimes\varepsilon\), of determinant \(\chi\varepsilon^2\).

For sufficiently large \(w\), the same \(\chi\) is the determinant of a regular non-CM cuspidal modular representation. The dimension of cusp forms grows linearly with weight at fixed level, whereas the CM contribution is bounded using finitely many quadratic fields, conductor ideals and ray class characters. This auxiliary representation supplies a determinant. It is **not** required to lift the residual representation of the given \(r\).

### The trace domain

Lemma 2.2 chooses a reduced horizontal fixed-determinant framed component \(B\) through \(r\). The real local orbit has dimension two. The relative relation estimate contributes at least three, since an invariant of the cyclotomic adjoint twist would give \(r\simeq r\otimes\varepsilon\), contradicting determinants. Passing from the characteristic-zero coefficient point back to an integral horizontal component gives
\[
 \dim B\ge6.
\]
Its closed trace image \(D\) is a complete local domain with the original coefficient point. Forgetting one frame loses at most three dimensions, giving \(\dim D\ge3\).

The family has a point whose algebraic monodromy is not virtually solvable. The proof bounds the bad trace loci: the reducible locus is proper, the finitely many quadratic-induced loci have integral dimension at most two, and the exceptional finite-projective cases have dimension at most one. For the last bound, \(A_4,S_4,A_5\) have exponent dividing 60, so finitely many trace generators satisfy explicit monic polynomials at fixed determinant. Coefficient-point density gives a point outside those loci. Its connected nonsolvable monodromy persists under any finite-index restriction, ensuring generic absolute irreducibility after base change.

### Potential Hecke support without a geometric original point

The paper does not directly invoke the companion's potential pro-modularity theorem for a regular de Rham target on the given nongeometric family. Proposition 2.3 instead uses that companion's curve, connectedness and localized propagation results with the determinant and residue specified separately.

A second modular seed lifts the actual semisimple residue, including character sums and scalar residue. Field preparation gives totally real abelian \(2\)-extensions
\(\mathbb Q\subset A\subset F_0\), completely split at two, with growing degrees but bounded numbers of allowed nondyadic primes. When the residue is globally reducible and locally scalar, local invariant lines are marked over one dyadic place of \(A\). Their number \(m\) is large while \(m/[F_0:\mathbb Q]\) is small.

The seed Hecke locus has dimension at least \(1+2[A:\mathbb Q]\), and an unrestricted trace locus containing the family has dimension at least \(2[A:\mathbb Q]-2\). Imposing characteristic two, finitely many Frobenius traces, and the one local scalar condition leaves enough dimension to avoid reducible, dihedral and finite-projective loci. The resulting curves have nonsolvable generic monodromy and finite allowed bad local images.

For marked curves, the lattice generated by the orbit of a vector on a local invariant line makes that vector primitive. Its reduced orbit spans, so the marked line avoids every global invariant line. Injectivity of residual extension restriction preserves this transversality, including the nonzero additive extension required in the scalar case. The imported connectedness lemmas then connect charts through large intersections. The key dimension condition is
\[
 2d-2m-4-u_0>d.
\]
Each intersection supplies another curve to which localized propagation applies. Finally propagation is applied to the **unrestricted** family locus. The marks select and connect characteristic-two curves without imposing extra local conditions on \(B\).

A finite common compositum and deeper tame level give one totally real solvable Galois field \(F\), of even degree and completely split at two, on whose completed definite-form Hecke support the whole restricted family lies.

## 3. From a product of local groups to one factor

The closed restricted trace image \(D_F\) makes \(D\) finite over the Hecke algebra upstairs. For \(n=[F:\mathbb Q]\), the monic trace-of-powers recurrence gives
\[
 P_n(T(g),\chi(g))=T(g^n)\in D_F.
\]
Finitely many topological trace generators are therefore integral over \(D_F\), and compactness makes their finite image closed.

Because two splits completely, all \(b=[F:\mathbb Q]\) dyadic restrictions have the same determinant-law parameter over \(D\). The action of the tensor product of their local pseudodeformation rings factors through multiplication to one ring \(R\). The companion's local-global block comparison consequently makes \(D\) finite over that single \(R\).

Let \(P\) be the block projective generator and \(\mathcal E=\operatorname{End}(P)\). Proposition 2.4 produces a nonzero finite \(D\)-torsion-free module \(L\) with commuting \(\mathcal E^{\widehat\otimes b}\)-action. Its inverse under the product block equivalence is admissible. The desired one-factor object is
\[
 V=L\widehat\otimes_{\mathcal E}P,
\]
using the first algebra action. This construction forgets factors on the **algebra module** and reconstructs a representation. It is not a bare assertion that restricting an admissible product representation to one group preserves admissibility.

The Paškūnas–Tung input includes scalar residual blocks, finiteness of \(\mathcal E\) over \(R\), exact compact equivalence, the noncharacter corner's integral torsion-free Cayley–Hamilton comparison, and the classification of coefficient Banach simples. At two the integral center may differ from \(R\) by a cokernel killed by two. The proof does not identify them integrally.

For a small uniform \(K\subset\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z_2)\), put
\[
 C_K=P/(\varpi,K-1)P.
\]
It is finite over \(R\), and coinvariants commute with the required finite coefficient changes. On a nonconstant characteristic-two curve, the noncharacter corner idempotent is full. The complementary character quotient is finite over \(\mathcal O\), since the determinant action on squares is fixed and \(\mathbb Q_2^\times/(\mathbb Q_2^\times)^2\) is finite, so that quotient has no generic support on such a curve.

The essential curve assertion is that a nonzero \(C_{K,H}\) survives tensoring with every simple right \(\mathcal E_H\)-module. An irreducible parameter has one simple. Repeated characters cannot occur on a nonconstant curve with fixed constant determinant in characteristic two. For distinct characters, the paper constructs both ordered principal-series families over a mixed-characteristic ring \(\mathcal O'[[X,Y]]\), proves they are finite free on the algebra side, and fixes their two corner character labels by density before reducing to the curve. It does not assume torsion-free quotient formation commutes with reduction modulo two.

On determinant-one stabilizers the two inducing characters are inverse. Since \(a-1\) and \(a^{-1}-1\) generate the same ideal, their \(K\)-coinvariants vanish simultaneously. At least one simple detects a nonzero finite module's semisimple head, hence both do.

If \(V/(\varpi,K-1)\) had positive-dimensional support, its support would contain a nonconstant curve. Uniform survival would propagate nonvanishing through every remaining factor, because their parameters are identical. This contradicts finite-dimensional product coinvariants. Compact Nakayama yields the asserted one-factor admissibility.

## 4. Growth forces an algebraic vector of a specific smooth type

For \(\mathscr A=\mathcal O[[K]]\), sufficiently deep \(K\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{gr}\mathscr A\simeq k[X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3].
\]
The growth degree \(g(M)\) of \(\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O}(M/\mathfrak a^nM)\) is at most four. Artin–Rees proves that quotienting by an injective endomorphism with nonzero cokernel lowers this degree by at least one. A torsion module over the Ore domain \(\mathscr A\) has degree at most three, by a finite filtration with cyclic torsion quotients.

Choose a coefficient point \(y\) outside the finite exceptional local character-ratio loci \(1,\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{-1}\). Finiteness over \(R\) bounds their inverse images by integral dimension one. A saturated chain of primes from \(0\) to the coefficient prime has length \(\dim D-1\). At each step the algebra module is specialized and its new domain torsion removed, retaining a nonzero fiber. Exact block equivalence transfers the needed injections and quotient maps to compact objects.

The last coefficient fiber has infinite-dimensional Banach simples and is \(\varpi\)-torsion-free, so its growth is at least two. Therefore
\[
 4\ge g(V)\ge\dim D+1\ge4.
\]
Thus \(V\) has positive Iwasawa rank.

The chosen smooth type is the inflated sign character
\[
 \sigma:\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z_2)
 \to\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_2)\simeq S_3\to\{\pm1\}.
\]
Its nontrivial action on the finite upper unipotent group makes it a cuspidal finite-group type. Positive rank gives a nonzero continuous map from functions with the prescribed central character to the Banach dual \(\Pi_V\). Polynomial approximation on the compact quotient by scalar units makes algebraic matrix coefficients dense. Multiplying by the nowhere-zero \(\sigma\) preserves density. Hence for some
\[
 U=\operatorname{Sym}^n(E^2)\otimes\det^a,\qquad
 n\ge0,\quad n+2a=w+1,
\]
the finite-dimensional space \(\operatorname{Hom}_{K_0}(\sigma\otimes U,\Pi_V)\) is nonzero. Negative determinant powers are allowed.

This type excludes every reducible local Galois parameter in the Banach classification. An upper unipotent matrix acts as minus a unipotent on the type, making \(u-1\) invertible. Evaluation at a principal-series base point would require a \(u\)-invariant functional, so it vanishes. Characters are excluded too. For continuous Steinberg, the pulled-back finite-dimensional extension splits over \(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z_2)\), using Lie-algebra semisimplicity and finite averaging, reducing to the same exclusion.

A common \(D\)-eigenvector gives an integral coefficient point \(x\). The corresponding finite-length Banach eigenspace has a simple containing the type. No exactness of the locally algebraic vector functor is claimed. Its local parameter is absolutely irreducible, so the global determinant is represented by an absolutely irreducible \(r_x\).

Uniqueness of the Banach simple with this parameter transports the type to every dyadic factor upstairs. Classical comparison and Jacquet–Langlands give a regular Hilbert packet of weight \(n+2\), with a common twist. The depth-zero type theorem and depth preservation in local Langlands make its Weil–Deligne parameter trivial on wild inertia. Complete splitting at two descends these local properties to \(\mathbb Q_2\). Only now does the companion's regular Fontaine–Mazur theorem apply to \(r_x\), yielding classical cuspidal modularity up to Tate twist.

## 5. The precise Hecke algebra accommodates levels and twists

Section 5 uses the companion dimension paper for reducedness, complete local factorization, classical density, the global determinant, four-dimensional irreducible components, and bounds on bounded-weight and Eisenstein closures. These imply density of classical cuspidal points of weight at least three on each component.

A form whose level divides \(2^aN\) must still give a continuous point of the algebra defined at odd level \(N\). Proposition 5.1 proves this on a common ordinary formal-group trivialization tower, including cusps. Tame forms evaluate using the standard multiplicative differential. At two, the Hasse invariant and \(E_4\), together with a lifted form of weight \(4m+1\), supply a trivialization of the Hodge line on the ordinary base.

The tower ring is topologically generated by the coefficients of the universal formal-group isomorphism and the inverse linear coefficient. After inverting two, formal logarithms express each coefficient separately in terms of the linear one. Positive-weight forms have dense rational span on the tower. The proof requires no uniform bound on denominators for all logarithm coefficients.

Roots of unity and the trivialization supply extra Drinfeld \(2\)-power level. Good Hecke correspondences, with \(\ell^{-1}\) a unit, preserve the integral unit ball. Any integral polynomial in the generators is therefore a contraction. Density turns convergence on every bounded weight range into convergence on a fixed higher-dyadic-level eigenform, proving the required continuity.

Finite-order cyclotomic twists preserve the algebra by classical twisting, the preceding level result, density and reducedness. For arbitrary continuous twists, the universal character sends \(5\) to \(1+X\) and \(-1\) to a fixed sign. Every relation vanishes at \(X=\zeta-1\) for all \(2\)-power roots of unity. Weierstrass preparation forces its bounded integral power series to vanish identically. Specialization at \(X=\theta(5)-1\) gives every continuous character, including all integer Tate twists, at the same tame level.

The paper also converts the companion's dual normalization explicitly:
\[
 V_f^\vee\simeq V_g\otimes\varepsilon^{1-k},
\]
with \(g\) the appropriate nebentype twist. Thus the auxiliary classical point has exactly the Frobenius normalization required in §1.

## 6. Full adjoint cohomology and propagation to the original point

The final tangent calculation uses the full four-dimensional adjoint \(W=\operatorname{End}(r_x)\), not just trace-zero endomorphisms. Newton–Thorne gives \(H^1_f(\mathbb Q,W)=0\) for the regular cuspidal representation when it is non-CM, or when its CM field is not contained in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^\infty})\).

The depth-zero type was chosen partly to ensure this qualification. Any relevant quadratic subfield of that cyclotomic extension gives a wildly ramified quadratic self-twist at two. A Weil–Deligne parameter trivial on wild inertia cannot be isomorphic to that twist, since a wild inertia element would act as \(I\) on one and \(-I\) on the other.

Local irreducibility excludes \(r_x\simeq r_x(1)\) at two, so \(H^2(\mathbb Q_2,W)=0\). Away from two, local-global compatibility and genericity give the corresponding vanishing. The nondyadic quotient \(H^1/H^1_f\) is zero. At two,
\[
 h^0=1,\quad h^1=5,\quad h^1_f=2,
\]
using distinct de Rham weights and the Bloch–Kato dimension formula. The global finite Selmer vanishing therefore yields
\[
 \dim H^1(G_{\mathbb Q,S},\operatorname{ad}r_x)\le3.
\]
The characteristic-zero tangent comparison bounds the embedding dimension of the **varying-determinant** global pseudodeformation ring at \(x\) by three, without restrictions on the semisimplified residue.

The same ring surjects onto both the fixed-determinant domain \(D\) and the appropriate full Hecke factor \(A\). Every component of \(A\) has integral dimension four. A coefficient-point quotient has dimension one, so catenarity gives local dimension three at \(x\). Thus
\[
 3\le\dim A_x\le\dim R_x\le\operatorname{edim}R_x\le3.
\]
The ambient local ring is regular of dimension three, and its same-dimensional Hecke quotient has zero kernel there.

For any global Hecke-kernel element \(a\), some \(s\notin x\) satisfies \(sa=0\). Its image in the domain \(D\) has nonzero \(s\), forcing the image of \(a\) to vanish. Hence the entire \(D\) factors through Hecke support. Merely sharing one classical point would not give this conclusion without the dimension and tangent comparison.

The original normalized coefficient point now lies on the required Hecke algebra. The continuous twist is undone. Although intermediate coefficients lie in a finite extension, the generator values are the original traces and determinants in \(\mathcal O_E\). Topological generation and closedness of \(\mathcal O_E\) descend the whole map to the original coefficient ring.
