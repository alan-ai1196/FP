## 1. Original question and exact claimed result

The theorem claims that every finite-order Hecke L-function over \(F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\), and every Dirichlet L-function, is nonzero on the open half-plane
\[
\operatorname{Re}s>\frac78,
\]
with the pole of a principal function at \(s=1\) allowed. The assertion includes the Riemann zeta-function. It is a fixed zero-free half-plane, rather than a region whose width shrinks with height or conductor. It does not assert nonvanishing on the boundary \(\operatorname{Re}s=7/8\), and does not prove RH or GRH.

The argument first obtains \(11/12\) using balanced scales, then improves the boundary to \(7/8\) with an asymmetric, compensated probe. The full Hecke family is used even when the eventual target is zeta: character twists generated inside the proof need to remain in the family. A real exceptional zero to the right of \(7/8\) would also be excluded by the claimed theorem. [§§1–3, 11–12, 20.]

The stated computational consequence is a polylogarithmic bound for the least quadratic nonresidue, with a bound of the form \((\log p)^{32}\), followed by deterministic polynomial-time square-root extraction over prime fields. This consequence imports the cited Bhargava–Ivanyos–Mittal–Saxena result under a weaker-than-GRH fixed-strip hypothesis. Searching for a nonresidue, testing by Euler's criterion, and then using Tonelli–Shanks supply the algorithmic route; the argument does not require factoring the odd part of \(p-1\). The cited paper's use was read, not its full independent proof. [§1.]

## 2. Logical core: one quantity, two bounds, continuation

Let \(\beta_*\) be the supremum of \(1/2\) and the real parts of zeros in the primitive finite-order Hecke family. The continuation criterion assumes \(\beta_*>\sigma_0\), writes \(\Delta_0=\beta_*-\sigma_0\), and uses an affine exponent \(C(s)=s+c\).

For every target character \(\eta\), a common quantity \(J_\eta(Z)\) must satisfy a low estimate and approximate a Mellin signal \(f_\eta(Z)\):
\[
|J_\eta(Z)|\ll_\eta Z^{C(\sigma_0)+\omega},\qquad
|J_\eta(Z)-f_\eta(Z)|\ll_\eta Z^{C(\beta_*)-\sigma},
\]
where \(0<\omega<\Delta_0\) and \(\sigma>0\) are chosen independently of \(\eta\). The signal contains \(H_\eta(s)/L^S(s,\eta)\), a Gaussian Mellin weight, and \(Z^{C(s)}\). The correction \(H_\eta\) is holomorphic and nonvanishing in the relevant region.

The triangle inequality bounds the signal at large \(Z\); shifting right treats small \(Z\). Its Mellin transform is holomorphic farther left than the presumed rightmost-zero barrier. Fourier inversion and the identity theorem identify it with the reciprocal L-function expression, contradicting the definition of \(\beta_*\). This isolates the logical implication from the arithmetic estimates.

Target-dependent constants, finite excluded primes, derivative orders and lower thresholds are permitted. The positive exponent saving must remain common to the family. The physical quantity and signal cannot secretly change when an analysis cutoff changes. These quantifier requirements return in the final order-of-choices proposition. [§2.]

## 3. Arithmetic conventions and analytic bookkeeping

The ring is \(\mathbb Z[\omega]\), with \(\lambda=\sqrt{-3}\), norm \(q_a=|a|^2\), primary representatives, and sextic residue characters. The fixed excluded set \(S\) contains the ramified primes and all genuinely fixed conductor data.

Several conventions carry mathematical content:

- Every integer power of a zero-extended character remains zero at nonunits. Exponents \(0\), negative exponents, and exponents divisible by six do not remove its coprimality mask.
- A moving prime cannot be absorbed into a supposedly fixed modulus. Primitive conductor and the radical of deleted Euler factors are tracked separately.
- A common joint smooth profile is Fourier-separated before row enlargement or Minkowski. A different Fourier density for each row would not provide the asserted common norm estimate.
- Fixed internal Sobolev and derivative orders precede the external height cutoff and final tail order. An integrated tail estimate is not automatically a bound on a horizontal contour trace.

Kummer theory supplies finite-order ray characters for fixed numerators. The relevant fixed \(S\)-unit and valuation classes are finite. The Gauss-sum identities involve a quadratic refinement \(G\); its nonsquarefree extension is not itself a Gauss sum. Reciprocity phases are handled on a finite group of classes. Completed indices \(cn^3\) allow common prime factors of \(c\) and \(n\).

Analytic preparations include polynomial strip bounds with explicit conductor dependence, bounds for deleted Euler factors, and logarithmic L-function estimates inside a buffered zero-free region. The paper uses Borel–Carathéodory and three-circles arguments to obtain the needed subpower bounds. A Gaussian logarithmic profile admits a summable annular decomposition. [§4.]

## 4. Part I: reflection, the balanced probe, and its low side

The completed sum ranges over a squarefree factor \(c\) and cube factor \(n^3\), retaining their possible intersections. Its reflected support has the form \(u\lambda^k n b^3\), with \(k\ge-4\), and finitely many cusp and ray sectors. Local branches distinguish active character factors from inactive zero masks; the latter still contribute conductor-free restrictions.

The residual \(j=1\) sector reduces to a quadratic character sum. Factoring \(b=gt^2\) and resolving \(\gcd(n,g)\) leaves a mask involving the row and \(ct\). The full Möbius expansion of this mask precedes application of the quadratic large sieve. The paper then invokes Goldmakher–Louvel, with a norm factor of the shape \(K+NB\). [§5.]

For completed scale \(Z^N\), row ball \(Z^M\), and powerful row part of norm \(Z^O\), the resulting unmarked mean-square exponent is
\[
\frac O2+\max(M-O,\,2M-O-N)+\varepsilon.
\]
The actual residual annulus obeys an inequality relative to \(M-O\); equality is not assumed. At \(M=N=1\), summing the powerful parts gives \(Z^{1+\varepsilon}\).

An auxiliary primitive character supported on fixed arithmetic data suppresses forbidden Poisson frequencies, including zero. The planar additive mean-square bound has size \((Q+Y^2)/Y\), with \(Q=q_{b_*}XY\). Combining it with the completed row estimate gives

## 6. Zero detection, row counts and the \(11/12\) conclusion

The zero detector works with a finite family of twists \(\nu\chi_u^{\pm1}\). A finite-height pigeonhole construction gives buffered bins with left parameter \(a\), and \(\delta=2a-1\le2\beta_*-1\). A truncated inverse polynomial at length \(U^t\), \(1\le t\le3/2\), together with a gamma-weighted contour, produces two witnesses belonging to the same character and twist. If their lengths are \(U^r,U^m\), they satisfy, up to controlled small losses,
\[
r\le t,\qquad r+m\ge t,\qquad
|M_rS_m|^2\ge U^{\delta(r+m)}.
\]
Pointwise upper bounds force \(m\le1/2\), \(r\ge t-1/2\), and individual near-saturation. The truncation is not presented as a formal identity without a remainder; the residue, tail and Möbius cancellation are treated explicitly. [§8.]

The sextic large sieve has the three-term shape
\[
K+D+(KD)^{2/3}.
\]
Its coefficients must be common to the row family; arbitrary pair-dependent masks are outside its statement. The norm recursion retains primitive conductors and zero masks before Poisson summation. Powerful-row counting and the sieve lead to

R(\delta)=\min\{1,\max(1-\delta/2,\,4/3-\delta)\}.

The first stage uses the inverse witness at \(t=1\). [§9.]

High-side contour shifts are performed on the complete holomorphic correction before pointwise amplitude subdivision. Buffered bounds apply on their retained segments; global reciprocal bounds handle the discarded pieces. The principal residues, intermediate rows, small rows and far rows each receive explicit positive margins. Fixed cumulative height costs are not restarted independently at each step.

The first stage uses \(C_{\mathrm I}(s)=s-2/3\). Its late-height argument gives a target-independent high saving, with \(1/4800\) recorded in the construction. The continuation criterion then gives \(\beta_*\le11/12\). ## 7. Part II: asymmetric geometry and physical compensation

Assume for contradiction
\[
\Delta=\beta_*-7/8\in(0,1/24],\qquad
\kappa=2\beta_*-1=3/4+2\Delta\le5/6.
\]
The second-stage exponent is \(C_{\mathrm{II}}(s)=s-11/16\), so \(C_{\mathrm{II}}(7/8)=3/16\). The geometry uses
\[
h=13/16,\quad \ell=1/6,\quad
l_x=17/48,\quad l_y=23/48,\quad M=5/6,\quad M+\ell=1.
\]

The physical prime slots have disjoint positive windows and total logarithmic length \(\ell\). Windows are fixed before ray restrictions and moving row masks. At each selected prime, the construction subtracts a rescaled unmarked probe from a marked probe:
\[
\overline{\eta(p)}\,I_{\mathrm{marked}\ p}(X,Y,Zq_p)
-q_p^{-3/2}I(X/q_p,Y/q_p,Z).
\]
Composing these operations gives a finite sum over subsets. A mark means \(p\mid cn^3\), not merely \(p\mid c\); every window stays at its original physical scale throughout the subset expansion. [§12.]

Complete finite Fourier correlations are evaluated with their actual common prime powers. A gcd-only simplification would miss some correlations. When a formula is extended outside coprime support, the artificial extension is explicitly specified and a full Möbius indicator is restored before factorwise estimates. [§13.]

Marked reflection separates an inactive \(q_p^{-1}\) term from an active term with \(q_p^{-1/2}\) and a zero-extended twist. The hybrid quadratic–cubic norm has shape
\[
(K+NB)B\{N+L+(NL)^{2/3}\},
\]
with stated independence conditions on row, tuple and dual coefficients. The reflected energy keeps powerful parts, supported row primes, Ramanujan terms and assignments to the squarefree and cube ideals distinct. Its bound is applied before enlarging positive row sums, so a saving from common support is not counted twice. [§14.]

A second Poisson calculation improves the additive Gram estimate to
\[
\sum_m|A_m|^2
\ll \frac Q{Y'}\left(1+P_a^{1/6}+\frac{P_a^2}{Y'}\right)Z^\varepsilon,
\qquad P_a=Y'^2/Q.
\]
The special coefficient structure forces the relevant nonzero frequencies to be sixth powers away from fixed or common support. This is not an estimate for arbitrary row coefficients. At the chosen geometry the last term is dominated, and the rescaled-subset costs sum favorably. The resulting modified low bound is \(Z^{3/16+\varepsilon}\). [§15.]

## 8. Holomorphic compensation and conductor savings

The raw local quotient involves \(P_p^*/P_p\), which is unsuitable at zeros of \(P_p\). The paper instead constructs a quotient-free factor \(G_p\), with no denominator \(P_p\) or \(1-W\). The full finite tuple correction built from \(G_p\) and unselected \(H_p\) is holomorphic on the required regions and has all-height bounds. Its equality with the physical Poisson expression is proved.

Only on the retained dynamic region is \(H_p\) bounded away from zero. There one may use \(B_p=G_p/H_p\) and split it into a prime-character main term and an error. This local split cannot replace the full holomorphic expression when moving contours.

Dynamic error bounds are taken jointly with the numerator. At a prime dividing a row to higher order, the inducing conductor has a deficit relative to the full row norm. Each distinct selected prime uses its own available deficit once; there is no fresh numerator allowance for every slot. At the principal residue, \(B_p=-1+O(q_p^{-7/8})\). Away from the dynamic region, absolute bounds return to the quotient-free expression. [§16.]

## 9. The inverse second moment

The marked inverse moment concerns rows up to \(Z^m\), inverse length \(Z^r\), and independently weighted disjoint prime slots of total length \(z\). With fixed positive margins, its principal restrictions are
\[
r+2z\le m-c_1,\qquad 2r+8z\le3m-c_2,
\]
and the mean square is bounded by \(Z^{m+\varepsilon}\). The slot weights are products of individual bounded prime weights, independent of the row. Either common character orientation is allowed. This lemma has no fine-mesh condition.

Its proof works with a canonical family whose coefficients include the inverse/Gauss phase, a fixed puncture and a squarefree averaged label. The label weight is divisor-bounded and depends only on that label. There is no license to insert an arbitrary extra coefficient depending on the residual inverse variable.

Short completed cases terminate by reflection. In the remaining cases, two Poisson transforms return a smaller-row canonical family. Exact local phase calculations preserve the zero masks, while divisor-fiber bounds justify removing an old averaged label. New labels remain averaged; freezing them all as fixed arithmetic would destroy the normalization argument.

The retained source projection is kept throughout the signed calculation. Eligibility is not redefined from an enlarged containing ball. Child row lengths decrease by a fixed amount, and the normalization identity balances row, label and scalar exponents. A finite backward induction chooses tolerance budgets and common smooth-profile orders before the final height orders. Raw tails are removed before artificial off-coprime extensions.

A separate sixth-power amplification gives, for sixth-power-free rows near \(U\),
\[
\sum |M(U^r)|^2\ll U^{e(r)+\varepsilon},\qquad
e(r)=\max\{1,(1+5r)/6\}.
\]
It uses the injectivity of multiplication by sixth powers even when the factors share primes, together with the exact column Möbius identity. Rowwise smooth-profile parameters are allowed under fixed height control; arbitrary row-dependent prime coefficients are not. [§17, pp. 114–151.]

## 10. The plain fourth moment

Write \(m\) for row length, \(q\) for displayed moving-twist radical length, and \(M=m+q\) for width. The common puncture is retained even when algebraic character powers cancel.

The paper bounds the mean square of a product of two plain sums and prime-slot factors by \(Z^{M+\varepsilon}\). With positive total prime length \(z\), the principal length condition is
\[
n_1+n_2+6\kappa z\le M.
\]
Rows must have inducing characters outside the fixed finite family \(\Theta\) determined by the slot coefficient components. For \(\kappa<1\), the theorem assumes \(\beta_*\le(1+\kappa)/2\); \(\kappa=1\) is the unconditional range. The zero-slot case permits unrestricted plain lengths for nonprincipal inducing characters. The fine mesh is chosen from the desired losses and bounded real ranges before the fixed number of slots; eventual derivative orders and thresholds may depend on that number.

Functional-equation reflection retains natural zeros and treats redundant Euler factors explicitly. The induction first establishes every zero-slot width band. Within positive-slot bands, an uncentered range precedes a centered range.

Centering subtracts two rectangles with equal product length and shared profiles, character, masks and norm powers. Their complete coefficient difference is retained through both transforms. Taking absolute values separately at the outset would lose the cancellation on exceptional rows.

A sixth-power prime amplifier is separated from the physical slots. Its column valuation errors are tracked and are not amplified again. The second full correlation extracts complete common support, not only a gcd. A scalar bound valid on one unit/nonunit partition is extended by zero outside that partition. The resulting child width strictly decreases.

For nonexceptional child rows, the comparison rectangles can be separated and whole prime prefixes clipped to restore the moment condition. When every slot disappears, the unrestricted zero-slot result is essential. For exceptional inducing characters in \(\Theta\), a sixth-power row parametrization and masked lattice asymptotics replace the generic moment bound. Their leading terms cancel because the two rectangles have equal products. The common coefficient of the leading lattice term is independent of scale and height after the mask expansion.

Finite width descent closes the estimate. Terminal losses occur once along a path, and the aggregate support enlargement is controlled as a whole rather than charged as a fresh epsilon at every generation. [§18, pp. 152–179.]

## 11. From moments to the final exponent

Physical prime amplitudes are binned by exponents \(g_i\in[0,\delta/2]\). Their weighted mean is denoted \(q\) in this section; it is not the moving-conductor width variable used in §18. Zero capacity and the neighborhood of \(r=1\) use unweighted estimates instead of a marked estimate with a disappearing strict margin.

For row norm \(U=Z^d\), the ideal high exponent relative to \(C_{\mathrm{II}}(7/8)\) is

The \(q/6\) term is in base \(Z\); it is not \(dq/6\). The floor bin, intermediate rows and far rows have separate bounds. Small rows receive a saving \(63/800\) relative to \(C(\beta_*)\); the far-row absolute line is selected only after fixing the moderate-range extension.

## 12. Final quantifiers, dependencies and limits

The last proposition fixes real geometry, loss allocations, capacities, slot mesh, amplitude bins, bin width and frequency extension before the target character. It then fixes the target's arithmetic data and internal seminorm orders. Only afterward are a small height exponent \(\tau_\eta\), an external tail order \(N_\eta\), and a lower threshold chosen.

The resulting estimate has the form
\[
|J_{\mathrm{II},\eta}-f_{\mathrm{II},\eta}|
\ll Z^{C(\beta_*)-m}(1+T_1)^{A_\eta}
+Z^{B_\eta}T_1^{-N},\qquad T_1=Z^{\tau_\eta}.
\]
Here the retained height degree \(A_\eta\) and discarded scale degree \(B_\eta\) do not grow with the later external derivative order. Detector ceiling conditions are checked for all moderate rows. The late-height lemma absorbs the first factor, then chooses the tail order, giving a common high saving \(\sigma=m/2>0\). The low allowance is \(\omega=\Delta/2\).

The same continuation criterion now contradicts \(\beta_*>7/8\). The earlier Hecke-to-Dirichlet transfer gives the claimed full theorem. [§20.6.]
