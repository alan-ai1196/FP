## Main result and computational model

All polynomials are formal, commutative polynomials over a field F. They are not merely functions on the finite set of field elements. A circuit has binary addition, subtraction and multiplication gates and counts operation gates. Intermediate results may be reused and intermediate degrees are unrestricted. A formula is a tree of gate occurrences. An algebraic branching program, ABP, is a directed acyclic graph summing products of affine edge labels over source–sink paths, with size counting both vertices and edges.

The paper permits arbitrary affine forms at input leaves free of charge. For polynomially bounded arity this preserves the standard polynomial-size classes. Constants are unrestricted field elements. Neither monotonicity, syntactic multilinearity nor constant depth is assumed.

For a finite loopless multigraph G=(V,E), assign a separate variable X_(v,α) to every local tuple α of q-valued labels on the edges incident to v. Its state sum is

\[
Z_{G,q}(X)=\sum_{a\in[q]^E}\prod_{v\in V}X_{v,a|_{E(v)}}.
\]

It is homogeneous of degree |V| and set-multilinear in the vertex tables, even though a computation for it need not preserve either property internally. The state of an edge is an index in [q]. It is not the persistent state of a learner.

Theorem 1.1 states, over every field,

\[
\mathsf{VF}_F\subsetneq\mathsf{VBP}_F
\subsetneq\mathsf{VP}_F\subsetneq\mathsf{VNP}_F.
\]

With n labels per edge, the explicit witnesses are:

| Graph | Exact upper class | Lower model and bound |
|---|---|---|
| n-by-n square grid | VNP | Circuit size n^(Ω((log n)^(1/12))) |
| Complete binary tree with floor(log2(2n)) levels | VP | ABP size n^(Ω(sqrt(log log n))) |
| Path on n vertices | VBP | Formula size n^(Ω(log log n)) |

The order-n determinant also has formula size at least n^(Ω(log log n)). All four stated lower bounds hold in the degree-component border sense described below. These are arithmetic-model separations. No Boolean P-versus-NP conclusion or neural-architecture separation is established here.

The upper-class memberships are constructive. The grid's label choices are encoded by Boolean strings. Indicator polynomials select each legal local table entry and kill unused binary encodings, in every characteristic. Summing the resulting polynomial-size computation gives the VNP representation. A binary tree has an O(dq³) bottom-up circuit because each child contribution is reused across parent entries. A path is an entry of a product of matrices and has a layered ABP with q edge-label states.

## Degree-component border complexity and projection

For a homogeneous degree-D target, border size at most s means that its coefficient vector lies in the Zariski closure over the algebraic closure of F of the degree-D components of outputs of size at most s. Approximating outputs may contain arbitrary other degrees.

This is stronger than an exact lower bound. It does not assert a lower bound for bounded norm error, probabilistic error, noisy observations or an arbitrary analytic model.

An affine substitution can lower degree. Lemma 2.1 therefore first extracts the target degree from each approximating output, substitutes, and only then reads the desired degree component. The overhead is O((s+1)(D+1)²) for circuits and O((s+1)²) for formulas or ABPs. Circuit homogenization computes all components through D. Formula and ABP outputs have degree at most s+1, so interpolation in a scaling parameter over the algebraic closure suffices.

The transformed coefficient map is linear, which preserves the required containment in a Zariski closure. This overhead is explicitly included when a large graph is projected to a small witness.

Subgraphs and smaller alphabets are obtained by pinning unused edges to label one, setting inconsistent entries to zero, retaining the desired table variables and setting deleted vertex factors to one. Each retained labeling has exactly one surviving extension. The minor projection later requires additional alphabet capacity.

## One invariant for different low-rank cuts, §3

Extract the coefficients using exactly one variable from every vertex table. Split each table index into its individual incident edge labels. The resulting graph tensor A(Z) is a product of independent q-dimensional equality wires, one per edge.

Across a fixed vertex cut C, its matrix rank is q^|∂C|. Different terms in a circuit decomposition can be low rank across different cuts, so one fixed flattening does not suffice.

The paper imports the compatible Euclidean spectral-price theorem, ordinary-sum inequality and Zariski-closed sublevels from [OAI-1124-722](OAI-1124-722.md), now fully read separately. Each edge receives its own Euclidean axis and its two ends are placed at opposite unit vectors. With e edges and w=log2 q, the target price is exactly 2ew.

Let a_v be the sum of the terminal vectors belonging to vertex v. For a set C,

\[
\left\|\sum_{v\in C}a_v\right\|^2=|\partial C|.
\]

Internal edge endpoints cancel and crossing edges occupy orthogonal axes. Moving the supply site for C a small distance in the direction of this sum saves outgoing-wire length to first order. A rank-R factorization across C is supplied by an R-valued equality label between two auxiliary sites. Its price is the separation of those sites times log2 R.

Lemma 3.2 applies when 2≤d≤w^(1/3), 1≤e≤2d and each of at most 2^d summands has rank at most R across some cut with at least b crossing edges. It gives

\[
\log_2R\ge \tfrac12w\sqrt b.
\]

If this failed, moving by λ=sqrt(b)/(4e) would save at least wb/(16e) on every summand. The common terminal set lies in the unit ball and has mean width O(sqrt(log(2d))). Adding 2^d terms costs only O(d sqrt(log(2d))), which is dominated in the stated range. Closedness excludes their algebraic border as well.

The auxiliary supply sites may differ between terms. The output terminals and the spectral invariant are common. Figures 1–2 show why the allowed cuts depend on the arithmetic model and how a low-rank summand is transported.

## Grids separate circuits from VNP, §4

Lemma 4.2 homogenizes a size-s circuit through degree d and processes gates in reverse topological order. It accumulates multipliers at shared gates before proceeding. At a product above degree 2d/3 it follows one larger-degree input and incorporates the other in the multiplier. The retained factors have degrees strictly above d/3 and at most 2d/3.

Extracting one variable per vertex table produces a sum indexed by balanced vertex subsets U. There are at most 2^d terms and each has rank at most C(s+1)(d+1)² across its own U cut. This argument allows arbitrary cancellations and reused gates. It does not rely on taking a derivative of a square, so the stated construction also covers characteristic two.

Every balanced partition of an r-by-r grid crosses at least ceil(r/3) edges. Either enough rows are mixed, supplying horizontal crossing edges, or both colors occupy complete rows, supplying a vertical crossing in every column.

Applying the common-price lemma gives degree-component border circuit size at least q^(Ω(sqrt(r))) when r²≤(log q)^(1/3). For the n-by-n grid with q=n, retain an r-by-r subgrid with r=floor((log n)^(1/6)). The original degree is n², so the projection lemma charges a polynomial n overhead. The lower exponent grows beyond every constant, leaving n^(Ω((log n)^(1/12))).

The proof uses large q relative to the small extracted witness. It is not a new fixed-q quantitative lower bound for every growing grid size.

## Binary trees separate ABPs from circuits, §5

A balanced cut of a binary tree can cross only one edge, so the grid argument alone cannot yield the needed gain. The ABP path expansion allows a stronger choice: prescribe the number k of vertex slots consumed before cutting a path.

For each U of size k and each ABP vertex v, collect prefixes whose kth selected variable lies on their final edge and complementary suffixes starting at v. The final selected variable makes the cut unique even when constant labels occur afterward. The prefix and suffix arrays form an outer product. Summing over at most s vertices gives rank at most s across U, with at most binomial(d,k)≤2^d terms.

The tree boundary argument chooses k depending only on the number h of levels. If U has few crossing edges, its cardinality can be written as a root-membership term plus a short signed sum of subtree sizes 2^j−1. At most 2 binomial(2(h−1)+u,u) cardinalities can arise with u crossing edges. For u a sufficiently small constant times h, this does not cover all nontrivial cardinalities. Some k therefore forces Ω(h) boundary edges for every k-vertex set.

The common-price lemma yields q^(Ω(sqrt(h))) ABP size when 2^h−1≤(log q)^(1/3). Projecting the taller n-label tree to h0=floor((log log n)/3) levels and paying the quadratic border-projection overhead gives the stated n^(Ω(sqrt(log log n))) bound.

Because determinants of affine matrices have polynomial-size ABPs over every field, the same argument also lower-bounds the order of a determinantal representation of this tree family in the stated border sense.

## Paths separate formulas from ABPs, §6

A path has a prefix with just one crossing edge at every nontrivial cardinality. The formula proof therefore charges nested splits at many scales.

Lemma 6.2 expands coefficient extraction down to the first product gate where both children contribute variables. It groups terms by an ordered binary split tree on the slots, a leaf labeling, and the distance ℓ_s traveled to each chosen product occurrence. There are at most

\[
4^d d!(h+1)^{d-1}
\]

groups for depth h. A group is a local image of independent diagonal labels shared among descendants B_s, with at most h address bits along each root-to-leaf chain:

\[
\sum_{s:v\in B_s}\ell_s\le h.
\]

Constant terms of skipped siblings and subtraction signs are assigned exactly once to designated leaves. The shared addresses select product occurrences. This is an exact coefficient identity, not an assumption that the original formula is set-multilinear.

For each split tree, Lemma 6.3 places leaf supply sites z_v in the unit ball so that

\[
\sum_v\langle a_v,z_v\rangle\ge c_0\log d,
\qquad
\delta(\{z_v:v\in B_s\})\le C_0|B_s|/d.
\]

At each dyadic scale, maximal small subtrees are clustered in leaf order. Every proper cluster has a nonzero boundary vector because the original graph is connected. Moving along its normalized boundary vector produces a gain at that scale. For any one subtree, all sufficiently coarse-scale moves are common translations and do not enlarge its mean width. Fine-scale width contributions form a convergent dyadic sum. Figure 3 shows the same descendant set at two scales.

At sites λz_v, the terminal delivery cost gains order wλlog d and loses at most ewλ². The common-address price is at most C0λh by averaging the root-to-leaf address budgets. If h is a small multiple of wlog d, taking λ proportional to (log d)/d leaves a saving of order w(log d)²/d.

The price penalty for the grouped summands is O(d sqrt(log(2d)) log(d(h+2))). It is smaller than this saving for 2≤d≤w^(1/3). Closedness gives depth at least c'wlog d for border binary circuits. Unfolding preserves depth, and balancing a formula to O(log(size+2)) depth gives formula size at least q^(c log d).

The theorem applies to every tree in this short-size range. For paths, recursive bisection gives q^(O(log d)), so the exponent matches up to constants. Projection extends the lower bound to d≤(log q)^B for any fixed B, with a B-dependent constant.

For d=q=n, project to the first floor((log n)^(1/3)) path vertices. This proves the formula-versus-ABP separation. To transfer it to the determinant, use a short layered ABP's strictly upper-triangular weighted adjacency matrix A. The path polynomial is the source–sink entry of (I−A)^−1, hence a signed cofactor because det(I−A)=1. Choosing q=floor(sqrt(n)), taking a short path and padding with an identity block realizes it as an affine projection of the order-n determinant.

## Classification of arbitrary graph families, §7

Theorem 7.1 fixes a field and a class of nonempty finite loopless multigraphs. It requires one polynomial in d+q to bound all state sums in the family for every q≥2. Under this uniform quantifier:

| Model | Necessary and sufficient bounded parameters |
|---|---|
| Formula | Maximum connected-component order and maximum degree |
| ABP | Cutwidth |
| Circuit | Treewidth and maximum degree |

For sequences G_n with polynomially bounded vertex count, the same criteria characterize polynomial size of Z_(G_n,n). Exact computations give sufficiency. The necessary conditions already apply to degree-component border size.

Maximum degree cannot be omitted. Two vertices with t parallel edges have treewidth one and component order two, but their state sum is a dot product of q^t entries. More generally, flattening across a degree-t vertex has q^t disjoint nonempty row supports. Its rank is q^t.

This still obstructs small computations with free affine inputs. The output coefficients linear in that vertex's variables lie in the span of the linear parts of the O(s+1) affine inputs. Rank is therefore O(s+1). Vanishing minors preserve this constraint in the border. For ABPs, degree is already bounded by twice cutwidth.

The exact upper constructions retain explicit bounds. Enumerating bounded-size connected components gives formula size O((d+1)q^(Δm/2)). Processing vertices in cutwidth-k order gives at most dq^(k+Δ) ABP transitions. A tree decomposition of width k gives circuit size O((d+1)q^(Δ(k+1))) by maintaining boundary-edge labels and reusing child tables.

For necessity, the minor-projection lemma routes labels inside each branch set to a weighted centroid. A tree edge needs to carry at most floor(Δ(H)/2) labels for the minor H. Thus Z_(H,q) projects from Z_(G,n) when

\[
q^{\max\{1,\lfloor\Delta(H)/2\rfloor\}}\le n.
\]

Canonical tuple propagation gives one surviving host labeling per minor labeling. Paths and binary trees retain q=n, while grids may use q=floor(sqrt(n)).

Once degree is bounded, unbounded component size forces arbitrarily long paths. Unbounded cutwidth forces unbounded pathwidth and, by the excluded-forest theorem, arbitrarily large binary-tree minors. Unbounded treewidth forces arbitrarily large grid minors. Fix a witness large enough to beat any proposed polynomial exponent, then take the alphabet sufficiently large along the corresponding subsequence. This order of quantifiers is essential.

The classification concerns independent formal variables in each vertex table and size measured in d+q. It does not classify the complexity of every numerical specialization, shared-parameter tensor network, fixed-alphabet model or probabilistic graph model.

### Applicability to FP

No source-backed reduction identifies FP, an attention model, a recurrent learner or a paid-observation process with one of the arithmetic classes under this formal-input and exact-output model. A graph's q-valued edge labels, an ABP's computational vertices and a physical learner's finite memory are different objects.

Any proposed transfer would have to specify the formal polynomial to be computed, how the learning or prediction task supplies its independent variables, why all permitted updates reduce to the target arithmetic model, how precision and auxiliary information are charged, and which approximation relation preserves the lower bound.
