## 1. Exact claims and their scope

The paper separates ordinary averages
\[
\frac1X\sum_{n\le X}a(n)
\]
from logarithmic averages. Its claimed advance concerns every terminal cutoff, rather than cancellation after averaging over scales or after excluding a sparse set of scales.

### Liouville: an absolute logarithmic exponent

Theorem 1.1 claims an absolute \(c>0\) such that, for every fixed
\[
a_1,a_2\ge1,\qquad b_1,b_2\ge0,\qquad a_1b_2-a_2b_1\ne0,
\]
\[
\left|\sum_{1\le n\le X}
\lambda(a_1n+b_1)\lambda(a_2n+b_2)\right|
\ll_{a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2}\frac{X}{(\log X)^c}
\]
for every real \(X\ge3\). Here \(\lambda(n)=(-1)^{\Omega(n)}\).

The exponent is independent of the fixed affine forms. The implied constants need not be effective, and the statement gives no uniformity when the coefficients grow with \(X\). There is no coprimality assumption on the coefficients. The intermediate Proposition 2.1 gives the same exponent for \(\lambda(n)\lambda(n+h)\) in every fixed residue class, including zero and nonunit classes.

### General complex multiplicative functions: qualitative cancellation

For a Dirichlet character \(\chi\), define
\[
D(f,\chi n^{it};N)^2
=\sum_{p\le N}\frac{1-\operatorname{Re}(f(p)\overline{\chi(p)p^{it}})}p.
\]
Uniform nonpretentiousness means that for every fixed \(\chi\),
\[
\inf_{|t|\le N}D(f,\chi n^{it};N)\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The same \(N\) is used for the prime cutoff and the twist-height bound. Divergence against each fixed \(t\) alone is not the stated hypothesis. Uniformity over a growing character modulus is not assumed.

Theorem 1.2 claims that if \(f_1,f_2\) are multiplicative and valued in the closed complex unit disk, and at least one has this property, then for fixed distinct nonnegative shifts,
\[
\frac1N\sum_{n\le N}f_1(n+h_1)f_2(n+h_2)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The functions need not be completely multiplicative or have modulus one. There is no conjugation in this product. A conjugated version follows by stability of the hypothesis. Part III extends this to every fixed progression and every fixed nonproportional affine pair, retaining the hypothesis on an original factor.

The general theorem has no quantitative rate. It applies to Liouville, Möbius and mixed pairs because their values at primes are the same. Only the Liouville-only route in Part I supplies the power-of-logarithm rate stated here.

### Prior results as described by the authors

The introduction distinguishes the corrected Elliott hypothesis from the original complex-function formulation, which allows a function to imitate different Archimedean twists at successive scales. It cites Matomäki–Radziwiłł short means and Matomäki–Radziwiłł–Tao averaged-shift and exponential-sum results. Tao's binary theorem and the Helfgott–Radziwiłł and Pilatte quantitative estimates retain logarithmic weighting. Tao–Teräväinen and Klurman–Mangerel–Teräväinen obtain ordinary cancellation on specified large sets of scales. The paper's own claim keeps every cutoff and fixes the affine coefficients.

These are the source's comparisons, not an independently completed priority survey. The graph arguments explicitly adapt earlier divisibility graphs, composite labels, prohibited paths and rank-truncated sieves.

## 2. Part I: prime supplies, padding and finite residue comparison

The quantitative proof uses
\[
L=(\log X)^{1/A},\quad \delta=1/200,\quad
J=\left\lfloor\frac{\delta\log L}{6W}\right\rfloor,\quad
\eta=e^{-J},\quad K=e^{4J}.
\]
First \(A\) is chosen for the bounded-independence comparison. The large absolute constant \(W\) is selected only after the graph constants are established.

There are \(J\) disjoint supplies \(P_i\) of primes congruent to one modulo five, in geometrically spaced bands of logarithms. Each has reciprocal mass \(V_i\in[W,2W]\), and every selected prime exceeds
\[
H_0=\exp(L^{1-\delta}).
\]
A tuple divisor \(d\) selects one prime from each supply, with \(\log d<2L\).

The disjoint padding set \(Q\) consists of the other prime classes up to \(e^L\), excluding primes dividing the fixed shift or modulus. Its squarefree divisors have weights
\[
u(q)=4^{\omega(q)},\quad
S=\prod_{p\in Q}(1+4/p),\quad
w(n)=\sum_{q\mid n}u(q)=5^{\omega_Q(n)}.
\]
Pairs \((d,q)\) are divided into bins of width \(\eta\) in \(\log(dq)\), subject to a bound on \(\omega(q)\). The retained harmonic mass lies between \(SV_*/2\) and \(SV_*\), where \(V_*=\prod_iV_i\).

The centered correlation uses
\[
\prod_{p\mid d}(\mathbf1_{p\mid n}-1/p).
\]
Its full-divisibility term becomes the original progression correlation at a cutoff between \(Xe^{-\eta}\) and \(X\). Complete multiplicativity and \(\lambda(qd)^2=1\) justify this exact substitution.

### Why the full prime period is not used

The primes grow with \(X\), so their full joint period is too large for direct averaging. Section 3 instead compares the particular residue tests represented by bounded-depth Boolean circuits. A fixed progression modulus is one coordinate, even if it is not squarefree. Every translated site uses the same prime coordinate with a shifted residue test. Different sites are not assigned independent copies.

The comparison applies to depth at most 20 and size at most \(\exp(L^6)\), over intervals of length at least \(\exp(L^A/2)\), with error \(\exp(-L^{10})\). Explicit scalar expansions with bounded total coefficient mass inherit an exponentially small error.

For each modulus, the proof adds an independent uniform jitter and encodes the resulting uniform point in binary. Under the product-residue law the bits are independent and unbiased. Under the integer-origin law, CRT controls every small bit marginal. This is only approximate independence at first.

Let \(f\) be the density of the bit law relative to the uniform cube. Remove its nonconstant Fourier coefficients of degree at most \(t\), calling their sum \(H\). Their total absolute mass is at most \(a\). The correction
\[
g=\frac{f-H+a}{1+a}
\]
is nonnegative, integrates to one, and is **exactly** \(t\)-wise uniform. Its total-variation distance from \(f\) is small. Braverman's theorem is applied to this corrected law. The positive additive term is needed even where \(f\) vanishes.

The construction follows the almost-to-exact independence idea of Alon–Goldreich–Mansour. It does not treat approximate marginal control as an immediate hypothesis of Braverman's exact-independence theorem.

Low-degree \(Q\)-states are enumerated explicitly before arbitrary padding predicates are evaluated. This permits truncated weights and denominator decisions. Integer averages never require an uncontrolled moment of the untruncated weight \(w^2\).

## 3. Quantitative centering and deletion costs

For a nonempty collection of centered prime factors replaced by constants, the remaining shift is a rough integer \(z\). Section 4 proves uniform Fourier bounds for arbitrary subsets of the appropriate rough interval:
\[
\|B\|_\infty\ll L^{-0.99},\qquad
\int_{\mathbb T}|B|^4\ll D^{-1}L^{-3.96}.
\]
The proof uses finite inclusion–exclusion with explicit CRT boundary errors. A fourth moment counts the equation \(z_1+z_2=z_3+z_4\).

These bounds combine with the Matomäki–Radziwiłł–Tao short exponential-sum theorem. Its frequency supremum stays **outside** the integral over starting points. Splitting frequencies at a multiplier threshold gives a rough-shift correlation bound \(O(L^{-21/20})\). This is stronger than \(L^{-1}\), so summing over \(O(L/\eta)\) bins leaves a power saving:
\[
\left|\sum_jC_j-S_0F(X)\right|
\ll SV_*\left(\eta+X^{-1}+\eta^{-1}2^JL^{-1/20}\right).
\]

The padding anti-concentration estimate is a separate ingredient. Under the tilt \(w/S\), a \(Q\)-prime divides the site with probability \(5/(p+4)\). Two independently chosen padding divisors at that site yield a logarithmic ratio whose characteristic function decays like
\[
(1+L|t|)^{-6/5}
\]
for bounded frequencies. Its integrability gives
\[
\mathbb E^*\sum_j\rho_j(n,d)^2\ll L^{-1}.
\]
The estimate is uniform in the tuple and bin locations. It need not gain the smaller bin width \(\eta\).

Delete sites with excessive \(Q\)-degree, padding mass \(\rho_j>K/L\), excessive \(P\)-degree, or a rare prohibited-path event. The absolute deletion cost is bounded by
\[
2^J\bigl(K^{-1}+L^{-100}+e^{-2WJ}\bigr)
+\exp(-L^{0.9})
\]
after normalization by \(SV_*\). The rare event depends only on residues, not on Liouville values. Endpoint translation preserves the relevant selected divisibilities, but it does not make the entire weight \(w\) constant across an edge.

## 4. The quantitative trace: arithmetic constraints and forest coding

A positive path has its entire tuple and padding divisor dividing each departure. Minimal prohibited words have unequal consecutive tuples, interval-shaped occurrences of each tuple prime, and a specified suffix congruence. Their existence at a site is exponentially rare. The final new prime in a prohibited word supplies a linear congruence with invertible coefficient, giving a reciprocal-prime saving. Deleting these sites imposes useful geometry on short positive blocks.

The matrix \(A_d\) is symmetric and has edge weight
\[
\frac{Lu(q)}{\sqrt{w(n)w(n')}}\,
(\text{residue and deletion factors})
\prod_{p\mid d}(\mathbf1_{p\mid n}-1/p).
\]
The \(P\)-degree cutoff is postponed to an orthogonal projection \(E\). This keeps the other residue factors independent of the \(P\)-draws during the signed integration.

On copies indexed by \(d\), define
\[
H_{d,d'}=\mathbf1_{d\ne d'}A_{d'}.
\]
This operator is not assumed self-adjoint. Its power records paths with unequal consecutive whole tuples. Squaring its Hilbert–Schmidt norm joins two paths into a closed word. External copy indices and the initial site are counted once.

### Singletons must be integrated before absolute values

A tuple prime appearing only once would cancel exactly without deletion. With deletion, the identity
\[
\mathbb E[(\mathbf1_{Z=a}-1/p)F(Z)]
=\frac1p\mathbb E[F(a)-F(Z)]
\]
turns its contribution into a mixed difference of the deletion predicate. Nonsingleton occurrences are expanded into indicator and scalar choices. Lit consistency forces one residue for a repeated prime, while every unlit scalar supplies an additional reciprocal.

Three exceptional classes are removed:

- Many unlit occurrences have extra powers of primes at least \(H_0\).
- Many jointly independent consistency constraints have a rank saving.
- Many singleton labels force positive witness paths in one common hybrid configuration, whose internal or comparison congruences can be eliminated in a triangular order.

The witness argument uses inclusion–exclusion only to obtain the existence of a common configuration and private labels. It does not pay for all subfamilies by taking absolute values of that full expansion.

The random-prime rank lemma assumes an integer matrix nonsingular over the reals, not invertible modulo every controlling prime. Two independent copies of the selected variables remove the arbitrary term depending on the controls. Zero rows are bounded using real rank and the largest atom of the prime law. Nonzero bounded integers have few prime divisors. The resulting bound is
\[
[\alpha(1+d_M)]^{r/2}.
\]
Dropping numerical distinctness is an enlargement of a positive product sum. Distinct abstract slots still retain separate reciprocal weights when their resampled prime values coincide.

### A coefficient-uniform forest code

In a retained perfect block, each prime occupies one interval and no nonempty subinterval has zero displacement. Quotienting the formal offset space by a maximal family of independent pairs leaves independent regular coordinate directions. Constant runs lie on affine lines with those directions. Their incidence graph with transition points is a forest.

A regular segment is the unique simple path between its two endpoint lines. A finite code records block and run boundaries, omitted labels, an abstract ordered forest, segment endpoints and imperfect positions. This bounds the **union** of possible equality patterns over all numerical coefficients and padding choices by \(\exp(Ck)\) per column.

That uniformity permits padding to be summed afterward in one common residue environment. A backward recurrence uses a row mass at most \(K\) at every translated site, giving \(K^{2k}\). It does not assert independence among those sites.

With at most \(Jk+S_1/2\) distinct tuple labels, reciprocal sums supply the square-root mass dependence. The final moment bound is
\[
\mathbb E\|H^k\|_{\rm HS}^2
\le [K(C_2\sqrt W)^J]^{2k},
\]
with \(C_2\) absolute before \(W\) is chosen.

## 5. Spectral transfer to every ordinary cutoff

Section 8 proves a transfer lemma for self-adjoint \(B_i\) and a projection \(P\), even when distinct \(B_i\) do not commute. If
\[
\|B_i\|\le a,\qquad
\sum_i\|B_iv\|^2\le b^2\|v\|^2\quad(v=Pv),
\]
and \(\mathscr B_{i,j}=\mathbf1_{i\ne j}B_j\), then
\[
\left\|P\left(\sum_iB_i\right)P\right\|
\le3\max(a,b,\rho(\mathscr B)).
\]
The proof uses each operator's own resolvent and positivity of a continuous family. It never commutes two different edge operators or assumes that \(E\) commutes with them.

Weighted row bounds and the degree projection give the hypotheses. The trace controls the spectral radius outside a tiny fraction of block origins. Testing against \(\sqrt w\,\lambda\) uses the already justified truncated-weight mean.

Overlapping blocks have an explicit edge multiplicity. The boundary comparison accounts for the displacement divided by block length, block length divided by averaging height, and floor errors. This transfers the estimate to every real prefix cutoff, without averaging away an exceptional set of scales.

The factor \(L\) in the edge matrix gives \(1/L\) per bin, cancelling the number-of-bins factor \(L\). After division by \(V_*\ge W^J\), the spectral term is
\[
\frac K\eta\left(\frac{C_3}{\sqrt W}\right)^J.
\]
Choose \(W\) after \(C_3\), with \(e^5C_3/\sqrt W\le e^{-1}\). All remaining errors are \(O(e^{-J})\). Thus
\[
c=\frac{\delta}{6WA}>0
\]
is absolute. Thresholds and implied constants may depend on the fixed shift and modulus.

The Liouville affine step is exact. With \(l=a_1a_2\), \(c_0=\min(a_2b_1,a_1b_2)\) and \(h=|a_1b_2-a_2b_1|\), complete multiplicativity reduces the affine sum to a progression prefix difference multiplied by \(\lambda(l)\). The finite initial prefix changes only the constant.

## 6. Part II resets the parameters and the order of limits

The qualitative proof starts again with fixed graph parameters. None of the quantitative choices of \(A,W,L,J,\eta\) remains in force.

At auxiliary scale \(B\), the core band has logarithms between \(B^{1-\eta}\) and \(B\), while the center band lies between \(B^{1-\varepsilon}\) and \(B^{1-\eta}\). Here \(\varepsilon=10^{-4}\), \(\eta=\varepsilon/100\), and \(c_*=\varepsilon/100\). Core primes have a large fixed weight \(A\), center primes weight one. Write
\[
\beta_C=(1+A)^{-1},\quad \beta_Z=1/2,\quad \theta=\beta_Z^2=1/4.
\]
The vertex normalization is
\[
W(x)=\beta_C^{-\omega_C(x)}\beta_Z^{-\omega_Z(x)},\qquad
L_0=\mathbb EW(x).
\]

Divisors lie in one fixed-ratio interval \((H,\tau H]\), have bounded logarithmic size and factor count, and use these finite prime bands. Core cutoffs depend only on core residues and require enough core divisors outside the edge label. The center factor is
\[
\mathbf1_{p\mid x}-\theta/p.
\]
It is not mean zero under uniform residues. Cancellation will use the vertex normalization at the two endpoints.

The graph estimate, for arbitrary bounded sequences and complex coefficients, is
\[
\limsup_{X\to\infty}\frac1X
\left|\sum_{x\le X}\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}
a_dF(x)G(x+hd)g_d(x,x+hd)\right|
\ll L_0B^{-1-c_*/2}.
\]
Every graph parameter is fixed during the \(X\)-limit. Full finite-period averaging is legitimate in this order.

### Extracting a biased divisor interval

If \(\sum_p(1-|f(p)|)/p\) diverges for either factor, a periodic prime-square majorant shows that its ordinary absolute mean vanishes. Otherwise both absolute defects have finite sums.

Assume a biased subsequence with \(|S(N_j)|\ge\gamma N_j\). No common complex argument of \(S(N_j)\) is assumed. A weighted divisor law retains divisors with \(|f_1(d)f_2(d)|\ge1/2\), and one of \(O(B)\) intervals carries reciprocal mass \(\gg L_0/B\).

Use \(a_d=\overline{f_1(d)f_2(d)}\). Except for explicit coprimality failures, multiplicativity yields a positive coefficient \(|f_1(d)f_2(d)|^2\) multiplying the same original correlation. Smooth core cutoffs lose a controlled proportion by Chebyshev. For \(X_j=HN_j\), the raw divisor sum has a lower bound \(\gg_\gamma L_0/B\).

Centering costs
\[
O\!\left(L_0(B^{-1-\varepsilon}+J/P_0)\right),
\qquad P_0=\exp(B^{1-\varepsilon}).
\]
Together with the graph bound this contradicts the raw lower bound after the long limit and then \(B\to\infty\).

### The qualitative analytic input

Fourier inversion of the core cutoffs creates finite-prime twists of the original multiplicative functions. They remain multiplicative, though not necessarily completely multiplicative. Changing finitely many prime values changes every squared pretentious distance by at most twice the corresponding reciprocal-prime sum.

For each fixed \(B\), this gives uniform nonpretentiousness over the Fourier parameters and the fixed finite family of characters needed by the corrected MRT theorem. The frequency supremum remains outside its short-interval integral.

The rough-shift multiplier has a supremum and fourth-moment bound proved by finite inclusion–exclusion, including position-uniform counting errors and a singular-factor bound. Two overlapping intervals produce an exact convolution identity with a vanishing endpoint error. A frequency split gives \(B^{-1-\varepsilon}\), using whichever original factor is nonpretentious. There is no rate assumption on its divergence.

## 7. Qualitative paths, conditioned cylinders and preserved free coordinates

The high trace uses closed lines of length \(\ell=2\lfloor B\rfloor\). A forbidden specification consists of a simple path, an extra prime absent from its edge labels, and a nonzero suffix displacement divisible by that prime. Qualification fixes one residue per distinct used prime. Minimal descent allows reversal and reaches a primitive specification. Their starting sites have density at most \(P_0^{-1+o(1)}\).

The tree of first visits records distinct integer offsets, with all later steps designated as returns. The fixed residue set \(\mathcal F\) contains core primes on the line and every repeated line prime. A center prime appearing only once stays free. Background core primes absent from the line are also not silently added to \(\mathcal F\).

After negligible arithmetic exclusions, fixed-prime active vertices have few components, few fixed primes have disconnected activity, and few free singleton edges have accidental congruences with other vertices. The proof uses path-hitting sets, short tree blocks, a two-block rerooting lemma and triangular prime constraints.

The rerooting argument extracts a common dependency order using chains, antichains, monotone subsequences and interval coloring. A common root-offset integer is specified separately. Dropping its original defining equation enlarges a positive count, while the retained local path tests still have the required nonzero or invertible-coefficient condition. Prime variables are then summed in reverse dependency order.

### Finite-cylinder truncation

Conditioned on the fixed residues, vertex deletion is avoidance of exact cylinders on the remaining coordinates. The rank of an intersection is the maximum number of compatible events with private coordinates. It is not simply the number of events in an arbitrary description.

A minimal generating family has at most the rank many events. An intersection of rank below \(t\), for cylinders of width at most \(w\), has support at most \(tw\). Möbius inversion on the intersection poset gives bounded coefficients and an error supported on intersections generated by at most \(t\) events but having rank at least \(t\).

Rank can jump. The proof re-minimizes the predecessor before the first jump, rather than assuming rank rises one at a time. Error terms therefore admit at most \(2t_0\) specifications: a generating list plus private-coordinate witnesses.

Private witness primes supply many intrinsic or comparison congruences with an explicit triangular order. Their arithmetic saving absorbs the coefficient budget. The retained main terms are short qualification lists. Their free-coordinate expectations are still signed.

All core coordinates may be held fixed while the free center coordinates are integrated, followed by a positive core average. This is an integration order, not a redefinition of \(\mathcal F\) or of the cylinder rank.

## 8. Exact subtree weights and the qualitative trace sum

For a rooted first-visit tree, let \(s(v)\) be the number of children. For a nontrivial subtree \(Q\), define
\[
u_a(Q)=A_a^{|E(Q)|}\prod_{v\in V(Q)}\beta_a^{s(v)}.
\]
Independent child-edge inclusion gives the exact identity
\[
\sum_Q u_a(Q)=\sum_v(1-\beta_a^{s(v)})=:S_a(\mathscr T).
\]
The degrees are those of the full tree. This identity cancels the background-prime exponential later.

Prime occurrences are recorded by subtree tokens. Tagged exceptional primes can have several tokens or unlit ghost tokens, grouped under one prime value. Short-list primes and exceptional tokens number \(o(B)\). Untagged prime tokens have independent numerical slots only after the valid assignments and their weights have been established.

A good tree edge has unary endpoints, neither incident to a return, and a nonroot origin. An uncorrupted center prime occurring only on that edge has exactly two normalization factors at its lit residue. For its full multiplier, including return-departure factors at other residues, the signed average is
\[
\frac{\theta}{p^2}\left(
1-\theta+\sum_{\substack{a\ne a_0\\\nu_p(a)>0}}
(1-\beta_Z^{\nu_p(a)})
\right)
\le\frac{\theta(\ell+1)}{p^2}.
\]
Here \(\nu_p(a)\) counts all departures with multiplicity. The terms of order \(1/p\) cancel because \(\theta=\beta_Z^2\). Removing return factors by absolute bounds before this average would lose the justified identity.

The resulting weight for a singleton on a good edge is reduced from \(\theta\) to \(\theta/2\). Other single-edge center tokens are bounded with a compensating increase. Repeated untagged fixed center primes cannot have a single good-edge token, because their further lit occurrence would force a return incidence or disconnected activity.

Core tokens give a different saving. At each eligible good origin, choose one named core slot whose token begins there. Its divisor-bin restriction gives \(O(B^{-1+\eta})\). The dependence is ancestor-first, so summation proceeds in reverse ancestor order. Origins without a core token and return origins receive a stronger cutoff penalty. The cutoff occurrences are explicitly distinct.

For each untagged token type with multiplicity \(j\), the prime set is unordered. Naming its slots and dividing by \(j!\) is an exact identity before dropping distinctness. Summing multiplicities produces
\[
\exp\left(\sum_a v_a\sum_Q\widetilde u_a(Q)\right).
\]
The unmodified subtree sums cancel the background exponent exactly. Losing these factorials or charging one independent residue per occurrence would destroy that accounting.

The remaining center saving, the core-bin savings, and strong return penalties dominate the topology count. If \(g\) and \(b\) are the good and nongood tree edges and \(r_*\) the returns, then
\[
\ell=g+b+r_*,\qquad b\le10(r_*+1).
\]
The numerical margin yields the conditional main term \(B^{-(1+2c_*)\ell}\), and after negligible errors the high trace is at most \(B^{-(1+c_*)\ell}\).

## 9. Localization and the exact affine expansion

The graph with arbitrary complex \(a_d\) is directed. Section 17 therefore uses
\[
\operatorname{Tr}((M^*M)^{\ell/2})
\]
and singular values, rather than treating it as self-adjoint. The denominator around a closed line equals the product of the departure vertex weights, counting repeated vertices with multiplicity.

Choose a power-of-two block length \(D_0\asymp B^{10}H\). All selected primes are odd for large fixed \(B\), so \(D_0\) is coprime to the residue period. Averaging block origins over that period reproduces the high trace. Exceptional blocks have exponentially small density in \(B\log B\).

Deleted vertices and exceptional blocks are controlled by Cauchy–Schwarz with the finite second moment of \(W\), without asserting independence from residues. Boundary positions and residues are independent over the full combined period because of the coprimality of \(D_0\). Weighted bilinear testing then proves the graph estimate.

For general multiplicative functions, Part III does not divide by \(f(a)\) or assume \(f(p^k)=f(p)^k\). It expands
\[
F_{a,f}(m)=\mathbf1_{a\mid m}f(m/a)
\]
as a signed sum of \(2^{\omega(a)}\) normalized bounded multiplicative functions. At a prime dividing \(a\), the desired local sequence has value zero at exponent zero. Write it as \(B_p-C_p\), with both new local sequences equal to one at exponent zero. All changed primes form a fixed finite set, so nonpretentiousness survives.

For an arbitrary residue \(b\bmod l\), set \(d=\gcd(b,l)\), \(q=l/d\), \(c=b/d\). Character orthogonality after this dilation gives
\[
\mathbf1_{m\equiv b\pmod l}
=\frac1{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi\bmod q}\overline{\chi(c)}F_{d,\chi}(m).
\]
The convention for \(q=1\) handles the zero residue. No zero character value is divided out. Place this progression weight at the nonpretentious factor, use finite-prime and character-twist stability, and apply the shift theorem term by term.

The affine deduction uses exact scaled arguments and subtracts a finite initial prefix. The nonpretentious factor is inherited from an original function in every component pair. The final Liouville lemma checks the full range \(|t|\le X\), with a lower bound \(\frac14\sqrt{\log\log X}-O_\chi(1)\). The Möbius specialization then follows from equality of prime values.
