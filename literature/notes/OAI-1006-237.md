## Problem and exact observation model

An encoder makes a possibly randomized one-bit summary of independent fair input bits. Independent binary symmetric noise then corrupts every input bit. How much information can the summary retain about the full noisy vector, given how informative it was before the noise?

Let \(X\) be uniform on \(\Omega_n=\{-1,1\}^n\). A binary auxiliary variable \(U\) is specified by its conditional mean

\[
u(x)=\mathbb E[U\mid X=x]\in[-1,1].
\]

Write \(L=\log2\), using natural logarithms, and define

\[
H(r)=-\frac{1+r}{2}\log\frac{1+r}{2}
     -\frac{1-r}{2}\log\frac{1-r}{2},
\qquad \psi(r)=L-H(r).
\]

The information functional is

\[
I(u)=H(\mathbb Eu)-\mathbb EH(u)
    =\mathbb E\psi(u)-\psi(\mathbb Eu)
    =I(U;X).
\]

For \(-1\le\rho\le1\), put \(Y_i=X_iZ_i\), with independent signs \(Z_i\) having mean \(\rho\) and independent of \((X,U)\). Then

\[
T_\rho u(y)=\mathbb E[u(X)\mid Y=y],
\qquad I(T_\rho u)=I(U;Y).
\]

The observer has the full noisy vector \(Y\). The theorem does not compress that observation to another bit. Uniform independent inputs, binary summary and independent symmetric coordinate noise are part of the model, not consequences of the result. [§1, pp. 1–2]

## Main statements and equality

**Theorem 1.1, sharp soft-channel contraction.** For every finite \(n\ge0\), every such \(u\), and every \(\rho\in[-1,1]\),

\[
I(T_\rho u)
\le
\psi\!\left(|\rho|\,\psi^{-1}(I(u))\right).
\]

The inverse is the nonnegative increasing branch on \([0,L]\). Every soft coordinate \(u(x)=a x_i\), \(|a|\le1\), attains equality when \(n\ge1\).

Thus the comparison fixes initial information, allowing arbitrary output means. It is not an assertion that a Boolean source of every prescribed bias attains the refined curve.

**Corollary 1.2, Boolean output with bias.** For \(f:\Omega_n\to\{-1,1\}\), let \(m=\mathbb Ef\) and \(r_m=\psi^{-1}(H(m))\). Then

\[
I(f(X);Y)\le\psi(|\rho|r_m)\le\psi(|\rho|).
\]

Signed coordinates attain the universal bound at \(m=0\). For \(0<|m|<1\) and \(0<|\rho|\le1\), the refined upper curve is strictly below the universal one. At crossover probability \(\varepsilon\in[0,1/2]\), \(\rho=1-2\varepsilon\), and the universal bound in bits is \(1-h_2(\varepsilon)\). This is the paper's resolution of the Courtade–Kumar conjecture.

The other main theorem strengthens an instantaneous production bound by retaining the mean. It is stronger than the scalar bound needed to integrate Theorem 1.1, with a separate near-boundary comparison against a concurrent profile. [Theorem 1.3, §§7–8]

## Entropy production and why a mean-dependent profile is needed

For an interior field \(g:\Omega_n\to(-1,1)\), define

\[
V(r)=\operatorname{atanh}r,\qquad
N=\frac12\sum_i(\mathrm{Id}-\mathrm{flip}_i),\qquad
P_t=T_{e^{-t}}=e^{-tN},
\]

\[
m=\mathbb Eg,\quad e=\mathbb EH(g),\quad
D(g)=\mathbb E[V(g)Ng].
\]

Mean conservation and differentiation give \(dI(P_tg)/dt=-D(P_tg)\). A soft coordinate with amplitude \(r\) has information \(\psi(r)\) and production \(rV(r)\). The scalar comparison rate is therefore

\[
F(\psi(r))=rV(r),\qquad 0\le r<1.
\]

Splitting one coordinate into faces \(g_+,g_-\) yields the exact identity

\[
D(g)=\frac{D(g_+)+D(g_-)}2+\mathbb E c(g_+,g_-),
\quad
c(a,b)=\frac{a-b}{4}\bigl(V(a)-V(b)\bigr).
\]

The scalar profile \(F(I)\) alone does not satisfy the required local inequality for every coupled pair law. The proof keeps both mean and information. Define

\[
S(I)=F(I)-2I\quad(0\le I<L),\qquad S(I)=0\quad(I<0),
\]

\[
\mathcal B_m(I)
=2I+(1-m^2)S\!\left(L-\frac{H(m)-I}{1-m^2}\right),
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal B}_m(I)=\max\{\mathcal B_m(I),F(I)\}.
\]

**Theorem 1.3** proves

\[
D(g)\ge\widehat{\mathcal B}_m(I(g))\ge F(I(g)).
\]

The proof establishes the local inequality for this maximum before using dimension induction. It does not infer that a maximum repairs an arbitrary unsupported branch automatically. [§1.2–1.3, §7]

## Scalar analytic inputs

Section 2 derives the scalar facts used later from a positive hyperbolic integral and two moment comparisons:

\[
\frac{r^2}{2}\le\psi(r)\le Lr^2,\qquad
H(r)\ge L(1-r^2),
\]

\[
F(0)=0,\quad F'(0)=2,\quad
F''(I)\ge\frac43,\quad F'''(I)\ge0.
\]

The quotient \(\psi(r)/r^2\) increases strictly on \((0,1)\). The zero extension of \(S\) is nonnegative, increasing, convex and \(C^1\), but is **not \(C^2\) at zero**.

With \(r=\tanh v\), the proof writes

\[
F''(\psi(r))
=\cosh^2v\,
2\int_0^1(1-s^2)\cosh(2vs)\,ds.
\]

This also proves that \(e^{-2I}F''(I)\) increases strictly. The scaled curvature

\[
P(h)=h^2F''(L-h)
\]

satisfies

\[
\frac12\le P(h)\le M:=\frac{4L^2}{3}<\frac23,
\qquad 0<h\le L.
\]

For this comparison, the source rewrites \(P(H(r))\) as \(b_t^2(1+n_t)\) under a density proportional to \((1-tu^2)^{-1}\). Jensen and Cauchy–Schwarz give the lower bound. Covariance differentiation for monotone functions, followed by a reweighting by \(1-u^2\), controls the two changing moments together for the upper bound. The strict margin \(2>3M\) is used in both common-output arguments. [Lemmas 2.1–2.4, pp. 5–7]

## Exact joining optimizer with a common entropy budget

The local cost is extended by zero at \((1,1)\) and \((-1,-1)\), and by \(+\infty\) at every other boundary pair. It is jointly convex and lower semicontinuous.

Fix child means \(a=m+\delta>b=m-\delta\), both interior, and an average conditional-entropy budget

\[
0<e\le\bar h=\frac{H(a)+H(b)}2.
\]

The cost problem is

\[
\mathcal K(m,\delta,e)
=\inf\left\{
\mathbb E c(A,B):
\mathbb EA=a,\ \mathbb EB=b,\
\frac{\mathbb EH(A)+\mathbb EH(B)}2\le e
\right\}.
\]

It allows arbitrary coupled pair laws, without an independence assumption. Only the average entropy is constrained. The theorem gives an exact optimizer with one ordered interior pair and possibly the two common-output corners:

\[
\lambda\delta_{(x,y)}
+\pi_+\delta_{(1,1)}
+\pi_-\delta_{(-1,-1)}.
\]

To prove this rather than merely propose it, §3 introduces logarithmic coordinates \(z,w\) for the interior pair. The prescribed means impose lower floors on \(z,w\). A nonnegative price \(p\) on the sum of entropies produces a strictly convex two-variable objective. Its normalized entropy has a positive double series with coefficient \(1/\max(i,j)\), which supplies strict convexity.

The constrained minimizer is turned into a global affine support in the pair variables. The two floor multipliers are nonnegative and complementary to the corner masses. The support is checked for both orderings of the pair, the diagonal, the equal corners and the infinite-cost boundary. Its equality set and the mean equations give uniqueness of the priced optimizer.

The optimizer's average entropy varies continuously from capacity at \(p=0\) to zero as \(p\to\infty\). A reflected core approaching the endpoints supplies the small-entropy comparison. Hence every positive feasible budget is attained exactly. Nonnegative pricing then proves optimality for the original inequality constraint. [Lemma 3.1 and Proposition 3.2, pp. 8–10]

### Two geometries

After reflecting to \(m\ge0\), the core is either reflected, \((r,-r)\), with two possible corners, or is mixed with only the positive common corner and has nonnegative center.

For the reflected case,

\[
k=\delta/r,\quad e=kH(r),\quad
\mathcal K=\delta V(r),\quad 0\le m\le1-k.
\]

At fixed \(\delta,e\), the core and cost do not depend on \(m\). The transition occurs at

\[
e_0(m,\delta)=(1-m)H\!\left(\frac{\delta}{1-m}\right).
\]

The reflected geometry has \(e\le e_0\), and the one-corner geometry has \(e\ge e_0\). This support threshold differs from the later analytic threshold \(I=L/2\).

Figure 1 is a triangle of **mixture weights**, not a picture of channel values. Its horizontal motion fixes core mass, separation, entropy and joining cost while increasing the common mean until the negative corner disappears. [§3.2, pp. 10–11]

## Paying the deterministic core and preserving the reserve

For a one-bit channel \(g(X)=m+\delta X\), let \(\alpha=1-m^2\) and let \(J\) be its information. Lemma 4.1 proves

\[
J<\alpha L,\qquad D\ge\alpha F(J/\alpha).
\]

The proof uses the two posteriors of the fair input given the output. Their weights are \(w_\pm=(1\pm m)/2\), their correlation magnitudes are \(r_\pm=\delta/(1\pm m)\), and \(i_\pm=\psi(r_\pm)\). Then \(J=\sum w_\pm i_\pm\) and \(D=\sum w_\pm F(i_\pm)\). Convexity of the secant slope \(F(s)/s\), with information-size-biased weights, reduces the assertion to a second-moment comparison. The increasing quotient \(\psi(r)/r^2\) supplies that comparison and establishes \(J/\alpha<L\) before evaluating \(F\) there. [§4, pp. 11–13]

Section 5 writes the reserve as the perspective

\[
R(v,e)=vS(L-e/v).
\]

The profile can also be expressed as allocating information between capacities \(c_m=H(m)-(1-m^2)L\) at rate 2 and \((1-m^2)L\) at rate \(F\). This is an identity for the profile, **not a decomposition of an actual channel**.

If the child means are \(m\pm\delta\), their average variance capacity is \(\beta=1-m^2-\delta^2\). Perspective convexity pools their reserve contributions. The amount a join must supply is bounded by

\[
W(m,\delta,e)=2J+R(\alpha,e)-R(\beta,e).
\]

Adding either common corner with retention \(\tau\) contracts this required payment by at least \(\tau\). There is no sign restriction on the original mean for this reserve assertion.

For the positive corner, the proof scales \(A=1-m,\delta,e\) together. An entropy Euler identity and the curvature bound give

\[
A\,\frac{dW}{dA}-W\ge(2-3M)\kappa\ge0.
\]

A logarithmic geometry inequality controls the remaining term. The clipped surplus needs only its \(C^1\) seam, not a nonexistent second derivative there. Reflection handles the negative corner.

The deterministic core is paid by the variance-refined one-bit inequality. Adding the two corners successively multiplies the bound by their combined retained core mass. This proves the local joining inequality for \(\mathcal B\). The pooling step always uses the **original individual child entropies**. Only the lower bound on joining cost replaces the pair law by its common-budget optimizer. [Theorem 5.3, pp. 13–16]

## Thinning pays the scalar information increment

The one-positive-corner optimizer has core center \(x_c\ge0\). At mass \(\lambda\), its parameters are

\[
x=1-\lambda(1-x_c),\quad d=\lambda d_c,\quad e=\lambda h_c.
\]

Let \(\nu=\bar h-e\), \(J=H(x)-\bar h\), and \(I=\nu+J\). An independent core-selection flag \(B\) gives the interpretation

\[
I=I(U;X,B),\quad \nu=I(U;B\mid X),\quad J=I(U;X).
\]

Theorem 6.1 proves

\[
F(\nu+J)-F(\nu)\le\lambda F(J_c).
\]

The proof differentiates the left side divided by \(\lambda\). For \(\Delta=F(I)-F(\nu)\), the exact Euler derivative contains a positive term \(F'(\nu)\kappa\ge2\kappa\) and a possibly negative curvature integral. The scaled-curvature bound reduces the loss to

\[
M\int_q^s\frac{(h-b)_+}{h^2}\,dh,
\qquad b=\log(1+x).
\]

The entropy of the **oriented thinning path** gives a lower bound on the interval's location and an upper bound on its length. This is not an entropy bound for every pair law with the same means. A separate interval lemma, proved through a rational logarithm bound, moving intervals and an explicit positive polynomial decomposition, gives an integral bound \(3\kappa\). Consequently

\[
\lambda\Delta'-\Delta\ge(2-3M)\kappa\ge0.
\]

Comparison with \(\lambda=1\) proves the theorem, without taking a zero-mass limit. The auxiliary \(\kappa\) is a prior-to-posterior KL quantity, distinct from \(\mathcal K\).

Combining thinning with the one-bit core inequality pays the scalar increment throughout the high-entropy, one-corner geometry. Original child entropies are again retained when convexity pools their scalar profiles. [§6, pp. 16–22]

## Completing the maximum profile and integrating noise

Section 7 supplies two comparisons.

1. If \(I\ge L/2\), then \(\mathcal B_m(I)\ge F(I)\), strictly at nonzero mean. The proof uses the increase of \(e^{-2I}F''(I)\), an exact exponentially weighted balance at \(L/2\), and a tangent bound for the surplus.
2. Below half information, the demand \(F(H(m)-e)-F(\bar h(m,d)-e)\) increases with positive common mean at fixed \(d,e\). This follows by comparing logarithmic slopes of \(F'\) with the corresponding entropy slopes.

When the parent maximum selects \(\mathcal B\), its local inequality suffices. When it selects \(F\) strictly, the parent is below \(L/2\). Convexity at the original children reduces the required amount to the scalar increment. A one-corner optimizer is paid by thinning. For a reflected optimizer, increasing the common mean until one corner vanishes preserves cost and entropy and can only increase the required scalar payment. The endpoint is paid by the same thinning theorem.

The proof does not require the same branch of the maximum to remain active during this motion. It needs only the information threshold and unchanged cost. The resulting local inequality for \(\widehat{\mathcal B}\), combined with the exact coordinate energy split, proves Theorem 1.3 by dimension induction from a constant field. [Theorem 7.3 and proof of Theorem 1.3, pp. 23–26]

For a nonconstant input, every positive noise time makes all field values interior. Walsh eigenvalues remain positive at finite time, so the field remains nonconstant and has strictly positive information. Set

\[
r(t)=\psi^{-1}(I(P_tu)).
\]

The production inequality and \(\psi'(r)=V(r)\) give \(r'(t)\le-r(t)\). Integrate from a positive time and let that time decrease to zero by finite-dimensional continuity. This covers initial Boolean fields without assuming finite initial production. Constant inputs, \(\rho=0,1\), and negative correlations are treated separately, with negative correlation reduced by a cube sign change. Soft coordinates realize equality directly. [§8, pp. 26–27]

## Concurrent work, evidence and limits

The paper attributes the differential local-to-global route to Chen–Gohari–Nair and the auxiliary-receiver method. Its cited August 2026 revision reports a failed earlier auxiliary inequality. This note records that attribution from the official source, without claiming to have read the dependency's Appendix B. The proofs here establish their own local comparisons.

The source explicitly acknowledges overlap with Chen et al.'s September 2026 proof: retaining initial information while integrating that paper's scalar production branch also gives Theorem 1.1. The additional comparison here concerns the displayed mean-dependent profiles. For fixed \(m\in(0,1)\) and \(h=H(m)-I\downarrow0\), §8.1 obtains

\[
\mathcal B_m(H(m)-h)\sim\frac{1-m^2}{2}\log(1/h),
\qquad
C_m(H(m)-h)\sim\frac{1-m}{2}\log(1/h).
\]

Their ratio tends to \(1+m>1\). This shows that the cited \(C_m\) does not dominate \(\mathcal B_m\) everywhere. It is not a claim that one complete method dominates the other at every state.

Other cited comparisons distinguish balanced high-noise results, fixed-mean Boolean optimizers, Gaussian/spherical analogues, a one-bit observation rather than the full noisy vector, classical logarithmic Sobolev bounds, and the dimension-normalized vector form of Mrs. Gerber's lemma. These models should not be merged.
