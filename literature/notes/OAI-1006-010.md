## 1. Research question and result

The manuscript asserts

\[
G=\beta(2)=L(2,\chi_{-4})
=\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{(-1)^j}{(2j+1)^2}\notin\mathbb Q.
\]

It addresses the individual real value \(G\). Earlier family results guaranteeing an irrational member among several even beta values do not identify \(G\). Rapid rational approximation is insufficient if clearing denominators destroys the decay. The introduction distinguishes approximation exponents below one from irrationality measures, the conductor-three result from conductor four, and a two-adic analogue from the real constant. Sun's announced result is cited as contemporary work but is not used. [Original, §1.]

A signed mixed-moment determinant \(\Delta_N\) has size \(n=48N\). Assuming \(G\) rational makes it rational. Three separate arguments supply:

| Ingredient | Author's conclusion |
|---|---|
| Finite places | Along nonzero determinants, \(\liminf(n^{-2}\log|\Delta_N|-\tfrac12\log2)>-2.29084\). |
| Nonvanishing | For every sufficiently large prime \(p\), \(v_p(\Delta_p)=-96p\). Here the scale \(N\) is itself \(p\). |
| Real place | Without rationality, \(\limsup(n^{-2}\log|\Delta_N|-\tfrac12\log2)\le-2.290939875<-2.2909\). |

The contradiction uses the same prime-scale subsequence. The conclusion is qualitative irrationality, with no irrationality measure or transcendence theorem.

## 2. Polynomial rows cancel the extra period

The parameters are

\[
n=48N,\ a=11N,\ b=7N,\ q=g=4N,\ h=2N,
\qquad L=59N,\ C=63N,\ H=65N,\ A=19N.
\]

For \(f=\sqrt{1-t^2}\), \(w=t/(1+f)\), the involution \(w\mapsto w^{-1}\) fixes \(t\) and negates \(f\). Define

\[
R_r=(1-t)^ht^{C-1}w^{r-g},\quad
P_r=(R_r+R_r^*)/2,\quad D_r=t(R_r^*-R_r)/(2f).
\]

Chebyshev formulas make \(P_r,D_r\) integer polynomials with the specified degree supports. Their essential contact identity is \(tP_r/f-D_r=O(t^L)\).

The moments are

\[
M(i,j)=\int_{-1}^{1}\int_0^1
\frac{|t|t^is^j}{\sqrt{1-t^2}(1-ts)}\,ds\,dt,\qquad
Z(i,j)=\int_0^1\int_0^1\frac{t^is^j}{1-ts}\,ds\,dt.
\]

Absolute convergence justifies the expansions. With \(c_l=4^{-l}\binom{2l}{l}\), and \(c_z=0\) outside nonnegative integer indices, rational recurrence arrays give

\[
M(i,j)=M^0(i,j)+4Gc_{(i-j)/2}
+\tfrac32\zeta(2)c_{(j-i-1)/2},\qquad
Z(i,j)=Z^0(i,j)+[i=j]\zeta(2).
\]

The nonrational starts are \(M(0,0)=4G\) and \(M(0,1)=\pi^2/4\), derived from elementary integrals, the arcsine-square series and an Abel limit. Diagonal and boundary recurrences determine the rest. The convention \(M^0(-1,j)=k^+_{j+1}\) is algebraic bookkeeping, not a new integral.

For raw columns \(F_j(r)=M(P_r,j)-\tfrac32Z(D_r,j)\), the coefficient of \(\zeta(2)\) is \(\tfrac32[t^j](tP_r/f-D_r)\), zero for \(b\le j<L\). Every raw entry belongs to \(\mathbb Q+\mathbb QG\). An integer finite-difference filter replaces the monomial columns by \(s^{b+k}(1-s)^q\). All \(n+q\) required raw columns remain in the contact range. This is why rationality of \(G\) alone suffices. [Original, §2.]

## 3. Finite-place lower bound

### Two-adic smoothing and alternation

The geometric moment series also converges in \(\mathbb Q_2\), because its even-moment valuations grow essentially linearly. Its difference from the rational recurrence solution has two fixed homogeneous discrepancies. Contracting with the row polynomials splits each raw column into a smoothed part and two exceptional parts.

Expanding a full raw minor while retaining alternation forces distinct coefficient-vector indices within each exceptional family. For \(m\) and \(l\) columns from the two families, the corresponding sums of indices are at least \(m(m-1)/2\) and \(l(l-1)/2\). Distinct raw column indices add another contribution. No distinctness between the two exceptional families is needed.

Chebyshev coefficient valuations, harmonic-sum denominators and the smoothed-column estimate reduce the leading loss to quadratic minimization. With \(\delta=(q+g+h)/n=5/24\),

\[
v_2(\Delta_N)\ge
-\left(\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{\delta^2}{8}\right)n^2
-O_G(n\log(n+2))
=-\frac{505}{4608}n^2-o(n^2).
\]

Every full raw minor has the same bound, preserved by the integer filter through Cauchy–Binet. The implied constant may depend on the fixed hypothetical rational \(G\). [Original, §2.2.]

### Odd primes need two denominator layers

For \(2\sqrt H<p\le H\), parity and carry calculations reduce \(p^2M^0(i,j)\) and \(p^2Z^0(i,j)\) to smaller recurrence arrays. The coefficient of \(G\) is integral after excluding its finitely many denominator primes. All raw columns lie in \(p^{-2}\mathbb Z_p^n\).

Some paired columns have opposite leading residues and a sum in \(p^{-1}\mathbb Z_p^n\). For \(p\le H/2\), counting these improved columns in the \(n+q\) pool yields

\[
v_p(\Delta_N)\ge-2n+(d_0-q)_+,\qquad
d_0=\bigl(\min(p,L-p,A)-\max(b,H-2p)\bigr)_+.
\]

For \(p>H/2\), bounding only the leading-residue rank would leave the next denominator layer uncontrolled. The central-column identity is

\[
pF_j\equiv-\sum_{0\le\ell<H-p}\frac{V_\ell}{j-\ell}\pmod p.
\]

The proof retains noncentral \(p^{-2}\) columns representing the \(V_\ell\), then adds integral multiples of \(p\) times those columns to central columns. The representatives' unknown next coefficients are already integral after this operation. A lifted kernel basis makes all but a bounded number of remaining central columns integral. All column changes have integral inverses.

With \(K=L-p,J=H-p\), the counts are

\[
R=K_++(K-A)_++(J-\max(b,K))_+,
\]
\[
S=(\min(A,K)-b)_++(\min(b,J)-\max(0,K))_+,
\quad v_p(\Delta_N)\ge-\min(2n,n+R,2R+S).
\]

These bounds hold for every full minor of the whole pool. No noncentral column is discarded or improved solely because its leading residue is dependent.

The resulting continuous piecewise-linear loss \(d(p/N)\) has support \([0,65]\) and area \(8609/2\). Small primes contribute \(o(n^2)\). Sufficiently large primes above \(H\) contribute no negative valuation. The ordinary prime number theorem, applied through step-function bounds for the fixed weight, evaluates the sum. The rational product formula then gives

\[
\liminf\left(n^{-2}\log|\Delta_N|-\tfrac12\log2\right)
\ge-\frac{8609}{4608}
-\left(\frac12+\frac{505}{4608}\right)\log2>-2.29084.
\]

This conclusion requires nonzero rational determinants. Nonvanishing is a separate part of the proof. [Original, §3 and its loss table.]

## 4. Nonvanishing at prime scales

At \(N=p\), Frobenius reduces the \(48p\)-dimensional matrix to a fixed rational \(49\times48\) base matrix \(\mathcal B\). Its first and last 48 rows are \(\mathcal B_0,\mathcal B_1\). The auxiliary row 48 has lower starting degrees. Its extra coefficients are retained and matched to the two different extraction shifts.

Two palindromic polynomial bases give an invertible matrix \(\mathbf a\), with diagonal \(2^{-i}\), and \(\mathbf b=\mathbf a\Pi\). The map \(\Pi\) kills \(e_0\) and exchanges \(e_i,e_{p-i}\). The scaled residue matrix is

\[
\mathcal L_p=\mathbf a^T\otimes\mathcal B_0+
\mathbf b^T\otimes\mathcal B_1.
\]

Right multiplication by \((\mathbf a^T)^{-1}\otimes I\) gives \(I\otimes\mathcal B_0+\Pi^T\otimes\mathcal B_1\). The side and transpose matter. The eigenvalues \(0,1,-1\) of \(\Pi\) factor its determinant into

\[
(\det\mathbf a)^{48}\det\mathcal B_0
\det(\mathcal B_0+\mathcal B_1)^{(p-1)/2}
\det(\mathcal B_0-\mathcal B_1)^{(p-1)/2}.
\]

The manuscript specifies the rational construction and all 48 Gaussian pivots for each fixed matrix modulo 101. Its pivot table certifies nonzero rational determinants according to the author. The prime 101 is used only for the fixed certificate. Every sufficiently large scale prime avoids the finite set of numerator and denominator factors of these determinants and of the hypothetical denominator of \(G\).

The reduction of \(p^2\mathcal F_p\) is therefore invertible, yielding \(v_p(\Delta_p)=-2(48p)\). This is arithmetic nonvanishing of a signed determinant, without a positivity argument. [Original, §4, pp. 23–25 and Table 1.]

## 5. Both branches in the real-place estimate

Two Andréief identities and Cauchy's double alternant yield a double \(n\)-fold integral with one \(t\)-Vandermonde and two \(s\)-Vandermondes. Factorials and measure masses are retained. After \(t=2x/(1+x^2)\), the rows mix near evaluations at \(x\) and far evaluations at \(1/x\). The far/near coefficients are \((1/2,1/2)\) for negative \(x\), and \((-1/4,5/4)\) for positive \(x\).

For configurations satisfying

\[
\sum_i\frac{1-x_i^2}{1+x_i^2}\le D_*=n-1-2g,
\]

a Blaschke product and a Cauchy integral along the imaginary axis construct a holomorphic interpolant taking \(x_i^{D_*}\) at negative nodes and \(-5x_i^{D_*}\) at positive nodes. Its norm is at most \(10e^{12}n\), independent of node separation. Endpoint contour deformations bound one fixed glued function.

In the finite-dimensional Hardy space \(\{v/Q:\deg v<n\}\), reproducing kernels and coefficient reversal identify the evaluation determinant with the determinant of a bounded operator times the ordinary Vandermonde. The evaluation determinant cancels algebraically. An inverse-Vandermonde norm estimate is unnecessary.

For the complementary configurations, a sheet expansion and Hadamard's inequality on unit-circle Vandermondes give a second estimate. Its prefactor, like the first, is \(\exp(O(n\log n))\). The two leading majorants have \(x\)-Vandermonde exponents \(\kappa=2\) and \(\kappa=1\). The estimates cover arbitrary approaches to collisions and endpoints. Exceptional null sets use the original integrable formula. [Original, §5.]

## 6. Uniform energy reduction before taking suprema

Chebyshev and power expansions of the transformed logarithmic interactions are regularized with

\[
\tau=1-\varepsilon,\quad
\rho(x)=1-\varepsilon(1-x),\quad
\sigma(s)=1-\varepsilon\sqrt{1-s}.
\]

Matching the same damped moments in pure and cross terms produces

\[
-\kappa\sum A_k^2/k,\qquad
-\tfrac12\sum(B_k-2C_k)^2/k.
\]

The cross-term comparison is uniform near \((x,s)=(1,1)\). Removing empirical diagonals retains explicit endpoint logarithms. Their coefficients weaken by \(1/(2n)\) and \(1/n\), while staying positive. This places maximizers in common compact sets. First take \(n\to\infty\) with \(\varepsilon\) fixed, then \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). Pointwise undamped expansions would not justify this uniform supremum bound.

Exponentially decaying trial sequences \(p,v\), with \(\|u\|_*^2=\sum u_k^2/k\), replace the negative squares by affine upper bounds. Define

\[
T(u,x)=\sum u_kT_k(x)/k,\qquad S(u,x)=\sum u_kx^k/k.
\]

The bound becomes

\[
(-1+\alpha+\gamma)\log2+\kappa\|p\|_*^2+
\tfrac12\|v\|_*^2+\sup X_\kappa+\sup Y_\kappa,
\]

where \(\alpha=11/48,\beta=7/48,\gamma=4/48,\eta=2/48\),

\[
X_\kappa=W_\kappa+\lambda D-2\kappa T(p,\cdot)-S(v,\cdot),
\qquad Y_\kappa=\beta\log s+\gamma\log(1-s)+2T(v,s),
\]
\[
W_\kappa=(\alpha+2\gamma)\log|x|+2\eta\log(1-x)
-(\kappa/2+\alpha+\eta+\gamma)\log(1+x^2),
\quad D(x)=2\gamma-\frac{2x^2}{1+x^2}.
\]

This scalar \(D\) differs from the row polynomials \(D_r\). Case 2 requires \(\lambda=0\). In case 1, \(\lambda\ge0\) uses the failed interpolation condition with an \(O(1/n)\) cost of the stated sign. [Original, §6.]

Its irrationality follows. Voight's arithmetic volume formula gives

\[
\operatorname{vol}(\Gamma^1(\mathcal O)\backslash\mathbb H^3)
=\frac G3\prod_{\mathfrak p\mid\mathfrak D}(N\mathfrak p-1).
\]

Commensurability changes volume by a positive rational ratio, giving the asserted irrationality for orientable arithmetic hyperbolic three-orbifolds over \(\mathbb Q(i)\). The split Bianchi case has volume \(G/3\).
