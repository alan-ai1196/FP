## 1. Problem and claimed result

Let \(E/\mathbb Q\) be an elliptic curve and
\[
s_2(E)=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb Z_2}
       \operatorname{Sel}_{2^\infty}(E/\mathbb Q),
\]
with ordinary Kummer conditions at every place, including the real place. This is the full two-power Selmer corank, not the dimension of the finite two-Selmer group.

The main theorem claims that \(s_2(E)\leq1\) implies
\[
\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb Q)
=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)
=s_2(E)=r,\qquad \#\Sha(E/\mathbb Q)<\infty.
\]
It also claims
\[
Q_E=
\frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)\,\#E(\mathbb Q)_{\rm tors}^{\,2}}
 {r!\,\Omega_E\,\operatorname{Reg}_E
           \prod_{\ell\ {\rm finite}}c_\ell}
\in\mathbb Q_{>0},
\qquad
v_2(Q_E)=v_2\bigl(\#\Sha(E/\mathbb Q)\bigr).
\]

The formula determines the **2-adic valuation** of the BSD quotient. It does not by itself establish equality of the entire rational quotient with the order of \(\Sha\). The claimed finiteness of \(\Sha\) concerns the whole group. The hypotheses impose no residual-representation restriction and no restriction on reduction at two (PDF pp. 3–7, 158–160).

An all-prime corollary imports the separate unrestricted Selmer converse, OAI-1006-003. Once low corank at another prime gives rank zero or one and finite \(\Sha\), the ordinary two-power corank has the same small value. The present theorem then applies. That imported converse is not the proof of the main two-primary theorem.

The density corollary imports the separate OpenAI Goldfeld result. For a fixed curve, signed squarefree twist parameters ordered by absolute value are claimed to give corank-zero and corank-one subfamilies each of density one half. The pointwise theorem supplies the two-primary formula on their density-one union. The density statement therefore has an additional manuscript dependency.

## 2. Normalizations and architecture

The real period integrates over **all** real components. A connected period in an intermediate reciprocity formula must acquire the real-component correction before entering \(Q_E\). The height convention is
\[
H(P)=\lim_{m\to\infty}4^{-m}h_x(2^mP),
\qquad
B(P,Q)=\frac{H(P+Q)-H(P)-H(Q)}2,
\]
so \(B(P,P)=H(P)\). The regulator uses the entire free Mordell–Weil lattice. A sublattice contributes the square of its index.

For a curve already known to have analytic rank at most one, write
\[
X(E)=v_2\!\left(Q_E/\#\Sha(E/\mathbb Q)\right).
\]
The author combines three comparisons:

| Comparison | Claimed conclusion | Location |
|---|---|---|
| Positive comparison | Non-CM curves of known analytic rank at most one have \(X\geq0\). A positive fundamental twist with small analytic rank and \(X=0\) transfers a determinant unit to the original curve of small Selmer corank. | §§4–7 |
| Split-pair anchor | A twist and a further split imaginary twist have a simple product zero and discrepancies summing to zero. | §§8–10, 12–15 |
| CM comparison | CM curves of two-power corank zero or one satisfy the rank conclusion and \(X=0\) directly. | §11 |

The split construction first obtains ordinary Selmer corank one through finite descent. An integral determinant unit then detects a nontorsion Heegner trace. The exact volume calculation follows after analytic rank one is known. The diagram on p. 7 makes this dependency order explicit.

The Gross–Zagier normalizations retain the absolute height, a single Hilbert trace, restricted polarization and actual Manin constant. In the split elliptic setting, the discrepancy identity is
\[
X(E)+X(E^k)
=v_2\!\left(
\frac{[E(K):\mathbb ZP]^2}
 {c_E^2\prod_\ell c_\ell^2\,\#\Sha(E/K)}
\right).
\]
The imaginary field, splitting conditions and trace \(P\) are those of §2. The index includes torsion. The proof does not assume that \(c_E\) is a unit (PDF pp. 8–10).

\]

\]
with \(K'\) invertible. The Pfaffian \(\mathcal P\) and its adjugate give a global lift from a local input.

The cofactor calculation retains **products of repeated factors**. For

No pairwise coprimality is required. The local block comparison proves the factorization simultaneously on rows, boundary inputs and global projections. The p. 138 block matrix was checked visually.

## 12. Assembly and dependency boundaries

The residual cases exhaust rational invariant line, cyclic cubic, and \(S_3\) with imaginary or real sign field. Each non-CM branch supplies a simple product zero and \(X(E^h)+X(E^{hk})=0\). Positive nonnegativity makes both terms zero. Because \(k<0\), exactly one of \(h,hk\) is positive, supplying the unit anchor for the original curve. The CM comparison handles CM curves directly.

Only after analytic rank zero or one is established does classical Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin yield the Mordell–Weil rank and finiteness of the whole \(\Sha\). Rationality and the exact volume normalization complete the stated valuation identity.
