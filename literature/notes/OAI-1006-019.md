## 1. The theorem and the algebra it concerns

For an odd positive integer \(N\), let \(M_i(N)\) contain all holomorphic forms of weight \(i\) and level \(\Gamma_1(N)\), including Eisenstein forms. On the sum of weights \(1,\ldots,k\), take the integral algebra generated by \(T_\ell\) and \(\ell S_\ell\), for \(\ell\nmid2N\), where \(S_\ell=\ell^{i-2}\langle\ell\rangle\) in weight \(i\). Put
\[
 A_k=\mathbb Z_2\otimes_{\mathbb Z}T_{\le k}^{(2)}(N),
 \qquad A=T_2(N)=\varprojlim_k A_k.
\]
The topology is the inverse-limit topology of the \(2\)-adic finite-stage topologies.

Theorem 1.1 states that **every irreducible component of \(\operatorname{Spec}A\) has Krull dimension four**. It includes every residual system, with no residual irreducibility, nonscalarity, distinguishedness or slope assumption. This is the full varying-weight algebra, not one ordinary family or one eigencurve.

The lower bound is imported from Emerton, Corollary 2.28, in precisely this full-algebra setting. The paper establishes the matching upper bound. It constructs an integral determinant and Noetherian structure at two, finds an appropriate classical cuspidal point on each component, bounds the characteristic-zero tangent dimension by three, and adds the one-dimensional arithmetic quotient through catenarity.

The Frobenius convention throughout is
\[
 Z^2-T_\ell Z+\ell S_\ell.
\]
The adjoint is the full four-dimensional endomorphism representation. Determinants are allowed to vary.

## 2. Integral structure without dividing by two

Classical rationality, integrality and simultaneous semisimplicity embed every nonzero finite stage into a finite product of integer rings of finite \(2\)-adic fields. The finite-stage algebra is finite free over \(\mathbb Z_2\), and its image is closed. Restriction maps are surjective because they send the same generators to the smaller weight range.

Lemma 2.1 deduces that \(A\) is compact, Hausdorff and reduced. The intersection of all classical eigenvalue kernels is zero, so those points are Zariski dense. Polynomials in the specified Hecke generators are dense in \(A\). Weight-one and Eisenstein systems are included in these assertions.

The classical Galois representations give traces and determinants at each finite stage. Chebotarev density of Frobenius conjugacy classes and closedness of the finite-stage image put their values in \(A_k\) at every group element. Uniqueness gives compatible continuous functions \(t,d\) over \(A\).

The integral determinant law is constructed explicitly. For a finite formal sum \(\sum_g x_g g\), its quadratic polynomial is
\[
 \sum_g d(g)x_g^2+
 \sum_{g<h}\bigl(t(g)t(h)-t(gh)\bigr)x_gx_h.
\]
Every classical specialization is the determinant of the corresponding matrix sum. Polynomial identities for multiplicativity and normalization therefore hold in \(A\) by density. This constructs a degree-two multiplicative polynomial law over arbitrary scalar extensions, without the characteristic-two error of solving for a determinant by dividing a trace identity by two.

Proposition 2.3 uses Jochnowitz's finiteness of residual systems at fixed prime-to-two level. Maximal ideals at finite stages are all detected by classical reductions, by finite integral lying over. The sets of residual factors increase under surjective transitions but have bounded cardinality, hence stabilize. Their inverse limits give finitely many profinite local factors with finite residue fields.

Compatible Witt coefficient maps and Chenevier's determinant deformation theorem provide a complete Noetherian universal ring mapping to each factor. Mazur's finiteness condition follows from finiteness of quadratic extensions with restricted ramification. The image contains the Hecke generators and is compact, hence closed and surjective. Compact-to-Hausdorff comparison identifies the quotient topology with the prescribed topology. Consequently each factor is complete Noetherian local with its maximal-ideal topology.

This supplies the dyadic pseudodeformation step while preserving scalar and reducible residual systems. It does not substitute an ordinary trace-only pseudocharacter theorem that requires two to be invertible.

## 3. The Eisenstein interpolation map is constructed continuously

Bounded-weight points factor through some \(A_k\), so their closure has dimension at most one. Proposition 3.1 bounds the closure of all Eisenstein points by two.

Above weight two, their good-prime values have the form
\[
 T_\ell\mapsto\psi(\ell)+\phi(\ell)\ell^{i-1},\qquad
 \ell S_\ell\mapsto\psi(\ell)\phi(\ell)\ell^{i-1},
\]
for finitely many pairs of Dirichlet characters of conductors dividing \(N\), including coefficient embeddings. Partition by character pair and weight parity. Finite weight subfamilies are already covered by the first bound.

For an infinite subfamily, write \(\ell=s_\ell5^{b_\ell}\) in \(\mathbb Z_2^\times\). Replace \(\ell^{i-1}\) by the power series
\(s_\ell^{i-1}(1+X)^{b_\ell}\). Its arithmetic specializations are
\[
 X=x_i=5^{i-1}-1\in4\mathbb Z_2.
\]
An assignment on generators alone does not yet define a continuous map from the inverse-limit algebra. The paper takes the closed graph \(C\) of the paired generators inside \(A\times\mathcal O[[X]]\). Projection to \(A\) is surjective by compactness and density. Every \((a,F)\in C\) satisfies \(\lambda_i(a)=F(x_i)\).

If \((0,F)\) lies in that graph, \(F\) vanishes at infinitely many distinct \(x_i\). The series \(F(4Y)\) is restricted, so Strassmann's theorem makes it identically zero. Thus the projection is injective too and is a homeomorphism. Its inverse supplies the continuous interpolation map
\[
 \Phi:A\longrightarrow\mathcal O[[X]].
\]

The dimension bound on its image is also proved, rather than inferred from being a subring of a two-dimensional ring. Choose a prime \(\ell\equiv1\pmod N\), \(\ell\equiv5\pmod8\). The image contains \(u=(1+X)^{b_\ell}\), with \(b_\ell\) a unit. Binomial inversion and closedness put \(X\), hence \(\mathbb Z_2[[X]]\), in the image. A finite \(\mathbb Z_2\)-basis of \(\mathcal O\) makes \(\mathcal O[[X]]\) finite integral over that image. Its dimension is therefore two.

The finite union of these loci, together with weights one and two, cannot fill a component whose dimension is at least four. Removing them and the other components leaves a nonempty open subset containing the chosen generic point. Classical density gives a cuspidal point of weight at least three lying on that component alone. Its characteristic-zero Galois representation is absolutely irreducible, regardless of its residual representation.

## 4. The characteristic-zero tangent comparison

Proposition 4.1 applies more generally to a compact Hausdorff Noetherian topological \(\mathbb Z_p\)-algebra \(R\), topologically generated by the values of a continuous two-dimensional determinant of a profinite group \(H\). At a continuous coefficient point whose determinant comes from an absolutely irreducible \(r:H\to\operatorname{GL}_2(E)\), it proves
\[
 \dim R_{\mathfrak x}\le\operatorname{edim}R_{\mathfrak x}
 \le\dim_E H^1(H,\operatorname{ad}r).
\]

The proof explicitly handles continuity of algebraic tangent vectors. The coefficient quotient \(R/\mathfrak x\) is finite over \(\mathbb Z_p\), and its fraction field \(E_0\) is finite separable over \(\mathbb Q_p\). A coefficient field across the square-zero ideal identifies derivations with the dual of the cotangent space:
\[
 \operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb Q_p}(R_{\mathfrak x},E)
 \simeq
 \operatorname{Hom}_{E_0}(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2,E).
\]
Noetherianity makes \(R/\mathfrak x^2\) finite over \(\mathbb Z_p\). Finitely generated ideals are closed as compact images of finite powers of \(R\). The quotient topology is the usual finite-module topology, so every derivation restricted to \(R\) is continuous.

Specializing the determinant along \(\lambda+\epsilon D\) gives a continuous dual-number determinant. Chenevier's absolutely irreducible lifting theorem applies over the Henselian ring \(E[\epsilon]\), whose residual representation at this point is the characteristic-zero \(r\). It does not require mod-\(p\) residual irreducibility.

Continuity of the resulting representation follows from its trace. Choose four group elements whose \(r\)-matrices form a basis of \(M_2(E)\). A fixed inverse Gram matrix for the trace pairing expresses every lifted matrix coordinate through the continuous traces of products with those four elements.

Writing the lift as \((1+\epsilon c(g))r(g)\) gives a continuous adjoint cocycle. The trace functions of tangent derivations lie in the image of the linear map from \(H^1\) taking \([c]\) to \(g\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(c(g)r(g))\). Coboundaries map to zero. The proof compares these function-space images, without assuming a chosen representation lift depends linearly on the derivation.

The derivation-to-traces map is injective. At this characteristic-zero step one may use
\[
 2d(g)=t(g)^2-t(g^2).
\]
Vanishing on all traces forces vanishing on determinants, then on the dense generator algebra, then on its closure by continuity. This division by two occurs only after passing to \(E\), not in the preceding integral determinant construction.

## 5. The adjoint Selmer and local dimension calculations

For the selected odd-level cusp form of weight \(k\ge3\), set \(V=\operatorname{End}(r)\). The global input is Newton–Thorne, Theorem 5.4: the finite full-adjoint Selmer group vanishes for a regular algebraic cuspidal representation that is non-CM, or whose CM field is not contained in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^\infty})\).

Odd level checks the CM qualification. The local smooth automorphic representation at two is unramified. A ramified quadratic self-twist cannot preserve its unramified Weil–Deligne parameter. Its CM quadratic character is therefore unramified at two, whereas all three quadratic subfields of the cyclotomic two-power extension are ramified there. Thus \(H^1_f(\mathbb Q,V)=0\), without residual restrictions.

Local Tate duality reduces the \(H^2\) terms to
\(\operatorname{Hom}(r,r(1))\). At two, the two Hodge–Tate weights differ by \(k-1\ge2\), so their set is disjoint from its shift by one. A nonzero map would have a Hodge–Tate image with weights in both sets. This excludes it without assuming local absolute irreducibility. Away from two, Carayol local-global compatibility and Allen's genericity criterion exclude the corresponding Weil–Deligne morphism, including after Frobenius semisimplification.

For \(v\ne2\), local Euler characteristic and unramified cohomology have the same dimension \(h^0_v\), so \(H^1/H^1_f=0\). At two,
\[
 h^1_2=h^0_2+4,\qquad
 h^1_{f,2}=h^0_2+1.
\]
The second identity uses Bloch–Kato and the adjoint's de Rham filtration degrees \(0,0,h,-h\), with \(h\ne0\). The unknown \(h^0_2\) cancels. No Schur-lemma value of one is required here.

Restriction from \(H^1(G_{\mathbb Q,S},V)\) to these local quotients is injective because its kernel is contained in the vanishing global finite Selmer group. Its target has dimension three. This proves the tangent upper bound needed at the classical point.

The corresponding argument in OAI-1006-018 uses different sufficient hypotheses: its auxiliary point may have dyadic level and weight two, so it uses local absolute irreducibility and a Weil–Deligne parameter trivial on wild inertia. The current paper uses odd level and weight at least three. These hypotheses should not be interchanged merely because the final bound is the same.

## 6. From a coefficient point to component dimension

For a minimal prime \(P\), take the classical point \(\mathfrak x\) constructed on its component. The tangent and cohomology results give \(\dim A_{\mathfrak x}\le3\). The quotient \(A/P\) is a complete Noetherian local domain, hence catenary. The coefficient quotient \(A/\mathfrak x\) is finite over \(\mathbb Z_2\) and contains \(\mathbb Z_2\), so it has dimension one. Consequently,
\[
 \dim A/P
 =\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak x/P)+\dim A/\mathfrak x
 \le\dim A_{\mathfrak x}+1\le4.
\]
Emerton supplies the opposite inequality. Since \(P\) was arbitrary, every component has dimension exactly four.

The extra one is the integral arithmetic dimension of the coefficient quotient. The theorem is a Krull-dimension statement for this full integral Hecke algebra. It should not be restated as a four-dimensional individual eigencurve.
