## 1. Original questions, claimed conclusions and scope

For the totient fiber size \(g(n)=\#\{m\ge1:\varphi(m)=n\}\), Theorem 1.1 asserts that every \(\varepsilon>0\) admits infinitely many \(n\) with \(g(n)>n^{1-\varepsilon}\). The paper identifies this as Erdős's conjectural optimal power exponent, with the upper estimate \(g(n)\ll_\eta n^{1+\eta}\) to be proved in its final section.

The arithmetic input, Theorem 1.2, claims that for each fixed \(0<\delta<1/4\),
\[
 \#\{2x<p\le5x:P^+(p-1)\le x^\delta\}\ge x^{1-o(1)}.
\]
The error may depend on \(\delta\). Smaller fixed exponents extend the conclusion to every fixed \(\delta>0\), without uniformity as \(\delta\to0\). This is a full power-exponent lower bound, not a positive limiting proportion or a Dickman law. The companion OAI-1006-022 is said to require a separate determinant-graph estimate and extraction argument for the Poisson–Dirichlet law. Those companion proofs are not read by reading this introduction.

The intended mechanism is to count primes \(2u+1\) with a prescribed factorization of \(u\), using marked small and big prime groups, geometric prime bands and a long rough-integer factor. A graph transference theorem and small independent-label kernels give shifted cancellation. A determinant identity later converts it to Type II estimates, and an independent congruence/proxy/sieve branch removes composites. The final product-and-pigeonhole transfer to totient multiplicities remains unread at this checkpoint.

## 2. Parameters and elementary analytic tools — §2

The scales are \(L=\log x\), \(T=\log L\), and \(W=\exp(\sqrt L)\). Every auxiliary constant is fixed before \(x\to\infty\). The order is sieve geometry, desired Type II accuracy, physical and ideal norm targets, comparison kernels, mark length \(J\), character/Mellin discrepancy, then cofactor-proxy precision. Constants may depend on earlier choices but cannot silently reverse this order.

The paper states Siegel–Walfisz with possibly ineffective constants and the multiplicative large sieve. Partial summation gives absolute harmonic nonprincipal-character bounds on arbitrary subintervals above \(\exp(L^c)\), including very short intervals. It does not assume relative prime asymptotics in every short interval. Fixed divisor moments control coefficient-square sums for a fixed number of convolution factors. The elementary Dirichlet-polynomial mean square has constant independent of the factorization used to create the coefficients. Fourier separation chooses a fixed logarithmic frequency cutoff exponent before the desired error, then increases derivative order to improve the error.

The block sieve partitions primes into \((z^{2^{-j-1}},z^{2^{-j}}]\), with even truncation degrees \(h_j=(j+1)h\). If \(U_j\) is the even inclusion–exclusion upper polynomial and \(E_j\) its next elementary-symmetric term, then
\[
 U=\prod_jU_j,\qquad
 V=U-\sum_jE_j\prod_{k\ne j}U_k
\]
bound the exact avoidance indicator. The upper, lower and correction supports are disjoint by the uniquely overfull block. Therefore their coefficients have absolute value at most one and are supported on squarefree \(d\le z^{4h+2}\). Under \(g(p)\le1-\eta_0\) and bounded block harmonic density, their independent expectations have relative error \(O(e^{-h})\), uniformly in growing sufficiently large even \(h\). The fixed choice \(h=2\) supplies a constant-factor upper bound.

For rough integers the nonnegative overcount has total at most \(|I|e^{-h}+z^{4h+2}\) on any finite interval, and the upper-polynomial coefficients satisfy \(\sum_d|\lambda_d|/d\ll\log(2z)\). Positivity is used for the overcount, while the correction coefficients may have signs.

## 3. Physical and independent-label operators — §3.1

Small prime groups lie between \(\exp(L^a)\) and \(\exp(L^b)\), big groups between \(\exp(L^c)\) and \(\exp(L^d)\), with
\[
 0<a<b<c<d<0.47.
\]
The groups are pairwise disjoint, each class has \(\asymp T\) groups, and their harmonic masses \(V_g=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_g}1/p\) are bounded above and below. Their label law is \(\mu_g(p)=1/(V_gp)\). Group counts \(\omega_g(n)\) count distinct prime divisors, regardless of multiplicity in \(n\).

There are \(M=J+\ell\) ordered slots in every group. A pattern frees at most \(m\) of the first \(J\) big slots and none of the first \(J\) small slots. The last \(\ell\) slots are always unshared. The dilation product \(D\) uses the remaining shared labels and independent auxiliary labels in the freed positions. Its auxiliary labels are forbidden from both endpoint active lists but may equal one another. Coefficients depend only on big labels and have total supremum mass at most \(L^{C_1}\), with \(m,C_1\) independent of \(J\).

The ideal operator acts on iid big-group lists, allowing repetitions, with multiplier \(K_\nu D_{\mathcal B}^{i\zeta}e(\Theta D_{\mathcal B})\). Both endpoints are symmetrized. A physical state is \((n,\mathbf p)\), where each group's active labels are distinct and divide \(n\). Its mass is \(\prod_gV_g^{-M}\). The physical edge changes \(n\) to \(n+kD\), sums the admissible target lists, and includes the two endpoint damping factors \(q^{(\omega(n)-Ms)/2}\). The reverse joint normalization is \(V_g^{-M-t_g}\), which establishes the stated adjoint. Absolute row and column bounds are \(L^{C_{\rm abs}}\), with exponent independent of \(J\), because only \(t_g\le\ell+m\) slots are newly summed.

Theorem 3.5 transfers a uniform ideal norm \(\sup_\Theta\|\mathcal T(\Theta)\|\le L^{-E_{\rm id}}\) to
\[
 \frac1{|I\cap\mathbb Z|}\sum_{n\in I}
 \langle u_n,(\mathcal A\mathcal A^*)^R u_n\rangle
 \le L^{-EN},\qquad
 R=\lceil L^{0.52}\rceil,\quad N=2R.
\]
Here \(I=[X,2X)\), \(x^\gamma\le X\le x^{1/\gamma}\), and \(u_n\) is one on every active list at position \(n\), not a normalized vector. Paths return to the integer position, while initial and final lists can differ. The ideal accuracy depends on the physical accuracy and fixed group data before the complexity-dependent choice of large \(J\). The theorem itself needs no separate bound on \(\zeta\) beyond the ideal hypothesis.

## 4. Residue averaging, lifespans and factorial memory — §§3.2–3.5

A fixed path has offsets \(h_i\). The product of its active congruence moduli has logarithm \(O_J(NTL^d)=o(L)\), using \(0.52+d<1\). Factor out deterministic damping from active primes, then truncate the remaining Bonferroni expansion at order \(O(NT)\). CRT counting errors are exponentially small compared with the active density and the independent damping mass. This gives a relative residue replacement even when deterministic factors are tiny. Absolute path bounds permit summing that relative error. Exact return to the integer root is imposed by \(\int_0^1e(\theta h_N)\,d\theta\).

For each big prime, put \(q_0=q_N=\sqrt q\), \(q_i=q\) internally, \(\eta_i=1-q_i\), and \(b_p=1-p^{-1}\sum_i\eta_i\). Prefix and suffix telescoping attach optional ghost endpoints to the first and last active visits. For a never-active prime, grouping the product expansion by its earliest and latest selected hits produces an orphan interval. Each represented numerical prime has one lifespan and pays \((pb_p)^{-1}\) only once. If it has \(r\) active runs, its total label factor is \(V_g^{-r}/(pb_p)\). Repeated congruence tests inside the lifespan must not pay further reciprocal factors.

The memory space uses pending particles \((p,r)\), with tilted prime law \(\widetilde\mu_g(p)=1/(V_gpb_p)\), **counting measure** on residues \(r\bmod p\), and factorial measure \(1/j!\) on symmetric \(j\)-particle lists. A zero-residue test therefore selects one point without introducing \(1/p\). Factorial measures belong to inner products and adjoints, not repeated row-transition factors.

At visits, old zero particles may terminate, continuing zeros are damped, and an unordered ghost batch may be born. Termination precedes birth. Across an edge, active particles may be stored, pending residues translate by the graph step, and old particles reaching zero may be promoted into ordered target slots with factor \(V_g^{-1}\). Newly stored particles cannot immediately be promoted. Stored or promoted primes must not divide \(D\). For large \(x\), all group primes exceed \(|k|\), so physical compatibility makes a prime active at both edge endpoints precisely a prescribed shared label.

Choose \(\rho^2>\sqrt q\). Splitting an internal hit as \(\rho(q_i/\rho^2)\rho\) and ghost endpoints as \((-\eta_i/\rho)\rho\) gives the exact original factors, including boundaries. With distinct numerical primes at different births, paths and lifespan assignments correspond bijectively. Birth costs and later promotions give exactly the factor above.

The proof then temporarily allows coincident births. Weighted Schur bounds with \(v^{j_{\rm tot}}\) control the entire memory evolution. Explicit adjoint accounting uses
\[
 \frac{(j)_a}{j!}=\frac1{(j-a)!}
 =\frac{(j-a+b)_b}{(j-a+b)!}.
\]
Ordered retrieval multiplicities remain valid even on numerical diagonals. Ghost adjoints move a \(V_g\) factor between birth and termination, without creating a prime factor. Edge reversal has only a bounded \(V_g^{b-a}\) adjustment. Truncating total memory at \(B=\lceil L^2\rceil\) loses \(\exp(-\Omega(L^2))\): doubling ghost-birth weights controls the tail, while stores can create only \(O(sN)\) particles.

## 5. Rare transfers and restoration of distinct births — §§3.6–3.9

An edge with any store or promotion is treated separately. Symmetrization omits an independent \(\ell\)-subset in every small group. The \(\binom{J+\ell}{\ell}^{|\mathcal S|}\) products of omitted primes are distinct positive integers smaller than every eligible big prime. Each pending particle can satisfy the promotion congruence for at most one omission choice. Thus these edges have norm at most
\[
 2L^{C_a}\sqrt{B/\binom{J+\ell}{\ell}^{|\mathcal S|}}.
\]
The reversed column estimate handles stores. The coefficient restriction to big labels is essential for unbiased small omission choices.

For an edge with no transfer, condition on shared small labels and use endpoint maps whose squared norms sum to at most \(L^{C_s}\), with \(C_s\) independent of \(J,m,C_1\). Fourier transformation on the small Haar torus and pending counting-measure residue spaces turns all translations into an additive phase for the ideal big-label operator. The tilted densities and the auxiliary/active collision ban cost superlogarithmically small perturbations. The resulting norm is at most \(2L^{-E_{\rm id}+C_s}\).

Choose the physical contraction exponent, then \(E_{\rm id}\), then \(J\) large enough for rare transfers. Ghost bounds cost \(L^{C_v}\) with \(C_v\) independent of kernel complexity. This produces a small signed edge norm before distinct births are restored.

A single absolute collision bound would be insufficient against \(L^{O(N)}\) path cost. Instead expand global distinctness over blocks of equal birth addresses, with coefficient \(\prod_B(-1)^{|B|-1}(|B|-1)!\) and equality rank \(r=\sum_B(|B|-1)\). A rank-\(r\) collection involves at most \(2r\) addresses and has aggregate coefficient cost at most \(L^{C'r}\).

At high rank, chronological fresh integrations pay \(O(P_*^{-1})\) for each dependent birth, where \(P_*=\exp(L^c)\). Pivot values are retained in the conditioning even after their particles die. This gives \(L^{C_h(N+1)}(C/P_*)^r\). At low rank, Fourier equalities are inserted only at birth operations. At most \(2r\) edges lose signed contraction. Ghost-batch phases are symmetrized inside the existing factorial integral, and their modulus remains at most one. No phase must follow a particle through later transfers. The threshold \(r_0\asymp NT/\log P_*\) has \(r_0/N=o(1)\), so high rank is controlled by point masses and low rank retains contraction on \(N-o(N)\) edges. Restoring tails, baseline and CRT errors proves the transference moment.

The pairing corollary needs only the first endpoint to be constant on its mark fiber. If its scalar magnitude is at most \(\exp(O(\sqrt L))\) and both endpoint norms are at most \(X^{1/2}L^{C_3}\), it gives \(|\langle f,\mathcal Ag\rangle|\ll XL^{-E+2C_3}\). Partitioning positions into maximum-step blocks, spectral Hölder and the trace bound for a positive semidefinite root matrix establish this without normalizing \(u_n\) or requiring list return.

## 6. Kernels uniform in additive frequency — §4

Big groups are now intervals of primes. For every desired ideal accuracy and fixed Mellin-frequency exponent, Theorem 4.1 constructs raw pattern plus signed comparison patterns, with probe number \(m\), total coefficient exponent and all kernel parameters fixed before \(J\). Coefficients are independent of additive frequency and the individual Mellin frequency.

A selected product using at most \(m\) slots per group has atom mass at most \(L^\kappa/n\). Collapsing an arbitrary ordered-tuple test onto its product by fiberwise Cauchy–Schwarz gives the needed integer coefficient-square bound. The test need not depend only on the product. An elementary bilinear exponential-sum estimate on arbitrary containing intervals, using separated rational-approximation phases, handles large denominators. Multiplicative Mellin phases separate between the tests.

For a nonempty slot set, the comparison kernel matches logarithmic cells and sums pairs of primitive Dirichlet characters of conductor at most \(Q\). Cells of mass below \(\xi\) are discarded. Holding every label but one fixed turns a cell condition into a possibly short prime interval. Absolute Siegel–Walfisz bounds make distinct primitive characters nearly orthogonal there. The conditional character Gram matrix has norm at most two. Its operator bound loses neither a cell-count factor nor an inverse retained-cell mass.

For small rational denominators, expand the rational additive phase in characters. Matched logarithmic cells control the remaining smooth phase, and the character frame reproduces each required induced character. Discarded-cell error is bounded in the bilinear pairing by independence of the two tuple spaces. It is not claimed that restriction to a small-probability event has small \(L^2\) operator norm. The precision order is dyadic error, denominator cutoffs, log-cell width, retained-cell mass, then character accuracy. All precede \(J\).

Split the big groups into two blocks and designate \(m\) probes per group. A comparison frees nonempty probe subsets \(A,B\), with coefficient
\[
 -(-1)^{|A|+|B|}\mathcal K_A(D_A,X_A)\mathcal K_B(D_B,X_B),
\]
where \(X_A\) uses target labels and \(X_B\) input labels. This asymmetry makes the unused input \(A\) coordinates and target \(B\) coordinates average out exactly.

Average the last \(\ell\) slots, then apply the orthogonal Efron–Stein decomposition to the first-block input probes and second-block target probes. Symmetry of the original \(M\)-slot vector gives the retained fraction
\[
 \frac{\binom{M-m-\ell}{j-m}}{\binom Mj}
 =\frac{(j)_m(M-j)_\ell}{(M)_{m+\ell}}
 \le2\ell^\ell m^{-\ell}=\theta_m.
\]
The estimate tensorizes through grouped cardinalities even for vectors not factored across groups. Components mean zero in all probes of one block consequently have norm at most \(\theta_m^{s_i/2}\).

For all other component pairs, alternating nonempty free-subset sums equal one, and the initial negative sign cancels the raw pairing. Conditional tuple comparison is uniform at the arbitrary frequency obtained after fixing shared labels. Exceptional raw pairs are summed before taking absolute values, avoiding a subset-count loss. Approximation errors pay at most \(3^P\), with \(P=O(mT)\). Choose \(m\) for the full-component norm, comparison accuracy for this subset cost, then kernel parameters and coefficient exponent. Only afterward choose \(J\) large enough for both the symmetric-probe fraction and the transference rare-transfer bound. This completes the ideal-kernel theorem without a circular precision choice.
