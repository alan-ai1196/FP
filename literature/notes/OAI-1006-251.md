## Question, model and main result

The question is the state cost of replacing nondeterminism by determinism when a finite automaton may move its input head both ways. The paper gives an exponential lower bound for unrestricted deterministic two-way simulation, already when the source machine never moves left. This concerns exact recognition of all finite words.

The machine has one read-only head and a finite state set. Two distinct endmarkers bracket the input. The head starts on the left marker in a specified state. A transition depends only on the current state and scanned symbol and moves left, right or stays in place. Endmarkers cannot be crossed. Deterministic transitions may be partial. All states, including initial and accepting states, count. There is no work tape, writable cell, extra head, advice or randomness. A finite visit to an accepting state accepts. An infinite computation with no such visit rejects. The target has no restriction on interior head reversals, running time or input length. [§1.1, p. 2, §4, p. 13]

For a set \(H=[h]\), let \(\mathcal R_H\) be the monoid of **all** binary relations on \(H\). Products follow paths from left to right:

\[
(x,z)\in AB
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists y\ ((x,y)\in A,\ (y,z)\in B).
\]

The language is

\[
\mathrm{OWL}_h
=\{R_1\cdots R_\ell\in(\mathcal R_H)^*:R_1\cdots R_\ell\ne0\}.
\]

The empty product is \(I_H\), so the empty word is accepted. The alphabet has \(2^{h^2}\) letters and grows with \(h\).

**Theorem 1.1.** For every \(h\ge2\), an automaton with \(h+3\) states and no left moves recognizes this language. Every equivalent deterministic two-way automaton with \(s\) states satisfies

\[
4(s+2)^2\ \ge\ 2^{\lfloor(h-2)/31\rfloor}.
\]

If an accepting initial configuration counts as acceptance, \(s+2\) can be replaced by \(s+1\). The displayed general version accommodates the convention that acceptance must follow a positive number of transitions. With source size \(n=h+3\), it gives

\[
s\ge 2^{\frac12\lfloor(n-5)/31\rfloor-1}-2.
\]

Corollary 1.2 rules out absolute constants \(C,c>0\) giving a \(Cn^c\)-state deterministic two-way equivalent for every \(n\)-state nondeterministic two-way machine over every finite alphabet. The source subclass here is already one-way. The quantifier is uniform over growing finite alphabets. The paper makes no separate fixed-alphabet claim. [§1.1, §5 and Remark 5.3]

## Algebraic objects and the two corner reductions

The proof uses the Brauer matching monoid \(\mathcal D_m\). An element pairs \(m\) left ports and \(m\) right ports perfectly. Drawing crossings have no significance. Multiplication glues corresponding middle ports, contracts external paths and discards closed components that have no outer port.

Rank is the number of left-to-right pairs. Support is the set of indices not fixed by the corresponding straight left-to-right pair. The basic bounds are

\[
\operatorname{rank}(ab)\le
\min\{\operatorname{rank}(a),\operatorname{rank}(b)\},
\qquad
m-\operatorname{rank}(a)\le|\operatorname{supp}(a)|.
\]

The rank-\(m\) elements are permutation diagrams. The only full-rank idempotent is the identity. Conjugating by permutation diagrams preserves rank. Elements supported in one fixed set form a submonoid. [§2.1]

### Matching corners, Lemma 2.2

For an idempotent \(e\) of rank \(r\), the corner \(e\mathcal D_m e\) admits an injective unital homomorphism

\[
R_e:e\mathcal D_m e\longrightarrow\mathcal D_r
\]

that preserves absolute rank. Also,

\[
|\operatorname{supp}(e)|\le2(m-r).
\]

The family statement is essential: for any fixed \(J\subseteq[m]\), there is **one** \(J'\subseteq[r]\), with \(|J'|\le|J|\), such that every \(a\) supported in \(J\) has \(R_e(eae)\) supported in \(J'\). A different support bound for each \(a\) would not supply the later common budget.

The proof retains the transversal endpoints \(X,Y\) of \(e\), whose pairing defines \(p:X\to Y\). In the connector between two copies of \(e\), idempotence forces a retained right endpoint \(y\) to connect to the left endpoint \(p^{-1}(y)\). Numbering \(x\) and \(p(x)\) alike makes this connector the identity. Only the prescribed outer caps are removed. Additional caps among retained ports remain, so rank and multiplication are preserved and the deleted caps can be restored to recover the original element.

Indices in \(X\cap Y\) are fixed, which bounds support by the two complements. For the common-support assertion, mark the transversal connector paths using a middle identity edge indexed in \(J\). Distinct paths are disjoint, so at most \(|J|\) are marked. Replacing the identity by any \(a\) supported in \(J\) cannot attach to an unmarked path, whose internal incidences are already paired. Figure 1 illustrates the inverse connector and the retained-port numbering. [§2.2, pp. 5–7]

### Minimum-rank lifts and relation corners

Lemma 2.3 supplies the group structure needed by the induction. Under a surjective homomorphism from a finite matching subsemigroup, choose a minimum-rank idempotent over an idempotent image. Reduction gives a smaller matching corner mapping onto the corresponding image corner. Every unit in that image corner has a permutation-diagram lift. The proof uses finite idempotent powers and minimality of the chosen rank. This is why permutation lifts can be obtained inside a suitably reduced corner even when they were not given in the original representation.

Lemma 2.4 concerns the full relation monoid. If \(P^2=P\), \(F\subseteq H\), and

\[
iPi=i,\qquad i=I_F,\qquad Q=PiP,
\]

then \(Q^2=Q\), \(PQ=QP=Q\), and restriction gives a unital isomorphism

\[
Q\mathcal R_HQ\longrightarrow\mathcal R_F,\qquad Z\longmapsto iZi.
\]

Its inverse sends \(A\) to \(PAP=QAQ\). The proof shows that a corner element is determined by its restriction and checks multiplication explicitly. Surjectivity onto the **entire** relation monoid is needed later to lift arbitrary smaller-corner elements. A representation of only the displayed relation generators would not suffice. [§§2.3–2.4, pp. 7–8]

## Exponential rank loss

Set \(L(h)=2^{\lfloor(h-2)/31\rfloor}\). Theorem 3.1 assumes an identity-containing \(S\subseteq\mathcal D_m\), a unital surjection \(\phi:S\to\mathcal R_H\), and permutation lifts for every permutation of \(H\). If \(a\) is an idempotent with

\[
\phi(a)=I_H\cup\{(x,y)\},\qquad x\ne y,
\]

then \(m-\operatorname{rank}(a)\ge L(h)\).

The induction converts a small common support into many successive losses of absolute rank. Write \(c=m-\operatorname{rank}(a)\). The base \(2\le h<33\) has \(L(h)=1\). An idempotent mapping to a nonidentity relation cannot be the identity, hence has positive rank defect.

For the step, choose disjoint sets \(U,V\) of size \(t=16\) and an extra point \(z\). Permutation conjugates of \(a\) give 16 additions \(u_i\to z\) and 16 additions \(z\to v_j\). Each conjugate has support at most \(2c\). Their union \(J\) therefore has size at most \(4tc=64c\).

Choose an idempotent lift \(b_0\) of \(P_0=I_{H\setminus\{z\}}\). Sandwiching the two relevant conjugates by \(b_0\) yields a lift \(g_{ij}\) of \(P_0\cup\{(u_i,v_j)\}\). After reducing once at \(b_0\), all 256 such generators share a set of at most \(64c\) support indices. Figure 2 distinguishes the two families of alternative arrows from the single chosen relation addition.

Order the 256 pairs. Let \(P_\ell=P_0\cup E_\ell\), where \(E_\ell\) contains the first \(\ell\) pairs. Since additions go only from \(U\) to disjoint \(V\), their products add edges by union without creating further compositions. All \(P_\ell\) are idempotent. Construct \(b_\ell\) as a positive idempotent power of \(b_{\ell-1}g_{ij}b_{\ell-1}\), keeping nested corners and image \(P_\ell\). With

\[
r_\ell=\operatorname{rank}(b_\ell),\qquad
\Delta_\ell=r_{\ell-1}-r_\ell,
\]

the one coordinate system at \(b_0\) supplies

\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^{256}\Delta_\ell=r_0-r_{256}\le64c.
\]

For each addition \((u_i,v_j)\), retain

\[
F=\{u_i,v_j\}\cup\bigl(H\setminus(U\cup V\cup\{z\})\bigr),
\qquad |F|=h-31.
\]

That pair has not yet been added, so \(I_FP_{\ell-1}I_F=I_F\). Lemma 2.4 supplies the full relation corner on \(F\). Reduce first at \(b_{\ell-1}\), and then at a minimum-rank idempotent over that relation corner. Lemma 2.3 supplies permutation lifts in the second reduction. The reduced \(b_\ell\) has support at most \(2\Delta_\ell\), and the family-support lemma transports that bound through the second sandwich. The sandwich need not itself be idempotent, so a positive idempotent power is taken after reduction.

The induction hypothesis on \(F\) now gives

\[
L(h-31)\le2\Delta_\ell.
\]

Summing all 256 inequalities and combining with the global budget yields

\[
128L(h-31)\le64c,
\qquad c\ge2L(h-31)=L(h).
\]

Table 1 records the 32 conjugates, 256 additions, common budget \(64c\), retained size \(h-31\) and lower loss per addition. The local changes of coordinates certify each already defined absolute rank loss. The total support is charged only in the fixed \(b_0\) coordinates. [§3, pp. 8–12]

Corollary 3.2 removes the permutation-lift assumption: any identity-containing submonoid of \(\mathcal D_k\) mapping unitally onto \(\mathcal R_H\) must have \(k\ge L(h)\). Choose a minimum-rank idempotent over \(I_H\), reduce, obtain the required lifts, and apply Theorem 3.1 to an idempotent lift of one off-diagonal addition. [pp. 12–13]

## Encoding an arbitrary deterministic machine locally

Theorem 4.1 assigns each input symbol and endmarker a matching in \(\mathcal D_k\), where \(k=4(s+1)^2\) under initial-configuration acceptance. The product for the whole marked input pairs a left test port with a right test port exactly when the machine accepts. Every symbol's matching is independent of input length, position and neighboring symbols.

First add one sink state \(f\). Any accepting state goes to \(f\) by a stay move, even if its old transition was undefined. State \(f\) moves right until its transition is undefined at the right marker. Reaching the designated sink configuration \(t\) is equivalent to original acceptance. In a partial functional graph, the undirected component containing a sink consists precisely of the configurations reaching it and is a tree. Other components may contain cycles. No global halting assumption is inserted. [§4.1]

For any finite tree and any cyclic ordering of incidences at each vertex, arriving along an edge and departing along the next incidence gives one cycle through all directed edge traversals. The proof removes a leaf and shows how reinsertion splices two traversals into the existing cycle. Opening the joins at two distinct leaves leaves two paths between their tips. [Lemma 4.3, §4.2]

The locality construction reserves a slot at every cell boundary for

\[
(d,p,q)\in\{\leftarrow,\rightarrow\}\times[N]\times[N],
\qquad N=s+1.
\]

Each slot has two travel lanes, giving \(2N^2\cdot2=4N^2\) ports per side. Its target end is **always** attached to the designated target state \(q\). Its source end is attached to \(p\) only when the local scanned symbol chooses that transition. Otherwise it attaches to a fresh leaf, different for every unused slot. The target cell therefore need not know the neighbor's symbol. Unused slots create no new state-to-state connection. Actual stay edges remain inside their cell, including loops with two distinct incidences. [§4.3 and Figure 3, pp. 15–16]

Outward marker boundaries receive test edges from the initial configuration and the sink to separate outer leaves. Their lane tips use ports 1 and 2. All remaining outward ports are paired independently. The empty input still has two distinct adjacent marker cells and both test edges, so the same construction applies.

Choose the local cyclic joins from local incidence roles. The unnumbered p. 16 table fixes the orientation convention:

| Cell boundary | Rightward lane | Leftward lane |
|---|---|---|
| Left side | Incoming tip | Outgoing tip |
| Right side | Outgoing tip | Incoming tip |

Equal-index gluing then connects an outgoing tip to the neighboring incoming tip. Internal points have degree two and exposed ports degree one. Contracting the paths within one cell gives a perfect matching. Contracting before or after gluing preserves the same external pairing.

In the accepting case the two test leaves lie in the sink tree. The opened tree tour creates two paths crossing between the two sides. Conversely, a crossing test path puts the initial configuration and sink in the same component and therefore implies acceptance. Figure 4 depicts the exposed test-port pairing. Other components do not affect it.

For positive-transition acceptance, first add a nonaccepting copy of the initial state whose transition row copies the original initial row and whose destinations are original states. This removes time-zero acceptance while preserving later behavior. The additional sink then gives \(k=4(s+2)^2\). The diagram vertices and wires encode the computation graph algebraically. They are not extra memory available to the automaton. [pp. 17–18 and Remark 5.3]

## Recovering the relation monoid and concluding the bound

The source recognizer uses \(h\) path states plus an initial, accepting and rejecting state. At the left marker it chooses a path state. Reading \(R\) in state \(p\), it may move right to any \(q\) with \((p,q)\in R\). Missing choices enter a rejecting stay loop. Reaching the right marker in a path state accepts. This recognizes nonempty relation products, including the empty word, with \(h+3\) states and no left moves. [Proposition 5.1]

Let \(d(w)\) be the product of ordinary-symbol matchings for a deterministic recognizer and \(r(w)\) its relation product. Equal \(d(u),d(v)\) make the two words indistinguishable in every surrounding input context. If \(r(u)\ne r(v)\), choose an entry \((i,j)\) in exactly one relation. The single-letter contexts \(I_{\{i\}}\) and \(I_{\{j\}}\) isolate that entry and distinguish nonzero from zero products.

Thus \(d(w)\mapsto r(w)\) is well-defined on the generated matching submonoid. It is multiplicative, unital including the empty product, and onto because every relation occurs as a single letter. Corollary 3.2 applies to its degree, while Theorem 4.1 bounds that degree by \(4(s+1)^2\) or \(4(s+2)^2\). This proves the stated state lower bound. [Lemma 5.2 and final proof, pp. 18–19]

## Dependencies, evidence and remaining scope

The manuscript proves the matching-corner, relation-corner, rank-loss and local tree-tour facts it uses. The 17 references place these arguments in finite-automaton and semigroup literature. Rabin–Scott and Shepherdson concern recognizability. Sakoda–Sipser supplies the state-simulation setting and complete language family. Sipser and Kapoutsis distinguish sweeping and reversal-restricted lower bounds. Chrobak and its erratum concern unary automata. The 2018 short-liveness result has two- and three-symbol restrictions. Adeogun–Kapoutsis's two 2026 revisions provide the immediate quadratic-bound context. Brauer, Auinger, East–Gray and Pin supply matching/semigroup background. Sipser's halting argument and Lange–McKenzie–Tapp supply the historical configuration-tree traversal context. Reading their citations here does not mark those papers fully read.

The companion [OAI-1006-250](OAI-1006-250.md), already fully read separately, concerns nondeterministic complementation and uses a different exponential rank-loss argument. The two notes preserve their distinct machine problems, algebraic representations and constants.

There are no experiments, datasets, empirical accuracy claims or training procedures. The result counts automaton states for exact language recognition. It does not bound description length including the \(2^{h^2}\)-letter transition table, give a fixed-alphabet separation, establish a uniform complexity-class separation, or price acquisition of an unknown transition law. Diagram degree is an intermediate algebraic resource.

No FP transfer is asserted. A predictive learner with noisy observations, charged communication, approximate risk or a restricted input distribution would require a specified reduction and its own resource correspondence. Potential further reading is limited to a concrete question about those model changes or a newer relevant result. No routine independent reproof or formal replay of this official paper is queued.
