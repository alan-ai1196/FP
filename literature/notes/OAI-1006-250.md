## Original problem and exact resource model

The question is whether an n-state two-way nondeterministic finite automaton can always be complemented with a number of states polynomial in n, using one polynomial that works for every finite alphabet.

The machine has one read-only head and a finite set of control states. It begins on the left endmarker. Transitions can move left, move right or stay. Moves beyond either endmarker are forbidden. Missing transitions reject that computation branch. Acceptance means that at least one finite computation reaches an accepting state. Infinite nonaccepting computations do not accept. An accepting initial configuration counts as acceptance, and initial and accepting states count toward the state total.

For a finite nonempty set H, let R_H be the monoid of all binary relations on H, with multiplication in path order. A pair belongs to AB when a path first uses A and then B. The input alphabet is the entire relation set R_H. A word's value is the product of its relation letters, with the identity relation assigned to the empty word. The language L_H consists of words with nonempty relation product.

For h=|H|, the source machine uses h+2 states: one initial state, h states for guessed path positions, and one accepting state. It chooses a starting point, follows one relation edge per letter and accepts at the right endmarker. It also accepts the empty word.

## Main theorem and limits

For every n≥4, set h=n−2. The n-state source automaton just described has the property that every 2NFA recognizing its complement requires at least

\[
\frac12\,2^{\lfloor(n-4)/127\rfloor}-1
\]

states.

The alphabet has size 2^{(n−2)^2}. The lower bound concerns the number of states uniformly over alphabets, not the total transition-table description length. It does not establish the same lower bound over one fixed alphabet.

There is no input-length restriction or running-time restriction on successful computations. The theorem permits arbitrary two-way head movement in the complementing machine, beyond the previously studied sweeping restriction. It compares machines without a work tape and does not claim a separation of uniform logarithmic-space complexity classes.

The deterministic consequence uses an external linear-overhead complementation construction that handles nonterminating deterministic runs. Thus deterministic recognition of the same language also requires exponentially many states. Simply swapping accepting and rejecting states would not handle infinite nonaccepting runs and is not the cited argument.

## Finite path representation

A path diagram has two directional label sets, each of size m, and four relations.

| Relation | Entry and exit |
|---|---|
| F | Left entry to right exit |
| B | Right entry to left exit |
| L | Left entry to left exit |
| R | Right entry to right exit |

Products join equal labels at adjacent boundaries and record finite paths until their first outer exit. Replacing a block traversal by its product edge, and expanding that edge back into a finite witness, proves associativity. The identity has identity through relations and empty return relations. The finite monoid is the partitioned-binary-relation monoid of Martin–Mazorchuk, written in directional coordinates.

The product formulas retain arbitrary internal returns. In particular,

\[
F_{ab}=F_a(L_bR_a)^*F_b,
\qquad
B_{ab}=B_b(R_aL_b)^*B_a.
\]

The return relations consist of paths returning inside the outer factor together with paths that visit the neighboring factor and return. Inclusion of all four relations defines an order preserved by multiplication. Reflection exchanges the two signs and reverses product order.

For an s-state automaton, the representation uses m=s+1 labels per sign. The added state records successful acceptance and sweeps to a designated right exit. A cell diagram represents any finite sequence of stay moves followed by one move out of the cell. Its state records the state after that move. This construction includes initially accepting configurations, the empty word, repeated boundary crossings and stay cycles. Infinite paths add no finite successful witness.

The resulting word-to-diagram homomorphism tau has fixed left and right endmarker contexts. Membership is exactly the existence of one designated forward edge in the contextual diagram. Consequently, increasing a word diagram can only add accepting paths in every fixed word context.

## Why complement recognition reverses order

Suppose a machine recognizes relation-product emptiness. Put singleton identity relations before and after a word to test the absence of one prescribed relation pair.

If tau(u) is included in tau(v), monotonicity of accepting paths says that a singleton-context emptiness accepted around u is also accepted around v. Therefore every pair absent from r(u) is absent from r(v), so r(v) is included in r(u).

Equal diagrams consequently have equal relation products. This makes the map

\[
\phi(\tau(w))=r(w)
\]

well defined, multiplicative and surjective. Surjectivity uses the fact that every relation is an input letter. The map is order reversing. The algebraic problem becomes a lower bound on the number of directional labels of a diagram monoid with such an image.

The algebraic theorem permits the initial surjection to be nonunital. The proof later passes to a minimal idempotent corner to obtain an identity-preserving map and the required lifts of permutation units.

## Recurrent classes, missing sets and a common loss budget

For an idempotent diagram e, the positive-sign definitions are

\[
K_e=(L_eR_e)^*,\qquad P_e=K_eF_eK_e.
\]

The negative definitions use the reflected relations. Idempotence makes P_e idempotent and transitive. Recurrent points have P_e loops. Mutual reachability partitions those points into classes, keeping the two signs distinct. There are at most2m classes in total.

The corner with identity e consists of diagrams z satisfying ez=ze=z. For such z, the through-loop test uses U_e(z)=K_eF_zK_e, or its reflected counterpart. A class is missing when its loop is absent. Corner absorption makes this independent of the chosen point in the class.

The proof develops three tools.

1. Missing classes of a product lie in the union of the factors' missing classes. Taking a positive power therefore cannot enlarge a missing set.
2. Recurrent classes supply rectangles covering all through edges of e. If no class is missing from z, every through edge of e survives. Return edges already survive by the corner identities, giving e included in z.
3. A fixed context transporting e to another idempotent d carries every replacement with missing set inside one fixed J into a common missing set J' of size at most2|J|. The same J' works for the whole replacement family.

The last common-set property is stronger than a separate cardinality bound for each replacement. Fixed witness paths and crossed rectangle edges show that one old class cannot be used by distinct new classes of the same sign. Accounting for both signs gives the factor2.

## Changing the corner identity

Nesting of idempotents means de=ed=d. It is different from inclusion of diagram edges.

The nesting argument first proves mixed absorption for the recurrent relations. Every recurrent class of the new idempotent then contains either a whole surviving old class or two distinct whole old classes. The proof factors a recurrent witness through two old through-edge rectangles. Reinserting recurrence at the endpoints is part of the argument that these old classes lie inside the new class.

For a nested chain b_0,...,b_k, suppose every b_j has missing set relative to b_0 inside one fixed J_0. Select one protected label in each class outside J_0 and retain those choices throughout. Protected labels remain recurrent and cannot belong to a class lost at a subsequent step.

Give each missing unprotected old class weight1/2 and every other unprotected old class weight1. The containment dichotomy supplies weight at least1 to each new unprotected class. Disjointness prevents double use. The resulting telescoping inequality is

\[
\sum_{j=1}^k
|\mathcal M_{b_{j-1}}(b_j)|
\le2|J_0|.
\]

This controls cumulative loss while the corner identity and its recurrent-class partition change.

## Amplification and the constant127

A minimal idempotent lift of a target idempotent gives a surjective corner map. Within that corner, an element mapping to a unit must itself be a unit. This supplies lifts of all permutations.

The induction studies an idempotent whose image is an identity relation plus one off-diagonal pair. Let its missing-class count be c. Set t=64 and

\[
D(h)=2^{\lfloor(h-2)/(2t-1)\rfloor}.
\]

The proof has three steps.

**One common budget.** Conjugate the single added pair to obtain t edges into a hub and t edges out of it. Composition and removal of the hub produce all t^2 pairs between disjoint sets U and V. The initial2t conjugates have a union of missing sets of size at most4tc. Transport into the hub-deleted corner makes one common budget of size at most8tc.

**A nested chain.** Add the t^2 pairs one by one. Their endpoints prevent additional compositions among added pairs. Idempotent powers of sandwiched lifts form a nested chain whose images are the desired successively enlarged relations. The chain inequality bounds its total loss by16tc.

**A smaller problem at every step.** Retain the new pair's endpoints and the points outside the construction. The retained set has h−2t+1 points. The prior relation restricts to the identity there. A relation-corner isomorphism and a minimal idempotent lift recover every hypothesis needed for the smaller induction instance. Transport bounds that smaller loss by twice the current step loss.

Thus every step costs at least D(h−127)/2. Summing gives

\[
16tc\ge\frac{t^2}{2}D(h-127),
\qquad
c\ge\frac{t}{32}D(h-127)=D(h).
\]

Since no missing set has more than2m classes, the algebraic image bound is

\[
2m\ge2^{\lfloor(h-2)/127\rfloor}.
\]

Substituting m=s+1 and h=n−2 gives the main complementation lower bound. This paragraph follows the source's proof and constants. It is not an additional independent proof certification.
