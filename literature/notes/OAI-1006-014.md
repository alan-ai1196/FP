## Question and claimed theorem

The paper addresses exhaustion of the rational Grothendieck–Teichmüller Lie algebra by one free generator in each odd weight at least three. Existence of a free odd-generated subalgebra and equality with the entire equation space are distinct questions. The main new task identified in the introduction is an all-weight upper bound for that full space.

Let \(L=\operatorname{Lie}_{\mathbb Q}\langle x,y\rangle\), graded by total letter weight. The weight-\(n\) equation space \(W_n\) consists of homogeneous ordinary Lie polynomials satisfying antisymmetry, the three-term relation and the infinitesimal pentagon:

\[
\psi(x,y)+\psi(y,x)=0,
\]

\[
\psi(x,y)+\psi(y,-x-y)+\psi(-x-y,x)=0,
\]

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\psi(t_{12},t_{23}+t_{24})+\psi(t_{13}+t_{23},t_{34})\\
&\quad=\psi(t_{23},t_{34})
+\psi(t_{12}+t_{13},t_{24}+t_{34})
+\psi(t_{12},t_{23}).
\end{aligned}
\]

The last identity takes place in the infinitesimal pure braid Lie algebra. Its defining relations are disjoint-chord commutation and
\([t_{ij},t_{ik}+t_{jk}]=0\). The derivation convention is
\(D_\psi(x)=0\), \(D_\psi(y)=[y,\psi]\), with Ihara bracket

\[
\{\psi,\phi\}=D_\psi(\phi)-D_\phi(\psi)+[\psi,\phi].
\]

Theorem 1.1 claims that \(W=\bigoplus_n W_n\) is closed under this bracket and is freely generated by rational elements \(\sigma_{2k+1}\), one in each weight \(2k+1\ge3\). It also claims the continuous isomorphism after completion in weight. The chosen generators are not asserted to be canonical.

The introduction places this against Brown's motivic free-subalgebra result, Willwacher's result on free families with nonzero odd depth-one components, and Naef–Willwacher's finite-weight computations. The paper supplies its own lower construction in §§6–8 as well as the upper bound. Its graph-complex consequence uses the cited identification with degree-zero graph cohomology. It does not determine other cohomological degrees.

## Overall proof structure

The argument has two different fields and two different filtrations.

First, rational solutions are placed in saturated lattices over \(\mathbb Z_{(2)}\), then reduced modulo two. A filtration counting \(B\)'s after the associative substitutions \(A=x^2\), \(C=[x,y]\), \(B=y\) yields an injective leading-term map into the ordinary free Lie algebra on \(\operatorname{ad}_C^kB\), \(k\ge1\). These letters have weights \(2k+1\). This bounds every rational equation-space dimension by the corresponding free-odd dimension.

Second, compatible derivations of completed chord categories form an actual rational Lie algebra with values inside \(W\). Regularized complex holonomy supplies a nonzero ordinary \(y\)-depth-one value in every odd weight. Rational finite-dimensional descent supplies a rational value. An integral induction combines these with the upper bound, selects reductions with exactly the required leading \(B\)-count, and proves equality and freeness.

Ordinary \(y\)-depth and the auxiliary \(B\)-count must remain separate. The letter \(C=[x,y]\) has ordinary \(y\)-depth one but auxiliary count zero. The proof does not identify all characteristic-two primitives with ordinary Lie elements, does not identify all characteristic-two equation solutions with reductions of rational solutions, and does not use a homomorphism between its two auxiliary ordering algebras.

## 1. Integral foundations and the special identity

Section 2 works over \(R=\mathbb Z_{(2)}\), with residue field \(\mathbb F_2\). A Hall–Lyndon construction provides ordinary free-Lie lattices whose leading word coefficients are one. Their graded pieces are finite free, embed into free associative words after reduction, and have the same dimensions over \(\mathbb Q\) and \(\mathbb F_2\).

For the braid algebra, forgetting the last strand exhibits a free Lie fiber on \(s_i=t_{iq}\). The base acts through
\(D_{ij}(s_i)=[s_i,s_j]\), \(D_{ij}(s_j)=[s_j,s_i]\), with zero action on other fiber generators. Recursive free-fiber ordering gives integral PBW bases and the torsion-free targets needed for reduction. The weight-one and weight-two equation spaces vanish by the pentagon and three-term relation, respectively.

A substantial preliminary result is the special identity, for rational solutions of weight \(n>2\),

\[
[a,\psi(b,a)]+[c,\psi(b,c)]=0,\qquad c=-a-b.
\]

The accompanying transverse derivative identity is
\(\partial_2\psi(a,-a)T=0\). The proof first works in characteristic zero. It rewrites the pentagon in a free strand-forgetting fiber, compares brackets with \(S=a+b+c\), and uses the centralizer of a free generator to identify the residual element when \(S=0\). Homogeneity gives a possible derivative
\(k\,\operatorname{ad}_a^{n-1}T\). Comparing mixed transverse derivatives forces \((n+1)k=0\) over \(\mathbb Q\), hence \(k=0\). The remaining special expression is constant in the required variable and vanishes on evaluation.

This order matters. The division implicit in the characteristic-zero argument is completed before reduction modulo two. The text does not divide by \(n+1\) in the residue field.

For the ambient free-Lie lattice \(K_n\), the intersection
\(L_n=W_n\cap K_n\) is saturated. Thus \(L_n\cap2K_n=2L_n\), and its reduction has dimension \(\dim_{\mathbb Q}W_n\) inside the ordinary characteristic-two free Lie algebra. The same statement holds for any rational subspace \(V_n\subset W_n\). Torsion-free targets allow the rational identities, including the derived special identity, to be read integrally and then reduced.

## 2. The characteristic-two degeneration and ordered transfer

Section 3 embeds the free associative alphabet \(A,C,B\) into the \(x,y\) algebra by
\(A=x^2\), \(C=[x,y]\), \(B=y\). Leading codes \(xx,xy,y\) give unique parsing. Ordinary Lie elimination shows that every weight-greater-than-one Lie polynomial in \(x,y\) lies in the ordinary Lie subalgebra on these new symbols:

\[
\operatorname{ad}_x^{2i}y=\operatorname{ad}_A^iB,\qquad
\operatorname{ad}_x^{2i+1}y=\operatorname{ad}_A^iC.
\]

This is an associative embedding statement. It does not assert that \(x^2\) itself was an ordinary Lie element on \(x,y\).

The descending filtration \(F^r\) counts at least \(r\) occurrences of \(B\). A leading component \(\chi\) of count \(r\) has original weight \(n=2m+r\), with \(A,C\) of weight two. The desired projection is \(f=\chi(0,C,B)\).

The source ordered alphabet comes from squared free-fiber generators and commutators in the braid enveloping algebra. The target is the integral holonomy Lie algebra of the arrangement
\(z_i=0\), \(z_i-z_j=0\), \(z_i+z_j=0\), subsequently reduced modulo two. Its flat incidences are computed over \(\mathbb Q\). The plus and minus hyperplanes remain distinct labeled generators after scalar reduction.

The tables in Lemma 3.3 give exact base–fiber actions in both algebras. The parts preserving the count of \(u\)-symbols agree. Source corrections increase that count by two, while the extra target term decreases it by one. Recursive ordering terminates by the number of base letters and inversions, then by the number of strands.

Source ordered restricted words are independent by the free-fiber PBW basis and prefix parsing. Target words only need to span under the permitted rewrites. The proof neither assumes target independence nor constructs a map between the two algebras.

The pentagon under the five substitutions has a lowest-count four-term portion. One whole term and the extra squared/commutator terms have higher source count. Source independence makes the retained coefficients vanish. Performing the same count-preserving ordering in the target transfers this cancellation, with an allowed remainder of strictly lower target count and fixed length \(m+r\). The fixed-length condition is essential to the subsequent scalar deletion test.

## 3. Integral deletion tests and injectivity of the projection

Section 4 uses the ratio alphabet

\[
e=1,\qquad p=\frac{z_2-z_3}{z_2+z_3},\qquad
w=\frac{z_2-z_1}{z_2+z_1}.
\]

Words have internal letters \(0,e,p\) and fixed endpoints \(0,w\). For a hyperplane \(H\), the deletion operator removes the \(j\)-th letter with coefficient
\(\nu_H(P_j,P_{j+1})-\nu_H(P_{j-1},P_j)\). For equal letters, the valuation is defined to be zero. It is not an attempted valuation of the zero function.

These are finite integer matrices on words of bounded length. Their logarithmic-form matrix is flat: contributions from nonadjacent deletions cancel, and adjacent deletions reduce to the three-term logarithmic identity, including repeated-letter cases. Taking two successive residues over \(\mathbb Q\) gives the integral arrangement relations. Coincident restrictions proportional to \(z,-z,2z\) each contribute their own residue one. There is no division by their number or by two. Integral matrix vanishing then permits reduction.

The complete edge-weight table shows that an \(a\)- or \(v\)-operation cannot delete a zero. A test word with \(r\) zeros therefore kills all lower-\(u\)-count remainders. At exactly \(r\) such slots, every \(u\)-slot must delete a zero. The rightmost matrix acts first, so terminal deletion reads the ordinary left-to-right associative coefficient.

To prove injectivity, suppose \(\chi(0,C,B)=0\) and take the least positive \(A\)-count \(h\). Test words with \(h\) letters \(e\), \(m-h\) letters \(p\), and \(r\) zeros. The four transferred pentagon terms have the explicit actions in table (4.7). One term reads the coefficient, two vanish, and the remaining term cannot empty a nonempty \(p,0\) block trapped after an \(e\). Removing its right boundary changes \(e\) to \(w\), but its final internal letter still has zero deletion weight. Figure 1 displays exactly this obstruction. Reversal fixes ordinary Lie polynomials in characteristic two and eliminates the remaining terminal-\(e\) possibilities.

The possible projection kernel is consequently a polynomial \(P(A,B)\). The special identity further compares \(A\) with \(A+C+B^2\), using the count-lowering derivation \(D(B)=C\), \(D(A)=D(C)=0\). Coefficients with two marked \(C\)'s force the relevant word coefficients to agree. Three marked \(C\)'s eliminate the \(m\ge2\) case. The remaining \(m=1\) cases are removed using symmetry and the explicit low bidegrees. Thus setting \(A=0\) is injective on the leading associated-graded piece.

## 4. Polynomial shifts identify the image and its bracket

Section 5 first obtains a parity restriction from symmetry in \(x,y\). Products of iterated adjoints representing the relevant bidegree must have at least \(r\) even indices, including zero. This is stronger than a casual assertion about the number of \(B\)'s after a substitution.

It yields invariance of the projected leading term under every polynomial shift

\[
f(C,B+s(C))=f(C,B).
\]

The identity holds over commutative characteristic-two coefficient extensions, including independent coefficient indeterminates. The proof uses the Ore extension with \(D(B)=C\), \(D(C)=0\), \(D^2=0\), and \(X^2=0\). Parity-sensitive lower bounds on the \(B\)-degree of iterated adjoints survive the necessary differentiations. This makes the shift unable to create lower-degree terms, while its highest-degree part is unchanged.

Encode a word \(C^{i_0}BC^{i_1}\cdots BC^{i_r}\) by
\(s_0^{i_0}\cdots s_r^{i_r}\). The coefficient of an independent shift parameter in \(B\mapsto B+\lambda C^j\) gives
\(\sum_i t_{i-1}^jR_iP=0\). Taking \(j=0,\ldots,r-1\) gives a Vandermonde system over the rational function field of independent indeterminates. It is valid even for even \(r\). No division by \(r\), or specialization to the two actual elements of \(\mathbb F_2\), occurs.

Each adjacent-identification map \(R_i\) has kernel generated by \(d_i=s_{i-1}-s_i\), so \(P\) is divisible by \(d_1\cdots d_r\). For
\(g_k=\operatorname{ad}_C^kB\), the encoding of a product of \(g_k\)'s is the corresponding product of powers of the \(d_i\)'s. This proves their associative independence. An ordinary Lie retraction killing \(g_0=B\) then shows that the image lies in the ordinary free Lie algebra on \(g_k\), \(k\ge1\). Primitive-element classification in characteristic two is not used.

Consequently

\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}W_n
=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\overline L_n
\le\sum_r d_{n,r}=d_n,
\]

where \(d_{n,r}\) is the dimension of the free odd-generated Lie algebra in weight \(n\), length \(r\). Hall–Lyndon lattices identify these dimensions across the two fields.

The leading Ihara calculation is separate. Before projection, brackets respect the sum of \(B\)-counts. After projection, the extra derivative term involves \(Df\), which vanishes on the image generated by the \(g_k\)'s. The leading derivation becomes inner, and the three Ihara contributions combine to the ordinary bracket of the projected terms. This does not assume closure of the whole equation space \(W\).

## 5. A rational Lie algebra of categorical values

Section 6 constructs categories in arities at most four. Objects are planar binary trees with ordered labeled leaves. Arrow spaces are completed enveloping algebras of infinitesimal braid Lie algebras. The distinguished coefficient-one arrow between two different trees is not an identity endomorphism. Composition multiplies the later arrow on the left.

Relabeling, outer block insertion and inner insertion are defined by chord substitutions. Compatible derivations preserve composition, are coderivations, commute with these operations and vanish on entire arity-two arrow spaces. They form a Lie algebra.

Evaluating a weight-\(n\) compatible derivation on the unit reassociation arrow produces a primitive element. Here the coefficient field has characteristic zero, so PBW identifies primitives with Lie elements. Splitting off the central total chord in arity three places the weight-\(n\ge2\) value in the free Lie algebra on \(x,y\). Tree reversals and cyclic paths give antisymmetry and the three-term identity. The two four-leaf reassociation routes give the pentagon, displayed in Figure 2.

For a value \(\psi\), the induced source loop derivation is \(D_\psi\), and its action on an arrow coefficient is
\(\psi h+D_\psi(h)\). The commutator of compatible derivations therefore evaluates to the stated Ihara bracket. The image \(V\subset W\) is a Lie subalgebra. Evaluation need not be injective.

The rational descent argument reduces fixed-weight derivations to finitely many rational linear equations on loop-generator values and object-change values. It proves
\(\mathcal D_n(K)=\mathcal D_n(\mathbb Q)\otimes K\) and the analogous statement for the evaluation image. A rational coefficient functional nonzero on one complex value is therefore nonzero on some rational value. It does not claim that the complex period itself is rational.

## 6. Regularized holonomy and odd depth-one nonvanishing

Section 7 uses
\(\Omega=(2\pi i)^{-1}\sum t_{ij}\,d\log(z_i-z_j)\), with \(dg=\Omega g\). Flatness follows from braid relations and Arnold's logarithmic-form identity. Primitivity of the connection makes transport group-like.

Tree endpoints are approached by explicitly separated positive real scales. Their frames are exponentials of commuting node chord sums. In each finite weight quotient, changing frame leaves an integrable remainder bounded by
\(C_N\epsilon^{\eta-1}(1+|\log\epsilon|)^N\). This establishes regularized transport, rather than merely postulating a tangential value.

The proof also checks interpolation of scale exponents, outer and inner insertions, and coherence of the path operations. Outer insertion errors are controlled by \(\epsilon^{M-2K}\), and inner errors by \(\epsilon^{M-K}\), times logarithmic factors at a fixed weight cutoff. Internal and total-chord scale frames commute and cancel. A collision-free homotopy square exchanges outer and inner paths, while nested insertions flatten by addition of their scale exponents.

Compare the original transport \(H_+\) with \(H_-\), formed by complex-conjugating the path and negating every chord. They agree on all arity-two paths, including half turns. Both induce the same associated-graded map on completed path categories. Pair-collision meridians generate degree one, satisfy the infinitesimal relations in the augmentation graded algebra, and are taken to \(1+t_{ij}+\cdots\). The resulting inverse graded maps lift through finite quotients to completed category isomorphisms.

Thus \(S=H_-H_+^{-1}\) has identity associated graded. Its operator logarithm is a compatible derivation and coderivation. At finite cutoff the binomial polynomial \(S^s\) preserves all required operations for every nonnegative integer \(s\), hence identically as a polynomial. Differentiation at zero supplies the logarithm's rules.

For real reassociation transport \(\Phi\), unit-arrow image \(U=S(\alpha)\), and source loop map \(\theta\), the exact relations are

\[
U\theta(\Phi)=\Phi(-x,-y),\qquad
\theta(x)=x,\qquad \theta(y)=U^{-1}yU.
\]

Both \(\Phi-1\) and \(U-1\) lie in the ideal \(J\) of words containing \(y\). Modulo \(J^2\), higher powers of the arrow operator \(S-\mathrm{id}\) vanish on the unit. Therefore the infinitesimal value is
\(\delta(\alpha)=\Phi(-x,-y)-\Phi(x,y)\pmod{J^2}\). This explicitly distinguishes the operator logarithm from an unmotivated ordinary series logarithm.

With \(\lambda=(2\pi i)^{-1}\), the convergent iterated integral gives

\[
[x^{n-1}y]\Phi
=-\frac{\lambda^n}{(n-1)!}
\int_0^1\frac{(-\log t)^{n-1}}{1-t}\,dt
=-\lambda^n\sum_{q\ge1}q^{-n}.
\]

The \(y\) occurs at the earliest integration time because later differentials multiply on the left. Endpoint frames cannot change this coefficient: their nonconstant contributions would prepend \(y\) or append \(x\). For odd \(n\ge3\),

\[
[x^{n-1}y]\delta_n(\alpha)
=2\lambda^n\sum_{q\ge1}q^{-n}\ne0.
\]

Only positivity of a convergent real series is used. No independence or irrationality theorem for odd zeta values enters. Rational descent now supplies the required rational categorical value with nonzero ordinary depth-one part.

## 7. Integral induction, spanning and completion

Section 8 keeps two issues separate: obtaining a rational element independent of old decomposables, and obtaining an integral generator with the correct reduction.

Ordinary \(y\)-depth adds under the Ihara bracket. Hence every bracket word of length at least two in the earlier generators has zero depth-one component. Hall-word values in already chosen integral generators have independent reductions, because their projected lowest-count components are the corresponding Hall words in the \(g_k\)'s.

The elementary saturation lemma says that vectors with independent reductions over \(\mathbb F_2\) are rationally independent, and that their rational span intersects the ambient \(R\)-lattice in exactly their \(R\)-span. In particular, clearing rational coefficients cannot manufacture a new count-one component from old bracket words.

At weight \(n\), the older Hall words account for \(d_n\) elements if \(n\) is even and \(d_n-1\) if \(n\ge3\) is odd. In the odd case the holonomy-derived rational value supplies a nonzero depth-one component, so it is independent. Together with the upper bound,

\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}V_n=\dim_{\mathbb Q}W_n=d_n.
\]

Equality of the total filtered dimensions forces equality in every nonnegative associated-graded dimension deficit. The count-one piece in odd weight \(2k+1\) therefore maps onto the line generated by \(g_k\). Lift its nonzero vector from the saturated lattice to choose \(\sigma_{2k+1}\). The initial holonomy-derived rational element need not already have this desired reduction.

The Hall images are independent and their counts exhaust each weight, giving \(V=W\), freeness and spanning. Closure of the full equation space under Ihara follows at this point. Finite-dimensional homogeneous pieces and finitely many bracket contributions at each fixed weight extend the isomorphism continuously to the weight completions.

## Evidence, dependencies and limits
