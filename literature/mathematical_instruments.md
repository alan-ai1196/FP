| Normalized projection-body volume | For full-dimensional polytopes $A\subset\mathbb R^r,B\subset\mathbb R^s$, $r,s\ge2$, $\Pi(A\times B)=(|B|\Pi A)\times(|A|\Pi B)$. Hence $R(A\times B)=R(A)R(B)$ for $R(K)=|\Pi K|/|K|^{d-1}$. A $d$-simplex has $R=(d+1)d^d/d!$. | OAI-1006-178, Lemma 2 and Propositions 3–5. A projection counts the two visible boundary crossings of almost every line. Product facets come in two coordinate families. The simplex projection body is a rescaled cube plus a diagonal segment. Independent arithmetic verifies the strict dimension-20 ratio. Publisher lists a Lean formalization. Local general Lean replay pending. A direction-sensitive physical workload can reverse the volume ordering. |
| Restricting Fourier mass on a product space | For independent Bernoulli($p$) inputs and Boolean $f$, $\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}\hat f_p(S)^2=\operatorname{Var}_p f$ and $\sum_S |S|\hat f_p(S)^2=p(1-p)I_p(f)$. Conditioning on a fixed visible subset projects onto its supported characters. | OAI-1006-338, equations (2.1), (3.1) and the proof of Lemma 3.1. The signed edge difference takes values in $\{-1,0,1\}$. Parseval gives the weighted identity. Independence is essential. The retained-energy argument and an adaptive counterexample have independent finite checks. Local general Lean replay pending. |
| Stronger influence under vertex relabeling | For fixed $r\ge3$, any vertex-invariant Boolean property of a simple $r$-uniform graph under independent Bernoulli($p$) edges satisfies $\mathrm{Var}_p f\le C_r I_p(f)/(\log n)^{r/(r-1)}$. | OAI-1006-338, Lemmas 2.1, 3.1–3.2 and Theorem 1.1. Leave edges touching a uniform vertex block free. A low-degree support has a vertex of small positive degree. Capturing that vertex alone cancels the block-selection factor in the influence upper bound. Monotonicity enters the threshold-width corollary. Reading covers the entire proof. Independent proof of the full asymptotic estimate remains open here. The result supplies a tangent for log loss under random erasure. |
| Extension of radial isometries | A surjective isometry on the unit spheres of nonzero real Banach spaces extends uniquely by positive radial homogeneity to a surjective real-linear isometry, according to the manuscript. | OAI-1006-626, Lemma 3.1, Propositions 4.2, 5.3, §6 and Appendix A. An ultrapower attains a maximal cross-radius defect. A contracting measure of noncompactness on a convex set of supported directions gives aligned chords. A radial perturbation or a secant-slope contradiction excludes either parameter regime. Midpoint reflection then forces linearity. Publisher lists Lean materials. Independent full proof/replay pending here. Finitely sampled or approximately distance-preserving learned embeddings do not satisfy the hypotheses. |
| Sequential change of measure | Under observable-history logging, a common initial law and support, the target-policy expectation of a time-$t$ quantity measured after the action is the logger expectation weighted by $\prod_{i\le t}\pi(a_i\mid h_i)/b(a_i\mid h_i)$. | Jiang and Li, §§3.2.2–4.2, with a separate KL-chain calculation and independent attack in FP. Missing an entire action path can leave a policy unidentified. Variance may grow exponentially. [Primary paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/jiang16.html) · [Prediction and control](../research/frontier/INTELLIGENCE_OBJECTIVE.md) |
| Proximity to a linear code | Given a fixed dimension-$b$ subspace $C$ of query masks, its basis parities permit exact answers to each query within Hamming distance $q$ of $C$ using at most $q$ raw bits. | Natarajan Ramamoorthy and Rashtchian, §1.2, Theorem 1, systematic linear model. FP's projection argument treats nonlinear encoders, adaptive readers, a uniformly random future task and binary log loss. Nonuniform queries and a cap on expected probes have different frontiers. [Primary publication](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2020.35) |
| Finite field tensor certificate | The published $4\times4$ matrix-multiplication tensor over $\mathbb F_2$ equals a sum of 47 rank-one tensors. Verification forms all $16^3$ coefficients modulo two. | Fawzi et al., primary paper and versioned supplement. Our independent contraction found zero field mismatches and 465 nonzero integer residuals. In separate reductions without shared intermediate sums, 47 ANDs and 340 XORs replace 64 ANDs and 48 XORs. A scalar cost improves only if AND/XOR exceeds $292/17$. Different schedules and fields change the conclusion. No GPU timing follows. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05172-4) · [Published factors](https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphatensor/tree/1949163da3bef7e3eb268a3ac015fd1c2dbfc767/algorithms) |
| Divisibility and size | For an integer $D$ with $|D|\le B$, if distinct primes dividing $D$ have product $>B$, then $D=0$. Hadamard bounds give $B$ for an integer minor. | Elementary proof by coprimality and absolute size, independently checked. The Alpöge material motivates auditing where divisor multiplicities are actually earned. Repeating modulus 2 on determinant 2 supplies no factor 4. A zero minor of an empirical table is a statement about that table. Population rank, identifiability and learning require separate assumptions. | Identifying a predictive state with random tests | If the states form a compact semialgebraic set of Hausdorff dimension $p$, a finite future block distinguishes the state laws, and their characteristic functions extend jointly real-analytically, then $k\ge2p+1$ independent random cosine probes identify all states almost surely. | Wieck-Sosa and Shalizi v4, Assumption 1 and Theorem 2. Their two-stage estimator regresses random future features on histories, then estimates a conditional distribution. The finite-witness proof, sample rates and appendix dependencies remain under review. Exact injectivity gives no lower bound on the physical separation of two probes after rounding.
