# Connections between proofs and computation

## How much state must exist before a future query is known?

**PROVED for the following access model.** Seven fair input bits arrive before a uniformly chosen seven-bit query. The desired next token is their binary inner product, independently flipped with probability $\eta\in[0,1/2)$. At prediction the reader can consult a previously computed synopsis and inspect one raw bit. With four synopsis bits it matches the loss of reading all seven. With three bits the best possible learner, including nonlinear encoders and adaptive readers, loses an additional $(\log 2-h(\eta))/2$ nats on average. Here $h$ is binary entropy in nats.

The Fourier projection used in the hypergraph manuscript gives an access bound for probabilistic prediction. The linear storage construction and its relation to Hamming distance are established in systematic data structures. The novelty of the full loss formulation in the wider literature is undetermined. [OpenAI, §§2–3](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/A-uniform-influence-bound-for-hypergraph-properties-October-5-2026/hypergraph-influences.pdf) · [Natarajan Ramamoorthy and Rashtchian, §1.2](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2020.35) · [Garg et al., systematic query model](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2026.41)

### The general bound

Let $X,S$ be independent uniform vectors in $\mathbb F_2^M$. The target $Y$ is $S\cdot X$ modulo two passed through independent flip noise $\eta$. An encoder sees $X$ before $S$ and writes $Z(X)$ with at most $2^b$ possible values. The reader gets $S,Z$, probes at most $q$ coordinates of the retained raw $X$ on every path, and returns a normalized binary forecast. Its addresses may depend on all previous answers. The parties' initial randomness and any previously learned parameters are independent of this test pair. Charge every other persistent channel carrying information about this $X$. For $0\le q\le M$ put $V(M,q)=\sum_{j=0}^q\binom Mj$ and $K=\min(1,2^{b-M}V(M,q))$.

Expected excess log loss above $h(\eta)$ is at least $(1-K)(\log 2-h(\eta))$. Zero excess requires $2^b V(M,q)\ge2^M$. The same condition is necessary to answer every noiseless query exactly.

**Proof.** Fix the random tape, revealing it to both parties for free. The characters $\chi_s(x)=(-1)^{s\cdot x}$ are an orthonormal basis under uniform $X$. For each encoder cell $C_z$, multiply its indicator by all $\chi_T$ with $|T|\le q$, giving a joint span $W$ of dimension at most $2^b V(M,q)$. Fix $s$. Within a cell, a path's indicator is a product of at most $q$ coordinate indicators. Repeated queries add no degree on the cube. Each transcript leaf indicator is thus in $W$. The posterior mean $m_s=E[\chi_s(X)\mid\text{transcript},S=s]$ is the orthogonal projection onto the subspace spanned by the leaves. It follows that $\|m_s\|_2^2\le\|P_W\chi_s\|_2^2$. Summing over an orthonormal basis gives $\sum_s\|P_W\chi_s\|_2^2=\dim W$, hence $E_{S,X}m_S^2\le K$.

Set $a=1-2\eta$. The Bayes forecast's entropy gain over a fair prediction is $D(am)=\log 2-h((1+am)/2)$. Integrating the power series for $\operatorname{artanh}$ gives $D(u)=\sum_{k\ge1}u^{2k}/(2k(2k-1))$, also at $|u|=1$ by continuity and monotone convergence. Positivity gives $D(am)\le m^2D(a)$. Average the inequality. Any other forecast pays an additional nonnegative conditional KL. Averaging over random tapes also covers randomized procedures.

### Attainment and execution cost

For a dimension-$b$ linear code $C\subseteq\mathbb F_2^M$, store the parities of $X$ against a basis of $C$. If $s=c+e$ with $c\in C$ and $|e|\le q$, combine the stored parities with the raw bits indexed by $e$. Outside the union of these Hamming balls every transcript leaf fixes linear equations for the synopsis and at most $q$ raw coordinates. Their span omits $s$. Translation by a vector in their null space toggles the target on each nonempty leaf, so its posterior is fair. This gives the exact optimal loss of the linear encoder from the fraction of masks covered. Disjoint balls or full coverage attain the general bound.

For $M=7$, take the three-row binary matrix with the seven distinct nonzero columns. Its kernel has dimension four. A nonzero syndrome equals exactly one column, so flipping that coordinate reaches the kernel. Distinct codewords are at distance at least three. A dimension-$b$ subcode for $0\le b\le4$ covers exactly $8\cdot2^b$ masks at radius one. Thus the optimum excess for **all** $b$ is $(1-\min(1,2^{b-4}))(\log2-h(\eta))$. [Exact checks](../research/frontier/audit_literature.py)

The original seven-bit record stays available. In a systematic basis with data positions $3,5,6,7$ and check positions $1,2,4$, the four basis rows have weights $3,3,3,4$. Building the four extra bits uses nine binary XORs with separate reductions. At query time the reader must compute the syndrome, obtain the correction address, access selected synopsis bits and combine them. The theorem gives free access to the synopsis and permits arbitrary preprocessing. Learning the code from prediction errors, amortization over future queries, and device timing remain open.

If $S$ arrives before storage, one synopsis bit can hold the exact answer. If all queries are singletons, one raw read suffices without a synopsis. If a probe can request an arbitrary parity, one probe returns the answer. Each changes an explicit assumption.

## From hypergraph influence to lost predictive information

Give the edges of a simple $r$-uniform hypergraph independent Bernoulli($p$) values, $0<p<1$. Predict a Boolean property $f(X)$ with independent flip noise $\eta$. Reveal which bits were independently erased with probability $u$. For fixed $M=\binom nr$, the derivative at $u=0+$ of optimal expected next-token log loss is
$$I_p(f)\,[h(\eta+(1-2\eta)p)-h(\eta)].$$
With zero erasures the loss is the noise entropy. A single erased edge incurs the displayed entropy difference precisely when it is pivotal given the others. The two possible orientations give equal entropy. Multiple erasures have probability $O(u^2)$ at fixed $M$. For vertex-invariant $f$ with $r\ge3$, the manuscript's Theorem 1.1 gives $I_p(f)\ge\operatorname{Var}_p(f)(\log n)^{r/(r-1)}/C_r$, with influence the unweighted sum of pivotal probabilities. The resulting loss slope retains the displayed $p,\eta$ factor. A derivative at zero leaves finite-erasure losses open. [Original, Theorem 1.1 and equation (2.1)](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/A-uniform-influence-bound-for-hypergraph-properties-October-5-2026/hypergraph-influences.pdf)

**PROVED.** A fixed subset of $q$ visible edges leaves mean unobserved variance at least $(1-q/M)\operatorname{Var}_p f$ for vertex-invariant $f$. Conditional expectation retains exactly the $p$-biased Fourier supports contained in that subset. Relabel a nonempty support uniformly. One chosen edge becomes uniform over all edges, so containment has probability at most $q/M$. Squared Fourier coefficients are constant on each orbit. Summing proves the bound. Random masks independent of $X$ satisfy the same bound conditional on size. Bernoulli Pinsker gives excess at least $2(1-2\eta)^2(1-q/M)\operatorname{Var}_p f$ nats. The observer has no prior summary of the graph.

**FALSIFIED for adaptive masks.** On six vertices the twenty triples form ten complementary pairs. At $p=1/2$ let $f$ indicate the presence of both triples in some pair. Query each pair until resolved, skipping the mate after a zero and stopping once a pair is present. With nineteen queries only one path remains uncertain, of probability $2^{-19}$ and conditional variance $1/4$. The residual $2^{-21}$ is below $(3/4)^{10}(1-(3/4)^{10})/20$. Preparing the one-bit value of $f$ before the query also changes the observer. [Finite checks](../research/frontier/audit_literature.py)

## Boundary crossings in a table of probability rows

**Direct transfer.** For a full-dimensional convex body $K$, $X$ uniform on $K$ and fixed $v$, the exit probability $\Pr[X+tv\notin K]$ has right derivative $h_{\Pi K}(v)/|K|$ at zero. Slice along lines parallel to $v$. A fiber of length $\ell$ loses $\min(t\|v\|,\ell)$ of its length. Divide by $t$, integrate over the projection and apply dominated convergence. A categorical row on $d+1$ outcomes can be stored in $\Delta_d$ by omitting the slack coordinate. For a raw change $v$ put $L=\max(\sum_i(v_i)_+,\sum_i(-v_i)_+)$. The old and new row are both legal on a translated simplex of side factor $(1-tL)_+$, so exact survival is $(1-tL)_+^d$. Independent uniform rows multiply these probabilities. A learned distribution concentrated at one interior point can have zero rate. [Projection-body paper, Lemma 2 and Proposition 3](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/A-product-counterexample-to-the-simplex-maximum-for-projection-body-volume-September-24-2026/paper.pdf) · [Belief states, §3.3](https://cs.brown.edu/courses/csci2951-k/papers/kaelbling98.pdf)

**FALSIFIED as a workload ordering.** The normalized dual volume is $R(K)=|\Pi K|/|K|^{d-1}$. The paper gives $R(\Delta_{10}\times\Delta_{10})/R(\Delta_{20})=5588869/5505024>1$. Scale the product by $s=\binom{20}{10}^{-1/20}$ to give equal primal volumes. Since $1/2<s<1$, a coordinate-axis perturbation has product exit rate $10/s<20$. Oppositely directed unit coordinate changes in different blocks give $20/s>20$ compared with the single simplex's rate of $20$. These two workloads reverse the ordering.

**Finite precision.** Suppose a row has integer counts summing to $m$ with given lower floors. Let $M$ counts remain after subtracting the floors. For an integer update with sum zero and total donor demand $H=\sum_i(-k_i)_+$, the fraction of uniform grid states accepting it is $\binom{M-H+d}{d}/\binom{M+d}{d}$ if $H\le M$, and zero otherwise. Subtract the required donor counts and count weak compositions. A summary that must answer every future single-donor threshold query without the original needs exactly $\lceil\log_2\binom{M+d}{d}\rceil$ bits. Different rows disagree on a threshold and an enumeration index suffices. With three categories and $M=4$, all thresholds distinguish fifteen states, unit transfers distinguish seven supports, and one nontrivial fixed threshold distinguishes two answers. ## Every optimal four-bit synopsis encodes higher-order information

**PROVED.** In the seven-bit experiment at zero excess, every deterministic optimal synopsis $Z$ is independent of every pair $(X_i,X_j)$ for $i\ne j$, despite carrying four bits of information about $X$. To see this, fix a nonempty cell $C_z$. Modulo a constant sign, the number of distinct restrictions of parity characters to $C_z$ is $2^{\dim\operatorname{aff}(C_z)}$. Indeed, two restrictions differ by a constant sign exactly when the difference of their masks annihilates the span of all within-cell input differences. A one-read binary decision is constant or a signed coordinate on the cell. Perfect prediction of every requested clean parity therefore requires at most eight restricted parity classes. Thus $|C_z|\le2^{\dim\operatorname{aff}(C_z)}\le8$. Covering all 128 inputs with at most 16 such cells forces equality in every cell. Each is an affine three-flat. The seven coordinate restrictions now give all seven distinct nonzero linear forms on its direction space. Every pair has rank two, so all four coordinate pairs occur equally often in every cell. This applies to arbitrary optimal deterministic encodings. The cell directions may vary. For a randomized optimum the deterministic conclusion applies after conditioning on the independent random tape.

**FALSIFIED for greedy acquisition.** Consider the *population* learning law that starts with no stored parity and appends the generator giving the greatest immediate reduction in optimal noisy token loss. For each proposed span $C$ the loss is determined by the uniform mass of $C+B_1$. The rank-three choices with masks $(7,25,42)$ and $(7,25,98)$ both cover 64 of 128 requests. The first extends to perfect coverage. Every fourth generator after the second leaves at least 16 requests undecodable. Complete enumeration finds 1,605 reachable rank-three spans, of which 1,260 are stuck at this lower fourth-step gain. Uniform random selection among each step's maximizing generators reaches perfection with probability $416/2299$. This probability weights local ties. There are 30 distinct perfect terminal spans and 840 imperfect ones. Independent enumeration reproduced the counts. The published script visits each distinct extension once, which gives the same transition probabilities because all generator cosets have the same size. [Population audit](../research/frontier/parity_packing.py)

The gain forces new computations even at the first step: the 99 optimal first masks all have Hamming weight at least three, since exactly those have a disjoint radius-one ball from the zero mask. A cached raw coordinate is inferior. The remaining tie has predictive consequences beyond its immediate loss. No individual, pairwise feature association or scalar immediate gain specifies which optimum to pursue. This population rule is an oracle comparator. It has access to the entire task law. Recovering the comparison with a trained decoder, sampled tokens and charged search is a separate question.

**A failed transfer from algebraic geometry explains a limitation of symmetry.** In the original Milne manuscript, §4, equation (4.2), a supermodular defect on a finite-height lattice has a unique largest maximizer, which is preserved by every symmetry of the defect. Two maximizers have maximizing meet and join by the supermodular inequality. A maximal-dimensional maximizer therefore contains all the rest. Raw useful-query coverage fails the needed inequality already on $\mathbb F_2^2$ with one probe. Each of the two coordinate lines and their sum cover four masks, whereas their intersection covers only three. Thus $4+4>4+3$. Subtracting a price per stored dimension leaves this counterexample intact because dimension is modular. Vertex or coordinate symmetry alone does not select a useful representation during learning.
