## How much state must exist before a future query is known?

**PROVED for the following access model.** Seven fair input bits arrive before a uniformly chosen seven-bit query. The desired next token is their binary inner product, independently flipped with probability $\eta\in[0,1/2)$. At prediction the reader can consult a previously computed synopsis and inspect one raw bit. With four synopsis bits it matches the loss of reading all seven. With three bits the best possible learner, including nonlinear encoders and adaptive readers, loses an additional $(\log 2-h(\eta))/2$ nats on average. Here $h$ is binary entropy in nats.

The Fourier projection used in the hypergraph manuscript gives an access bound for probabilistic prediction. Kamath, Montasser and Srebro's Theorem 19 and parity example give the equivalent dimension–squared-loss lower bound for any shared feature space. An even-moment expansion of the entropy deficit appears in Kumar et al., Proposition 7. The access-model step here is to place every query-specific adaptive transcript subspace in a single space determined before the query. [Squared-loss antecedent](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v125/kamath20b/kamath20b.pdf#page=10) · [Reliability moments](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.7543)

The linear storage construction and its relation to Hamming distance are established in systematic data structures. The novelty of the full loss formulation in the wider literature is undetermined. [OpenAI, §§2–3](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/A-uniform-influence-bound-for-hypergraph-properties-October-5-2026/hypergraph-influences.pdf) · [Natarajan Ramamoorthy and Rashtchian, §1.2](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2020.35) · [Garg et al., systematic query model](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2026.41)

### The general bound

Let $X,S$ be independent uniform vectors in $\mathbb F_2^M$. The target $Y$ is $S\cdot X$ modulo two passed through independent flip noise $\eta$. An encoder sees $X$ before $S$ and writes $Z(X)$ with at most $2^b$ possible values. The reader gets $S,Z$, probes at most $q$ coordinates of the retained raw $X$ on every path, and returns a normalized binary forecast. Its addresses may depend on all previous answers. The parties' initial randomness and any previously learned parameters are independent of this test pair. Charge every other persistent channel carrying information about this $X$. For $0\le q\le M$ put $V(M,q)=\sum_{j=0}^q\binom Mj$ and $K=\min(1,2^{b-M}V(M,q))$.

Expected excess log loss above $h(\eta)$ is at least $(1-K)(\log 2-h(\eta))$. Zero excess requires $2^b V(M,q)\ge2^M$. The same condition is necessary to answer every noiseless query exactly.

**Proof.** Fix the random tape, revealing it to both parties for free. The characters $\chi_s(x)=(-1)^{s\cdot x}$ are an orthonormal basis under uniform $X$. For each encoder cell $C_z$, multiply its indicator by all $\chi_T$ with $|T|\le q$, giving a joint span $W$ of dimension at most $2^b V(M,q)$. Fix $s$. Within a cell, a path's indicator is a product of at most $q$ coordinate indicators. Repeated queries add no degree on the cube. Each transcript leaf indicator is thus in $W$. The posterior mean $m_s=E[\chi_s(X)\mid\text{transcript},S=s]$ is the orthogonal projection onto the subspace spanned by the leaves. It follows that $\|m_s\|_2^2\le\|P_W\chi_s\|_2^2$. Summing over an orthonormal basis gives $\sum_s\|P_W\chi_s\|_2^2=\dim W$, hence $E_{S,X}m_S^2\le K$.

Set $a=1-2\eta$. The Bayes forecast's entropy gain over a fair prediction is $D(am)=\log 2-h((1+am)/2)$. Integrating the power series for $\operatorname{artanh}$ gives $D(u)=\sum_{k\ge1}u^{2k}/(2k(2k-1))$, also at $|u|=1$ by continuity and monotone convergence. Positivity gives $D(am)\le m^2D(a)$. Average the inequality. Any other forecast pays an additional nonnegative conditional KL. Averaging over random tapes also covers randomized procedures.

### Attainment and execution cost

For a dimension-$b$ linear code $C\subseteq\mathbb F_2^M$, store the parities of $X$ against a basis of $C$. If $s=c+e$ with $c\in C$ and $|e|\le q$, combine the stored parities with the raw bits indexed by $e$. Outside the union of these Hamming balls every transcript leaf fixes linear equations for the synopsis and at most $q$ raw coordinates. Their span omits $s$. Translation by a vector in their null space toggles the target on each nonempty leaf, so its posterior is fair. This gives the exact optimal loss of the linear encoder from the fraction of masks covered. Disjoint balls or full coverage attain the general bound.

For $M=7$, take the three-row binary matrix with the seven distinct nonzero columns. Its kernel has dimension four. A nonzero syndrome equals exactly one column, so flipping that coordinate reaches the kernel. Distinct codewords are at distance at least three. A dimension-$b$ subcode for $0\le b\le4$ covers exactly $8\cdot2^b$ masks at radius one. Thus the optimum excess for **all** $b$ is $(1-\min(1,2^{b-4}))(\log2-h(\eta))$. [Exact checks](../research/frontier/audit_literature.py)

The original seven-bit record stays available. In a systematic basis with data positions $3,5,6,7$ and check positions $1,2,4$, the four basis rows have weights $3,3,3,4$. Building the four extra bits uses nine binary XORs with separate reductions. At query time the reader must compute the syndrome, obtain the correction address, access selected synopsis bits and combine them. The theorem gives free access to the synopsis and permits arbitrary preprocessing. An empirical likelihood learner subsequently recovered this code from noisy labels. The CPU receipt and a workload-dependent amortization calculation are recorded separately. [Learning and physical accounting](../research/frontier/EVIDENCE.md#learning-a-four-bit-synopsis-from-noisy-tokens)

If $S$ arrives before storage, one synopsis bit can hold the exact answer. If all queries are singletons, one raw read suffices without a synopsis. If a probe can request an arbitrary parity, one probe returns the answer. Each changes an explicit assumption.

## From hypergraph influence to lost predictive information

Give the edges of a simple $r$-uniform hypergraph independent Bernoulli($p$) values, $0<p<1$. Predict a Boolean property $f(X)$ with independent flip noise $\eta$. Reveal which bits were independently erased with probability $u$. For fixed $M=\binom nr$, the derivative at $u=0+$ of optimal expected next-token log loss is
$$I_p(f)\,[h(\eta+(1-2\eta)p)-h(\eta)].$$
With zero erasures the loss is the noise entropy. A single erased edge incurs the displayed entropy difference precisely when it is pivotal given the others. The two possible orientations give equal entropy. Multiple erasures have probability $O(u^2)$ at fixed $M$. For vertex-invariant $f$ with $r\ge3$, the manuscript's Theorem 1.1 gives $I_p(f)\ge\operatorname{Var}_p(f)(\log n)^{r/(r-1)}/C_r$, with influence the unweighted sum of pivotal probabilities. The resulting loss slope retains the displayed $p,\eta$ factor. A derivative at zero leaves finite-erasure losses open. [Original, Theorem 1.1 and equation (2.1)](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/A-uniform-influence-bound-for-hypergraph-properties-October-5-2026/hypergraph-influences.pdf)

**PROVED.** A fixed subset of $q$ visible edges leaves mean unobserved variance at least $(1-q/M)\operatorname{Var}_p f$ for vertex-invariant $f$. Conditional expectation retains exactly the $p$-biased Fourier supports contained in that subset. Relabel a nonempty support uniformly. One chosen edge becomes uniform over all edges, so containment has probability at most $q/M$. Squared Fourier coefficients are constant on each orbit. Summing proves the bound. Random masks independent of $X$ satisfy the same bound conditional on size. Bernoulli Pinsker gives excess at least $2(1-2\eta)^2(1-q/M)\operatorname{Var}_p f$ nats. The observer has no prior summary of the graph.

**FALSIFIED for adaptive masks.** On six vertices the twenty triples form ten complementary pairs. At $p=1/2$ let $f$ indicate the presence of both triples in some pair. Query each pair until resolved, skipping the mate after a zero and stopping once a pair is present. With nineteen queries only one path remains uncertain, of probability $2^{-19}$ and conditional variance $1/4$. The residual $2^{-21}$ is below $(3/4)^{10}(1-(3/4)^{10})/20$. Preparing the one-bit value of $f$ before the query also changes the observer. [Finite checks](../research/frontier/audit_literature.py)

## Boundary crossings in a table of probability rows

**Direct transfer.** For a full-dimensional convex body $K$, $X$ uniform on $K$ and fixed $v$, the exit probability $\Pr[X+tv\notin K]$ has right derivative $h_{\Pi K}(v)/|K|$ at zero. Slice along lines parallel to $v$. A fiber of length $\ell$ loses $\min(t\|v\|,\ell)$ of its length. Divide by $t$, integrate over the projection and apply dominated convergence. A categorical row on $d+1$ outcomes can be stored in $\Delta_d$ by omitting the slack coordinate. For a raw change $v$ put $L=\max(\sum_i(v_i)_+,\sum_i(-v_i)_+)$. The old and new row are both legal on a translated simplex of side factor $(1-tL)_+$, so exact survival is $(1-tL)_+^d$. Independent uniform rows multiply these probabilities. A learned distribution concentrated at one interior point can have zero rate. [Projection-body paper, Lemma 2 and Proposition 3](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/A-product-counterexample-to-the-simplex-maximum-for-projection-body-volume-September-24-2026/paper.pdf) · [Belief states, §3.3](https://cs.brown.edu/courses/csci2951-k/papers/kaelbling98.pdf)

**FALSIFIED as a workload ordering.** The normalized dual volume is $R(K)=|\Pi K|/|K|^{d-1}$. The paper gives $R(\Delta_{10}\times\Delta_{10})/R(\Delta_{20})=5588869/5505024>1$. Scale the product by $s=\binom{20}{10}^{-1/20}$ to give equal primal volumes. Since $1/2<s<1$, a coordinate-axis perturbation has product exit rate $10/s<20$. Oppositely directed unit coordinate changes in different blocks give $20/s>20$ compared with the single simplex's rate of $20$. These two workloads reverse the ordering.

**Finite precision.** Suppose a row has integer counts summing to $m$ with given lower floors. Let $M$ counts remain after subtracting the floors. For an integer update with sum zero and total donor demand $H=\sum_i(-k_i)_+$, the fraction of uniform grid states accepting it is $\binom{M-H+d}{d}/\binom{M+d}{d}$ if $H\le M$, and zero otherwise. Subtract the required donor counts and count weak compositions. A summary that must answer every future single-donor threshold query without the original needs exactly $\lceil\log_2\binom{M+d}{d}\rceil$ bits. Different rows disagree on a threshold and an enumeration index suffices. With three categories and $M=4$, all thresholds distinguish fifteen states, unit transfers distinguish seven supports, and one nontrivial fixed threshold distinguishes two answers. ## Every optimal four-bit synopsis encodes higher-order information

**PROVED.** In the seven-bit experiment at zero excess, every deterministic optimal synopsis $Z$ is independent of every pair $(X_i,X_j)$ for $i\ne j$, despite carrying four bits of information about $X$. To see this, fix a nonempty cell $C_z$. Modulo a constant sign, the number of distinct restrictions of parity characters to $C_z$ is $2^{\dim\operatorname{aff}(C_z)}$. Indeed, two restrictions differ by a constant sign exactly when the difference of their masks annihilates the span of all within-cell input differences. A one-read binary decision is constant or a signed coordinate on the cell. Perfect prediction of every requested clean parity therefore requires at most eight restricted parity classes. Thus $|C_z|\le2^{\dim\operatorname{aff}(C_z)}\le8$. Covering all 128 inputs with at most 16 such cells forces equality in every cell. Each is an affine three-flat. The seven coordinate restrictions now give all seven distinct nonzero linear forms on its direction space. Every pair has rank two, so all four coordinate pairs occur equally often in every cell. This applies to arbitrary optimal deterministic encodings. The cell directions may vary. For a randomized optimum the deterministic conclusion applies after conditioning on the independent random tape.

**FALSIFIED for greedy acquisition.** Consider the *population* learning law that starts with no stored parity and appends the generator giving the greatest immediate reduction in optimal noisy token loss. For each proposed span $C$ the loss is determined by the uniform mass of $C+B_1$. The rank-three choices with masks $(7,25,42)$ and $(7,25,98)$ both cover 64 of 128 requests. The first extends to perfect coverage. Every fourth generator after the second leaves at least 16 requests undecodable. Complete enumeration finds 1,605 reachable rank-three spans, of which 1,260 are stuck at this lower fourth-step gain. Uniform random selection among each step's maximizing generators reaches perfection with probability $416/2299$. This probability weights local ties. There are 30 distinct perfect terminal spans and 840 imperfect ones. Independent enumeration reproduced the counts. The published script visits each distinct extension once, which gives the same transition probabilities because all generator cosets have the same size. [Population audit](../research/frontier/parity_packing.py)

The gain forces new computations even at the first step: the 99 optimal first masks all have Hamming weight at least three, since exactly those have a disjoint radius-one ball from the zero mask. A cached raw coordinate is inferior. The remaining tie has predictive consequences beyond its immediate loss. No individual, pairwise feature association or scalar immediate gain specifies which optimum to pursue. This population rule is an oracle comparator. It has access to the entire task law. The registered sampled learner also found a greedy trap and recovered a globally optimal layout by exhaustive empirical fitting. [Observed labels, losses and search costs](../research/frontier/EVIDENCE.md#learning-a-four-bit-synopsis-from-noisy-tokens)

**A failed transfer from algebraic geometry explains a limitation of symmetry.** In the original Milne manuscript, §4, equation (4.2), a supermodular defect on a finite-height lattice has a unique largest maximizer, which is preserved by every symmetry of the defect. Two maximizers have maximizing meet and join by the supermodular inequality. A maximal-dimensional maximizer therefore contains all the rest. Raw useful-query coverage fails the needed inequality already on $\mathbb F_2^2$ with one probe. Each of the two coordinate lines and their sum cover four masks, whereas their intersection covers only three. Thus $4+4>4+3$. Subtracting a price per stored dimension leaves this counterexample intact because dimension is modular. Vertex or coordinate symmetry alone does not select a useful representation during learning. [Original proof, §4](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Milnes-rationality-conjecture-for-abelian-varieties-September-23-2026/paper.pdf)

## How much can a learner predict on the first example of an unseen task?

**PROVED.** Let $G=\mathbb F_2^M$ have $N=2^M$ elements. A uniformly random bijection assigns the $N$ names of tasks to the $N$ parity masks. No data or side information reveal the assignments of $r\ge1$ specified names. Grant the learner the true assignments of all the other names and every raw bit of an independent uniform input $X$. Conditioned on these assignments, let $R$ be the $r$ remaining masks. An unseen name has a uniformly random meaning in $R$. The target has independent flip rate $0\le\eta<1/2$. The Bayes-optimal **first** prediction on that name has excess log loss
$$D(R)=E_X\,h\big((1+(1-2\eta)m_R(X))/2\big)-h(\eta),\qquad m_R(x)=r^{-1}\sum_{s\in R}(-1)^{s\cdot x}.$$
Here $h$ is binary entropy in nats. Conditioning on a complete input removes access constraints from this lower bound. Earlier observations about another remaining name can change the posterior.

**Information required by an opaque name.** Orthogonality gives $E m_R^2=1/r$. Put $\phi(u)=\log 2-h((1+u)/2)$. Its series is $\sum_{j\ge1}u^{2j}/[2j(2j-1)]$, with convergence also at the endpoints. Since $|m_R|\le1$, each even moment is at most $1/r$. Therefore
$$D(R)\ge (1-1/r)(\log 2-h(\eta)).$$
Equality requires $|m_R|\in\{0,1\}$ everywhere because all series coefficients are positive. The set where $|m_R|=1$ is the annihilator of the span of the differences in $R$. Its mass is $2^{-\dim\operatorname{aff}(R)}$, which can equal $1/r$ only if $R$ fills its affine hull. Conversely the characters sum to zero off this annihilator for an affine coset. With arbitrary posterior masses $p_s$ independent of the fresh input the same argument replaces $1/r$ by $\sum_s p_s^2$. Sharp equality then forces a uniform distribution on an affine coset by the same annihilator argument and Cauchy–Schwarz. [Entropy-moment expansion](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.7543)

**Which remaining assignments make transfer easiest?** For every $r$ and fixed $\eta<1/2$, $D(R)$ is minimized precisely by the affine images of the binary initial segment $I_r=\{0,\dots,r-1\}$. In particular, non-power-of-two cardinalities incur a strict gap above the simple bound. To prove this, observe that $r^{2j}E m_R^{2j}$ counts the ordered $2j$-tuples from $R$ whose XOR is zero. Huczynska, Rakhmonov and Yip's Theorem 1.5 maximizes every such count at $I_r$. Their argument compresses in two-point fibers along a common direction. A full fiber fixes the number of lifts by parity, and singleton fibers can only gain zero sums as they are compressed. Binary position strictly decreases until each set is initial. Summing the inequalities with the positive entropy coefficients proves minimality. Their functional Theorem 4.9 also proves minimality for binary-ordered posterior weights when the multiset of weights is fixed. [Primary paper, §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07855v1)

For the equality classification, a minimizing set must match the initial segment in *every* even moment. Taking the large-order limit shows its affine dimension is $k=\lceil\log_2 r\rceil$ because that is the dimension of $I_r$. Identify its affine hull with $\mathbb F_2^k$. Translation affects only the signs and an invertible linear map permutes the characters. For every nontrivial character the unnormalized sum on the complement equals the negative sum on $R$; the trivial-character contribution is fixed by the cardinality. Thus the complement also matches the initial-segment complement in every even moment. The latter is a translate of $I_{2^k-r}$. If $r=2^k$ we have the entire hull. Otherwise $0<2^k-r<r$ and induction identifies the complement up to affine equivalence. Complementing in this same hull proves the result for $R$. Any affine equivalence of smaller affine hulls extends to the full space by extending bases. This argument uses all even moments. The primary paper classifies a different equality problem for a **single odd** tuple count.

When $r\le M+1$ the *largest* excess occurs precisely at affinely independent sets. Every zero-XOR tuple in which each point appears an even number of times is present in every configuration and gives the same contribution. An affine dependency gives an additional even-length tuple of distinct points and hence a strict increase in the positive entropy-gain series. An affinely independent configuration has exactly the universal contributions. Its excess is $E_{J\sim{\rm Bin}(r,1/2)}h(\eta+(1-2\eta)J/r)-h(\eta)$, since a possible global sign changes no entropy. A worst-case geometry for every size at every dimension is still open here. [Additive-energy boundary in Remark 5.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07855v1)

The mean over random bijections is also exact. At $X=0$ all clean answers agree. At each other $X$, half of the $N$ masks output one, so the number of ones among the remaining $r$ has law $J\sim{\rm Hypergeom}(N,N/2,r)$. Thus the mean excess is $(1-1/N)[E_J h(\eta+(1-2\eta)J/r)-h(\eta)]$. For $M=7$ and $\eta=1/8$, learning seven whole assignments leaves $r=121$. The expected loss on the first example of a new task is at least 0.690548 nats (exact expression above), even with all seven input bits and the seven meanings revealed perfectly.

At $r=4,\eta=1/8$ the minimum, average and maximum excesses were 0.237283, 0.240529 and 0.240799. Two independent implementations agree. This result concerns log loss. For three remaining masks in $\mathbb F_2^2$ with zero noise, the Bayes classification error is $1/4$ although $(1-1/3)/2=1/3$. ### What compositional information changes the answer?

If a public ID is a vector $t$ and its hidden meaning is $At$ for one unknown binary matrix $A$, correct observed meanings of a spanning set determine all the others by linearity. Under the uniform prior on invertible $A$, after learning its restriction to $W$, images of $t\notin W$ are uniform on $G\setminus A W$. This follows by the transitive action of invertible maps fixing $A W$ pointwise. Under an unrestricted uniform matrix prior, each noise-free ordinary label $y_i=x_i^\top A t_i$ instead gives one linear equation in the unknown entries. A fresh label is determined exactly when its matrix $x t^\top$ belongs to the binary span of the matrices $x_i t_i^\top$. Otherwise adding an annihilating matrix flips that label and preserves all observations, making the posterior clean bit fair. The unrestricted prior and the invertible prior can yield different answers. These are information tests, each with an explicitly supplied composition rule.

A distinct tool appears in Lemma 5.6 of the additive-tuples paper. Let the unobserved keys $S$ be sum-free in a vector space $K$, and $D=K\setminus S$. If learned meanings on $D$ respect every addition with both inputs and the output in $D$, they extend linearly to $K$, coordinate by coordinate. For any zero-sum relation of length at least three in $D$, a pair has its sum in $D$. Otherwise the three pairwise sums of its first three terms form an additive triple in $S$. Replacing the pair by its sum shortens the relation, proving consistency on the span of $D$. Extension from the span uses a basis, and is unique on all $K$ exactly when $D$ spans it (for a nonzero unrestricted output space). If $S$ is the other coset of a hyperplane, the data on that hyperplane leave its complement undetermined. Consistency of an extension from observed keys cannot prove an arbitrary unobserved teacher follows it. Finite noiseless linear tests and a validated inductive assumption give different information about a new task.
