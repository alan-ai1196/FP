# Which latent re-encodings can preserve independent Bayesian factors?

Status: **PROVED, SCOPED; EXHAUSTIVE ENCODING AND EXACT NATIVE AUDITS**.
The [PRODUCT-belief learner law](FACTOR_SIMPLEX_POSTERIOR.md) already derives
posterior-marginal updates from the native simplex U. Its first all-pair
obstruction fixed the original latent coordinates. Here the obstruction
extends to **every fixed bijection** of worlds into independent categorical
factors, including nonlinear changes of coordinates.

The organizing object is the binary matroid of the legal query parities.
For pair queries its components are cycle blocks, not ordinary connected
components. This permits compact exact factors for forest query families,
but forces one factor with all 2^(n-1) categories for the complete all-pair
family. This is a factor-alphabet statement, not a lower bound against count
encodings, conditional graphical models, approximate beliefs or arbitrary
decoders. No Foundation action or production Runtime interface changes.

## 1. Fix the legal future family, while allowing arbitrary coordinates

Let the anchored latent world be z in F2^d, initially uniform. A nonconstant
query e observes the bit `v_e dot z` through known positive noise: its two
likelihood values are a_e and b_e, with a_e,b_e>0 and a_e!=b_e. The present
relation model has d=n-1 and values9/10 and1/10. Constant self-pair queries
impose no factor constraint and can be omitted from this analysis.

Choose any fixed bijection

`T: F2^d -> X_1 x ... x X_m`.

There is no assumption that T is linear, affine, local or cheaply computed.
Under this bijection the uniform initializer is the product of the uniform
categorical factors. Say that T has **exact independent-factor closure** if
the joint posterior remains a product over these same factors after every
finite legal query/label history. Positivity gives full support throughout.
The map and its factor meanings stay fixed during that history.

**Two-value lemma.** A strictly positive, two-valued function on a full
Cartesian product is multiplicatively separable iff it depends on at most
one factor. To prove necessity, write it as a product of positive functions.
If two of them vary, fix all others, increase the first from its minimum to
its maximum, then increase the second. These give three strictly ordered
values, a contradiction. Sufficiency is immediate.

Divide a product posterior after one event by its positive product prior.
Its likelihood must be multiplicatively separable. The lemma therefore
shows that exact closure holds iff **each legal query bit is a function of
one factor alone**. Necessity already follows from one event at the uniform
initializer; sufficiency follows by multiplying local likelihood factors
for any history. The same necessity applies at any positive product prior.

## 2. Minimal parity dependencies cannot cross independent factors

Remove duplicate query vectors and consider their binary linear dependencies.
A circuit is a minimal nonempty set C whose vectors XOR to zero. Partition
the queries by the equivalence relation generated by belonging to a common
circuit; an element in no circuit forms a singleton component. These are
the classical matroid components. See [Tutte, *Lectures on Matroids*,
section3.4, pages8-9](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/69B/jresv69Bn1-2p1_A1b.pdf)
for the separator/component framework. The following argument supplies the
specific link to FP's positive noisy likelihoods and factor alphabets.

Assign each query to the factor on which it depends. For a circuit C,

`PRODUCT_(e in C) (-1)^(v_e dot z) = 1` for every z.

If C used different factors, group these functions by factor. A product of
functions of independent Cartesian coordinates can be identically1 only
if each grouped function is constant. But a product of binary characters
is constant only when their vectors XOR to zero; its constant is then1.
Every nonempty proper group would therefore be a smaller dependency inside
C, contradicting minimality. Thus **every component belongs entirely to
one factor**, even when T is an arbitrary nonlinear bijection.

If a component has binary rank r, choose r independent query vectors from
it. Their bit values realize all 2^r patterns as z varies. Since all are
functions of one categorical factor, that factor needs at least 2^r values.
Components assigned to the same factor have additive ranks, giving the
corresponding 2^(sum of ranks) requirement.

**Attainability.** Choose a vector basis within each component. Their union
is independent: a dependency would contain a circuit crossing components,
or a dependency within one chosen basis, both impossible. The union spans
the query vectors; extend it to a basis of F2^d. Use its dot products with z
as new coordinates, grouped by component, with remaining free coordinates
as fair bits. This is an invertible linear map. Each query depends only on
its component's r bits, so factors of size2^r attain the lower requirement.
Unused bits remain independent and uniform under every legal query history.

The theorem identifies the irreducible components of an exact fixed product
representation. Several independent components may be packed into a larger
factor, but no component can be split by a more ingenious nonlinear bijection.
It does not require explicitly storing every factor entry: an encoded factor
may still have a compact description with a difficult decoder.

## 3. Pair queries: forests are easy, cycles enforce joint factors

Anchor z_0=0. The vector of pair(i,j) has ones at its non-anchor endpoints.
Minimal binary dependencies are exactly simple graph cycles. The components
above are therefore the graph's cycle blocks (including singleton bridges),
with rank equal to the number of vertices in a nontrivial block minus1.
They are not the components obtained merely by testing ordinary connectivity.
For example, two triangles sharing one vertex give two rank2 components,
not one rank4 component. The proof needs only the cycle definition.

For a forest, every edge is a rank1 singleton. Its edge parities, extended
by free orientation bits if necessary, are independent coordinates. Exact
inference can therefore use binary factors even when the observed forest
is connected and its original vertex coordinates are correlated.

For the complete pair family K_n, n>=3, triangles through the anchor connect
all query vectors into one matroid component. The anchor edges are a basis
of rank n-1. Consequently **every fixed independent-factor re-encoding with
exact closure must have one factor of at least2^(n-1) categories**. As this
is the entire world cardinality, every other factor is trivial. The n2 case
has the same conclusion with its single rank1 query.

This closes the fixed-coordinate escape route for the current complete
all-pair learner, without claiming that arbitrary state encodings need an
explicit exponential table. In particular the existing likelihood-count
encoding is not contradicted. Nor does the theorem cover a coordinate map
that changes after every observation, mixtures of product laws, conditional
factorizations, or declared approximations.

In particular, a uniform current posterior can be encoded compactly and
later updated by a faithful general decoder. The theorem does not forbid
that representation; it forbids requiring every successor to remain a
product in the same fixed nontrivial coordinates under all legal queries.

## 4. Native attainment and an actual closing-cycle failure

For the attainable encoding, let m be the number of categorical factors,
give each parameter block mass1/m and use the existing rate1/m simplex U.
For a one-hot query source x_e assigned to factor j(e), construct

`A_y = 8*SUM_e x_e * [SUM_(b: f_e(b)=y) q_(j(e),b)]
       * PRODUCT_(i != j(e)) S_i`,

where q_jb=m*theta_jb and S_i=SUM_b q_ib. The unit source, m and8 are
constructed from positive SUMs; bases are(1,1). This is an ordinary native
Program. It retains the inactive S_i factors and their ambient gradients.
On an existing left/right one-hot pair interface, x_e can itself be formed
by positive PRODUCTs, summing the two orientations for an unordered pair.
This adds counted native syntax rather than a posterior-valued input.
Its constant coefficient-column total8 meets the earlier learner theorem,
so each actual U updates the exact local posterior and preserves the full
product law. Prefix/suffix products share the inactive factors. This is a
constructive existence result, with actual syntax and complete states audited
in small examples, not a production constructor or free-query interface.

The boundary is observable in an existing native learner. With three
vertices choose independent coordinates u=z_1 and v=z_1 XOR z_2. A fixed
two-factor Program accepts all three pair queries. The history

`(0,1,label0), (1,2,label0), (0,2,label0)`

first updates u and v independently. The third observation closes the cycle
and creates dependence. The actual native U retains the two posterior
marginals but loses their joint law. On the next(0,2) query it forecasts
`761/882`, whereas the full posterior forecasts `37/42`; the exact gap is
`8/441`. No arithmetic refusal, changed optimizer or erased gradient causes
this discrepancy. The program's product state lacks a required correlation.

## 5. A separate snapshot law and the retained model tapes

At a current cut, let a_e be the count of label0 minus label1 for unordered
pair e. The exact known-noise posterior satisfies

`log P(z) = constant + (log 9)/2 * SUM_e a_e*(-1)^(v_e dot z)`.

The active vectors are those with a_e!=0, not merely every edge ever seen.
Balanced contrary observations cancel from this posterior expression; their
clocks and other complete native state are not thereby erased.

Under an invertible **linear parity-coordinate map**, the finest independent
snapshot factors are the components of this active vector configuration.
For necessity, a product density has an additive log density. Its Walsh
coefficients vanish on characters crossing coordinate blocks. Distinct active
vectors have distinct nonzero coefficients in the expression above, so they
must each lie inside one block's character subspace. The circuit argument
then applies. The basis construction proves sufficiency.

This snapshot necessity is only for linear parity coordinates. The theorem
for arbitrary nonlinear bijections in sections1-3 instead concerns closure
over a complete legal future family. The scopes must not be interchanged.
