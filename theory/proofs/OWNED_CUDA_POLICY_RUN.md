# One owned Compiler policy through a finite CUDA run

This is the v1 policy requiring historical class selection. The
[v2 prospective extension](OWNED_PROSPECTIVE_SELECTION.md) adds a separately
audited path that keeps incomplete classes unresolved after installation.

Status: **implemented scoped policy/run composition.** This extends the
registered CPU strategy to its already implemented CUDA physical path.
Foundation R4, XVII.31 and ERC-1 remain frozen. A sealed finite stream is
not `CERTIFIED_COMPLETE`, a population result or a target release certificate.

## 1. The strategy is unchanged; the physical evidence path is explicit

The [owned Compiler strategy](OWNED_COMPILER_POLICY.md) searches a declared
native constructor class, starts fresh paired comparisons and attempts its
registered installation. Its control decisions depend on owned completion,
admission, crossing and installation results. Its graph language and legal
information interface do not depend on whether the physical learner is CPU
binary64 or the registered CUDA half/single implementation.

`CudaCompilerPolicy` and `CudaCompilationStep` bind the latter choice before
execution. A CUDA step names a native search class, an earliest ordinary
cursor, a finite search allowance, a reference rule and a CUDA rule. The
same Runtime strategy executes both backends; it dispatches only the
physical admission/comparison/installation operations. No graph example,
model endpoint, callback, supplied policy state or new architecture action
enters this registration. Path-specific immutable records retain the name
`cuda_identity`; CPU evidence is not relabelled, and the existing CPU record
encodings retain their original fields.

The CUDA policy requires its actual CUDA prefix and a live registered host
resource contract. Nonempty compilation also requires the owned resident
installation. Empty steps give the closed ordinary CUDA baseline. A CPU
policy cannot control a CUDA root, or vice versa. Step/rule validation fixes
matching epoch schedules and their distinct reference/CUDA score paths.

In owned mode only ordinary context/target transport and passive snapshots
remain external. Search, admission, cancellation, comparisons and install
ports are exclusively controlled by Runtime. The future control-port rule
defaults to denial. A caller cannot substitute the reference crossing for
the physical one or choose a new rule after seeing evidence.

## 2. Transition argument on complete state

At a complete optimizer boundary the strategy takes the first unfinished
registered step. It starts and advances the named native search through the
existing paid endpoint. An exhausted strategy allowance closes the frontier
as unresolved; it does not certify grammar exhaustion. Completed historical
selection retains its exact constructor-endpoint class plus actual baseline.
The categorical empirical-upper solver keeps its separate bounded-proof
meaning. Neither result optimizes all CUDA parameters or future trajectories.

If the owned best lineage is the deployed baseline, the stage ends without
fresh allocations or a rebuild. Otherwise reference and CUDA admissions each
execute their existing whole-domain, complete-start, freshness and alpha
checks before the next context. Admission failures preserve path-specific
reasons, owned identities and spent allocations. In particular, a declared
future comparison horizon must fit the remaining registered stream; expected
early crossing is not permission to omit that resource premise.

Each later ordinary event advances the same continuously executed learners.
The strategy invokes the owned paired-CUDA result at complete optimizer
boundaries and requires both current crossings. Loss of an identity ends
the stage without refunding alpha. It never rebases a same-graph newborn,
rescores a fresh observation under a substituted forecast, or copies CPU
or reference wealth into the CUDA path.

Successful installation uses the
[resident identity transition](OWNED_CUDA_INSTALLATION.md). The completed
`INSTALLED_CUDA` policy record, its owned bytes, the new deployment, all
learners, retained search/evidence history and the receipt publish in that
same root transaction. No allocating policy bookkeeping follows the
publication. Failure to prepare the completed policy record therefore
cannot leave an installed learner with a still-retryable old policy step.

This is a composition of existing search, evidence and physical transport
operations on their owned states. It introduces no semantic action beyond
those already represented in the frozen Foundation. The single-root external
stochastic-law assumptions and alpha scope remain unchanged.

## 3. A finite target report has its own publication boundary

After the final ordinary event and its owned control phase, Runtime runs
the existing paid finite-report transition. Partial optimizer units are
retained; there is no extra observation, forced update or evidence flush.
Waiting or unfinished stages are reported unresolved while their actual
states, identities and wealth remain owned. Successful stream completion
does not assert that any installation or evidence comparison succeeded.

For CUDA, report preparation additionally verifies the complete quiescent
device frame, all retained current learners at their own clocks, initialized
tensor extents and their actual raw states. It checks their current complete
reference/CUDA relations. A target diagnostic scans already retained forecast
words through exact encoding injection, reporting the observed activation,
mass, normalizer and rational-encoding ranges. These are observed-prefix
diagnostics, not new whole-domain range or hardware-significand theorems.

`CudaRunClosure` retains the bound device/framebuffer record, current device
transport frame, checked/nonchecked phase counts, the historical reference
decisions, native P/S/edge counts, stage outcomes, partial-unit count and spent
alpha. The complete report consists of this header **and the full Runtime
snapshot**, with all raw phase, learner, resource and evidence history.
The aggregate nonchecked count does not erase individual failure classes.

The report's bytes/work are paid and allocated before final device-frame
verification. Only then is `_run_closure` published. A prepared report object
in an owned buffer is not a completed run. Any late device failure keeps that
buffer and its costs but prevents publication and revokes continuation.
Restoring a corrupted view or observer does not restore authority. A report
failure after successful installation retains the installation and completed
ordinary event; it cannot manufacture a successful terminal report.

`SEALED_CUDA_STREAM` denotes this finite owned protocol. All continuation and
installation ports are then closed. The retained historical class proofs
keep their original scope and do not become current target optima.

## 4. Resource composition and executed controls

All runs in `scripts/audit_cuda_policy_run.py` use the actual RTX 3090 and
the existing native tensor arena, inside a Windows worker fenced before
execution at 4 GiB private commitment. The
[actual device binding and 24 GiB framebuffer envelope](WHOLE_BOARD_CUDA_RESOURCES.md)
are mandatory CUDA coordinates. Host, native tensor, framebuffer and packed
reference/evidence measures remain separately typed; observing them does
not create an additive disjoint-byte total. Work includes the existing
construction, evidence, install and report debits. Host CPU ticks are
observations; no hard GPU-time or all-instruction cap is inferred.

A two-stage run selects two complete 35-member classes, installs at cursors
22 and 38, spends fresh total alpha 3/4 and seals at 60. Its 456 CUDA and 456
CPU binary64 phases have independent replays. With the otherwise identical
40-event stream, the second 30-epoch admission at cursor 24 is unresolved:
only 16 events remain. The first install and second selection survive, no
new identity or alpha debit is invented, and the report retains the reason.

The audit also covers the trained 774-member class, full recurrent queues,
absence of the optional CPU learner, complete short binary branches,
baseline selection, search allowance exhaustion and partial/unfinished
stream endings. Reporting resource/device/allocation/unexpected failures,
failed policy retention and failed combined install-policy preparation have
their own endpoint controls. An actual same-value tensor-shape corruption
after report allocation is refused by final verification; its prepared bytes
cannot substitute for the unpublished closure.

The complete recorded audit passes in 32 independently fenced workers. The
trained and recurrent runs replay 970 and 320 CUDA phases respectively;
all 16 four-event binary streams replay another 776 phases and retain eight
baseline decisions and eight unresolved evidence outcomes. Every worker
exits successfully with the expected process identity and lifetime. The
largest completed job commitment is 2,794,962,944 bytes, below its registered
4 GiB cap. These finite controls support this implementation boundary, not
exhaustiveness over arbitrary target programs or streams.
