# What can prediction teach a machine to compute?

Research branch based on main at `a35ef18`. The object of study is a learner that pays to obtain information and predicts the next observation. The question is which of its computational distinctions can be inferred from the prediction problem. The historical FP definitions and experiment records retain their original meaning.

## Part I — Historical diagnosis

The strongest evidence for changing the research question is a pair of failures at opposite ends of FP's history. The R4.2 program had a PRODUCT constructor, but its real training trace built zero PRODUCT nodes. Much later a native reference path retained enough information to reconstruct every value, yet selected live allocations alone exceeded the original text trial's entire host budget. The first problem was how to learn a computation. The second was the price of realizing a particular observable history. Neither is answered by how succinctly an ideal circuit expresses a function. ## Part II — Axiomatic reconstruction

Three independent declarations suffice to **pose** a test of emergence.

1. **A causal prediction experiment.** Specify what arrives before the forecast, what target arrives afterwards, and how errors across time and contexts are scored. A finite tape with a reporting rule suffices for a descriptive score. A population claim additionally needs a law. Giving today's target one step earlier can reduce log loss to zero on pure noise.
2. **A physical observation and memory interface.** Specify which cells, words or channels a machine may access, their availability, which state it can retain, and the units charged for reading, computing, writing and initializing. A different input encoding can change the optimal prediction cost by an arbitrary factor, as proved below. Physical prices and scientific loss are independent data.
3. **A learner and comparison contract.** Specify the initial information, the transitions from a scored observation to the next state, the budgets and the comparison quantifiers. An oracle given a hidden address at initialization and a learner receiving its first example have different risks on the same machine. A claim of a *learned* structure requires the second trajectory. A claim of a *forced* structure also quantifies over competing feasible learners or policies and identifies symmetries.

Normalized predictive probabilities follow from choosing a probabilistic forecast and log loss. Semantic positivity of internal registers, graph syntax, lineage certificates and atomic replacement do not follow. Fresh evidence follows when estimating prospective improvement after selection. A device claim requires the behavior of the actual numerical implementation. Exact replay, safety against every possible future control, and an anytime installation test are additional observables when the question requires them. Measured state, predictive quality and per-step computation can all matter even when replay is absent.

For example, let $H$ be the observed past, $U=f(H)$ a retained statistic and $Y_{1:d}$ the future of a fixed passive law. With ideal conditional forecasts and the same subsequently revealed tokens, the **exact excess cumulative log risk** of the projection is

$$H(Y_{1:d}\mid U)-H(Y_{1:d}\mid H)=I(H;Y_{1:d}\mid U).$$

This follows by the chain rule and the strict propriety of log loss. A one-step equality can fail at the next step. For instance the first future bit can be fresh fair noise and the second can repeat a forgotten fair past bit. Future actions which change the stream, reconstruction demands, model fitting error and physical execution require their own account. This gives a quantitative permission to forget for a specified predictive decision.

**Obstruction to a foundation based on prediction alone.** Let $M\ge2$ independent fair bits occupy separate cells. Let $Y=X_1\oplus E$ where $E$ is independent Bernoulli noise of rate $0\le\eta<1/2$. In a second encoding replace $X_1$ by the XOR of all cells and leave the other $M-1$ cells unchanged. This transformation is an involution over $\mathbf F_2$. The full statistical experiments are isomorphic. In the second encoding the target without noise is the parity of all cells. Give an adaptive randomized machine an expected budget of $b\ge0$ unit reads per fresh row, with no access to preprocessing of that row. Write $h$ for binary entropy in nats and $D=\log 2-h(\eta)$. The exact optimal losses are

$$L_{\rm direct}(b)=\log 2-D\min(1,b),\qquad
L_{\rm parity}(b)=\log 2-D\min(1,b/M).$$

Condition on a stopped transcript and private coins independent of the row. An unread necessary bit leaves the clean target fair. Reading the necessary set leaves entropy $h(\eta)$. The probability of completion is bounded by the expected budget divided by the size of that set. Toss a coin before starting and read the whole necessary set on success to attain both bounds. With an integer hard cap, parity has zero gain below $M$, even at $M-1$. Every individual first read can be worthless although all are jointly valuable.

Thus **no rule invariant under invertible recoding of the full context can recover physical risk versus reads for all such presentations**. Charging the recoding resolves the contradiction by specifying an interface. The obstruction persists with known distributions, unlimited training and optimal adaptive policies. Its unit is a cell read. A machine receiving a packed parity word, a preprocessing index or a persistent summary pays a different cost. This proves a limit on what the learning objective can determine, including for strong conventional networks.

## Part III — Competing native representations

**1. Residual future laws.** Take the state of a positive-probability prefix to be its conditional law of all future words. Predict from its first-symbol marginal and update by conditioning on the observed symbol. Equal residuals have equal outgoing probabilities and equal successor residuals on supported symbols. Every deterministic exact recursively sufficient predictor refines this partition. Discrete states and shared continuations arise as equality classes. They can be infinite. The classical causal-state result assumes a process and an observer of its future. R4 already generalizes this kind of congruence to other observers. [Shalizi and Crutchfield](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9907176) · [Foundation II](../../FP_THEORY.md)

A finite learning realization for independently reset length-$d$ blocks stores prefix/next-symbol counts. After each reveal increment the count at the actual prefix. Use add-one conditional probabilities for unobserved branches, with uniform transitions below an unseen prefix through the declared horizon. At a given depth, intern identical vectors of probabilities and successor identities bottom-up. This yields the minimal same-depth graph for the fitted finite predictive tree by induction. Keep per-prefix counts for later updates since equal current fitted laws can separate after one label. Worst-case full counts take $O(A^d)$ entries for alphabet size $A\ge2$, each up to $\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$ bits for $n$ blocks, plus address and update work. Bottom-up recomputation is a finite algorithm even without incremental reuse. It does not learn an unbounded process's causal states from one arbitrary tape. If two laws differ in the deterministic label after a visible context of probability $p$, an independent fair choice of law forces expected fresh excess at least $p(1-p)^n\log 2$ after $n$ independent samples. On the unobserved context the two laws give the same training data. Even an unrestricted learner pays this price. A count tree also misses compact reusable arithmetic such as parity until the right residuals have evidence. Context-tree weighting gives a strong, already established sequential alternative for bounded suffix-tree sources. [Willems, Shtarkov and Tjalkens](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/1383848/Metis122608.pdf)

**2. A stochastic predictive code.** Learn a channel $q(z\mid h)$ on a finite code alphabet, with decoder $p(y\mid z)$ obtained by marginalizing the empirical joint law. Minimizing $I(H;Z)-\beta I(Z;Y)$ prices statistical rate against log risk. For positive active cells the variational update is proportional to $q(z)\exp[-\beta D_{\rm KL}(p(Y\mid h)\Vert p(Y\mid z))]$. Normalize each row, recompute the marginal and decoder, and repeat a declared finite number of iterations after ordinary sample-count updates. This derives a real fitting rule. It needs an alphabet, empirical rows, a starting channel and a precision policy. A direct sweep costs $O(|H||Z||Y|)$ scalar work and stores the joint counts and channel. It naturally yields soft and deterministic assignments and can share predictions across histories. [Tishby, Pereira and Bialek, equations 16–18 and Theorem 5](https://www.princeton.edu/~wbialek/our_papers/tishby%2Bal_99.pdf)

There is a decisive learning failure even for two symbols. Take $H=Y$ fair and $|Z|=2$. From any positive initialization independent of $H$ the two decoder rows agree, and the update leaves the whole channel invariant. For $\beta>1$ the identity code has objective $(1-\beta)\log 2<0$ versus zero at that fixed point. The optimizer cannot discover the distinction from this initialization. A nonsymmetric start adds information or random variation whose effects have to be tested. Also $I(H;Z)=\log 2$ for both a direct bit and parity of $M$ bits. The physical read curves above still differ by $M$. Statistical rate can price communication under a specified coding model, but it omits the work of forming the code and the recursive-state constraint in a stream.

**3. Sequential prediction with priced observations.** The state consists of the information the learner has actually retained. A read is a stochastic experiment with a specified source and price. A prediction is the conditional distribution given the obtained transcript, an update conditions on the later ordinary label. With finite known joint laws, a terminal transcript costs its conditional entropy. With a hard cap, compare stopping there against averaging successor values over each affordable read. Backward induction gives the exact Bayes value. The full recursion can have exponentially many transcripts, and an unknown law must be acquired. A Bellman equation alone would relocate FP's oracle.

This third formulation earns further study because there is a channel-invariance condition under which the computation, its learner and an optimal execution policy can all be derived explicitly. The condition can itself be falsified. It trades the general program grammar for an assumption about which transformations of an observable value the predictor must respect.
