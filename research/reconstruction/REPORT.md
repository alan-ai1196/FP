# What can prediction teach a machine to compute?

Research branch based on main at `a35ef18`. The object of study is a learner that pays to obtain information and predicts the next observation. The question is which of its computational distinctions can be inferred from the prediction problem. The historical FP definitions and experiment records retain their original meaning.

**Verdict.** Prediction can teach a machine to stop reading particular sources. It cannot by itself specify what a read costs or what a source means. When a readout must respect arbitrary changes of value representation, its underlying object is a probability law on source-address paths. We derive its learning and execution laws, including a case where discovering a one-bit latent distinction takes exponentially many examples while knowing it improves prediction by exponentially little. A positive FP program wins a direct falsification test. On ordinary text, even hindsight cannot make a fixed bank of lag-copy weights competitive with the recorded baselines. The evidence supports a foundation for studying routing and argues against promoting routing to the universal FP algebra. ## Part II — Axiomatic reconstruction

Three independent declarations suffice to **pose** a test of emergence.

1. **A causal prediction experiment.** Specify what arrives before the forecast, what target arrives afterwards, and how errors across time and contexts are scored. A finite tape with a reporting rule suffices for a descriptive score. A population claim additionally needs a law. Giving today's target one step earlier can reduce log loss to zero on pure noise.
2. **A physical observation and memory interface.** Specify which cells, words or channels a machine may access, their availability, which state it can retain, and the units charged for reading, computing, writing and initializing. A different input encoding can change the optimal prediction cost by an arbitrary factor, as proved below. Physical prices and scientific loss are independent data.
3. **A learner and comparison contract.** Specify the initial information, the transitions from a scored observation to the next state, the budgets and the comparison quantifiers. An oracle given a hidden address at initialization and a learner receiving its first example have different risks on the same machine. A claim of a *learned* structure requires the second trajectory. A claim of a *forced* structure also quantifies over competing feasible learners or policies and identifies symmetries.

Normalized predictive probabilities follow from choosing a probabilistic forecast and log loss. Semantic positivity of internal registers, graph syntax, lineage certificates and atomic replacement do not follow. Fresh evidence follows when estimating prospective improvement after selection. A device claim requires the behavior of the actual numerical implementation. Exact replay, safety against every possible future control, and an anytime installation test are additional observables when the question requires them. Measured state, predictive quality and per-step computation can all matter even when replay is absent.

For example, let $H$ be the observed past, $U=f(H)$ a retained statistic and $Y_{1:d}$ the future of a fixed passive law. With ideal conditional forecasts and the same subsequently revealed tokens, the **exact excess cumulative log risk** of the projection is

$$H(Y_{1:d}\mid U)-H(Y_{1:d}\mid H)=I(H;Y_{1:d}\mid U).$$

This follows by the chain rule and the strict propriety of log loss. A one-step equality can fail at the next step. For instance the first future bit can be fresh fair noise and the second can repeat a forgotten fair past bit. Future actions which change the stream, reconstruction demands, model fitting error and physical execution require their own account. This gives a quantitative permission to forget for a specified predictive decision.

**Obstruction to a foundation based on prediction alone.** Let $M\ge2$ independent fair bits occupy separate cells. Let $Y=X_1\oplus E$ where $E$ is independent Bernoulli noise of rate $0\le\eta<1/2$. In a second encoding replace $X_1$ by the XOR of all cells and leave the other $M-1$ cells unchanged. This transformation is an involution over $\mathbf F_2$. The full statistical experiments are isomorphic. In the second encoding the target without noise is the parity of all cells. Give an adaptive randomized machine an expected budget of $b\ge0$ unit reads per fresh row, with no access to preprocessing of that row. Write $h$ for binary entropy in nats and $D=\log 2-h(\eta)$. The exact optimal losses are

$$L_{\rm direct}(b)=\log 2-D\min(1,b),\qquad
L_{\rm parity}(b)=\log 2-D\min(1,b/M).$$

Condition on a stopped transcript and private coins independent of the row. An unread necessary bit leaves the clean target fair. Reading the necessary set leaves entropy $h(\eta)$. The probability of completion is bounded by the expected budget divided by the size of that set. Toss a coin before starting and read the whole necessary set on success to attain both bounds. With an integer hard cap, parity has zero gain below $M$, even at $M-1$. Every individual first read can be worthless although all are jointly valuable.

Thus **no rule invariant under invertible recoding of the full context can recover physical risk versus reads for all such presentations**. Charging the recoding resolves the contradiction by specifying an interface. The obstruction persists with known distributions, unlimited training and optimal adaptive policies. Its unit is a cell read. A machine receiving a packed parity word, a preprocessing index or a persistent summary pays a different cost. This proves a limit on what the learning objective can determine, including for strong conventional networks.

## Part III — Competing native representations

**1. Residual future laws.** Take the state of a positive-probability prefix to be its conditional law of all future words. Predict from its first-symbol marginal and update by conditioning on the observed symbol. Equal residuals have equal outgoing probabilities and equal successor residuals on supported symbols. Every deterministic exact recursively sufficient predictor refines this partition. Discrete states and shared continuations arise as equality classes. They can be infinite. The classical causal-state result assumes a process and an observer of its future. R4 already generalizes this kind of congruence to other observers. [Shalizi and Crutchfield](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9907176) · [Foundation II](../../FP_THEORY.md)

A finite learning realization for independently reset length-$d$ blocks stores prefix/next-symbol counts. After each reveal increment the count at the actual prefix. Use add-one conditional probabilities for unobserved branches, with uniform transitions below an unseen prefix through the declared horizon. At a given depth, intern identical vectors of probabilities and successor identities bottom-up. This yields the minimal same-depth graph for the fitted finite predictive tree by induction. Keep per-prefix counts for later updates since equal current fitted laws can separate after one label. Worst-case full counts take $O(A^d)$ entries for alphabet size $A\ge2$, each up to $\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$ bits for $n$ blocks, plus address and update work. Bottom-up recomputation is a finite algorithm even without incremental reuse. It does not learn an unbounded process's causal states from one arbitrary tape. If two laws differ in the deterministic label after a visible context of probability $p$, an independent fair choice of law forces expected fresh excess at least $p(1-p)^n\log 2$ after $n$ independent samples. On the unobserved context the two laws give the same training data. Even an unrestricted learner pays this price. A count tree also misses compact reusable arithmetic such as parity until the right residuals have evidence. Context-tree weighting gives a strong, already established sequential alternative for bounded suffix-tree sources. [Willems, Shtarkov and Tjalkens](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/1383848/Metis122608.pdf)

In the frozen compiled tree one observed symbol requires an indexed transition from the current state. Scoring one given symbol can read its single rational probability, while producing the full vector writes $A$ entries. The smoothed numerators and denominators fit in $O(\log(n+A))$ bits. Merging rows by approximate floating equality can change future behavior, so this construction compares exact probabilities and successor identities.

**2. A stochastic predictive code.** Learn a channel $q(z\mid h)$ on a finite code alphabet, with decoder $p(y\mid z)$ obtained by marginalizing the empirical joint law. Minimizing $I(H;Z)-\beta I(Z;Y)$ prices statistical rate against log risk. For positive active cells the variational update is proportional to $q(z)\exp[-\beta D_{\rm KL}(p(Y\mid h)\Vert p(Y\mid z))]$. Normalize each row, recompute the marginal and decoder, and repeat a declared finite number of iterations after ordinary sample-count updates. This derives a real fitting rule. It needs an alphabet, empirical rows, a starting channel and a precision policy. A direct sweep costs $O(|H||Z||Y|)$ scalar work and stores the joint counts and channel. It naturally yields soft and deterministic assignments and can share predictions across histories. [Tishby, Pereira and Bialek, equations 16–18 and Theorem 5](https://www.princeton.edu/~wbialek/our_papers/tishby%2Bal_99.pdf)

There is a decisive learning failure even for two symbols. Take $H=Y$ fair and $|Z|=2$. From any positive initialization independent of $H$ the two decoder rows agree, and the update leaves the whole channel invariant. For $\beta>1$ the identity code has objective $(1-\beta)\log 2<0$ versus zero at that fixed point. The optimizer cannot discover the distinction from this initialization. A nonsymmetric start adds information or random variation whose effects have to be tested. Also $I(H;Z)=\log 2$ for both a direct bit and parity of $M$ bits. The physical read curves above still differ by $M$. Statistical rate can price communication under a specified coding model, but it omits the work of forming the code and the recursive-state constraint in a stream.

The encoder alone has $|H|(|Z|-1)$ free probabilities. Once the input history has been acquired, evaluating the complete output of the soft encoder costs up to $O(|Z||Y|)$ arithmetic. A sampled message has different output semantics. A numerical implementation has to account for exponentials in the assignment step, especially when an underflowed small assignment would become a permanently unused code. Thus the fitting algorithm, its stationary objective and the cost of deploying its channel are three separate claims.

**3. Sequential prediction with priced observations.** The state consists of the information the learner has actually retained. A read is a stochastic experiment with a specified source and price. A prediction is the conditional distribution given the obtained transcript, an update conditions on the later ordinary label. With finite known joint laws, a terminal transcript costs its conditional entropy. With a hard cap, compare stopping there against averaging successor values over each affordable read. Backward induction gives the exact Bayes value. The full recursion can have exponentially many transcripts, and an unknown law must be acquired. A Bellman equation alone would relocate FP's oracle.

This third formulation earns further study because there is a channel-invariance condition under which the computation, its learner and an optimal execution policy can all be derived explicitly. The condition can itself be falsified. It trades the general program grammar for an assumption about which transformations of an observable value the predictor must respect. The following parts evaluate that trade.

## Part IV — The strongest candidate and its scope

**An address process with priced observations** is the strongest scoped candidate. Suppose each time $t$ has $M$ value-producing sources. After conditioning on fixed side information, require that independently postprocessing the source values at each time by a stochastic channel can be deferred until after prediction. Use separately tagged values once as a *mathematical* input. Their joint output is a unique law $\mu$ over address paths. Applying the actual value channels back to those tags proves

$$P(y_{1:d}\mid p)=\sum_{j_{1:d}}\mu(j_{1:d})\prod_t p_{t,j_t}(y_t).$$

Conversely every such law has the required invariance. Prefix consistency is exactly marginal consistency of the address laws. This derives the algebra of a readout from a measurable property of its inputs and outputs. Finite positive sums and products are coordinates of marginalization for this restricted problem. R4 can also compute transformations of values that violate the invariance. [Proof of the characterization](ADDRESS_PROCESS.md#the-multitime-characterization)

The mathematical tags grant the learner no source labels. On independent fair binary columns with independent known noise $\eta<1/2$, each address-word mass can actually be acquired from an ordinary correlation of observed columns and next labels. The empirical signed moments, positive projection and normalization give a consistent causal learner for a fixed reset-block horizon. The literal learner takes exponentially many counters in that horizon. For a persistent but unidentified address, Bayes' rule instead gives a compact and inspectable online update. It has the following consequences.

| Question | Result with its assumptions |
|---|---|
| Where does a learned edge come from? | With zero noise, eliminate only addresses contradicted by an observed label. After $n$ full training rows exactly $1+\mathrm{Binomial}(M-1,2^{-n})$ addresses survive in law. Every survivor is necessary for an exact full forecast. The sharp expected cumulative training-read count is $n+2(M-1)(1-2^{-n})$. The actual accessed graph shrinks from $M$ live sources to one when the data identify it. |
| What is a symbol? | With at most $K$ source-dependent deployment messages and one fresh read, even an encoder given the true uniform address maximizes predictive gain by isolating $K-1$ identities and pooling the remainder. Permutations of the isolated addresses tie. A concrete finite-data encoder approaches this frontier as the evidence accumulates. The winning partition sizes follow from log loss and the memory/read interface. |
| How do categorical and continuous descriptions meet? | The discrete address is a vertex of the simplex. Its conditional law is a continuous certainty state, and updating that state changes which reads pay. Values may be any finite-alphabet distributions. Finite precision costs apply to their evaluation. This accounts for uncertainty about selection; it supplies no universal continuous coordinate system for a computation. |
| When is attention justified? | With a uniform prior, full training observations and a binary symmetric channel, posterior weights are exactly a softmax of past sign-dot-products with a temperature fixed by noise. At zero noise, a compatible tape leaves a uniform distribution over consistent keys. A general address law can instead have no dot-product factorization of this size. The proof supplies neither learned embeddings nor a reason to use Transformer blocks. |
| What should be read? | Among every policy with a hard budget of $k$ equally expensive, independent fair cells, reading the $k$ largest posterior weights minimizes expected fresh log loss. At positive noise the loss paid for skipping the tail has upper and lower bounds proportional to its squared mass norm. Correlated cells can reverse the ordering. Exact likelihood evaluation and sampling from the same kernel can require $M$ and one value reads respectively. |

The [read, learning and memory theorems](ROUTING.md) give the assumptions, equations, resource units, algorithms and proofs. Structural change here means learning an executable input dependency. Arbitrary higher-level variables, reusable label transformations and general module construction require an additional result.

**Why identifying a structure can be the wrong target.** For any two equally likely finite episode laws, let $g$ be the expected first-episode causal log loss saved by knowing the world, and $s$ the information about it revealed by unscored observations. Their sum is the Jensen–Shannon divergence. The optimal sample cost for identifying the world with error at most $1/4$ under independent resets is $\Theta(1/(g+s))$ when the laws differ. This connects an established binary testing result to the actual prediction filtration. With a common causal input kernel $s=0$. It need not vanish under an informative input channel, and three worlds already defeat a sufficiency bound based on average gain alone. [General law, constants and counterexamples](ADDRESS_PROCESS.md#general-binary-law-and-the-role-of-unscored-observations)

Two compact address laws illustrate the consequence. They use one running parity bit and a block clock to generate respectively even and odd hidden paths. Only a block in which every pair of source cells differs can expose their distinction. For $d$ events and label reliability $\theta=1-2\eta$, a fully observed block has total variation $2^{-d}\theta^d$ between the worlds. Knowing the world improves expected **whole-block** log loss by exactly $g_d=2^{-d}[\log 2-h((1+\theta^d)/2)]$. Identifying it costs $\Theta(2^d/\theta^{2d})$ blocks. A learner given these two possible laws can predict by their posterior mixture with cumulative regret at most $\log 2$ on every supported tape. This separates classifying a mechanism from predicting its observable consequences. [Acquisition and value proof](ADDRESS_PROCESS.md#a-two-state-separation-of-observation-identification-and-value)

## Part V — Falsification

| Hypothesis under attack | Counterexample or refuting observation |
|---|---|
| Predictive equivalence determines physical structure. | Part II's reversible linear recoding multiplies the reads needed to obtain the same gain by $M$. Requiring a sample instead of a scored probability separates costs even without recoding. |
| Each read should justify itself by immediate information gain. | Every proper subset of independent parity bits is worthless for predicting parity. Only joint acquisition pays. The proposed price signal must evaluate attainable sequences of reads. |
| A short code ensures a cheap causal learning algorithm. | The one-bit bottleneck for parity is expensive to form. The independent channel is stationary at a strictly suboptimal objective for the perfectly predictable two-symbol source. The same first-observation law can also correspond to persistence or complete redraw, with different second forecasts. |
| Compact generation makes the hidden computation learnable. | The even/odd address separation holds even when a tester is told both possible mechanisms and given every physical input. Unrestricted moment acquisition is possible but costly. No numerical tolerance resolves the missing observations. |
| Arbitrary prediction is a routing problem over unchanged values. | An opaque readout must preserve unanimity. The noisy XOR example violates it. An R4 positive recurrence and an ordinary table both learn the transformation on the registered tape; an oracle router still loses. A signed network can also express clean XOR on bits as $x+z-2\max(0,x+z-1)$. That formula alone says nothing about training it. |
| A small posterior can always be forgotten. | Sixteen net matches at noise $1/4$ give the losing address mass $1/43046722$, below the binary16 round-to-zero threshold. Sixteen opposite observations restore equal exact weights. Persistent integer evidence and the probability emitted at one time have different precision requirements. |
| A sparse learned read policy is already a language model. | The full-text class exclusion in Part VII survives arbitrary training and even fitting the fixed vector on the scored labels. The generic routing theorem loses its source-independence premise on ordinary lagged tokens. |

These attacks leave a specific falsifiable scientific proposal. On a task claiming a learned physical dependency, first exhibit which value relabelings or postprocessings the proposed readout respects, then predict how the dependency changes *paid* reads and held-out loss. A violation of commutation refutes the proposed operator class for that task. Failure to acquire the necessary state from the ordinary prefix refutes a learnability claim even if a hand-supplied state works. Equal loss and cost from a conventional implementation defeats a claim of advantage.

Removing internal positivity loses R5's nonnegative responsibility decomposition and the monotone-support theorems. Signed moments are nevertheless legal acquisition statistics for a probabilistic prediction whose output is nonnegative. Removing SUM/PRODUCT as axioms retains them where summation over alternatives and independent emissions entail them. It loses R4's native ability to *construct* new interacting values unless a new representation derives that operation. Removing an explicit graph retains a causal transducer or measure, while its cost still requires a realization and the given access interface. Removing categorical factors retains operational symbols as distinguishable messages and, where proved, posterior vertices. Removing a named optimizer requires an actual replacement update such as the filter or empirical moment estimator. Removing historical replay requires sufficiency for the chosen horizon, output operation and future learner. None of these implications makes R4's full installation obligations necessary for an offline capacity bound.

## Part VII — Minimal decisive experiments

The [predictions and comparison protocol](EXPERIMENTS.md) preceded the respective tests. Results are separated by what the observer actually saw.

1. **Exact synthetic verification.** A literal latent-world oracle and an unrestricted adaptive-read recursion check the conditional probability laws, including 19,401 training worlds, 1,098 policy decisions and 2,308 memory partitions across noise choices. Likelihood products compare log risks exactly. Correlating two columns reverses the ranking as predicted. A second, explicit emission/noise enumeration verifies the multitime result for depths 1–4 and recovers 60 asymmetric path probabilities across eight settings. At depth four, observational total variation is $1/16$ without noise and $1/256$ at noise $1/4$. A separate check enumerates the optimal testing threshold for all 36 pairs of Bernoulli rates on the eighths grid and verifies the general binary bounds. An early read with zero immediate predictive gain wins when its value for a second forecast is included. [Routing audit](audit_routing.py) · [Address process audit](audit_address_process.py)
2. **Direct R4 and conventional alternatives.** In the preregistered four-context test, the exact repository evaluator and a conventional independently updated add-one table agree on every forecast before its label. The positive program has eight delayed counts and 24 PRODUCTs. Its graph is supplied, all counts start at zero, and its one coefficient is fixed at one. All label assignments enter through post-forecast observations. After 16 events, both predictors assign $2/3$ to the clean label in all four contexts. At input $(1,1)$ even an oracle choosing the two noisy source values must assign $3/4$ to the wrong clean label. For expected fresh log loss $L$, the exact values of $\exp(-16L)$ are $4096/43046721$ for the two trained tables and $59049/4294967296$ for the optimal gate, a strict loss separation. This exercises the positive arithmetic and delay semantics with an explicitly supplied graph. [Comparative audit](audit_foundation_comparison.py)

## Part VIII — Final research verdict

**Established.** Physical access and the requested output operation are independent of the statistical prediction law. Conditional on value-channel invariance, a law of address paths underlies prediction across time. For specified passive experiments we derived an actual learning algorithm, its finite-sample loss, and the reads and memory distinctions it can save. For a binary structural question the statistical price of identifying the mechanism is governed by the information in the obtained episode. Only the part revealed at scored targets measures the predictive value of knowing the answer. Compact generation, cheap evaluation, cheap identification and valuable prediction are distinct properties.

**Promising but unproved.** Use preservation of value semantics as an experimentally testable boundary between routing existing information and computing new values. Permit forgetting when its excess predictive loss over the declared horizon is acceptably small. Whether practical learning can discover useful *transformations* of previously available values, acquire their inputs and pay less overall than strong ordinary sequence models remains unresolved.

**Falsified.** Task loss alone cannot determine physical organization independently of access. A short stochastic code does not imply cheap acquisition. Opaque routing fails to express interactions that the original FP positive algebra and a table can learn. Fixed lag copying with one trained unigram base cannot match the two stronger baseline scores on the original text suffix, even with ideal retrospective training. A two-vector context gate over those same sources cannot match the Transformer.

**Computational limit.** In the displayed parity family, constant-confidence passive identification requires exponentially many full blocks for any algorithm. For an unrestricted finite address law the direct constructive learner has exponential storage, with efficient acquisition of broad useful subclasses still open. Realistic GPU cost and from-scratch language quality of a general successor are unmeasured. The old eager-graph host exclusion applies to its named realization.

**Decision.** Retain Foundation R4 as the canonical positive-program theory and comparison implementation. The address process deserves use as a solvable theory of learned routing. Its invariance deliberately forbids changing the meaning of a value, and both the R4 counterexample and the complete text bound show the price of that choice. A general successor must learn from ordinary labels which new values to compute as well as which existing ones to read. Its improvement must survive the cost of learning and executing those choices.
