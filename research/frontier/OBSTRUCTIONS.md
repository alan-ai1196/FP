# Where learning or representation fails

## An exact predictive price for copying

Let a raw prefix be drawn from a law $\mu$, followed by one of $m$ equiprobable fixed suffixes. For their clean sign functions define projective distance $d(g,h)=\min_{\sigma=\pm1}P_\mu(g\ne\sigma h)$. Suppose every pair is separated by at least $\delta$. A width-$k$ signed-copy learner, $k<m$, has at most $k$ choices of prefix bit up to sign for all these suffixes, regardless of its training or prefix encoding.

**PROVED.** Its mean sign error is at least $\delta(m-k+1)/(2m)$. Group suffixes by the prefix bit eventually read. For a group of size $t\ge2$ write $e_i$ for the individual error after its output sign is applied. The triangle inequality gives $e_i+e_j\ge\delta$ for every pair. Summing gives $\sum e_i\ge t\delta/2$. At most $k-1$ objects can be in singleton groups. The bound follows. The argument even gives the comparator a free choice of every prefix bit as a function on the tested histories.

For the six-state source in the [foundation](FOUNDATION.md), draw six prefixes uniformly that reach the distinct vertices using only rotation $q$ and global negation $m$. Independently choose $\epsilon,q,qq,vqq$ uniformly. The four readouts are $x,y,z,-(x+y+z)$. Each pair differs on at least two of the six states after either orientation, so $\delta=1/3$. The optimal three-bit sign error is **exactly $1/12$**. To attain it, retain the original three coordinates on $q,m$ and implement $v(x,y,z)=(x,y,-x)$. Only the last probe can miss and it misses two vertices. This construction is also a feasible time-homogeneous signed-copy program.

For a clean sign error $e$ and constant predicted amplitude $\beta\in[0,1]$, excess Brier risk over the true forecast equals $(\rho^2+\beta^2-2\rho\beta(1-2e))/4$. The best common calibration at this error is $\max(0,\rho(1-2e))$. The best width-three router at noise $1/4$ consequently has loss $119/576$; the learned transformation and the four-bit router attain $3/16$. The gap is $11/576$ even after optimizing the router's calibration. On this distribution the extra two input taps of the three-bit transform occur with probability $1/4$. Against the attaining router the transform buys $11/576$ expected loss for half an additional tap per word, before pricing arithmetic, parameter storage, validation or setup. An indexed six-state machine is a different, exact competitor. The comparison gives a resource decision for two explicitly priced implementations.

## Acquisition is an independent obstruction

**PROVED — rare exceptions.** Consider two laws agreeing on every word except $w_\star$, which has passive probability $p$. At that word their conditionals are $(1\pm\rho)/2$. With a fair prior over the two laws, no visit in $n$ independent episodes has probability $(1-p)^n$ and leaves the prior unchanged. Every identification rule has error at least $(1-p)^n/2$. Every predictor has expected fresh excess Brier at least $p(1-p)^n\rho^2/4$. The latter comes from forecasting the midpoint on the unobserved exceptional word. A length-$L$ exception is recognized by tracking the matched prefix and a dead state, with at most $L+2$ states; uniform length-$L$ words give $p=|A|^{-L}$. Compact realization permits exponential passive identification cost. Without a rank or horizon promise, any finite table of observations is consistent with a later exception.

**FALSIFIED — preserving the current model suffices for later training.** On each of the words $\epsilon,a$, one history observes three ones and one zero, another observes three copies of that history. Both fits have the same positive signs and amplitude $1/2$. Append three zeros at $\epsilon$ and refit. The two forecasts there become $4/11$ and $2/3$. Their old forecasts were both $3/4$. Counts or equivalent evidence matter to this update.

**FALSIFIED — a useful update must improve the current forecast immediately.** Under balanced two-bit XOR with symmetric noise, both unary splits preserve the label mean $1/2$. A second bit can still resolve it. In the six-state source two vertices with equal first coordinate can give distinct forecasts after rotation. Testing only current outputs can discard that distinction. With repeated indistinguishable copies, an equivariant deterministic update preserves the symmetry by induction. Basis tie-breaking chooses coordinates and cannot identify a unique physical hidden mechanism.

**FALSIFIED — predictive fit forces mechanism recovery.** Add a cheap observed shortcut $S$ to the input. Let clean sign $F$ be fair and let $S$ flip it independently with probability $q$. The proper one-read forecast has amplitude $\rho(1-2q)$ after orienting the shortcut. Its excess Brier against computing $F$ is $\rho^2q(1-q)$ by conditional variance. If the price of computing $F$ exceeds this saving, the shortcut has lower priced risk. When the shortcut becomes independent at deployment, the trained shortcut has excess $(\rho^2+\rho^2(1-2q)^2)/4$ against the true sign predictor. Training under the old law supplies no identification under this shift.

**FALSIFIED — a previously valuable feature should always be retained.** Under the same word distribution, reverse the clean output signs after a checkpoint. The formerly calibrated candidate now has fresh excess $\rho^2$ while a fair forecast has excess $\rho^2/4$. Refitting on all historical counts can delay recovery for arbitrarily many observations by repeating the original prefix. Retesting a candidate on a new stream consumes observations. A single favourable validation sweep gives no lifetime guarantee.

**UNRESOLVED.** A real-valued next-token predictive law typically has many conditional probability levels. Even two equally likely contexts with the same sign and amplitudes $1/4,3/4$ incur $1/64$ excess Brier under the best common amplitude. Exact sign recovery, the three-quarter boundary and fixed-length synthetic performance cannot establish ordinary-text quality or device feasibility.


**PROVED — a text improvement alone cannot identify composition.** In any depth-one successor table, an observed token $a$ sends every old predictive state to the same row $p_a$. All histories ending in $a$ have identical future laws under the frozen table. The fitted positive-price table nevertheless beats the previously certified fixed lag-mixture lower bound on the original full vocabulary. Thus this particular score comparison can be won by learning new output distributions with a complete state overwrite at every step. Its word order and gain leave general reusable composition undecided. [Actual text calculation](EVIDENCE.md#original-full-alphabet-successor-values)


**FALSIFIED — accept one profitable intermediate at a time.** A four-state, three-letter law has transition rows $(0,2,0),(0,0,2),(0,0,3),(3,1,0)$ from states 0 through 3, initial state 0 and positive output only in state 0. Fit it from ordinary noisy terminal labels. Its recovered breadth-first suffix basis has four bits and two optional columns. At letter counts $(2,3,9)$ the exact costs for neither, the first only, the second only and both are $284,291,308,252$. Every single addition raises cost from the initial layout. **PROVED — a finite orbit can still be prohibitively large.** Take $r\ge2$ reachable categorical states with two opposite output labels. Include one rotation letter reaching every state and, for each binary labeling of the $r$ states, a letter sending each state to one of the two designated output states according to that labeling. Every sign column on these states is a one-letter continuation. The rows are distinguishable, their rank is $r$ since the cube spans $\mathbb R^r$, and the exact projective orbit has $2^{r-1}$ members. The large alphabet is part of this example. Even three letters suffice: a cycle and a transposition of adjacent states generate all permutations. Conjugating one letter that maps state 1 to state 2 while fixing the others gives every elementary collapse $i\mapsto j$. For any desired state map choose one representative in each fiber, collapse all other elements of that fiber to its representative, and apply a permutation extending the bijection of representatives to the desired distinct image states. This generates the full transformation monoid and hence all sign columns. For a fixed small alphabet, expanding the entire orbit before training a layout can therefore require exponential space in the learned linear width. An $r$-state indexed machine is already an exact compact competitor for this family.

**FALSIFIED — observing two new actions together identifies either one.** Take two categorical states, start at the first, read out $1/4,3/4$, and let familiar $q$ swap the states. In one system $b,c$ are both identities. In another they are both swaps. Every word formed from the blocks $q$ and $bc$ has exactly the same forecast in both systems because $bc$ is the identity. An isolated $b$ gives $1/4$ or $3/4$. This persists with unlimited noiseless observations on that support. Extra restrictions can also improve one-occurrence identification. A measured value $(x+y)/2=1$ forces $x=y=1$ on the sign cube even though the query $(1,0)$ lies outside the linear span of the measured row.

**FALSIFIED — accurate one-step fits imply uniformly accurate compositions.** Two scalar systems on the repeater $a$ predict $1/2$ and $(1-\eta)^t/2$ at $a^t$. Their one-step difference is $\eta/2$. For $\eta=1/64$ the difference already exceeds $0.31$ at 64 steps. Arbitrarily small one-step differences allow a constant gap at horizons of order $1/\eta$. In an independently sampled two-action fit, 427 of 503 unseen finite-word predictions left the probability interval before clipping. [Sparse-action audit](audit_action.py)

**FALSIFIED — one predictive scalar always gives a bounded update in probability units.** A two-state swap with output probabilities $3/4,1$ forces $F(z)=7/4-z$ on its reachable interval. Extending that unique affine rule to zero exceeds one. More generally, if two accessible words have current probability $1/2$ and the same next input gives $1/4$ after one and $3/4$ after the other, no deterministic update of the current probability alone can fit both. The exact interval fitter detects an empty feasible set in this case. [Controls](audit_affine.py)

## The sharp window length for distinguishing token actions

**PROVED.** For every $d\ge2$, two stationary, full-support, rank-$d$ sources on just three tokens can agree on **every word through length $2d-2$** while disagreeing on whether $a$ and $b$ may be merged in a fixed refinement model. Within each source the two normalized signatures also agree whenever either flank has length at most $d-2$, regardless of the length of the other. Distinguishing them can require $d-1$ tokens on *both* sides. In particular there is no fixed universally sufficient context length over unbounded predictive rank. [Definition of a lossless merge](FOUNDATION.md#when-two-raw-tokens-can-share-one-internal-symbol)

Let $\mathbf1$ be a column of $d$ ones, $T=\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/d$ and $S=(\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top+\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,d))/(2d(d+1))$. Use initial row $\alpha=\mathbf1^\top/d$ and terminal column $\mathbf1$. Set
$$M_c=S,\qquad M_a=(T-S)/2+\delta zz^\top,\qquad
M_b=(T-S)/2-\delta zz^\top,$$
where $z_i=(-1)^i\binom{d-1}{i}$ for $i=0,\ldots,d-1$. Compare $\delta=0$ with $\delta=1/(8d\max_i z_i^2)$. Each matrix is strictly positive and they sum to $T$, so they define stationary probability laws by $P(w)=\alpha M_w\mathbf1$. The hidden chain even has independent uniform successive states when observations are marginalized.

The vectors $S^j\mathbf1$ have entries polynomial in the coordinate index of exact degree $j$ through $d-1$. Multiplication by the diagonal raises degree by one with a nonzero leading coefficient, while the all-ones matrix only adds a constant. Consequently these $d$ vectors are independent. The Hankel block with rows and columns indexed by $c^0,\ldots,c^{d-1}$ is their Gram matrix divided by $d$, so both laws have rank exactly $d$. By the order-$d-1$ finite-difference identity, $z$ annihilates every vector of degree at most $d-2$ and has a nonzero inner product with $S^{d-1}\mathbf1$.

A product of $j\le d-2$ letter matrices applied to $\mathbf1$ stays in the degree-at-most-$j$ subspace, by induction. This holds at the left as all three letter matrices are symmetric and words can be reversed. Short flanks thus kill the perturbation $zz^\top$. The single-letter probabilities of $a,b$ agree. Replacing one central $a$ by $b$ cannot change the signature when one flank is short. To compare the two sources themselves, telescope the difference of their matrix products one position at a time. Every nonzero summand inserts $\pm\delta zz^\top$. For a word of length at most $2d-2$ there is a short flank at every position, so all summands vanish. In the perturbed law $P(c^{d-1}ac^{d-1})-P(c^{d-1}bc^{d-1})=2\delta(z^\top S^{d-1}\mathbf1)^2/d>0$. In the unperturbed law $M_a=M_b$ gives an exact merge for all words. An independent derivation supplied this construction. Exact checks at ranks two through five cover 11,068 word probabilities and the first separating pair at each rank. [Executable integer check](audit_symbol_depth.py)

At fixed rank, taking $\delta\downarrow0$ keeps the perturbed partition different and makes every fixed finite dataset law converge in total variation to the unperturbed one. No finite sample size uniformly recovers the exact partition with a fixed success probability above one half over these positive laws. This is a limit on exact representation identification. Prediction error can be small when the perturbation is small.

### A hidden action can matter far more on a long stream

In the six-token experiment, the two stay IDs and two flip IDs each have probability $1/8$ in either hidden state. Under the stationary fair initial state each is followed by the same distribution of *every* future word. Its odds given *every* past word are also constant. A past and a future together can distinguish them. If $c$ is raw ID 0, the probability that both neighbors equal $c$, conditional on a particular neutral middle ID, is $5/64$ for a stay and $3/64$ for a flip. To gain this information a causal predictor retains the action while it is reading the middle token and uses it on the following token. Pair windows cannot identify this distinction in the population.

Suppose a representation merges all four neutral IDs. A length-three word pays for this merge only when an informative token occurs on both sides of a neutral, an event of probability $1/8$. Given these events the endpoints agree with probability $5/8$ for a stay and $3/8$ for a flip. Thus $D_2=0$ and $D_3/3=(\log2-h(5/8))/24=0.001315998$ nats per token, where $h$ is binary entropy.

On a continuing stream, knowing the initial bit and the raw history fixes each hidden state because every action is deterministic. Hence $H(X^T)$ lies between $T(2\log2+h(3/4)/2)$ and that value plus $\log2$. After coarse merging, an observed neutral resets the observer's hidden-state posterior to fair. The number $k$ of informative tokens since the last reset has weight $2^{-k-1}$. Given $m$ occurrences of ID 0 among these $k$, the posterior is $\pi=3^m/(3^m+3^{k-m})$ and the conditional probability of that count is $\binom{k}{m}(3^m+3^{k-m})/(2\cdot4^k)$. The next token is informative with probability one half. Within that event its entropy becomes $h(1/4+\pi/2)$. The lost predictive information rate is therefore
$$\tfrac12 E[h(1/4+\pi/2)-h(3/4)]\ \in\ [0.053464952,\ 0.053464953]\quad\text{nats per token}.$$
The exact log-interval calculation retains runs through $k=40$ and bounds the full remaining tail by $2^{-42}(\log2-h(3/4))$. This long-run number is the best achievable population gap for the specified forced merge. The empirical experiment measured three-token episodes. ## Pairs cannot identify all two-state permutation updates

Keep the six-token source from the alphabet experiment and change just raw ID 1 from a stay to a flip. The stationary distribution is fair in both sources because the neutral token probabilities are independent of hidden state. All singleton and all adjacent-pair probabilities are identical. For the altered letter both its unnormalized post-state row from the fair prior and its vector of current emission probabilities are unchanged. But the probability of the raw word $(0,1,0)$ changes from $5/512$ to $3/512$. Both observable laws have rank two, positive emissions, and an ergodic hidden chain. Three tokens can be necessary even if the stationary proportions and all six emission rates in each state are supplied. [Sufficient reconstruction](FOUNDATION.md#recovering-a-continuing-update-from-three-token-windows)

A uniform emission lower bound alone cannot give a uniform finite-sample guarantee of finding the action. Give two informative staying letters probabilities $1/4\pm\varepsilon$ in one state, reversed in the other, and two neutral letters probability $1/4$ each in both states. Make one neutral always flip and let the other either stay or flip across the two worlds. Both have fair stationary proportions, positive rank two and identical pair laws. At each fixed dataset size both observed laws converge to the same iid law as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, while the correct action remains different. Practical recovery requires a separation in observable probabilities.

## Finite prediction windows can conceal an infinite positive-state requirement

For the [three-output probability clock](FOUNDATION.md#exact-and-attainable-prediction-on-a-probability-clock), write $A$ for the nonnegative transition matrix associated with the advance token in a proposed finite HMM. Each reachable state $i$ has positive forward mass after some word $u$. Since $P(ua^j)=P(u)\rho^j$, positivity bounds each reachable row sum of $A^j$ by a constant times $\rho^j$. Its spectral radius is at most $\rho$. Following an observed reset, the probabilities $P_0(a^j b)$ have three distinct exponential modes $\rho,\rho e^{i\theta},\rho e^{-i\theta}$ with nonzero coefficients. Cayley–Hamilton and linear independence of the three geometric sequences force these eigenvalues in any realization. An irreducible diagonal block of $A$ carrying a peripheral eigenvalue has the corresponding root-of-unity phase by Perron–Frobenius. Equivalently, equality in the eigenvector triangle inequality forces phases to rotate by that angle along edges and to return to their starting value along every directed cycle. A primitive phase of order $n\ge3$ therefore needs at least $n$ states, achieved by age modulo $n$. The rational complex phase $(3+4i)/5$ has infinite order. Otherwise $2\cos\theta=6/5$ would be a rational algebraic integer. Hence no finite HMM realizes this positive stationary word law exactly. The broad rank-versus-positive-state separation is established prior work. [Jaeger's probability clock](https://publica-rest.fraunhofer.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/c5799916-b434-4186-94ab-717cb74db946/content) · [Related positive-realization separation, Benvenuti and Farina](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-6911(98)00098-X)

For any fixed $K$, censor the first post-reset run after $L=K+2$ tokens. Matching this finite distribution would match $\beta A^j\mathbf1=\rho^j$ for $0\le j\le L$, and $\beta A^j(b-c)=(1-\rho)\kappa\rho^j\cos(j\theta)$ for $0\le j<L$. Differences of the two survival sequences have a linear realization of dimension at most $K+1$; differences of the two signed-exit sequences have dimension at most $K+2$. Consecutive zeros through that dimension's number of initial terms force equality for all $j$ by Cayley–Hamilton. On states reachable through $a$ alone, survival again bounds the spectral radius, contradicting the irrational peripheral eigenvalue. Compactness therefore gives strictly positive minimum total variation $\gamma_K$ on the censored marked run. Each fixed $K$ has asymptotic per-token excess at least $2(1-\rho)\gamma_K^2$. At $K=2$ a three-by-three minor built from $\epsilon,a,aa$ and $\epsilon,b,ab$ has determinant $-64/91125$. Every entry is an event of the four-step censored run. A two-state candidate's determinant vanishes. The determinant changes by at most 18 times total variation, hence $\gamma_2\ge32/820125$ and the rate penalty is at least $2048/2017815046875$ nats per token. This numerical lower bound is loose. The variational formula gives the exact objective for stronger competitors.

Conversely the optimal saturated $K$-state counter matches *all stationary windows through length $K$ exactly*. Start it in the aggregated stationary age distribution. Up to the first mark, if $j\le K-1$ advance tokens have occurred, then the true ages falling in its tail have the geometric conditional distribution from $K-1$ onward by memorylessness. The fitted stationary tail average gives the correct mark probability in aggregate. After that first mark there are at most $K-1$ further tokens, too few to emit a mark at a differing post-reset age. Words of advances alone agree automatically. In this rational example a word consisting of $b$, $K-1$ advances and $b$ distinguishes the cap at length $K+1$. Equality would require $\tan((K-1)\theta)=-5/2$, since the relevant tail multiplier is $(9+8i)/29$. This would make its cosine $\pm2/\sqrt{29}$, whereas the complex phase raised to any integer power has rational real and imaginary parts. Unlimited short-window data can therefore favor an exact finite-window model while leaving a positive continuing prediction loss. ## A uniquely identified code can be the wrong demand

For a noisy delayed binary stream, a binary-linear summary followed by an arbitrary decoder cannot beat the coordinate-storage chord at any payload. The same obstruction holds for a deterministic partition of the last block invariant under lane-wise bit translations, since such a partition consists of cosets. Consequently a strictly better partition in a translation-invariant family with an invariant price has equally good distinct translated alternatives. Ordinary population prediction can justify creating and using one of these computations while leaving its identity underdetermined. A tie convention need not cost predictive accuracy. This conclusion allows other forms of identification, including equivalence modulo the symmetry. ## A strict local training move can be unable to create the correction

**PROVED.** On uniform clean $n$-bit groups with independent future crossover noise below one half and a fixed common forecast confidence above one half, start by storing $k\ge1$ raw bits and always reconstructing the other $m=n-k\ge2$ as zero. Treat each bit in the complete encoder and decoder truth tables as an independent parameter. Every change of at most **two** such bits leaves the population loss equal or worse. Three changes suffice for strict improvement.

With the decoder fixed, the old encoder already assigns each input a nearest prototype. With the encoder fixed, every output coordinate is a conditional median under uniform inputs. The mixed case has one flip of each type. Changing one retained coordinate of a decoder word merely duplicates another old prototype, so even reoptimizing the encoder cannot help. Changing one omitted coordinate leaves the old encoder nearest for every input: for any other retained address a possible one-bit improvement on the omitted coordinates costs at least one retained mismatch. The old assignment still has its original expected loss by the median tie. For the strict improvement, change two omitted coordinates from zero to one in the prototype at one address $s$. This leaves expectation unchanged with the encoder fixed. For one input whose address differs from $s$ by one kept bit and whose two changed omitted bits are both one, flip that encoder address bit. It saves two mistakes at the cost of one. The resulting clean gain is $2^{-n}$ per group.

The same raw initialization is flat under each individual free-matrix wire flip with nearest repair. A weight-one free column duplicates a pivot. Two flips in the *same* free column install a sum of two pivots. The quarter of syndromes containing both pivots can now reach their target in one fewer step, all other distances stay equal, and the clean gain is $1/4$ per group. The change in local trainability thus depends on executable primitives, even with the same initialization and loss. Allowing neutral moves, using continuous parameters or defining one edit to change many entries gives different optimization dynamics. At three input bits, direct enumeration of all truth tables within three bit edits verified the sharp minimum. ## Acquiring one predictive bit can encode minimum bisection

**PROVED.** Even with the exact rational law given, finding the best one-bit summary of a context for categorical next-token log loss is NP-hard at inverse-polynomial additive accuracy. The context can be uniform, every target can have strictly positive probability in every context and the integer counts can have equal row sums and polynomial magnitude. The same obstruction holds when the final target is binary and a uniformly sampled independent query becomes visible *after* that history bit has been stored. Construction and acquisition cost are different even with a one-bit deployment.

For the reduction take an unweighted simple graph with even $N\ge2$ vertices. The source provides a uniform vertex $U$, after which the learner may carry only $g(U)\in\{0,1\}$. Predict the next raw token $Y$. At vertex $u$ each incident edge token is emitted with probability $\tau=N^{-3}$ and the remaining probability goes to a private token identifying $u$. There are $K=N+|E|\le N^2$ possible targets. Episodes restart independently. Scoring each initial vertex also adds the same $\log N$ to all predictors. The baseline which retains $U$ knows the true conditional law.

For a fixed partition of cell sizes $n_0,n_1$, install its exact conditional distributions. Its excess loss over full retention is
$$H(Y\mid g)-H(Y\mid U)=H(U\mid g)-H(U\mid Y,g)
=\sum_i(n_i/N)\log n_i-\gamma\,e_{\rm in},\qquad
\gamma=2\log2/N^4.$$
Here $0\log0=0$ and $e_{\rm in}$ counts edges whose endpoints are in the same cell. A private token identifies its vertex. An edge leaves exactly two equiprobable endpoints if both are in the same cell, and otherwise the carried bit identifies the endpoint. Each edge has probability $2\tau/N$. For an unbalanced partition the first term exceeds its balanced value $\log(N/2)$ by at least $2/N^2$, since $\log2-h(p)\ge2(p-1/2)^2$. All edge credits together are smaller than $(\log2)/N^2$. Thus every minimizing partition is balanced. Among balanced ones, each additional crossing edge costs exactly $\gamma$. An unbalanced partition is also more than $\gamma$ above optimum. Minimum bisection is NP-hard. One direct reduction takes an unweighted MAX CUT graph on $n$ vertices, adds $n$ isolated vertices and complements it. Every old cut extends to a balanced cut whose crossing count in the complement is $n^2$ minus the old count. [Garey, Johnson and Stockmeyer, Section 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(76)90059-1)

Strict positivity preserves the computational gap. Mix each row with the uniform $K$-token law with weight $\delta=N^{-12}$. For every partition the optimized loss increases by between zero and $h(\delta)+\delta\log K$, by conditioning on the mixture tag and concavity. This upper bound is at most $N^{-12}(1+14\log N)<\gamma/4$. For the last inequality use $\log N\le N$, $N\ge2$ and $15/N^7<(\log2)/2$. Thus additive accuracy $\gamma/4$ for the new loss suffices to return an optimal bisection of the original graph. The smoothed rows have common integer denominator $K N^{15}$. Randomized encoding does not improve the optimum. Given explicit readouts, send each vertex to whichever readout has smaller expected cross entropy at that vertex and refit the two means. This turns an approximately optimal randomized encoder with accessible readouts into an equally good or better deterministic assignment. Computing just the loss to sufficient precision recovers the optimal cut *value* from the original formula and the smoothing bound. The probabilities and the required loss precision take $O(\log N)$ bits per scalar.

The usual weighted Shannon entropy impurity objective is this conditional entropy times the total count. A deterministic fully polynomial-time approximation scheme for two clusters on these inputs would solve minimum bisection because relative tolerance $\gamma/(8\log K)$ gives more than the required additive precision. The conclusion is conditional on P differing from NP and the outcome dimension being allowed to grow. The general entropy-impurity problem and two-cluster hardness are established research. Laber, Molinaro and Pereira give a PARTITION reduction in their supplement and explicitly ask about an FPTAS in the 2018 paper. Cicalese and Laber prove strong hardness via 3-PARTITION for a variable cluster count. Priority of the specific restrictions and gap above is under review. [2018 two-cluster proof, Theorem 4.6](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/laber18a/laber18a-supp.pdf) · [2018 paper, Section 6](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/laber18a/laber18a.pdf) · [General hardness, Theorem 11](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.05241)

**A scored binary target with an independent query.** Let $P'_u$ be the positive categorical row, draw $Q$ uniformly over its $K$ coordinates independently of $U$, and publish $B\sim{\rm Bernoulli}(\alpha P'_u(Q))$, $\alpha=N^{-6}$. Forecast the query as well if desired. Its optimal loss is always $\log K$. At the binary readout the current query is available and the past vertex cannot be reread. The one-bit restriction applies at the boundary before the query. A stored query identifier and a query-dependent readout have their own costs.

For a cell-mean categorical distribution $p$ its optimal expected binary loss is $K^{-1}\sum_q h(\alpha p_q)$. The identity $r(x)=x+(1-x)\log(1-x)=\sum_{j\ge2}x^j/[j(j-1)]\in[0,x^2]$ shows this loss equals $(\alpha/K)[H(p)+\log(1/\alpha)+1]$ up to an additive error in $[-\alpha^2/K,0]$. This holds uniformly over partitions. Since $\alpha<\gamma/4$, binary additive precision $\alpha\gamma/(4K)$ suffices to recover an optimal bisection. Averaging over additional uniformly predicted tokens scales the gap by the fixed episode length. The bit has **zero marginal information about $B$** for every encoder, since $P(B=1\mid U)=\alpha/K$. A decoder restricted to additive logits $d_q+c\,g$ can already attain its global optimum using only the query. At $c=0$ the calibrated $d_q$ give zero gradients in every direction by independence of $Q$ from $g$ and $B$ from $g$. Convexity gives a global optimum. Any improved forecast here requires an interaction between the query and the saved bit. The proof allows full calibrated readouts. ## A fitted address can lose to raw context counts

The log-ratio learning law assumes that the available forecasts are frozen while fitting the history map. Jointly acquiring arbitrary calibrated readouts and assignments restores the two-cluster hardness obstruction. Choosing among pre-existing forecasts also cannot identify a distinction on which all such forecasts agree. On real text the learned address paid its rent relative to its frequency initialization. A conventional trigram using the same calibrated output probabilities beat it at exactly the same 6,159-byte allowance.
