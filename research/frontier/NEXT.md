# Next mathematical attack

## Can an ordinary predictor learn across an interval of uninformative memory sizes?

Use length-six reset episodes of independent signs with a following observed binary outcome. Three separate sources use signed mean $W/8$, $U/8$, or zero. Here $W,U$ are the raw contrasts of the two degree-five commutators in the preceding registration. The learner receives only the signs, outcome and episode boundaries. On either signal source, every homogeneous predictor through three states has optimal gain zero. An unrestricted search can install several states together. Determine the exact population-best deterministic gains up to four states and the population price per live bit at which leaving a one-state solution is worthwhile. Separate constraints on executing a fitted forecast from the work needed to find it.

**Prospective comparison.** Use 32 independent nested training and separate validation draws at 512, 4096 and 32768 records per split per source. Search all binary-input deterministic transition tables with free calibrated Bernoulli heads, a fixed start address, and widths one to four. Match tables producing the same partition of length-six words, retain the cheapest representative, and fit counts with a Jeffreys prior. Within each width maximize integrated training likelihood, then select by validation NLL plus $1/1024$ nat per live address bit per outcome, with ties favoring smaller width. Contrast joint selection with a search that advances only if the next width immediately improves validation. Compare with the uniform head, a 64-context smoothed predictor and a fit of all 64 Walsh coefficients from ordinary outcomes, using a prespecified simultaneous threshold $\sqrt{2\log(128/0.05)/n}$ and signed forecasts clipped to $[-0.95,0.95]$. Score on the exact fresh episode law and count training, deployment and live storage. Predict that large samples let joint search install a useful four-state update on the two signal sources, leave the null at one, and let the unrestricted Walsh fit recover the generating sparse support. Quantify any finite-sample exceptions, calibration losses and stronger conventional predictions. Results here concern externally marked episode boundaries and six paid input reads. Access to a target contrast is restricted to generating and scoring the data.
