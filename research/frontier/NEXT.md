# Next mathematical attack

## Can linked ordinary tokens teach a computation that transfers when its orientation changes?

A known lag reduced the preceding width decision to counting agreements. Remove the lag rule. Let each binary task have its own fair hidden sign and share an unknown set of input positions. The signed next-token mean is the task sign times $a$ times the product of signs at those positions. The allowed positions are $1,\ldots,M$ steps back with $M$ in the set. Start each task with a fresh uniform $M$-word and supply task boundaries. Ask how many consecutive observations must retain a shared orientation to identify the reusable computation, how much of its predictive benefit survives a $b$-bit installed packet, and how well it predicts on a fresh task after observing one raw response. Test whether the two-response experiment is exactly reducible in both directions to ordinary noisy parity learning. Price the full initial word of every task.

**Prospective audit.** Use $M=8$, strengths $a=1/2,3/4$ and fair-null controls. Use 32 independent replicates, fresh shared masks, panel lengths of 1, 2, 4 and 16 scored responses, and nested totals of 128, 1,024 and 8,192 training responses. Report raw token count, workspace and arithmetic. Fit a priced likelihood over masks and hidden orientations, a generic search over all Walsh functions of the observed recent history, and a direct smoothed conditional table. The latter two see all available adjacent response pairs inside each task. Withhold the true subset and orientations from every fit and disclose the declared strength and search family. Compare ordinary log loss at response two of a fresh task by exact enumeration and project the learned mask onto budgets of 0, 2, 4 and 7 retained bits. Check the acquisition estimates and the packet frontier with rational small instances. Determine the dependence on the number of linked responses without assuming that the overlapping pairs in a panel are independent.

**Installation experiment, specified before generating the data.** Charge 0.002 nats per installed mask bit per fresh task. For each nested prefix fit the shared mask on three of every four complete tasks. Use the remaining tasks' adjacent pairs to choose a packet of zero through seven low-order mask bits. Compare direct selection of the best priced packet with starting at zero and accepting each next bit only if it improves the priced validation score. Also allow the fair forecast at zero cost. Evaluate both choices on the exact fresh-task population, count every training and validation sign once, and record the restrictions on codebook and decoder. The first experiment above still fits each unrestricted comparator on its full declared prefix.
