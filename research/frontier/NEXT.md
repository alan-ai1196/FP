## Registered test of a learned operation on unseen task descriptions

Predict a noisy parity of seven raw bits given a public seven-bit task description $t$. The training examples are ordinary triples $(t,X,Y)$ with independent uniform $X$ and flip rate $1/8$. The learner receives neither the true masks nor a composition table. Fit three worlds separately. In the first, the target mask is $At$ for a uniform invertible binary matrix $A$. The second uses the same $A$ with $T(t)=A(t+e_k t_i t_j)$ for distinct coordinates chosen at random. The third uses a uniform, unrelated permutation of all 128 masks. The learner may consider any of 21 pairwise Boolean products with arbitrary seven-bit coefficients. It is given binary input coordinates and arithmetic. Whether an interaction is needed, its two inputs, its vector coefficient and its induced operation on task IDs must be learned.

Use the 7 singleton and 21 doubleton IDs for fitting, with 256 examples each. Use 128 fresh examples on each of the seven triples whose one-based coordinate labels XOR to zero for validation. These triples cover each pair exactly once, independently of the hidden choice. Reserve the remaining 92 nonzero IDs solely for scoring, with 128 fresh examples per ID. For the matrix, ordered interaction coordinates and permutation use NumPy PCG64 seeds 23112401, 23112402 and 23112403. Sample matrix columns uniformly by rejection of singular matrices. Use seeds 23112405, 23112406 and 23112407 for the three data splits. Share the raw inputs and noise across the three worlds as paired controls.

Estimate the best positive-orientation mask on each observed ID by empirical likelihood. Use the seven singleton masks to fit a linear interpreter. At a doubleton, compare the observed best mask with the XOR of its singleton masks. Install their XOR difference as an interaction coefficient if its gain in training log likelihood exceeds $12\log2$, paying for a five-bit pair address and seven-bit coefficient. A common five-bit term count and 49 singleton-coefficient bits are additional retained storage. Ties in estimated masks choose the smaller mask. Score the fitted linear and interaction interpreters against a lookup model that assigns uniform posterior weights to the masks left after removing the distinct estimated meanings of previously observed IDs. Its forecast averages those parities at the known flip rate, including the determined answer at raw input zero. Fix the choice by lowest total validation log loss, breaking ties in order linear, interactions, lookup. For scored unknown tasks the lookup can remove meanings learned on the seven validation IDs as well. Keep the two interpreters fixed after fitting.

Report held-out NLL on every reserved task, exact recovery of its clean rule, the selected predictor, fitted interaction count, training work and retained storage. For a bijective fitted interpreter, derive its predicted composition by inverting its learned mask map on XORs of fitted meanings. Compare this law on all 16,384 ordered pairs with the generative law only in the post-fit diagnostic. A control that prefers lookup on randomly assigned meanings counts as evidence about when to withhold extrapolation. The exact decoder with the true masks is a diagnostic. A successful result concerns supervised identification within this supplied Boolean grammar. The general problem of learning an unconstrained grammar stays open.

## What the held-out task test resolved

The registered fit learned one Boolean interaction and recovered every one of the 92 unseen meanings in the nonlinear world. It chose a linear interpreter in the linear world and withheld extrapolation in the opaque world.
