# Next mathematical attack

## How much of a learned product's word prediction needs its highest-order interaction?

Freeze the previously selected learned sign masks, output head and pooled calibration for the two-, three- and four-input products, for both gross and shared-rank selection. On the observed sign cube, compare the exact product with every logit of degree at most $r-1$ in those signs along the same learned output direction. Derive the best approximation to the published probability law for the empirical distribution of training histories and the effect of unequal or missing sign corners. Establish how a learner could use ordinary residuals to decide whether to add the missing interaction to an already fitted lower-order program. Test on exact finite data, including full support, a missing corner and the small-amplitude limit.

Fit the lower-order law twice on the original 131,072 training histories at 51M. In one fit use the published probabilities as targets. In the other use ordinary raw targets and a weak shared boundedness regularizer if needed, specified in the receipt. Include saturated signed-head fits, the actual frozen product, and its pooled base. For each parent report the number and frequency of observed corners, highest-order coefficient, full-law KL and raw next-word score, with eight blocks per report. Disclose storage and score cost of the alternative. A reduction in approximation error to the teacher is a distinct measurement from better prediction of the next raw word.

Evaluate on fresh 32,768-word windows starting at 59M, 61M and 63M. Their inclusive-prefix SHA-256 values are 4d9fee9a9a2225df711f22368b3a15aa1e8afd02eaa13607fa23bc74c8ba3b72, 89c71020cf6e053066d5a6cd06a14d23323ca570f9fabe711f8a8a08c688c05a and 1e128ab99a5505e08feb0083135e144b14ab79d1868d578d99a23b480662bb47. Use the same positive lexical/trigram base and full-vocabulary grouping throughout.
