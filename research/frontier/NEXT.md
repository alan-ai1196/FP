# Next mathematical attack

Can a learner identify a reusable distinction **for the context-conditioned loss it actually pays**, with a predictive gain large enough to beat an equally informed conventional sparse model?

The original-stream result isolates the problem. The posterior partition was exactly optimal for the pooled training objective, yet its best tested use of its own past bit lost to both the frequency-bit model and a resource-matched raw-token-pair learner. With side information even two possible output types per context make exact bit learning a weighted Max-Cut problem. A greedy scan retains over 2,721 fitted information nats on the original prefix's two-successor subproblem. How much transfers to later observations remains unknown. [Prior evidence](EVIDENCE.md#learning-a-distinction-on-the-original-stream)

Test the context-conditioned objective itself. Derive a certificate for predictive gain of a training-chosen bit under sampling and deployment prices, accounting for repeated contexts, rare unseen successors and the work needed to discover the bit. Identify a minimal stream where the greedy fitted cut looks useful but every continuation-supported gain disappears. Compare a single frozen, train-only cut on later token positions against the same-budget raw trigram and the existing frequency bit. Evidence capable of changing the canonical decision would also have to survive stronger language and physical comparisons.
