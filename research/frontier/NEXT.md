# Next mathematical attack

## What is one observation worth when only a few bits survive?

Continue with the balanced raw-pair law. Measure the expected *additional* fresh second-word gain over the zero-training equality predictor after observing exactly one training pair and compressing it to one of $H=2^b$ messages. The fully learned dictionary yields order $\kappa^2 b^2/D^2$ additional gain at this cut. A single observed relation has a different distribution of posterior uncertainty. Determine the optimal order in $H,D,\kappa$, distinguish uniform-prior from worst-assignment guarantees, and charge encoding an arbitrary raw name when relevant.

Work on the unordered off-diagonal edges of the complete graph on $D$ names. The centered sign products $s_i s_j+1/(D-1)$ have zero sum at every vertex. Test whether the posterior covariance is a multiple of the projection onto this subspace, including the special cases $D=4$ and equal-word observations. Derive the strongest corresponding multi-observation bound for a finite message space. Test whether a one-bit learner can attain the upper rate early in training and after a dictionary is supported.

**Registered checks.** At $D=4,6,8,12$, form the balanced covariance two ways using exact arithmetic and verify its rank, eigenvalue by squaring, and the conditional mean for every possible first training pair. For $D=4$ and $6$, find the best average one-bit encoder of one training observation by exhaustively partitioning its unordered pair types plus one equal-word type. Compare it with remembering whether one specified pair occurred. At $D=4,6$ compute the full-information average gain after one and two pairs and test the predicted loss of one-bit compression at the available horizons. Use $\kappa=1/2$ on the same uniform balanced population. Report actual inference memory, code choice and enumeration work. The previously used natural-text windows do not enter this calculation.
