# Next mathematical attack

Short noisy pairs can identify an enduring periodic update, while every pair law can conceal a decisive three-report difference. The acquired full-history filter learns from repeated reads and known inverse actions. The parity bound quantifies exactly how much that ability to revisit a quantity can be worth. ## Discovering the compound test from ordinary observations

The parity access bound gives the target coordinates to the forecaster. What if the learner must discover which earlier reports should enter the computation? Consider a hidden affine constraint on an unknown subset of a chronological binary row. A raw sign may be noisy on both sides of the constraint. Derive the information available for selecting the subset, the conditional log gain after installation, and the sample and arithmetic costs of acquiring it. Test whether affine elimination on revisited ordinary signs can replace an exhaustive likelihood search, and identify which conclusions survive a single pass. Price the clock or supplied coordinate labels in the compiled predictor. Relate the result to the previously observed failure of a directed text correction to transfer. ## Frozen registrations

### Discovering an unknown input parity through noisy coordinates, 2026-11-25

Generate an independent uniform input vector of $d-1$ latent bits per reset. The latent final bit is an unknown affine parity of exactly $s-1$ of them. The learner knows row boundaries, coordinate order, binary symmetric report amplitude $1/2$ and the affine candidate family, and receives only counts of ordinary signs. The designated target is the final raw coordinate. Both the support and its intercept are hidden, randomized with NumPy seed [seed, d, 2711] for seeds 173, 419 and 773. Test $(d,s)=(12,6)$ with $t=8,24,128,1024$ and $(64,32)$ with $t=96$. Spend exactly $97dt$ observed signs per fit in each acquisition arm. One arm gets $97t$ independent single-pass rows. The other gets $t$ independent rows with 97 fresh nondestructive reads of every coordinate. Charge raw chronological advances and resets separately. Use independent NumPy seeds [seed, d, t, reads, 6021] for training.

Run ordinary binary Gaussian elimination on the majority signs in both arms, including an unknown intercept. Abstain to probability one half on inconsistency or insufficient rank. At dimension 12, compare against exact likelihood over all $2^{11}$ earlier subsets, either orientation, using the full observed counts and the same known noise. Install a hypothesis only when its log-likelihood gain over independent fair latent coordinates exceeds $\ln(2^{12})$. Include the calibrated true model and the fair predictor. Use 2,048 fresh rows for each test condition, with one or 17 reports of each earlier coordinate and exactly one unseen terminal report, seeds [seed, d, test_reads, 8102]. Record acquired support, abstentions, terminal conditional KL and realized loss, observed access prices, installed bits and live chronological state. The large experiment uses the elimination learner and the two prediction controls. No exhaustive large-support fit is proposed. Check the information lower bound for all one-pass learners and the fixed-precision requirements of an implementation that reads the fitted mask in chronological order.
