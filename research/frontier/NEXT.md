# Next mathematical attack

When does an ordinary prediction objective pay for a new recurrent state after charging the cost to construct, store and use it? Treat a learned state as an executable statistic of observable histories. Its update must preserve the distinctions required by its later readouts. An information bound on a one-shot representation needs an online realization and a training rule before it answers this question. The rank-three clock demonstrates how exact finite-state impossibility and attainable predictive cost can differ. [Clock evidence](EVIDENCE.md#how-much-of-a-probability-clock-does-learning-retain)

The unknown lag belongs to $\{131073,\ldots,262144\}$ and equals 258,048 in the generator. Fit it from 256 independent stationary raw windows of length 262,145 by maximizing last-token likelihood over all candidate lags, breaking ties by smaller lag. Training seed is NumPy PCG64 2026112311. Only the raw window, candidate range and hypothesis family enter fitting. Count the input volume and actual fitting comparisons. The deployment interface allows one forward token pass, finite state, and no rereading of older inputs.

Compare a constant fair predictor, a packed ring of past raw bits, and a packed ring that encodes each 63-bit microblock using the nearest word of the standard binary Hamming (63,57) code. A remainder shorter than 63 uses raw bits. Here the code family is provided; its parameters and architecture are not inferred from nothing. Test whether the predictive objective chooses to install it. Forecast from the old code before replacing its slot with the encoding of the newly observed bits. Use independent streams with seeds 2026112312, 2026112313 and 2026112314. Each begins with one observed true-lag block for warm-up, followed respectively by 8, 4 and 16 true-lag blocks for conditional error-rate fitting, model choice, and frozen reporting. When the fitted lag differs, initialize solely from its available suffix and score the same target boundaries. Fit a separate add-half-smoothed error probability for the raw and coded predictions on the rate-fitting stream.

For the priced deployment comparisons charge the fair predictor zero incremental bits, the raw ring its byte-rounded payload plus 18 bytes of controls/parameters, and the coded ring its byte-rounded payload plus 128 bytes for controls, the two input/output words, masks and parameters. Shared runtime code and unpriced machine traffic are reported separately. The objective is mean causal log loss plus $\lambda$ times charged bits divided by the fitted lag, at $\lambda=0,0.30,0.40$ nats. The mathematical question is whether all causal encoders at a given bit rate obey a sharp loss frontier, whether an online learner can attain it, and whether a deterministic nonlinear construction can outperform *all* binary-linear summaries plus arbitrary decoders after the stated price.
