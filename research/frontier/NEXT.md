# Next mathematical attack

## How many state probabilities suffice to report any finite-memory prediction rule?

For a binary horizon-$L$ forecast, consider each history's vector of current and all possible future single-token conditional means under supplied continuations. Its affine dimension $d$ lower-bounds the size of every state-probability mixture by $d+1$. Appending any $L$ fixed signs erases differences between histories. Determine whether this finite forgetting permits a strictly positive homogeneous stochastic realization at exactly $d+1$ states after uniformly weakening the signal, and identify what fails at full strength. A reported probability vector, one sampled address and a finite-state source generating future raw records have different interfaces.

**Prospective audit.** Compute exact continuation affine ranks for both six-sign laws, their 36-state discrete recurrent partition, pure delays through length six and a sample of unrestricted short tables. Construct rational positive kernels and bounded heads from the inferred affine maps alone. Verify full conditional means after every word of the given length and every one-symbol successor from the entire reachable table. Record kernel probability range, sufficient signal strength, coefficient bit sizes, reads and arithmetic. For delay test the prediction that weak exact reporting needs only $L+1$ stochastic mixture components, while the full ordinary $L$-word next-block law has rank $2^L$ at every nonzero strength. Check a finite-amplitude obstruction using the future coordinate cube, including $L=2$ and strength $3/4$. Train a short finite conditional table on a passive continuous stream with unknown output rule and check which exact recovery and rank conclusions need discrete separation assumptions. Compare with a deterministic register, the direct conditional table and the learned context-tree baseline.
