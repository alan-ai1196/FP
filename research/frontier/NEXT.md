## Can prediction error learn which individual raw symbols should erase or preserve a value?

A minimal two-state machine permits exactly four deterministic updates per raw symbol. The data must decide which words overwrite either value and which preserve or flip it. From reset and with noiseless distinct current answers, at most $2d+1$ endpoint labels on $d$ names recover the entire assignment, and an order-$d$ lower bound follows by counting. On running text this word-query permission is absent. The stationary age curve can price a learned overwrite rate but cannot by itself discover which retained bit predicts the target. Two registers with identical age curves can have completely different task gain.

The decisive comparison is to a fitted one-bit raw partition with an ordinary suffix, a direct class n-gram, and a model retaining uncertainty about the updates. Charge both the learned raw-name table and every live state. Study which predictive comparisons on a continuous stream identify a word-specific update, how sampling of rare words changes the price, and when a learned table can transfer to a separate text source. Established noisy-state and synchronizing results already handle special cases; a stronger result must earn its added structure in predictive loss or execution cost. ### Registered raw-word fit and untouched reporting blocks

Use raw uint16 word IDs, the existing 50,257-word lexical backbone and the same positive 17-class Q16 readouts. Fit on 262,144 targets starting at index 9,000,000 of the training file. Continue the trajectories on the next 32,768 words for validation. Estimate the common Dirichlet base from the earlier 1,048,576-word vocabulary prefix, with a half-count for every class, and give each readout 64 prior observations. Start the register at zero before consuming the immediate predecessor of each independent sequence. The validation segment continues the fit sequence. The established 17-class trigram uses its earlier boundary convention.

Give every observed input name its own choice among write-zero, write-one, hold and flip. Compare a learned raw-word partition with a literal one-bit or two-bit suffix, constant/17-class bigram/trigram heads, and a direct raw-word bigram with the same full-vocabulary lexical law. Fit by strictly improving one raw name at a time under the integrated conditional Dirichlet likelihood of the labels. For the search, charge 0, 1/4 or 1 times the nominal override cost in nats (16 bits per binary exception and 18 for an arbitrary map). Try both a frequent-word partition and a partition initialized by the training association with the frequent next-word set. Include a general-table start using a hold default and the most frequent names as resets. Visit observed names by decreasing frequency, alternating direction on subsequent sweeps. Stop on a stationary sweep or after eight. Include the fitted overwrite solutions in the general model menu.

Select on validation both by unpriced loss and by loss plus the serialized bytes amortized once over 65,536 words at eight bits per byte. Use the shorter of a sorted exception list and a dense packed alphabet table. Include the Q16 heads. Compare the uniform-prior Bayesian mixture over the distinct fitted general-table programs, updated with the validation likelihoods and frozen for reporting. Count all its constituent packets and weights. As an ablation, replace each feedback-dependent update of the unpriced selected table with its most frequent emitted bit on training occurrences, replay, and refit its heads. Record the frequency of each learned update and of state-sensitive histories. Report shared lexical costs separately.

Freeze the selected programs or hashes of byte-reproducible fitted packets before opening report targets. Score 65,536 consecutive next words per source in eight blocks, starting at 11,262,144 of the training file and 524,288 of the other file. Each reported forecast must be reproducible from the decoded packet and preceding inputs. Make no population or architecture-superiority claim from these two files.
