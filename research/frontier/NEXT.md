# Next mathematical attack

When can ordinary observations identify a computational state whose response to each token is to retain, reverse or erase a previously learned distinction?

Every deterministic update of one bit has exactly four possible actions. The previous text experiment forced each new stored bit to be a function of one token. Its learned membership table consumed most of the extra cost beyond a raw trigram. A reusable bit can persist across many tokens if the observations identify its transitions. Derive a criterion in terms of observable next-token distributions, an estimator and the cost of learning it. Distinguish actual synchronization from forecasting with a continuous posterior over an unknown bit.

Examine an unsegmented positive two-state sequence source. Test whether the rank and orientation of probability blocks distinguish reset from permutation, and whether those signatures suffice to validate a two-state deterministic update. Construct a rank-two stochastic counterexample if they do not. Give the same raw observations to a conventional categorical or spectral learner. Test exact population identification, noisy finite acquisition and an ordinary order-one successor baseline before drawing any broader language or runtime conclusion.
