# Next mathematical attack

Can next-token errors teach a new output distribution cheaply enough to beat a conventional calibrated context model? On four fresh blocks, intermediate history fractions improved on hard assignments. On the same 16,384 targets even hindsight selection of the better readout per target cannot bring their mixtures within 0.41 nats/token of the five-gram. Adding more precision to a history address cannot close that gap. [Four-block experiment and numerical bound](EVIDENCE.md#how-much-extra-prediction-did-fractional-addresses-buy) · [Precision theorem](FOUNDATION.md#how-much-precision-can-a-history-distinction-use)

Examine a normalized multiplicative correction to a deployable base forecast. A history statistic supplies a scalar response. Prediction errors also determine which output token IDs share a correction. Seek a finite-step loss guarantee, an acquisition method using ordinary observations of full-alphabet forecasts and a test that rules out gain for this whole family when the residual vanishes. Compare against refitting the global output probabilities and buying raw context rows. Charge the learned output grouping, per-history response, normalization work and precision. Earlier experiments learned an output split for a supplied binary input or selected a fraction within fitted readouts. The open question concerns acquiring both sides of a correction directly against the current predictive errors. [Earlier output grouping](FOUNDATION.md#learning-the-output-distinction)

The current literature review gives a separate, signed-incidence proof of the earlier two-cluster hardness boundary. Historical priority for the full Shannon restriction remains open. [Source report](../../literature/notes/REQUEST-2026-11-23-TWO-CLUSTER-ENTROPY.md)

## Prospective single-output correction learned from ordinary errors

Use the same pooled calibrated bigram, named raw-token list, fit targets and price as the earlier studies. The target $j$ of a correction can be any of the 50,257 output IDs. For each earlier raw token $x_{t-2}$, use its identity among the shared 512 named IDs or the single remaining group as history class $u$. After saving a response, the forecast sees $q=x_{t-1}$. Learning a multiplicative correction on one coordinate admits exact normalization by looking up just two base probabilities, the candidate output and the observed output.

1. On purchase positions $[1048576,1572864)$, for every occupied history class compute $r_{uj}$, the sample mean of $1_{\{Y=j\}}-p_t(j)$ within the class. Set $\bar r_j=\sum_u (n_u/n)r_{uj}$ and choose the single raw output ID with largest $\sum_u(n_u/n)(r_{uj}-\bar r_j)^2$. Break ties by smaller token ID. This selects a history-dependent discrepancy after removal of its global mean. Accumulate expected counts from the full normalized forecast. Record the leading scores and independently recompute them for the winning and runner-up IDs.
2. Multiply the base probability of $j$ by one of $\rho\in\{1/4,1/2,3/4,1,5/4,3/2,2,3,4\}$ and divide all outputs by $1+(\rho-1)p_t(j)$. Choose the best constant multiplier on the purchase labels as the default. Per history type choose the highest purchase log gain over that default, with $\beta\in\{0,1,2\}$ times the root sum of squared individual log gains subtracted. Try sparse three-byte exceptions with training rent four times the deployment rent, and a dense 513-byte response table with zero marginal rent. Encode each fitted candidate in its cheaper exact format. Charge the 2,584-byte pooled readout, an eight-byte correction header including the selected raw output, and one live response byte for nonconstant maps. Constant correction requires the header alone above the pooled model. Choose the adjusted model by priced NLL on the same 131,072-target earlier selection block $[1572864,1703936)$. Include the pooled model and fitted constant correction as candidates. Ties prefer the simpler model, then smaller $\beta$ and sparse fitting. Retain the best acquired conditional map separately if selection chooses a constant.
3. Give the standard whole-row trigram the identical total byte cap and pooled backoff. It has the original raw trigram counts, purchase data, integer knapsack and smoothing candidates 4, 16 and 64. Include the pooled model in its validation selection. Also report the earlier frozen 6,159-byte calibrated trigram and the earlier five-level address. These two larger references receive their actual storage charges.
4. Test on exactly two subsequent blocks of 131,072 targets starting at 2,359,296. No refitting and no choice from their scores. Report per-block and mean NLL and the original price charged for each equal-length deployment. Check whether a history response is retained and beats its fitted constant, pooled forecast and matched raw rows. Count how often the installed forecast on an observed target leaves the range spanned by both old readouts and the pooled base. Such a count proves a change in attainable predictions, whereas the prospective loss comparison measures its value. Measure actual acquisition and sequential or teacher-forced scoring. Give one full-distribution and one independent integer-mass replay check.
