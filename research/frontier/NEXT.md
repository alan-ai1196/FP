# Next mathematical attack

## How much of an unknown word distinction can finite memory retain?

Let $D$ be an even vocabulary size and give its raw names an unknown balanced sign assignment $s$. Each independent subject supplies two ordinary words of joint probability $D^{-2}(1+\kappa s_xs_y)$, with $0<\kappa\le1/2$. This is the pair law of a symmetric latent split at a fair start. The learner sees only the two raw names in their ordinary time order. One stored bit is sufficient to execute a supplied assignment after reading the first name. Its $D$-entry dictionary and the cost of acquiring it are separate. [Known-direction test](INTELLIGENCE_OBJECTIVE.md#the-cost-of-detecting-a-weak-distinction-when-restarting-has-a-price)

Compare three demands. First, decide whether the pairs are uniform or have some split, at fixed error. Try the equality count as a conventional detector and a uniform balanced-prior second moment as a converse. Second, allow unlimited training but only $b$ retained bits at the cut before a new subject. Determine the best possible expected gain on a fresh second word compared with the uniform predictor, including the baseline gain from equal raw names. Seek matching bounds for sublinear $b$, and distinguish installed dictionary storage from the temporary first-word identifier. Third, derive a concrete procedure for learning enough of the assignment to receive a constant fraction of its supplied gain, with its actual training sample count and computational work. The pair experiment is related to the two-community block model. Check known detection and recovery theory before making any priority claim.

**Numerical attack.** For $D=4,6,8,12$ and $\kappa=1/2$, compare the exact posterior-replica overlap formula with direct mixture second moments for short tapes where enumeration is feasible. Price the best predictive law after revealing the relative signs of $b+1$ raw names for every $0\le b<D$. For $D=4,6$, compare this code to the best two-message compression by exhaustive assignment partitions up to label symmetry where feasible. On a fixed ordinary-pair training simulation at $D=12,24$, compare uniform, a diagonal-only predictor, an explicit learned partition, and the supplied oracle. Use $\kappa=1/2$, training counts $D,4D,16D,64D$, 128 independent fits per setting with seed 20261126. Use exact expected fresh-pair NLL for each fitted assignment. Charge all scratch space and disclose whether the full partition search is exponential, approximate or polynomial. These synthetic results concern the formation of an unknown lexical dictionary. They do not settle its value on open text.
