# Next mathematical attack

Can next-token errors teach a new output distribution cheaply enough to beat a conventional calibrated context model? On four fresh blocks, intermediate history fractions improved on hard assignments. On the same 16,384 targets even hindsight selection of the better readout per target cannot bring their mixtures within 0.41 nats/token of the five-gram. Adding more precision to a history address cannot close that gap. [Four-block experiment and numerical bound](EVIDENCE.md#how-much-extra-prediction-did-fractional-addresses-buy) · [Precision theorem](FOUNDATION.md#how-much-precision-can-a-history-distinction-use)

Examine a normalized multiplicative correction to a deployable base forecast. A history statistic supplies a scalar response. Prediction errors also determine which output token IDs share a correction. Seek a finite-step loss guarantee, an acquisition method using ordinary observations of full-alphabet forecasts and a test that rules out gain for this whole family when the residual vanishes. Compare against refitting the global output probabilities and buying raw context rows. Charge the learned output grouping, per-history response, normalization work and precision. Earlier experiments learned an output split for a supplied binary input or selected a fraction within fitted readouts. The open question concerns acquiring both sides of a correction directly against the current predictive errors. [Earlier output grouping](FOUNDATION.md#learning-the-output-distinction)

The current literature review gives a separate, signed-incidence proof of the earlier two-cluster hardness boundary. Historical priority for the full Shannon restriction remains open. [Source report](../../literature/notes/REQUEST-2026-11-23-TWO-CLUSTER-ENTROPY.md)
