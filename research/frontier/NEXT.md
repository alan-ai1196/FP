# Next mathematical attack

Can a predictor learn which actions pay when an unfamiliar environment reveals only a reward for the action actually taken? On four new text blocks, a statistic trained on the source bit “ of” helped predict members of two other word groups once given 262,144 raw adaptation tokens. A conventional rank-one model learned directly from the groups was better. The next test asks whether the same frozen forecasts help identify initially unknown action meanings from one-bit feedback, what reward that produces and how much coverage costs. The mathematical question is how closely a single bounded action can track full probabilistic loss. The ordinary text baselines remain substantially better at predicting unrestricted text. [Transfer evidence](EVIDENCE.md#can-a-state-learned-from-one-word-predict-other-words)

## Prospective test of learning which actions pay

Freeze this protocol before reading any training-tape target at or after 4,390,912. Freeze all predictive priors at the preceding study's large adaptation fit.

1. Beginning at 4,390,912, read 32,768 disjoint triples $(u,q,y)$ for acquisition and then four blocks of 65,536 disjoint triples for reporting. An opportunity exists when $y$ is in one of the previous four-word groups. Its group is announced. The environment presents $u,q$, the group, a named ticket $J$ uniformly chosen from four, and its price. Each group in each unfamiliar environment has a fixed hidden permutation mapping its four ticket names to the four word identities. Draw the two permutations independently using NumPy PCG64 and eight replicate environment seeds 0 through 7. Generate offers with separate deterministic random streams, paired across methods. The learner can buy one unit or pass. Buying pays the match bit minus the price and reveals that bit. Passing pays zero and reveals nothing about the word. The environment's raw target, hidden permutation and later triples are outside the training interface. Contexts are exogenous. No real transaction takes place.
2. Use the probability floor $a=1/32$ and $b=1-3a$. Convert each four-word prior $d$ to $a+(1-4a)d$. Within a group initialize equal weights on its 24 possible permutations. For the offered name, average its predicted correctness over these possibilities. Buy if this probability reaches the price. After a purchase update each permutation's weight by the Bernoulli likelihood of the observed bit. Otherwise carry the old weights forward. Log a checkpoint after 0, 8, 64 and 512 opportunities per group. In training, with probability $\epsilon$ price the offer at $a$, otherwise sample a log-uniform price on $[a,b]$. The primary acquisition menu uses $\epsilon=1/4$. Also run $\epsilon=0,1$. Deployment uses the same log-uniform menu for all arms, with $\epsilon=0$.
3. Compare a uniform prior, the pooled current-token bigram, the fitted global conditional heads, frozen source map with transferred group heads, the frequency partition, the jointly learned rank-one prior, the two independently fitted codes and calibrated raw rows. The 24-hypothesis updating rule is identical for all. As a direct contextual competitor, use four ridge least-squares reward estimates per group, one per named arm, initially $1/4$, with unit ridge and features $(1,d_0-d_3,d_1-d_3,d_2-d_3)$ of the raw pooled group prior. Update only the purchased arm on its observed bit. Project the four estimates to the probability simplex, apply the same floor and follow the same buying rule. This competitor learns directly from partial feedback without a supplied permutation hypothesis. Compare against the behavior of each frozen word prior with the correct action meanings supplied to an informed reference.
4. On the four reporting blocks, freeze feedback updates and score every method under identical offers and hidden words. Report actual reward and buy rate, menu-integrated reward conditional on the actual word, the fraction correct if choosing the single most likely ticket, and exact mapping recovery where applicable. Use the posterior-average readout as primary and compare its 32 FP64 deployment weights across both groups with a cheaper two-byte MAP-permutation readout. Report signed paired reward differences, variation over environments and the gap to the informed counterpart. Record how many cases actually reveal a bit during acquisition. A difference in log loss only determines reward for the specific integrated ticket task. The single-choice reward and the partial-feedback acquisition must be measured separately.
5. Independently check the ticket integral, its sharp one-offer coefficient, the cost of guaranteed exploration and a minimal indistinguishable pair under purchase-only feedback. Audit that rejected offers do not update the learner, the executable report-time compilers agree with posterior forecasts, concealed outcomes cannot change training updates when observed bits agree and a degenerate prior stays uninformative. Report opportunity and byte counts together with training and execution work. A result here concerns selecting an action in a disclosed group with exogenous text contexts. Broader intervention and long-horizon transfer remain open.
