# Learning transformations from continuation predictions

The current candidate is an **executable statistic of the observable history**, learned and retained when its future predictions repay its acquisition, storage and execution costs. It admits categorical states, continuous posterior coordinates and programs that update packed registers. The same definition admits an ordinary recurrent implementation. For a noisy delayed stream we can now give the optimal asymptotic loss at every memory rate, identify a finite nonlinear computation that beats every binary-linear summary under one explicit price, and measure where the compiled implementation loses that advantage. ## A causal state and the cost of forming it

Fix an observable history $h$, input alphabet or observation space, allowed external reads, the workload of later queries $q$, and a downstream loss. A training algorithm maps ordinary observations $D$ to an installed initialization, update $U_D$ and readout $G_D$. Its reachable state satisfies $S_D(hx)=U_D(S_D(h),x)$. The readout publishes $G_D(S_D(h),q)$ before the outcome. Any side information, external tape, mutable parameter, cache or random seed which conveys information about this history belongs in the interface or state account. Price training data and work, installed program and tables, peak working memory, live state per stream and execution traffic separately. To combine them into a single objective, choose exchange rates, a deployment horizon and the number of streams sharing the installation. An update is learned only to the extent that $D$ selects or constructs it under the specified acquisition rule.

For a fixed readout target $\phi(h)$ and an old deterministic state $S$, there is an exact answer to how much state will make $\phi$ reusable after arbitrary extensions. Put histories $h,h'$ in the same cell iff $S(h)=S(h')$ and $\phi(hv)=\phi(h'v)$ for every finite extension $v$, including the empty one. Use all words, or represent illegal paths by a separate outcome. This relation is stable under appending a symbol, hence its cells have a deterministic update and read out $\phi$. Any state retaining $S$ and all the required future readouts must separate histories in different cells. If $r_s$ cells refine old state $s$, an implementation that retains $S$ can use exactly $\lceil\log_2\max_s r_s\rceil$ additional fixed-width history bits, with indices reused across old states. Replacing both registers by a joint cell costs $\lceil\log_2\sum_s r_s\rceil$ bits. This is the right-congruence minimization principle for the declared observer. It counts reachable values. Learning the cells, addressing their transition table, and physically implementing the update can be much more expensive. The same construction applies to a vector of future readouts.

For stochastic data and unrestricted calibrated readouts, a state refinement from $S$ to $(S,Z)$ saves exactly $I(Y;Z\mid S,Q)$ expected log loss on the declared query. This follows by subtracting the two conditional entropies. For several scored queries, sum their gains with their workload weights and account for observations made between them. A paid computation must be reachable from training, retain enough information to execute future updates, and save enough loss or work against an equally informed baseline. A new node, a renamed symbol and a smaller real-linear dimension alone supply none of these guarantees. The quotient criterion for token identities, the two-state posterior, and the finite approximation of the clock are instances with different observers and costs. ## A sharp memory and prediction tradeoff for a delayed stream

**PROVED.** Let $X_{t+d}=X_t\oplus E_t$ for independent Bernoulli$(\eta)$ innovations, $0<\eta<1/2$, with an iid fair initial $d$-block. The stationary law interleaves $d$ independent two-state chains. Score ordinary causal log loss on a single forward stream. There is no uncharged access to past bits. Fixed fit parameters and public randomness are independent of the test trajectory. Write $h$ for binary entropy in nats, $h_2=h/\log2$, $s=1-2\eta$, and $h_2^{-1}$ for the inverse on $[0,1/2]$. At history-dependent memory budget $b\le d$ bits, every predictor, including a stochastic update, has expected per-token loss averaged over each stationary $d$-block at least
$$F_\eta(b/d)=h\!\left(\eta+s\,h_2^{-1}(1-b/d)\right).$$
As $d$ grows with $b/d\to R$, this is the attainable limiting **online** loss. A construction can include its code tables, ring controls, buffers and finite-probability precision within $Rd+o(d)$ bits. Training scratch and per-token work have independent accounts below. Wyner and Ziv's binary entropy inequality supplies the information bound. Its binary information-bottleneck curve was already known. Here the additional requirements are a continuing update that replaces the old representation while forecasting, feasible construction, a causal acquisition rule, and a comparison with all binary-linear state summaries. [Wyner and Ziv](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1973.1055107) · [Asoodeh and Calmon, Lemma 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7712227/)

Let $U$ be the preceding uniform block and $V=U\oplus E$ the next block. At the boundary all retained information is $S$, so $H(U\mid S)\ge(d-b)\log2$. A predictor given $S$ and all subsequently observed bits still pays at least $H(V\mid S)$. To apply the binary entropy inequality conditionally, condition each output further on the corresponding clean prefix $U_{<i}$. Given $S,U_{<i}$, the earlier output noise is independent of $U_i$. Each Bernoulli input entropy $u$ then gives output entropy $f(u)=h(\eta+s h^{-1}(u))$. The function $f$ is increasing and convex. One direct check writes its derivative as $s\,\mathrm{atanh}(sr)/\mathrm{atanh}(r)$, where $r=1-2h^{-1}(u)$. As $r$ increases, the positive series for this ratio gives increasing weight to higher powers with smaller ratios $s^{2j+1}$. Thus the derivative increases with $u$. Jensen on coordinates and contexts gives $H(V\mid S)\ge d f(H(U\mid S)/d)$. The bound concerns block averages. At finite $0<b<d$ equality would require $S$ to be a deterministic uniform partition of $U$ and every supported conditional input bias to have the same nondegenerate magnitude. The latter gives positive probability to every word inside every cell, contradicting the former. Finite experiments should not be equated to the limiting optimum.

**Online attainment.** For $0<R<1$ pick $D>h_2^{-1}(1-R)$ with $D<1/2$. Split the $d$ lanes into groups of $\ell$ and at most $\ell-1$ raw leftovers, using the same boundaries each period. A Hamming ball of radius $\lfloor D\ell\rfloor$ has size $W$ with $\log_2 W/\ell\to h_2(D)$. While words remain uncovered, some center covers at least a $W/2^\ell$ fraction of them by averaging over all centers. After at most $\lceil(2^\ell/W)(1+\ell\log2)\rceil$ centers the cube is covered. Encode each observed group by its center and keep the codes in a ring. Forecast its next group from the old center, buffer arriving bits, then overwrite that slot. The expected mismatch with the noisy next block is at most $p=\eta+sD$ on average. Announcing error probability $p<1/2$ costs at most $h(p)$ by linearity of cross entropy in the actual error rate. Raw leftover lanes cost $o(d)$. Choosing $\ell=\lfloor\log_2\log_2 d\rfloor$ eventually makes both directions of lookup and the code construction affordable: tables cost $O(2^\ell\ell)$ bits, construction can use $O(\ell 2^{3\ell})$ bit operations, and buffers and ring addresses need $O(\ell+\log d)$ bits. Deployment uses one code lookup per group in each direction, besides reading and predicting its bits. Addresses, bandwidth and hardware latency still have physical cost. Quantizing $p$ to vanishing error costs $o(d)$ further bits. The payload rate approaches at most $1-h_2(D)<R$, absorbing all this overhead. Let $D$ decrease to the stated inverse. At $R=0$ predict fair; at $R=1$ the limit follows from the raw shift or the interior limit. A cold start pays for its initial observations separately.

**When formation pays.** If a unit of stored bits per delay lane costs $\lambda$ nats per output and other costs are amortized, minimize $F_\eta(R)+\lambda R$. The slope at $R=0$ is $-(\log2)s^2$, and it approaches zero at $R=1$. Strict convexity gives zero optimal rate for $\lambda\ge(\log2)s^2$ and a positive interior rate for $0<\lambda<(\log2)s^2$. This threshold is specific to the source and price.

The best binary-linear sketches have a different frontier. If $A$ is a rank-$r$ binary matrix, $I(AU;V)=r\log2-H(AE)$ because $V$ is uniform and independent of $E$. Conditioning on all nonpivot noise bits leaves an invertible transform of $r$ independent noise bits. Thus $H(AE)\ge r h(\eta)$. Even with an arbitrary decoder and the full observed prefix of the future block, such a sketch pays at least $\log2-(r/d)(\log2-h(\eta))$ per token. Storing raw coordinates attains this chord at the block interface. An affine offset has the same information. A fixed linear sketch of earlier stationary blocks can be reduced to a linear sketch of $U$ plus independent backward noise, since each fair bit chain is reversible. The restriction is binary linearity of the *stored function*, not of the output readout or the name of the algorithm.

There is a finite, physically executable separation. For each $\ell=2^m-1$ raw bits send the index of the nearest Hamming codeword, using $\ell-m$ bits. Compute the syndrome, flip its indicated bit if it is nonzero, and retain the systematic part. Radius-one balls partition the cube. Under the uniform source, the correction is uniformly distributed over zero and the $\ell$ singleton errors, independently of the codeword. Each prototype bit differs from its raw bit with probability $2^{-m}$. Its noisy prediction error is therefore $\eta+s2^{-m}$. The code is linear, but nearest-codeword storage is nonlinear. At $m=6,\eta=1/8,\lambda=0.30$, the frozen construction's population score including its declared controls is at most 0.671581220. Even a zero-overhead binary-linear competitor's best population score is at least $h(1/8)+0.30=0.676770161$. Charging additional native code and CPU time can reverse this comparison. ### Why an optimal stored computation need not be identifiable

The stationary delayed law is invariant under complementing any fixed subset of its $d$ lanes in every period. For a deterministic boundary encoder $f(U)$, require that each translation of $\mathbb F_2^d$ permute its unlabeled cells. Then $f(u)=f(v)$ iff $f(0)=f(u+v)$, by translating both arguments by $u$. Its zero cell is closed under addition and contains zero, so it is a subspace and every other cell is a coset. Decoding from an arbitrary renamed cell index consequently has exactly the binary-linear-sketch lower bound. If a candidate family and its price respect the translations, a unique optimum partition would have to respect them as well. An optimum beating the chord at its effective payload must therefore have distinct, equally good translated partitions. This falsifies a demand for a unique latent computation recovered from population prediction loss in this symmetric setting. A learner can select a representative by deterministic tie breaking, finite training data or a priced random frame. The statement concerns deterministic functions of the last block and assumes translation-invariant prices; older history can itself act as a frame. Lean checked the two algebraic identities on Boolean words of arbitrary coordinate type. [Checked coset and closure statements](CausalState.lean)

### What ordinary data identifies

With unknown lag in $(M/2,M]$, independent stationary length-$M+1$ windows expose ordinary raw tokens. In the registered last-token fit each wrong antecedent is fair and independent of the true antecedent and label noise. Against any one wrong lag the per-window match advantage is $+1$ with probability $(1-\eta)/2$, $-1$ with probability $\eta/2$, and zero otherwise. A Chernoff comparison gives failure probability at most $(\lceil M/2\rceil-1)(1/2+\sqrt{\eta(1-\eta)})^n$ for $n$ windows. At the registered values this is below $3.15\cdot10^{-16}$. This procedure inspects $O(nM)$ raw inputs and candidate matches. The source grammar and compression algorithm are supplied. Ordinary data identifies the lag, calibrates the decoder and selects a deployment under the price.

A stronger likelihood comparison uses the **entire** window. Put $L=M+1$, $t=[(\sqrt{1+s}+\sqrt{1-s})/2]^n$. At lag $k$, the $L-k$ related pairs form a matching. For distinct candidate lags the union of their matchings is a forest. All edges run from the left half to the right; an alternating two-edge walk translates by the nonzero difference of lags so cannot close. Under a uniform reference word, edge XORs on a forest are independent fair bits. The likelihood ratio for a matching is the product of factors $1+s\sigma_i\sigma_{i+k}$ in sign notation. Hence the $n$-window Bhattacharyya affinity of two candidates is $t^{(L-k)+(L-j)}$. A likelihood union bound gives worst-case error at most $t^3/(1-t)$, independent of the size of the lag range. The two hardest adjacent large lags have affinity $t^3$, giving equal-prior error at least $t^6/4$ by the standard total-variation/affinity bound. These bounds assume known noise and at least two candidates. Reading $nL$ symbols still costs $nL$ symbol accesses on a full-window replay. A naive computation of all autocorrelations costs $O(nM^2)$, or $O(nM\log M)$ arithmetic via convolution with adequate precision. ## What would count as creating a value?

Fix the score-time interface, training law, initialization, observation horizon, permissible downstream decoders and resource budget. Let a register be its function of **accessible histories**, identified on the declared support (or on every legal history for an exact claim). For stochastic registers include their joint kernel with the other observable registers. New deterministic registers carry no information beyond their inputs in the Shannon sense.

* Selection returns an available register. Conditional routing chooses an index or weights using information available before the read. The price includes computing the weights and reading the inputs. A selector allowed to compute the answer in its keys changes the comparison class.
* A transformation computes a function of the old registers and current input. To establish expressive creation relative to an interface, exhibit a function outside its specified selection/decoding class. For predictive creation, exhibit positive excess risk for the old feasible class and a reachable learner with strictly lower risk. For computational creation, exhibit a strict resource improvement at equal observable forecasts or a loss/resource Pareto improvement. State whether the comparison is to one realization or an entire class.
* An intermediate is reusable if its one materialization serves multiple continuations, consumers or time steps under a stated execution schedule. Count reads, lifetime and formation. Equality of functions identifies a duplicate unless the changed physical realization saves a measured or proved resource.
* Learning requires an actual update from a declared seed on ordinary observations. Specify the event on which the representation is reached, its probability and its cost. A subsequent update can change both its value and its future use. A different graph spelling or a good unreachable setting establishes neither claim.

In this investigation “signed copy” allows a symbol-indexed read of one old bit and optional negation for each new bit. The output reads one bit with optional negation. The symbol has no access to the contents of the registers when choosing the address. This gives a stronger comparison than unmodified copying. Allowing an arbitrary Boolean function inside a key would change the interface.

## A native linear update learned from scalar labels

Let $A$ be a finite input alphabet. Each episode resets, reveals a word $w$, obtains a probability forecast, and finally reveals $Y\in\{0,1\}$. Suppose

$$P(Y=1\mid w)=(1+\rho f(w))/2,\qquad f:A^*\to\{-1,1\},\quad 0<\rho\le1.$$

The hidden function, noise level and transitions are unknown. Conditional labels in the sampling theorem are independent. Use Brier loss $(Y-p)^2$. For $Z=2Y-1$ the squared loss is $(Z-\rho f)^2/4$. At fixed positive amplitude each word's empirical minimizer is the sign of its accumulated $Z$. With those signs fixed, the minimizer in $[0,1]$ is $\hat\rho=\sum_w|\sum Z_w|/\sum_w n_w$. This homogeneous two-level assumption makes exact recovery from noisy labels possible. Continuous conditional probabilities need a different stability argument.

Write $g_s(u)=f(us)$ for the forecast sign after a continuation $s$, and $H_{u,s}=g_s(u)$. Let $r$ be the real rank of this Hankel matrix. The classical finite-rank realization theorem relates this rank to minimal real linear state dimension. Spectral learning already recovers transition operators from empirical Hankel blocks under specified coverage and conditioning. The deductions here concern the signed observer, a bounded integer execution, the exact competing copy width, and its passive acquisition cost. [Carlyle and Paz](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-0000(71)80005-3) · [Balle and Mohri](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/hash/700fdb2ba62d4554dc268c65add4b16e-Abstract.html)

**PROVED — constructive recovery.** Declare a capacity ceiling $R\ge1$, chosen before the data. Set $U=A^{\le R-1}$ and $W=A^{\le 2R-1}$, including the empty word. At a predeclared checkpoint, use only previously revealed observations. Maintain two integer counts per observed word in $W$. Wait on an absent cell or tied sign. Form the short Hankel block on $U\times U$. Row elimination in length then alphabet order supplies independent rows $P$ and pivot columns $S$, giving a nonsingular $B=H_{P,S}$. Refuse a rank above $R$. The first pivot column is the empty suffix since its entries are signs. Define

$$x(u)=H_{u,S},\qquad B_a=H_{Pa,S},\qquad T_a=B^{-1}B_a.$$

For each letter compile each column of $T_a$ into coprime integer coefficients with a positive common scale. At inference initialize at $x(\epsilon)$, accumulate signed integer coefficients from the previous bits, and take their signs simultaneously. The readout is coordinate zero. Use the empirical amplitude for the final probability. A one-term column is one signed read. For an ambiguous or inconsistent empirical table the learner can keep its incumbent. An explicit short-word replay checks a constructed candidate before using it.

To prove recovery, consider the span of Hankel rows of length at most $j$. If it ceases growing, appending any letter maps each spanning row back into this span: on a row as a function of $s$, the corresponding linear operation sends $h(s)$ to $h(as)$. It is then closed at every later depth. Starting with a nonzero empty row, the whole row space is spanned by depth $r-1$. The same argument on columns prepends letters. The finite block therefore has rank $r$ when $r\le R$. Each full row expressed in basis rows $P$ has coefficients $x(u)B^{-1}$. Applying the shift to those rows proves $x(ua)=x(u)T_a$. Induction proves correct signs for **every word length**, even when the training words were short. Conversely any $k$-dimensional homogeneous linear realization factors $H$ into prefix and suffix matrices of inner dimension $k$, hence $k\ge r$. The guarantee is conditional on the true rank bound and correct observed signs.

Every coordinate is a prediction about a possible continuation. Its meaning is learned from outcomes even when that continuation is never executed at inference. Cramer's rule bounds every numerator and denominator before cancellation by $D=\lfloor r^{r/2}\rfloor$ by Hadamard's inequality on sign matrices. With column-wise positive denominators, a reachable dot product is exactly the denominator times the next sign. Integer threshold execution is exact with accumulator magnitude at most $rD$; the state stays at $r$ bits at every step. Multiple coordinates can read the same old bit. A two-buffer implementation needs $2r$ state bits during a transition. No growing rational hidden state is required for this observer.

**Statistical cost.** If each word has at least $k$ visits at a checkpoint determined independently of the labels, Hoeffding's one-sided bound and a union bound give probability at most $|W|\exp(-k\rho^2/2)$ of any wrong or tied sign. For $n$ iid word draws with probabilities $d_w$, add $\sum_{w\in W}P(\mathrm{Bin}(n,d_w)<k)$ for coverage. With the correct signs, fresh excess Brier loss is $(\hat\rho-\rho)^2/4$. On that event $\hat\rho$ is the ordinary sample mean of $Zf(w)$ over retained examples. A two-sided error bound at threshold $t$ adds $2\exp(-nt^2/2)$ when all $n$ examples are retained. A positive lower bound on $\rho$ is only needed to set a prospective sample budget.

## How much computation do the learned bits require?

Let $N$ be the number of distinct full Hankel rows and let $C$ be the number of distinct columns up to a global sign. For finite signed rank, the basis observations distinguish all rows. Evaluating columns on $r$ independent rows distinguishes all columns. Hence $N\le2^r$ and $r\le C\le2^{r-1}$.

**PROVED — exact signed-copy width.** A signed-copy machine of width $m$ can read only one of its $m$ current bits, up to sign, after any fixed suffix. Consequently $C\le m$. Conversely store one $\pm1$ value per projective column class $[g_s]$. After letter $a$ its new value is $g_s(ua)=g_{as}(u)$, represented by one old bit and a fixed sign. Initialize each bit at its empty-prefix value. This constructs a width-$C$ machine. Equality concerns parallel read-and-sign instructions and a single-bit readout. A deterministic indexed transition table may use a different encoding.

**PROVED — sharp density boundary.** If $N>3\cdot2^r/4$, every column of every $T_a$ in any basis of suffix bits is a signed coordinate. Indeed, any real linear form that is binary on this many vertices cannot have two nonzero coefficients $b_i,b_j$. Condition on the other $r-2$ cube bits. For $L=t+b_i x_i+b_j x_j$, the alternating sum of $L^2$ over the four corners is $8b_i b_j$. If every corner had $L^2=1$ the sum would vanish. At most three corners work for each conditioning. A one-term form taking sign values must have coefficient $\pm1$. The same argument applies to every suffix readout. Therefore $C=r$ in this regime. The proof concerns the count of distinct reachable sign states, independently of their sampling frequencies.

At equality take the six cube vertices excluding $(1,1,1)$ and $(-1,-1,-1)$. Start at $(1,1,-1)$ and output the first coordinate. Three letters act by cyclic rotation, global negation and $(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,y,-x-y-z)$. With $x=y$ the last operation fixes the third coordinate, and with $x=-y$ it flips it. Rotation and negation reach all six vertices. Suffix readouts include $x,y,z$ and $x+y+z$ up to sign. The latter is exchanged with $z$ up to sign by the third letter, and the four lines are closed under all three operations. They are distinct on the six vertices, which span three dimensions (the three rotations of the initial vector have nonzero determinant). Hence $N=6,r=3,C=4$. A width-three exact signed-copy program with a signed-copy readout is impossible **in any encoding**. A learned three-bit linear update can execute it. Width four trades the addition for a cached extra value. The input fixes the letter, while the third transformation changes meaning according to distinctions already formed by the learner.

This derives a limited attention-like regime: under an explicit state-density condition the learned value update is hard addressing with optional polarity. It gives no dot-product, softmax or general optimality theorem for attention. Fields of continuous-valued coordinates permit other linear maps. The classical Boolean Fourier rigidity theorem concerns approximation under a specified cube measure; the four-corner argument above is an exact support statement. [Friedgut, Kalai and Naor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03246)

## Learning and execution have different states

For a frozen predictor with $\rho>0$, two prefixes have equal future forecasts for every suffix exactly when their full rows coincide. This is the minimal deterministic predictive state: equal states must agree on every continuation, and equal rows have equal one-letter successor rows. The $r$-bit coordinates give an injective encoding of those $N$ states. Equality of the first coordinate only identifies the current forecast.

For ongoing training, the word counts, fixed configuration, published model, and the current episode/phase suffice for deterministic replay of future forecasts at the specified checkpoints. Within a validation block also keep its fixed candidate and running loss/work totals. This follows by factoring each update through precisely those variables. Different histories can give the same published model while their counts make the next refit diverge. A physical observer may additionally see allocation, elapsed work or even every training record. For an installation claim one would also need its declared lineage and persistence evidence. Foundation R4 already defines equivalence relative to the observer. The reduction here comes from specifying an actual smaller update law. [R4, sections 5–7](https://github.com/alan-ai1196/FP/blob/a35ef18b0576048ea93f0b87ea6f6a78760bb447/FP_THEORY.md)


## Pricing the change

A checkpoint uses previously observed counts to fit a challenger, with a hard capacity $R$. Freeze it on a subsequent validation block. For $n_v$ events let $G$ be the sum of the fair predictor's squared losses minus the candidate's, and let $J$ count stored-bit taps on those words. For a declared deployment horizon $h$, price $\pi$ per tap and fixed remaining charge $c$, publish the candidate iff $h(G-\pi J)>n_v c$. Otherwise use the fair forecast. This is an empirical decision on a specified resource axis. A realized fit always costs training work even when declined. Costs common to the two deployments cancel in the comparison; the remaining unequal costs can be included in $c$ or charged separately. Repeat the count, fit and validation updates at declared checkpoints for continued learning. The fair initialization, exact update and tie convention require no latent-state labels, target graph or supplied sign products.

For independent validation words and labels with deterministic per-word tap bound $J_{\max}$, each per-example net gain lies in $[-3/4-\pi J_{\max},1/4]$. Conditional on a frozen candidate, the probability of an empirical net gain exceeding its expectation by $t>0$ is at most $\exp[-2n_vt^2/(1+\pi J_{\max})^2]$. This fixed-block statement cannot certify a sequence of adaptive reuses of one validation set.

## Acquisition without visiting every short word

**PROVED — rank one.** In a nonzero signed rank-one realization the empty readout can serve as the sole state bit. Each symbol acts by multiplication by an unknown sign. Write the sign bits as $\theta\in\mathbb F_2^{|A|+1}$ including the unknown initial sign. Any raw word gives a public design vector $v(w)=(1,(\#_a(w)\bmod2)_{a\in A})$. Its terminal output sign is $(-1)^{v(w)\cdot\theta}$. These features follow from the rank-one restriction. Supplying them to an unrestricted learner would be a substantive inductive bias.

Start with no equations. After labels arrive, group input words by their observed mask; noiseless signs give linear equations directly. Under independent common flip noise, a fixed minimum number of visits per retained mask and its strict majority supply an estimated equation. Insert by binary elimination, skipping ties and reporting inconsistent rows. Maintain a calibration count over the retained cells. For exact recovered signs, a test sign is determined **iff** $v(w)$ belongs to the observed row span. In the span, elimination gives the sign; outside it, there is a null vector $h$ with $v(w)\cdot h=1$, so the two parameters $\theta,\theta+h$ give identical evidence and opposite predictions. With a uniform prior and noiseless equations this pairing gives the exact fair forecast off the span. At known $\rho$ the exact excess Brier against a clairvoyant sign predictor is $\rho^2 P(v(w)\notin\mathrm{span})/4$. Majority reduction with noisy data is a frequentist recovery procedure; the uniform posterior formula applies to the noiseless observation experiment.

A spanning set of at most $|A|+1$ reliable masks suffices for all words. For $b$ prespecified distinct masks with $k$ independent labels each, the preceding bound $b\exp(-k\rho^2/2)$ applies. Counting one word toggles one bit per symbol. Elimination stores at most $(|A|+1)(|A|+2)$ bits of rows including right sides, apart from indexing. Each inserted or queried row takes at most $|A|+1$ row XORs of that width. Training also pays for two integer counts and a mask for each retained input class. A fully identified deployment stores $|A|+1$ sign bits and calibration. A partially identified deployment needs the span/mask calculation to know when to abstain. General noisy parity with sparsely repeated, high-dimensional designs poses a different estimation problem. [Blum, Kalai and Wasserman](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/noise-tolerant-learning-parity-problem-statistical-query-model/)

In particular, every two-symbol word supplies an even design. Using all ordered pairs on $b\ge2$ symbols gives a space of dimension $b$: a repeated symbol supplies the intercept and distinct pairs generate the even-parity subspace. A single observed odd design raises this to $b+1$. Even an unlimited sample from the even-only law cannot fix the action on odd words. This is a limit on extrapolation with a fully usable trained computation on the observed support.

## A genuinely different optimizer

For an independently sampled **pair** of categorical raw inputs, fit a signed scalar outcome by a dot product of learned lookup rows. Neither lookup contains a supplied interaction. Assume equal observed counts in every cell of an $m\times n$ Cartesian design. Let $M$ be the matrix of empirical signed means. Start with $U=aI_m$, $a>0$, and $V=0_{n\times m}$. After each complete balanced training block, an ordinary squared-error objective with a quadratic parameter price is

$$J=\tfrac12\|M-UV^\top\|_F^2+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(\|U\|_F^2+\|V\|_F^2),\qquad\lambda>0.$$

The empirical squared loss on individual outcomes differs only by scaling and a constant in this design. Use a specified number of alternating ridge updates. First solve $V=M^\top U(U^\top U+\lambda I)^{-1}$, then $U=MV(V^\top V+\lambda I)^{-1}$ using ordinary row elimination. Scalar regression scores may be clipped to $[-1,1]$ when published as probabilities. The update minimizes the displayed unprojected regression objective. One-sided identity initialization is independent of the labels. Both-zero initialization is exactly stationary.

**PROVED — reachable limiting shrinkage for this initialization.** In the singular bases of $M$, rotate the arbitrary hidden coordinates by its left singular vectors. The isotropic seed and both updates then separate into modes. In a mode of singular value $s$ the updates are $v=su/(u^2+\lambda)$ and $u'=sv/(v^2+\lambda)$. Zero singular directions contribute zero. All other modes remain positive. Each block is $\lambda$-strongly convex, so each sweep decreases the objective by at least $\lambda/2$ times the sum of the two squared increments. Coercivity bounds the iterates and the increments tend to zero. Every cluster point is stationary. Subtracting the stationary equations after multiplying respectively by $u,v$ gives $u^2=v^2$. The only nonnegative stationary points are zero and, for $s>\lambda$, $u=v=\sqrt{s-\lambda}$. A bounded sequence with increments tending to zero and only finitely many cluster points converges: traveling repeatedly between disjoint small neighborhoods would create another cluster point outside them. At $s>\lambda$ the derivative of the full scalar sweep at zero is $(s/\lambda)^2>1$, so a positive orbit cannot converge to zero. The fitted singular value therefore tends to $(s-\lambda)_+$. Equal singular values leave basis orientation unidentified. The proof concerns this alternating update; deep linear gradient-flow results use other updates and initialization hypotheses. [Saxe, McClelland and Ganguli](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6120)

The factor learner can fit continuously varying conditional values. Its pair architecture imposes a fixed cut, and finite nonzero small modes usually still incur computation. Norm shrinkage is a price on parameters, not a measurement of bytes or execution time. It earns a physical width reduction only after paying to discover and execute a smaller factorization. The signed sequence learner has exact finite gates but pays for evidence about continuations. Neither implication extends to general sequences by taking a limit.


## Ordinary next tokens on one stream

The terminal-word experiment can be removed. Declare an alphabet of size $K$, a finite context ceiling $L\ge1$ and the initial raw context. At time $t$, forecast $X_t$ using only $c=X_{t-L:t-1}$ and previously revealed symbols. Then update the count of $(c,X_t)$ and shift the raw context. At a training checkpoint a finite table gives a causal forecast for each context. The following construction learns a smaller recurrent realization from that table. A full conventional context table remains available on identical training information.

**PROVED — continuous conditional values on a finite physical grid.** Let $Q\ge K$ be an integer mass budget. In each context fit $k_y\ge1$, $\sum_y k_y=Q$. The empirical multiclass Brier objective is minimized by the Euclidean-nearest such probability vector to the empirical frequencies. Start each $k_y$ at one. At each of the remaining $Q-K$ increments select the smallest $(2k_y+1)n-2Qn_y$, breaking ties by symbol order. These are the marginal increases of $n\sum k_y^2-2Q\sum k_y n_y$; within each coordinate they increase. Selecting the smallest available marginals gives the global optimum by exchange. For an unseen cell any allocation minimizes the empty objective; use the closest feasible vector to uniform. A binary cell has the equivalent nearest-integer formula with endpoint clamping.

Let $v_y(c)=k_y(c)$ for the fitted table and let $D_a v(c)=v(\operatorname{shift}(c,a))$. Start with the head columns and repeatedly apply each $D_a$ to each newly independent column. Retain it if ordinary elimination yields a nonzero residual. Once the queue is empty its span is invariant under every shift, so by induction it contains every head after every continuation. Each retained coordinate is itself a fitted prediction at an actual continuation. With $r$ independent columns choose $r$ independent context rows; solve on that square submatrix for every shifted column and every output head. These coefficients reproduce the columns on all contexts. Conversely every homogeneous linear state producing these heads after all continuations must span them. Thus $r$ is the minimum homogeneous linear width for this **fitted** observable, and the learner attains it after finite, paid work. Different coordinate scalings, redundant label coordinates and allowing affine bias change the width convention. A constant can be included as a coordinate.

Each integer minor used in Cramer's rule is bounded by $H=r^{r/2}Q^r$ in magnitude. A transition uses integer coefficients, sums at most $r$ products of magnitude at most $QH$, and divides by its positive denominator. The division is exact on reachable states. The result always lies in $[1,Q]$. The same holds for the heads before the final normalization by $Q$. This gives bounded execution over arbitrarily long continuations without accumulating rounding error. Parameters, output writes, acquisition and the $K^L$ possible raw contexts are separate costs. An arbitrary fitted table can make acquisition impractical.

For stationary order-at-most-$L$ conditionals, suppose every symbol has conditional probability at least $\epsilon>0$. Collect the first $k$ next tokens at each context. One can generate the stream with independent stacks of draws for the contexts, consuming an entry at each visit. The first $k$ entries of each stack have the specified categorical law. This argument avoids conditioning a binomial estimate on its trajectory-dependent final visit count. For $m$ disjoint length-$L$ blocks, each specified context occurs at the end of a block with conditional probability at least $\epsilon^L$ given preceding blocks. Coupling the block indicator by a uniform draw to a Bernoulli$(\epsilon^L)$ trial gives the lower binomial domination, allowing dependence in subsequent blocks. By time $mL+1$ the probability that any context still lacks $k$ labels is at most $K^L P(\mathrm{Bin}(m,\epsilon^L)<k)$.

With probability at least $1-2K^{L+1}\exp(-2kt^2)$ all completed empirical conditional coordinates differ from their true values by at most $t$ (or add the preceding coverage failure at a fixed time). For any simplex point $p$, round $1+(Q-K)p_y$ to integers by floor and largest remainders. Its distance after rescaling from $p$ is at most $e_Q=(\sqrt{K(K-1)}+\sqrt K)/Q$: the shift vector is $(\mathbf1-Kp)/Q$, maximized in norm at a vertex, and rounding changes each mass by less than one. The fitted grid optimum is at least as close to its empirical table. Hence its conditional excess multiclass Brier is at most $(e_Q+\sqrt K\,t)^2$ uniformly. The normalized log-loss excess is at most $Q$ times this quantity by $\log x\le x-1$ and $q_y\ge1/Q$. Neither error bound assumes recovery of a particular basis or equality of empirical and true ranks.

For the earlier binary homogeneous observer, fit each context's strict majority and a pooled amplitude. Use the same closure with sign columns and the signed integer executor above. The failure probability for the true signs after $k$ visits per context is at most $2^L\exp(-k\rho^2/2)$. Because every next symbol has positive probability, equality of future single-token forecasts after every possible suffix is equivalent to equality of the full future word laws. Multiply successive conditionals in one direction; in the other, divide positive prefix probabilities. In this finite-context realization the learned maps update after each **actual scored token**. Predictive-state and observable-operator models also use future predictions as state. Their general stochastic realizations and spectral HMM learning theorems have different hypotheses. Here the input shift is observed, the output grid is paid, and elimination preserves an empirically fitted conditional table exactly. [Littman, Sutton and Singh](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2001/file/1e4d36177d71bbb3558e43af9577d70e-Paper.pdf) · [Jaeger](https://www.ai.rug.nl/minds/oomresearch/) · [Hsu, Kakade and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.4413)


## When does the learner materialize an extra value?

A prediction basis requires three stored bits in the six-state example. A fourth could cache a sum that would otherwise be recomputed. Its maintenance creates new dependencies on later steps. We can derive the choice from the same learned future predictions and an observed workload.

Fix the fitted sign law and a mandatory independent suffix basis $B$. Compute its finite orbit of suffix columns modulo sign, $\mathcal C$, by starting from the basis and prepending every letter to each new column. Signs of a column on independent rows identify its orientation. A direct implementation may enumerate the $N$ reachable state rows and compare their columns there. Each letter $a$ gives a learned demand $i=d(a,j)$ for each next register $j$, together with its polarity. An absent $i$ can always be synthesized from $B$ using its exact learned integer form. These columns and dependencies are calculated from the fitted predictions.

Declare a machine with simultaneous updates and a transient scratch value shared by all consumers of the same missing column on one letter. Let $z_j$ indicate a resident register, with $z_j=1$ on $B$. Let $u_j\ge0$ price retaining, initializing and transferring that register for the chosen horizon. Let $s_i\ge0$ price a single transient synthesis, and $n_a$ be recorded or expected letter counts for that horizon. All resident designs give exactly the same forecasts. Their variable physical cost is

$$E(z)=\sum_j u_jz_j+\sum_{a,i} n_a s_i(1-z_i)\mathbf1\{\exists j:\ z_j=1,\ d(a,j)=i\}.$$

**PROVED — joint selection, sharing and removal.** An ordinary minimum cut gives the global minimum of this cost, including updating the newly materialized values. Make one node for each column and a directed arc of capacity $u_j$ to the sink. Force the basis to the source side. For each letter and demanded input make an auxiliary node $h_{ai}$. Add a forcing arc $j\to h_{ai}$ for each consumer and a finite arc $h_{ai}\to i$ of capacity $n_as_i$. A forcing capacity of one plus the total finite capacities suffices for integer prices. For any choice of source-side columns, place an auxiliary on the source side iff a selected consumer needs it. The resulting cut costs exactly $E$. In the other direction any minimum cut respects the forcing arcs, so its cost is at least $E$ for its selected columns. This proves equality and handles simultaneous users of the same scratch computation.

The implementation augments residual flow along shortest paths by breadth-first search until the sink is unreachable. Each augmentation preserves flow feasibility and conservation. At termination residual reachability gives a cut whose forward arcs are saturated and whose backward arcs carry no flow, so its value equals the feasible flow. Every cut bounds every feasible flow, proving optimality. The reachable side is contained in every minimum source side and breaks ties by omitting optional registers. There are $O(dC)$ vertices and arcs for $C=|\mathcal C|$ and $d=|A|$, and a shortest-augmenting-path implementation has $O(VE^2)$ arithmetic work. The orbit can itself be exponentially large in $r$. This specializes established graph-cut methods to dependencies created by a prediction fit. [Kolmogorov and Zabih](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~rdz/Papers/KZ-PAMI04.pdf) · [Edmonds and Karp](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~pettie/matching/Edmonds-Karp-network-flow.pdf)

Use three units per destination bit for an addressed bit read, polarity operation and write. In this abstract machine a three-input signed form costs seven extra units for three coefficient-weighted bit reads, two additions, sign testing and one scratch write. On the six-state law only letter $v$ asks the three-bit basis for its missing fourth column. Let $H=\sum n_a$. The two costs at zero rental are $9H+7n_v$ and $12H$. The learned design adds the fourth bit iff $7n_v>3H$. A subsequent workload can reverse this decision. A positive fixed charge for initially creating the bit raises the threshold by that charge. Both designs have the same inferential loss and the same acquired mathematical law. Each predicted bit has a definition, and ordinary observations determine both its meaning and, via the empirical workload, whether to retain its result.

A fixed mandatory basis, synthesis from that basis, one-step scratch lifetime, simultaneous update and additive resource prices are essential hypotheses. A hard bound on peak bytes, alternative arithmetic factorizations or incidental sharing between different synthesized expressions define a different optimization problem.


**PROVED — basis dependence can reverse materialization.** For a finite orbit, enumerate independent $r$-subsets containing the published head, run the preceding cut for each and minimize over the returned costs. This is an explicit finite optimizer over such bases, with up to $\binom{C-1}{r-1}$ cuts. On the six-state law the four lines $x,y,z,s=x+y+z$ have every triple independent. The letter $q$ cycles $x,y,z$ and fixes $s$. The letter $v$ exchanges $z,s$ up to sign and fixes $x,y$. The letter $m$ fixes every line up to sign. Omitting $s$, $y$ or $z$ therefore requires one three-input synthesis respectively on $v$, $q$, or both. Once the head $x$ is held resident, these exhaust the three-register choices. Any synthesis of the missing line in that basis requires all three coefficients by independence of every triple.

The minimum cost over both bases and caches is thus $\min(12H,9H+7\min(n_q,n_v))$ at zero rental. For $n=(2,1,4)$ the fixed basis would add a fourth register while the best basis keeps three. At $(48,4,48)$ even the best three-register basis loses and the fourth value is materialized. At $(48,48,4)$ the three-register design wins again. These are identifiable changes in computational organization within the specified machine interface. A lookup-table state machine can still implement all the same forecasts.


**PROVED — a certificate before full discovery.** The cost of the complete-orbit optimizer can be avoided on some workloads. Start with just the mandatory basis columns $B$ and their fitted short moment blocks. If the signature of a prospective column on the independent rows is $b_i\in\{\pm1\}^r$, its expansion is $B_{\rm mat}^{-1}b_i$ where $B_{\rm mat}$ is the square observation matrix. Prepending $a$ has signature $B_aB_{\rm mat}^{-1}b_i$. Normalize its first sign to identify it up to polarity. This computes a successor on demand using $r$ observed probe rows, without traversing all reachable states. It assumes valid signed moments for the fitted law.

Expand the outgoing demands of a set $K$ of known columns containing the basis. Its immediate boundary $D$ consists of revealed successor columns outside $K$. The **upper** cut permits only $K$ to be resident. It charges each demanded missing input once and synthesizes it from the known basis, including inputs on $D$. It produces an executable feasible program for every letter. The **lower** cut permits $K\cup D$ to be resident and charges their full per-register fees, while dropping dependencies *issued by* $D$ and by unrevealed columns. For any full solution, restricting its selection to $K\cup D$ preserves all known requests and can only remove nonnegative costs. Thus the lower optimum is at most the global optimum. Expanding $K$ cannot decrease the lower optimum by the same restriction argument; the previous upper solution stays feasible so the upper optimum cannot increase.

If the bounds differ, the minimizing lower layout must contain a boundary column. Otherwise it already pays the exact feasible cost. Expand one such column, breaking ties by discovery order, and repeat. Each positive-gap step reveals a new member of the finite orbit. Equality certifies exact global optimality for this basis. If a further expansion costs at least the entire gap in the same horizon and currency, even perfect subsequent optimization cannot repay that first charge. This early exit still returns the executable upper layout and its numerical gap. Discovery work already paid is additional to both deployment bounds.

A simple family shows how sharp the distinction can be. Use the three-letter full-transformation-monoid construction with $r\ge3$ states, a head positive at one state only, and the $r$ singleton sign functions as basis. Their sign matrix $2I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top$ is nonsingular for $r\ne2$. The cycle and adjacent swap permute the basis; the single collapse exposes at most a constant and one doubleton as new projective columns. If the synthesis savings from every observed collapse request for either boundary column are smaller than its unavoidable full register fee, the lower cut excludes both. The upper and lower cuts then coincide after expanding only $r$ columns, although the complete orbit has $2^{r-1}$. A full future workload with different letter frequencies can invalidate that optimality decision. The forecast calculation remains exact.


## Exact finite grids and the continuous limit

**PROVED — sharp finite-grid restriction.** Let $G_m=\{0,\dots,m-1\}$, $m\ge2$. Fix an input symbol and an affine coordinate $F(x)=b+\sum_j a_jx_j$. If at least two coefficients are nonzero, the fraction of the full register grid $G_m^r$ for which $F(x)\in G_m$ is at most

$$\theta_m=1-\frac{\lfloor m^2/4\rfloor}{m^2}.$$

Thus a learned affine update that stays exactly on-grid on more than $\theta_m$ of the input states is constant or reads one coordinate with slope $+1$ or $-1$ and an integral offset. If that input coordinate reaches both endpoints of its grid, the only nonconstant possibilities are $x_j$ and $m-1-x_j$. If the law preserves every constant input ($F(t,\ldots,t)=t$), only $x_j$ is possible in the dense regime. The occupancy hypothesis concerns the actual reachable states in these physical coordinates.

To prove the bound, if some $a_j$ is nonintegral, two consecutive positions on a line in direction $j$ cannot both land on integers. The successful fraction is at most $\lceil m/2\rceil/m\le\theta_m$. Otherwise choose two nonzero integer coefficients and fix the remaining coordinates. Reflect either selected axis to make both slopes positive. Partition the $m$-by-$m$ square into increasing chains of lengths $2m-1,2m-3,\dots,1$: traverse one boundary row and then its final column, remove them and repeat on the remaining square. Each step raises $F$ by at least one, so a chain of length $k$ contributes at most $\min(k,m)$ successes. Summing gives $m^2-\lfloor m^2/4\rfloor$. The central $m$ consecutive sums of $x_1+x_2$ contain exactly that many pairs by the triangular sum counts. Subtracting the smallest of those sums gives an attaining affine map. In the one-coordinate case an integral slope of magnitude at least two gives at most $\lceil m/2\rceil$ successful points per line. An integral offset is necessary for any success when the slope is integral. This proves the conclusions including $m=2$. The bound is sharp over affine maps; the attaining shift need not preserve constant inputs.

For real coordinates in $[0,1]^r$, an affine map preserving constants and staying in $[0,1]$ on the entire cube is precisely a convex mixture. Constant preservation gives $b=0$ and $\sum a_j=1$. Evaluating at each unit vector gives $a_j\ge0$; conversely these conditions suffice. Conditional on the observed symbol this permits different mixing weights. The result identifies a hard addressing regime for exact finite values and a continuous mixing regime from the same restriction on value semantics. It derives neither query/key dot products nor a softmax training rule.

The distinction is testable in the limit. Exact midpoint mixing succeeds on just $(\lceil m/2\rceil^2+\lfloor m/2\rfloor^2)/m^2$ of ordered grid pairs. On the grid rescaled to $[0,1]$, rounding that same mean to its nearest level costs at most $1/(2(m-1))$ pointwise. For an honest Bernoulli mean the excess Brier cost is the square of that error. For a composition of maps with sup-norm Lipschitz constant $\gamma$, per-step rounding at most $\varepsilon$ yields $e_t\le\gamma^t e_0+\varepsilon\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\gamma^j$ by induction. An infinite-horizon bound therefore needs an appropriate stability argument. Taking finer grids does not turn an exact finite-cube identity into a theorem about an approximate learned machine.


## Learning an action from sparse contexts

Suppose ordinary labels on a familiar subalphabet have identified an $r$-dimensional realization $(\alpha,T,e)$ of a scalar forecast, with row state $\alpha$ and output column $e$. Hold this basis fixed during the following acquisition block. A new symbol $b$ occurs once in each scored word $ubv$, with $u,v$ familiar. If $M$ is its unknown action, the target mean is $\alpha T_u M T_v e$. Define $d_{uv,ij}=(\alpha T_u)_i(T_v e)_j$. Each observation thus scores a linear functional of the $r^2$ entries of $M$. No transition entry or product of the correct hidden signs is supplied to the learner.

Initialize the new action as unidentified. After forecasting and receiving ordinary labels, accumulate $G=\sum_\ell n_\ell d_\ell d_\ell^\top$ and $h=\sum_\ell n_\ell d_\ell\bar Y_\ell$. Solve $Gm=h$ by elimination on independent coordinates, setting free coordinates to zero. These are the normal equations for squared prediction loss. They are consistent because the nullspace of $G$ is the orthogonal complement of the design rows and $h$ lies in their span. Forecast a query in the span by its fitted functional and clip it to $[0,1]$. Outside the span the declared learner uses its fair fallback. After a full-rank fit reshape $m$ as the new transition and compose it in the ordinary way. The fallback and free-coordinate convention do not give unknown functionals an identification guarantee.

**PROVED — sparse observability.** For unrestricted real actions with exactly specified observed means, the mean at a new one-occurrence context is identified iff its design vector $d$ lies in the observed span. Sufficiency follows by linear combination. Otherwise a null vector $z$ has $d^\top z\ne0$, and $m+tz$ matches every observed mean while changing this query. Over a finite probability experiment whose admissible predictions all lie strictly inside $(0,1)$, small $t$ preserves those constraints. Further sign, global realization or boundary constraints can identify more. Full rank identifies $M$ in the chosen coordinates, including its repeated compositions under the declared exact realization. No complete rectangular probe block around the rare symbol is required.

For fixed designs and independent binary outcomes, any $w$ with $d=\sum_\ell w_\ell d_\ell$ gives an unbiased estimator $\sum w_\ell\bar Y_\ell$. Hoeffding bounds its absolute error above $t$ by $2\exp(-2t^2/\sum_\ell w_\ell^2/n_\ell)$. The least-squares query attains the smallest such denominator: take $z$ solving $Gz=d$ and $w_\ell=n_\ell d_\ell^\top z$. Any other feasible weights differ by $v$ with $\sum v_\ell d_\ell=0$, so their extra weighted squared norm is $\sum v_\ell^2/n_\ell$. Thus a direction in the span can still be expensive to acquire. These sampling bounds condition on accurate, frozen anchor operators and a design independent of the rare labels. Uncertainty in the anchors and extrapolation through repeated noisy fits require additional bounds.

Acquiring each design costs $O((|u|+|v|)r^2+r^2)$ scalar operations, plus one $O(r^4)$ Gram update per distinct aggregated observation type or unaggregated event. A dense implementation stores $r^4+r^2$ sufficient-statistic entries per unknown symbol and solves a system of order $r^2$ in $O(r^6)$ operations. Once identified the transition has $r^2$ entries and uses $O(r^2)$ work per rare-token occurrence. A conventional linear regression on identical observable features gives the same estimates and uncertainty. The contribution is identifying those features as predictions obtained from a previously acquired computation and accounting for acquiring the next transformation.

The effect of approximate anchors is explicit at one occurrence. Work in a common coordinate system. Let $m$ be the true action with $\|m\|_1\le S$, let each estimated row $\tilde d_i$ have sup-norm error at most $\epsilon_i$, and let $|\bar Y_i-d_i^\top m|\le b_i$. For a query with row estimate $\tilde d$ and error $\epsilon$, any weights $w$ give a clipped forecast with absolute error at most

$$S\left(\left\|\tilde d-\sum_i w_i\tilde d_i\right\|_\infty+\epsilon+\sum_i|w_i|\epsilon_i\right)+\sum_i|w_i|b_i.$$

Subtract $d^\top m$, insert the estimated rows, and apply the triangle inequality and dual norms. Clipping cannot increase distance to a probability. The bound requires justified error radii in the same basis and a bound on action magnitude. Large extrapolation weights or a nearly singular moment matrix can erase the value of full rank.

## When does the current probability suffice for composition?

**PROVED.** For a nonconstant terminal-label law $p(w)$ form the rows $R_w=(p(wv))_{v\in A^*}$. Their affine hull is a line if and only if each input letter has an affine map $F_a$ with $p(wa)=F_a(p(w))$. On a line, evaluation at the empty suffix is nonconstant because $p$ is nonconstant, so this evaluation is an injective affine coordinate. Reading the coordinate for suffix $a$ gives $F_a$. Conversely, composing the $F_a$ gives an affine expression in $p(w)$ for every suffix coordinate. This confines every row to the same line. Equivalently the Hankel matrix with an additional constant-one column has rank two. A constant law has affine dimension zero. These statements concern terminal Bernoulli forecasts on externally read words. Jaeger's observable-operator framework treats states defined by future observations, including the distinct problem of normalizing states after observing a symbol generated by the model. [Jaeger, *Observable Operator Models for Discrete Stochastic Time Series*](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976600300015411)

Let $I=[\inf_w p(w),\sup_w p(w)]$. Every $F_a$ maps the reachable values into $I$, hence by affinity and continuity maps $I$ into itself. On rescaling $I$ to the unit interval, its endpoint images $u_a,v_a$ are probabilities. A two-state categorical machine with transition rows $(1-u_a,u_a),(1-v_a,v_a)$ advances its second-state probability by $(1-z)u_a+zv_a$. Reading out the original interval endpoints realizes exactly the given law. The converse follows from the same affine recursion for the second-state probability. This proves equivalence for this family and identifies one sufficient predictive value. In the original probability units, requiring $F_a([0,1])\subseteq[0,1]$ is an additional hypothesis. The law alternating between $3/4$ and $1$ has unique update $7/4-z$ whose value at zero is $7/4$.

**Learning from adjacent observed words.** Use the full-unit-interval subclass in the original coordinates. Initialize all forecasts at $1/2$. At a checkpoint keep the word observation counts and means $m_w$. For each observed pair $w,wa$ fit the endpoints in $[0,1]^2$ to minimize $\sum n_{wa}((1-m_w)u_a+m_wv_a-m_{wa})^2$. This is ordinary child-label Brier loss with empirical prefix features. The counts for both sides have an acquisition cost. With distinct inputs solve scalar weighted regression. If its unconstrained optimum falls outside the square, convexity puts a minimum on an edge. Solve its four one-dimensional quadratics and keep the cheapest. With constant input the constant weighted mean minimizes the loss. A letter without pairs retains the fair fallback. Install $m_\epsilon$ as the initial forecast where available. Each installed token advances the scalar by one multiply and one addition using a stored intercept and slope. An equivalent positive two-state stochastic calculation gives identical predictions. If a queried letter has $u_a=v_a$, it erases the old distinction.

For a deployment distribution over an accurate scalar law, write $Z$ for the old forecast and $A$ for the current input. The best predictor restricted to $A$ alone is $E[F_A(Z)\mid A]$. Its excess Brier over the composed law is exactly $E[(v_A-u_A)^2\operatorname{Var}(Z\mid A)]$ by conditional variance. Positive slopes, negative slopes and complete overwrite follow from the same update. At a training checkpoint the empirical Brier gain over the best constant for that letter can likewise be compared directly to a price for the extra read and operation. This local calculation holds the observed input features fixed. A change in a deployed recurrent transition can affect later letters too.

**A simultaneous certificate.** From raw counts obtain intervals $I_w$ for all measured true means. For $K$ fixed words with $n_w$ independent binary labels each, the failure probability is at most $\sum_w 2\exp(-2n_w\delta_w^2)$ for radii $\delta_w$. For each sign of $v_a-u_a$, intersect the endpoint square and corresponding half-plane with the conditions $F_a(I_w)\cap I_{wa}\ne\varnothing$ for every observed pair. On one sign each intersection test is two linear inequalities evaluated at opposite interval endpoints. Polygon clipping therefore gives both feasible regions exactly. On the simultaneous event, a true map in the declared subclass belongs to one of them. Starting from $I_\epsilon$, bound a word by propagating the smallest and largest values over both signs, their polygon vertices and the two endpoints of the current interval. For fixed input the expression is linear in the map, and for fixed map it is linear in the input. Induction encloses every word at every length on the same event. Reusing a map across steps can make these propagated intervals conservative. An empty feasible set rejects the assumed model or intervals. ## Learning the output distinction

Let $B$ be a binary distinction computed from accessible past tokens and $Y$ the next token. From prior training pairs count $n_y$ and $h_y$ with $B=1$. For a group assignment $g$, compare the pooled maximum-likelihood unigram with a head predicting $g(Y)$ from $B$ and using the pooled empirical distribution inside each group. The reduction in fitted log loss per label is exactly $I_{\rm emp}(B;g(Y))$ by the entropy chain rule. Maximizing it minimizes the empirical conditional entropy of $B$ given the assigned group. The constraint on the output decoder matters here. The comparison assumes pooled within-group distributions.

The binary-input channel quantization theorem of Kurkoski and Yagi gives an optimal partition into at most $k$ contiguous intervals of the sorted posterior $r_y=h_y/n_y$. A direct proof uses the Bernoulli cross entropy. With group means $p_j$ held fixed, assigning token $y$ to group $j$ costs $n_y[-r_y\log p_j-(1-r_y)\log(1-p_j)]$. Two such costs cross at most once as functions of $r_y$, after dividing out the positive weight $n_y$. The lower envelope therefore has contiguous intervals of minimizers, with ordered tie breaking. A group mean of zero or one can accept only compatible endpoints at finite cost. Reassigning according to this envelope and then refitting the means cannot raise loss. Applied to an optimum, this produces a contiguous optimum. Groups with zero token count or equal posterior require a convention for deployment. [Kurkoski and Yagi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.5637)

A contiguous segment with count $n$ and $h$ positives costs $n\log n-h\log h-(n-h)\log(n-h)$ with $0\log0=0$. ## Certifying a learned computation on a continuing stream

Let two predictors publish positive distributions $p_t,q_t$ from data available before each target. Define the observed full-token gain $X_t=\log(p_t(Y_t)/q_t(Y_t))$, its conditional mean $\mu_t=E[X_t\mid{\cal F}_{t-1}]$, and a predictable upper bound $w_t$ on the range of possible $X_t$. For the same within-group decoder and binary group rates $p,q$, the range width is exactly $|\operatorname{logit}p-\operatorname{logit}q|$ if both groups are possible. It remains an upper bound on restricted supports. This calculation includes outcomes missing from the training counts. Equal predictions have width zero. The bound can be restricted to eligible contexts using an indicator known before the target.

**PROVED.** Fix $\lambda>0$. With probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for every time $s$,
$$\sum_{t\le s}\mu_t\ \ge\ \sum_{t\le s} X_t-\frac{\lambda}{8}\sum_{t\le s}w_t^2-\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\lambda}.$$
Condition on the past. The log moment-generating function of $X_t-\mu_t$ has value and derivative zero at zero. Its second derivative is the variance under exponential tilting, bounded by $w_t^2/4$ since every tilted outcome stays in the same interval. Integration gives the conditional bound $\lambda^2w_t^2/8$. Exponentiating the cumulative centered gains minus these bounds gives a nonnegative supermartingale starting at one. Stopping at the first crossing of $1/\delta$ up to any finite horizon bounds crossing probability by $\delta$. Let the horizon grow. A finite union bound permits predeclared comparisons and selection of the best reported $\lambda$. This is the bounded-increment exponential supermartingale argument. [Howard et al., time-uniform Chernoff bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.03204)

Any predictable difference in deployment price can be subtracted from both sides. A new computation may spend at most the remaining certified gain on acquisition at explicitly stated exchange rates. Counts of fit operations and physical units must be supplied separately. The conclusion concerns the cumulative conditional means along the observed histories of a sequential sampling law. It permits arbitrary temporal dependence. It does not project a fixed corpus gain onto another source, refund model selection on the audit block, or certify a GPU speedup.

The continuing-stream learner fits the past-token bit for the current-token-conditioned loss. ## Identifying a binary operation from noisy task responses

Let $d\ge2$ and $N=2^d$. The task names are opaque. Pilot input $j$ gives each task one binary response, so a noiseless dictionary with $m$ pilots associates a row in $\{0,1\}^m$ to each name. Consider a fixed binary operation $g$ applied pointwise to any two rows. Say it preserves the observations if the result is always another row. Require both arguments to affect its truth table. This requirement excludes operations that can only return an existing operand or a constant. It does not specify an operation.

**PROVED.** At least $d+1$ binary pilot columns are necessary to identify **both** all $2^d$ rows and a unique such operation by preservation. Fewer than $d$ columns cannot distinguish the rows. At exactly $d$, every possible row must occur, so every one of the 16 binary operations preserves the observations. The bound is sharp in two different families. Take all $d$-bit signatures and add the single column $h(s_1,s_2)$, for $h$ either XOR or OR. Closure now requires
$$g(h(a,b),h(c,e))=h(g(a,c),g(b,e))\quad\text{for all }a,b,c,e.$$
All four bits can vary freely. For XOR this makes $g$ a linear functional on the binary plane, so the preserving gates are zero, the two projections and XOR. For OR it makes $g$ a homomorphism of the join operation. If $g(0,0)=1$ then $g$ is the constant one. Otherwise the values at $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ determine it by joins, giving zero, either projection or OR. Only the true $h$ uses both arguments. This uses the classical connection between commuting operations and homomorphisms. The observation count and forecasting experiment impose additional information and resource constraints. **PROVED.** With one binary input distinction and one live bit, each input induces one of four unary maps on the bit: constant zero, constant one, identity or a flip. If a reset occurs, that occurrence fixes the state; subsequently only the parity of flips since the most recent reset matters. This follows by composing the unary maps in chronological order. Before the first reset the same parity formula holds from the initial state. If both inputs reset, the state is determined by the last input alone. These cases exhaust the 16 updates.

## When two raw tokens can share one internal symbol

**PROVED for a fixed refinement decoder.** Let $P(w)$ be the cylinder probabilities of a stationary source with a finite alphabet $A$ and positive word probabilities. An internal alphabet is a partition $g:A\to C$. The model uses an arbitrary causal law $Q$ on cell sequences and emits each raw token according to a fixed distribution $r(\cdot\mid c)$ supported on its cell. Its full probability for a word $w$ is $Q(g(w))\prod_i r(w_i\mid g(w_i))$. The coarse state may have arbitrary memory. Brown et al. used the same within-class emission model with a class $n$-gram and derived the adjacent-class mutual information objective for bigrams. Here the question is exact preservation under every continuation and the length of evidence needed to decide a merge. [Brown et al., Sections 3–4](https://aclanthology.org/J92-4003.pdf)

There is a **unique coarsest lossless partition**. Put two tokens $a,b$ in the same cell exactly when, for every prefix $u$ and suffix $v$,
$$\frac{P(uav)}{P(a)}=\frac{P(ubv)}{P(b)}.$$
These are probabilities of the *joint* neighboring context conditional on the central token. In a lossless model the coarse law must be the pushforward of $P$, by summing over each coarse-word fiber. Its one-symbol marginals then force $r(a\mid c)=P(a)/P(c)$. Substitution in the word formula gives the displayed equality. Conversely, equality of signatures is an equivalence relation. Replacing one letter at a time shows that $P(w)/\prod_iP(w_i)$ is constant on each coarse-word fiber. Summation on that fiber gives the required factorization with exactly these $Q,r$. Every lossless partition refines the resulting one. A decoder allowed to condition within-cell odds on history can distinguish a different class. The registered FineWeb-Edu test used such a decoder through the last raw token.

**Finite identification with a sharp horizon.** Write $d$ for the real rank of the Hankel matrix $P(uv)$. In a minimal linear realization $P(w)=\alpha M_w\omega$, a merge is lossless exactly when $M_a/P(a)=M_b/P(b)$. The prefix rows $\alpha M_u$ and suffix columns $M_v\omega$ span both $d$-dimensional spaces. Starting from the empty word, a span that stops increasing is invariant under every extension and cannot increase later. Thus words of length at most $d-1$ on each side span. Testing proportionality on those words suffices; on known row and column bases only $d^2$ entries per letter are required. A minimal realization can operate on $|C|$ action matrices and a per-token refinement table in place of $|A|$ matrices. The general rank bound is tight even for positive stationary sources on three letters. [Classical finite-rank realization and shifted Hankel blocks, Balle et al., Section 2](https://xaviercarreras.github.io/pubs/2014-mlj-bclq.pdf) · [Sharp lower construction](OBSTRUCTIONS.md#the-sharp-window-length-for-distinguishing-token-actions)

**Shorter test for a state-emitting HMM.** The sharp lower pair above emits a token on a hidden transition (Mealy form). Suppose instead that $M_a=D_aM$ with diagonal emissions, a positive stationary row $\pi$, and exactly $d$ hidden states and Hankel rank $d$. Every reachable row lies in the row space of $M$, including $\pi=\pi M$, so $M$ is invertible. One-sided equalities $P(av)/P(a)=P(bv)/P(b)$ for $|v|\le d-1$ force $\pi(D_a/P(a)-D_b/P(b))M=0$ by suffix spanning. Invertibility, positivity of $\pi$ and diagonality then force the two normalized emissions to coincide. Thus total length $d$ suffices for the predicate in this subclass. The cloud reading session derived this qualification. Its bounded primary-source search found the classical $2d-1$ upper bound and older stationary identification results but left priority of the specific three-letter lower example unsettled. **Predictive cost of a merge.** Write $X^T$ for a length-$T$ raw window, $G^T=g(X^T)$, and $\mathrm{TC}(U^T)=\sum_i H(U_i)-H(U^T)$. For each $T$ the smallest expected excess cumulative causal log loss over the true law in this model class is
$$D_T=H(G^T)+T H(X_1\mid G_1)-H(X^T)
=\mathrm{TC}(X^T)-\mathrm{TC}(G^T).$$
Indeed any $Q,r$ pays the displayed term plus $\mathrm{KL}(P_{G^T}\Vert Q_T)+T\sum_c P(c)\mathrm{KL}(P_{X\mid c}\Vert r_c)$. The same stationary marginal refinement and coarse process minimize all horizons simultaneously. At a block boundary $D_{m+n}-D_m-D_n$ equals the mutual information of adjacent raw blocks minus that of their two deterministic images. Data processing makes this nonnegative. Superadditivity and the finite-alphabet upper bound imply $\lim_T D_T/T=\sup_T D_T/T$. A zero asymptotic penalty therefore requires exact preservation at *every* finite horizon. A finite-window loss supplies a lower bound on the long-run excess under the stationary law. At length two this formula recovers the mutual information discarded between adjacent tokens. [Watanabe's total correlation](https://www.neuralmachines.com/references/correlation.pdf) · [Brown et al., equation 9](https://aclanthology.org/J92-4003.pdf)

One pair can certify a lower bound without fitting $Q$. Let $p=P(a)+P(b)$, $\lambda=P(a)/p$, and let $K_a,K_b$ be the laws of the joint flanks at one position in a length-$L$ window given the indicated center. A fixed refinement forcing a merge makes these context laws identical under the candidate model. Condition the KL on the event that the center is one of the two letters. The smallest KL to a product law on that event is its mutual information, the $\lambda$-weighted Jensen–Shannon divergence of the flank laws. Applying Pinsker to each law and its mixture gives
$$\lim_T D_T/T\ \ge\ (p/L)\,\mathrm{JS}_\lambda(K_a,K_b)
\ \ge\ (2p/L)\lambda(1-\lambda)\,\mathrm{TV}(K_a,K_b)^2.$$
A merge test needs the context mass as well as its difference in conditional probabilities.

**Learning and cost.** The finite-window likelihood uses ordinary causal predictions. For $n$ independently started episodes of length $T$, fit the empirical coarse-word distribution and the pooled within-cell token frequencies. Their product is the maximum likelihood in the stated class. Its causal forecasts are ratios of successive coarse prefix masses times the fitted refinement. The six-token experiment optimizes this likelihood with the registered parameter penalty over 203 partitions, beginning with raw token IDs alone. Each candidate reads the occupied raw word counts. For six tokens and length three this is feasible. Exhaustive set-partition search has Bell-number growth and a dense coarse table has $|C|^T$ entries. A known rank offers another acquisition path through the context signatures. With unknown bases, testing all flanks uses up to $|A|(\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}|A|^i)^2$ coordinates. Equal actions can reduce dense matrix storage by $(|A|-|C|)d^2$ scalars before paying for the group map and individual refinements. Neither formula promises faster memory access.

For a quantitative recovery condition, assume independent stationary windows of length $2d-1$, minimum single-token mass $\rho>0$, and a known lower bound $\Delta>0$ on the largest tested signature-coordinate difference between inequivalent letters. With $M$ tested flank pairs, threshold empirical sup distances at $\Delta/2$. A binomial lower-tail bound gives probability at most $|A|e^{-n\rho/8}$ that any center has fewer than $n\rho/2$ observations. Conditional on a center's count, its flank observations are independent samples from that letter's context law. Hoeffding and a union bound make all coordinate errors at most $\Delta/8$ except with probability $2|A|M e^{-n\rho\Delta^2/64}$. On both good events thresholding recovers exactly the equivalence relation. This bound requires justified coverage, separation and independent windows. ## Recovering a continuing update from three-token windows

**PROVED for stationary rank-two permutation sources.** There are two hidden states, both with positive stationary mass. In state $i$ each token $a$ has positive probability $e_{ia}$, and observing it induces either the identity or the swap on the two states. All letters use their own previously unknown update. Suppose the observable law has real Hankel rank two. Ordinary word probabilities through length three determine both emission rows, all token actions and the initial state distribution up to exchanging the hidden-state names. There need not be any word that synchronizes two different starting states.

To see this, form a spanning block $B=(P(uv))$ on empty and one-letter prefixes and suffixes as in the [two-state rank argument](#learning-a-synchronizing-bit-from-the-token-stream). Obtain $T_a=B^{-1}(P(uav))$ and terminal vector $\beta=(1,0)^\top$. The $T_a$ are simultaneously similar to the hidden transition matrices. Their determinants are positive for a stay and negative for a flip, with absolute value $e_{0a}e_{1a}$. A staying letter with different emissions supplies a diagonal operator with distinct eigenvalues in the hidden basis. If all stays have equal emissions, consider ordered products of two flips. The eigenvalues of such a product are $e_{0a}e_{1b}$ and $e_{1a}e_{0b}$. If every pair had equal eigenvalues, all flips would share the same emission ratio. Summing the two emission rows forces that ratio to equal one whenever flips exist. With no flips and only state-independent stays the same conclusion holds. Both cases would give an iid observable law of rank one. Hence a separating operator of length one or two always exists. Its product uses already fitted operators, so a four-token observation is unnecessary.

Let $\Pi_0,\Pi_1$ be its two spectral projectors. The columns $\Pi_i\beta$ form the hidden coordinate basis with its scaling fixed, since in that basis the terminal vector is $(1,1)^\top$. Conjugating every $T_a$ into that basis gives its emitting row masses and permutation. The initial functional in the same basis gives the initial weights. Exchanging the two projectors only renames the states. This argument builds on classical minimal linear realization. It also specifies exactly which non-synchronizing models are being inferred. [Shifted Hankel operators, Balle et al.](https://xaviercarreras.github.io/pubs/2014-mlj-bclq.pdf)

**Acquisition can use a number of word counts linear in the alphabet size.** An independent derivation gives an explicit reconstruction. Write $p=\Pr(S=0)$, $v=p(1-p)$, $\Delta_a=e_{0a}-e_{1a}$ and $\mu_a=P(a)$. The adjacent covariance $C_{ab}=P(ab)-\mu_a\mu_b$ equals $u_a\Delta_b$, with $u_a=v\Delta_a$ for a stay and $u_a=(1-2p)\mu_a-v\Delta_a$ for a flip. This follows by comparing the joint mass of $a$ and its post-emission state zero with $p\mu_a$. Some $C_{xx}\ne0$. Otherwise all informative letters would have to flip, and $v\Delta_a=(1-2p)\mu_a$ for every nonzero $\Delta_a$. This contradicts $\sum_a\Delta_a=0$ or eliminates all informative letters.

Find such an $x$ from $P(a),P(aa)$. Set $d_a=C_{xa}/C_{xx}=\Delta_a/\Delta_x$. Three further word masses per letter give the signed determinant
$$t_a=\frac{P(a)P(xax)-P(xa)P(ax)}{C_{xx}}.$$
Let $z=(1-2p)\Delta_x$ and $w=v\Delta_x^2$. The equations
$$|t_a|-\mu_a^2=\mu_a d_a z-d_a^2 w$$
follow by expanding $e_{0a}e_{1a}$. Choose opposite nonzero signs of $d_a$, which exist by the zero sum. Those two equations are independent because $\mu_a>0$. Choose the hidden naming with $\Delta_x=+\sqrt{z^2+4w}$. Then $p=(1-z/\Delta_x)/2$, $e_{0a}=\mu_a+(z+\Delta_x)d_a/2$ and $e_{1a}=\mu_a+(z-\Delta_x)d_a/2$. The sign of $t_a$ gives the action. Two passes over independent triples suffice to accumulate the necessary statistics in $O(|A|)$ counters, with constant updates per triple and one square root after a two-equation solve. Counts can be pooled across equivalent positions under stationarity. The reported prospective experiment used the registered full triple histogram. Finite-data coverage is a separate issue. If all tested word probabilities have error at most $\varepsilon$, a $2\times2$ probability determinant has error at most $4\varepsilon$ and each self-covariance at most $3\varepsilon$. Picking the largest empirical absolute self-covariance gives true magnitude at least $c_*-6\varepsilon$, where $c_*=\max_x|C_{xx}|$. For minimum emission $m>0$, the conditions $c_*-6\varepsilon>3\varepsilon$ and $(c_*-6\varepsilon)m^2>4\varepsilon$ suffice to recover every determinant sign. Accurate emission estimates also require stable divisors in the two-equation solve. Independent stationary windows give ordinary concentration bounds for the finitely many possible word counts, including selection of $x$. A stationary mixture with only stays can reveal a single component on a single trajectory.

**Continuous computation and finite precision.** Let $L$ be the log odds of hidden state zero given the observed past. A token $a$ acts by $L'=s_a(L+\log(e_{0a}/e_{1a}))$, with $s_a=1$ for a stay and $-1$ for a swap. The next-token distribution is the probability-weighted sum of the two emission rows. Thus a learned categorical permutation model executes as a continuous scalar posterior with one shared update per token. A conventional positive two-state Bayesian filter implements the same law. Each registered emission and initial weight was fitted from ordinary triple likelihood. This representation does not require an externally supplied assignment of token actions.

For fitted positive probability rows and the correct permutations, exposing the initial state gives a bound on expected error at *every* horizon:
$$\frac1T\mathrm{KL}(P_{1:T}\Vert\widehat P_{1:T})
\le \frac{\mathrm{KL}(\pi\Vert\widehat\pi)}T+\sum_i\pi_i\,\mathrm{KL}(e_i\Vert\widehat e_i).$$
Conditional on the initial state and the raw past, the common permutation sequence fixes the hidden state. The KL chain rule gives equality for the initial-state-augmented joint law. Marginalizing the initial state yields the inequality. This is an expected per-token bound with aligned hidden names. ## Exact and attainable prediction on a probability clock

Jaeger's probability clock gives a rank-three observable-operator process outside all finite hidden Markov models. His original construction uses two outputs, an irrational rotation and a reset. It also illustrates how unconstrained learned operators can produce negative probabilities. The following three-output variant has constant advance probability, strictly positive event probabilities, and two observed reset marks. This change makes its expected prediction cost reducible to one marked run. [Jaeger, Sections 6 and 9.1.3](https://publica-rest.fraunhofer.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/c5799916-b434-4186-94ab-717cb74db946/content)

At age $j$ emit $a$ with probability $\rho$ and advance. Otherwise emit $b$ with conditional probability $q_j=(1+\kappa\cos j\theta)/2$ or emit $c$ with its complement; either mark resets age to zero. Assume $0<\rho<1$, $0<|\kappa|<1$ and $\sin\theta\ne0$. Stationary ages have probabilities $w_j=(1-\rho)\rho^j$. The span of $1,\cos j\theta,\sin j\theta$ is closed under every shifted word probability. The advance rotates and scales these coordinates, and each reset multiplies evaluation at age zero by a linear combination of them. Three consecutive ages reached after a reset are independent as evaluation rows. The suffixes $\epsilon,b,ab$ distinguish all three coordinates. Thus the stationary word-law Hankel rank is exactly three. For $\rho=2/3$, $\kappa=1/2$, $e^{i\theta}=(3+4i)/5$, the stationary initial phase mean $(1-\rho)/(1-\rho e^{i\theta})$ and every word probability are rational.

**PROVED for all finite positive-state competitors.** Here a $K$-state HMM may use arbitrary nonnegative symbol matrices whose sum is stochastic. Let $R(j,s)=(1-\rho)\rho^j q_j(s)$ be the true joint law of the age and binary mark of a complete run. The smallest asymptotic expected excess log loss among these HMMs equals
$$ (1-\rho)\min_{\beta,A,b,c}\mathrm{KL}(R\Vert R_{\beta,A,b,c}), \qquad R_{\beta,A,b,c}(j,s)=\beta A^j b_s,\quad b_b=b,\quad b_c=c.$$
Here $\beta$ is a distribution on $K$ phases, $A,b,c\ge0$ and $A\mathbf1+b+c=\mathbf1$. Include an atom at infinite run length for a defective candidate. This variational formula permits arbitrary stochastic evolution while observing $a$. It does not impose deterministic ages or a constant candidate advance rate.

For the lower bound, after any observed reset the true run law is always $R$. An arbitrary competitor begins that run at some posterior $\beta$ and uses its own $A$ and exit columns. Score each run only through its first $L$ tokens, including the mark if it appears. The selected token sets are disjoint and selection of each next prediction is predictable. The KL chain rule gives at least the optimal censored-run KL per run. Starts occur with frequency $1-\rho$ and starts too near the end have negligible density. The censored KL objectives increase with $L$ and are lower semicontinuous on the compact set of kernels and initial weights. A convergent subsequence of their minimizers, tested first at each fixed censor length, shows that their minima increase to the minimum uncensored KL. For the upper bound, give each mark the transition matrix $b_s\beta$. This implements independent candidate runs and reaches the run KL per mean true run length. If a minimizing candidate can fail to exit, condition its finite runs on eventual exit. With exit probabilities $h_i$, use $\beta'_i=\beta_i h_i/(\beta h)$, $A'_{ij}=A_{ij}h_j/h_i$, $b'_{s,i}=b_{s,i}/h_i$ on $h_i>0$. Its finite-run probabilities increase by $1/(\beta h)$. A proper finite realization can be initialized stationarily. For a finite-KL competitor each supported run has negative log probability at most linear in its length, by its smallest positive transition and initial weight. The geometric true tail bounds the expected contribution of partial boundary runs. These arguments also cover competitors initialized at arbitrary fixed distributions.

**Deterministic reset machines admit an exact optimizer.** Following $a$ from the reset state gives a transient of length $m$ followed by a cycle of length $\ell$. All $K$ reachable states have $m+\ell=K$; a smaller machine can split off the first visit to its cycle. Optimizing the per-state next-token law gives advance probability $\rho$ everywhere. The $\ell$ repeated cells have weights $(1-\rho)\rho^j/(1-\rho^\ell)$ for $m\le j<K$. Their optimal conditional mark probabilities are
$$\bar q_j=\tfrac12\left[1+\kappa\,\Re\!\left(e^{ij\theta}\frac{1-\rho^\ell}{1-\rho^\ell e^{i\ell\theta}}\right)\right].$$
The transient has the individual probabilities $q_j$. Only the weighted cross entropy within these age groups varies between layouts. Enumerating $\ell=1,\ldots,K$ therefore finds the unrestricted optimum *within deterministic single-reset machines*. A saturated age counter is the case $\ell=1$. Its rate cost is at most $(1-\rho)\rho^{K-1}[\log2-h((1+|\kappa|)/2)]$, even by guessing fair on the tail. Thus an infinite exact-state requirement can coexist with exponentially small attainable finite-state loss.

**Learning and precision.** Runs following observable resets are independent. In the supplied clock grammar, $\rho=\Pr(J\ge1)$, $\kappa=E[Y\mid J=0]$ and $\cos\theta=E[Y\mid J=1]/\kappa$ for marks $Y=\pm1$. The two necessary short-run conditions have probabilities $1-\rho$ and $(1-\rho)\rho$. Ordinary binomial concentration on their counts and empirical mark means gives consistent estimates away from $\kappa=0$. The sign of $\theta$ is observationally irrelevant. Expected prediction loss is stable without certifying an irrational angle. For example, fixing the correct $\rho,\kappa$ and estimating $\theta$ changes each mark probability by at most $|\kappa|j|\widehat\theta-\theta|/2$. The Bernoulli KL upper bound by chi-square, averaged over the geometric age, gives per-token excess at most $(1-\rho)\kappa^2 E[J^2](\widehat\theta-\theta)^2/(1-\kappa^2)$, or $(10/9)(\widehat\theta-\theta)^2$ in the registered source. The stationary infinite-past rate obeys this bound. A common stationary initial age gives the same finite-horizon expected bound by revealing that age. A post-reset start has smaller age moments.

The exact rational recurrence for $\cos(j\theta)$ generally needs growing precision. A finite table uses a bounded counter and stored probabilities, and the observation stream determines how often it pays for an imperfect phase.
