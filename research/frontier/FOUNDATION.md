## A causal state and the cost of forming it

Fix an observable history $h$, input alphabet or observation space, allowed external reads, the workload of later queries $q$, and a downstream loss. A training algorithm maps ordinary observations $D$ to an installed initialization, update $U_D$ and readout $G_D$. Its reachable state satisfies $S_D(hx)=U_D(S_D(h),x)$. The readout publishes $G_D(S_D(h),q)$ before the outcome. Any side information, external tape, mutable parameter, cache or random seed which conveys information about this history belongs in the interface or state account. Price training data and work, installed program and tables, peak working memory, live state per stream and execution traffic separately. To combine them into a single objective, choose exchange rates, a deployment horizon and the number of streams sharing the installation. An update is learned only to the extent that $D$ selects or constructs it under the specified acquisition rule.

For a fixed readout target $\phi(h)$ and an old deterministic state $S$, there is an exact answer to how much state will make $\phi$ reusable after arbitrary extensions. Put histories $h,h'$ in the same cell iff $S(h)=S(h')$ and $\phi(hv)=\phi(h'v)$ for every finite extension $v$, including the empty one. Use all words, or represent illegal paths by a separate outcome. This relation is stable under appending a symbol, hence its cells have a deterministic update and read out $\phi$. Any state retaining $S$ and all the required future readouts must separate histories in different cells. If $r_s$ cells refine old state $s$, an implementation that retains $S$ can use exactly $\lceil\log_2\max_s r_s\rceil$ additional fixed-width history bits, with indices reused across old states. Replacing both registers by a joint cell costs $\lceil\log_2\sum_s r_s\rceil$ bits. This is the right-congruence minimization principle for the declared observer. It counts reachable values. Learning the cells, addressing their transition table, and physically implementing the update can be much more expensive. The same construction applies to a vector of future readouts.

For stochastic data and unrestricted calibrated readouts, a state refinement from $S$ to $(S,Z)$ saves exactly $I(Y;Z\mid S,Q)$ expected log loss on the declared query. This follows by subtracting the two conditional entropies. For several scored queries, sum their gains with their workload weights and account for observations made between them. A paid computation must be reachable from training, retain enough information to execute future updates, and save enough loss or work against an equally informed baseline. A new node, a renamed symbol and a smaller real-linear dimension alone supply none of these guarantees. The quotient criterion for token identities, the two-state posterior, and the finite approximation of the clock are instances with different observers and costs. ## When one predictive bit can be learned by sorting

A one-bit state can be cheap to deploy and computationally hard to acquire from ordinary prediction. There is an explicit family where finding the best bit, even given all exact probabilities, solves minimum graph bisection. Its rows can all be strictly positive with equal weights, and the final scored target can be binary if the readout sees an independent query. This worst-case result leaves useful high-dimensional distributions with simpler geometry. [Reduction, precision and physical interface](OBSTRUCTIONS.md#acquiring-one-predictive-bit-can-encode-minimum-bisection)

**PROVED for affine conditional probability rows.** Let $u$ be one of $N$ finite histories and let $P_u$ be the conditional distribution of a future target $Y$, or the joint distribution of a later observed query $Q$ and a scored target $Y$. The encoder retains one bit before the query. Use freely calibrated readouts and arbitrary positive history weights. If $P_u=a+t_u v$ for all $u$, a globally optimal bit is a threshold on the scalar $t_u$. Indeed, fix the readouts of an optimal partition. Each input's difference in expected log loss between its two assignments is a linear functional of $P_u$, including for query-conditional readouts. It has at most one change of sign on the affine line. Assign each input to its cheaper readout, use one consistent side for ties, and refit the cell means or the within-cell conditional laws. This gives a threshold optimum. Strictly positive rows ensure finite log costs; finite alphabets with zeros follow by uniform smoothing and a constant-partition subsequence. Once sorted, a sweep over split positions uses accumulated counts to find the best one. For $D$ observed joint coordinates, arithmetic costs $O(ND+N\log N)$ when the scalar order is given. On an exact nonconstant line a single varying coordinate supplies the order. Minimum-impurity hyperplane separation and the binary-class threshold case are classical. [Laber, Molinaro and Pereira, Sections 2–3](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/laber18a/laber18a-supp.pdf)

**A computable approximation bound.** Suppose rows on an affine segment approximate the actual rows within total variation $\epsilon\le1/2$ individually. For any fixed partition, the optimized loss on the two laws differs by at most $f(\epsilon)=h(\epsilon)+\epsilon\log|\mathcal Y|$, even if the query alphabet is large. Each pair of cellwise joint distributions has variation at most $\epsilon$ and can be split into a common component and residual laws of mass $d\le\epsilon$. Conditional entropy falls between the component-weighted conditional entropies and that sum plus $h(d)$, by revealing or hiding the mixture tag. Each residual contributes at most $d\log|\mathcal Y|$. Thus score the thresholds in segment order, refitting readouts on the *actual* rows. The best threshold is within $2f(\epsilon)$ of the unrestricted optimum. This is an absolute log-loss bound for known rows. Learned probabilities need separate sampling control.

The order can be recovered from observed rows alone. Enumerate pairs of rows as segment endpoints. Project every other row onto each segment in total variation, sort by its scalar coordinate, and record the largest projection residual. One-dimensional absolute deviation is minimized by a weighted median of the coordinatewise interpolation factors, with coordinate weights equal to the absolute differences of the two endpoints. Clip to the segment. If some unknown line approximates all rows to $\delta$, the two observed rows at its extreme latent positions span a segment within $2\delta$ of every observed row, by interpolation and the triangle inequality. The best endpoint pair is at least this good. Any observed residual at most $\epsilon$ supplies the preceding bound. Enumerating pairs with sorted weighted medians takes $O(N^3D\log D)$ arithmetic in a direct implementation. Prices on row tables, constraints on the update and unseen data can change the preferred split.

**The same source can require a different bit when the query is scored.** Take four equally likely histories $u=0,1,2,3$. Let the next query be $Q=\lfloor u/2\rfloor$ and the following binary target have probability $(2u+1)/8$ of one. Conditional on $u$, the target's law depends only on a single monotone scalar. If $Q$ is available as side information and only the binary target is scored, any bit separating 0 from 1 and 2 from 3 attains the full-history risk. No threshold on $u$ separates both pairs. If the same learner must also forecast $Q$ from its saved bit, the unique optimal partition is the high bit $Q$ itself, up to names. It gains $I(g;Q,Y)=\log2$, the maximum one bit can gain. Equality requires $g$ to be recoverable from $(Q,Y)$. Both binary targets have positive probability under each member of a pair, so $g$ has to be constant within that pair. After independently flipping the announced high bit with probability $1/1024$, all query/target rows have full support. An interval check of every partition gives the low bit as the unique label-only optimum and the high bit as the unique jointly scored optimum. This explains why the affine-line condition for a free query uses the **joint** query/target rows if query frequencies depend on history. [Exact small-channel checks](audit_predictive_split.py)

For an arbitrary bit of the last $L$ binary observations, all $2^L$ histories must survive as distinct deterministic online states exactly when flipping the oldest bit changes the output at every fixed suffix of length $L-1$. If any oldest-bit pair agrees now, the pair also has identical future histories after one new symbol and can merge. Conversely take the most recent position at which two histories differ. Shift that position to the front with identical appended inputs. The remaining suffixes then agree, so their outputs differ. For $L=3$ just 16 of the 256 Boolean functions need eight states. On the new FineWeb-Edu block the likelihood-selected function required two states and read only the newest supplied history bit. ## What two fitted readouts teach about history

Fix two strictly positive full-vocabulary forecasts $d_0(y\mid q),d_1(y\mid q)$ before fitting the encoder. The learner sees a raw history type $U$ when it can still write memory, then a query $Q$ and a raw target $Y$. Put $W=\log[d_1(Y\mid Q)/d_0(Y\mid Q)]$. The downstream readout difference includes the query's effect on both forecasts. It can be estimated from ordinary observed targets without learning the full law of $(Q,Y)$ at each $u$.

Let $b_u$ be a supplied default bit, $a_u=E[1_{\{U=u\}}(1-2b_u)W]$, and $\kappa_u$ the amortized loss-equivalent cost per target of storing that exception. **PROVED.** Flipping the set $E$ of addresses gives exactly $\sum_{u\in E}(a_u-\kappa_u)$ net expected improvement over the default. Install the positive terms. Under a cap of $k$ equal-sized entries, take at most the $k$ largest positive net gains. This follows by expanding the realized log loss for each address, then summing disjoint events. A fixed setup charge for a nonempty table is paid only if that sum exceeds it. Compare whole formats separately when a constant can discard a readout, or when a bitmap has a fixed cost. Under free addresses the maximum gain over the better constant is $(E|E[W\mid U]|-|EW|)/2$. The conditional mean comes before the absolute value because the bit precedes the target.

For an uncapped fixed format with additive rent, the regret from installing estimated signs is exactly $\sum_u |a_u-\kappa_u|1_{\{\mathrm{wrong\ decision}\}}$, bounded by $\sum_u|\hat a_u-a_u|$. Ties give zero regret. If $n$ observations are independent of each other and of the fitted readouts and $|W|\le B$, empirical sums divided by $n$ therefore give expected regret at most
$$\sum_u \min\!\left\{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(1_{\{U=u\}}W)/n},\;2p_u E[|W|\mid U=u]\right\},\qquad p_u=P(U=u).$$
The first bound is Cauchy–Schwarz. The second is the triangle inequality for sample and population means. A matching lower order holds over bounded-score laws even with normalized binary experts. At each occupied address let the sign of $W=\pm B$ have conditional mean $\theta_u e_u$, with $\theta_u$ an unknown fair sign and $e_u=\tfrac14\min\{1,(np_u)^{-1/2}\}$. Opposite binary forecasts with odds $e^B$ realize these log ratios. Flipping only $\theta_u$ gives sample KL $np_u e_u\log[(1+e_u)/(1-e_u)]\le3np_u e_u^2\le3/16$. Pinsker's inequality puts the mean sign error above $1/4$ even if all other signs are disclosed. The average free-address regret is at least $(B/16)\sum_u\min(p_u,\sqrt{p_u/n})$, which bounds worst-case regret from below. Dependent text can give repeated observations of one event. Its chronological loss is measured directly.

A pre-existing scalar order $t(u)$ imposes an additional restriction. Group tied histories, let $s_j$ be the sum of $E[1_{\{U=u\}}W]$ over the $j$th group and let $P_j$ be its cumulative sum, including $P_0=0$. A one-threshold bit can send either a prefix or a suffix to readout one. Its exact best gain over constant zero is $\max_j\{P_j,P_{\rm last}-P_j\}$. The gap to the unrestricted sum of positive address scores is the price in population loss of using that order. Obtaining the order from labels has its own estimation and storage cost. A learned executable state must also be able to retain the advertised address until the readout. On text a lag-two raw token meets that requirement with one installed mapping and one carried bit. ## How much precision can a history distinction use?

Keep the same two positive forecasts but allow the installed history value to be a fraction $w\in[0,1]$. It publishes the categorical distribution $d_w=(1-w)d_0+w d_1$. Write $r=d_1(Y\mid Q)/d_0(Y\mid Q)$ and condition on an occupied history type throughout. The expected improvement over the first readout is $H(w)=E\log[1+w(r-1)]$. The same log-of-a-linear-mixture objective occurs in constant-rebalanced portfolio prediction. The following approximation question concerns a stored history value when only a fixed grid of fractions is available. [Cover's original portfolio formulation](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9965.1991.tb00002.x)

**When interpolation improves prediction.** For bounded positive ratios, $H'(w)=E[(r-1)/(1+w(r-1))]$, and $H''(w)$ is the negative expected square of that integrand. Unless $r=1$ almost surely, the optimum is unique. An interior fraction beats both hard choices exactly when $Er>1$ and $E[1/r]>1$, by the strict signs of the two endpoint derivatives. For any finite grid and default $a_u$, the value of an exception is $p_u\max_{w\in J}[H_u(w)-H_u(a_u)]$. Subtract its rent and buy the $k$ largest positive net gains for a cap of $k$ equal-sized entries. The same calculation works for realized training observations. Sorting follows from exchanging two entries of equal size. Unequal sizes or paying jointly for output tables changes the combinatorial optimization.

**Sharp finite-precision loss.** Suppose $1/M\le r\le M$, $M>1$, and $J$ is a finite grid containing zero and one. Set $t_w=[1+(M-1)w]/(M+1)$. Among all conditional laws and positive pairs of readouts meeting this bound, the exact worst loss from the best grid fraction in place of the best real fraction is
$$\max_{0\le w\le1}\min_{v\in J} D_{\rm KL}\bigl({\rm Bern}(t_w)\,\|\,{\rm Bern}(t_v)\bigr).$$

To prove it, an endpoint optimizer is represented exactly. At an interior optimizer $w$, the random variable $X=(r-1)/(1+w(r-1))$ has mean zero. Its range is contained in $[-(M-1)/(M-(M-1)w),(M-1)/(1+(M-1)w)]$. The loss of using $v$ is $E[-\log(1+(v-w)X)]$. This convex function lies below the straight line joining its endpoints. At mean zero that line equals the expectation under the endpoint distribution assigning the upper point probability $t_w$. The two factors inside its log are $t_v/t_w$ and $(1-t_v)/(1-t_w)$, giving the displayed divergence. Equality is achievable simultaneously for all $v$. Use two normalized binary forecasts with probabilities of one equal to $1/(M+1)$ and $M/(M+1)$ and a true probability of one equal to $t_w$. This proves the worst-case statement even when the true law is a realizable mixture and there is just one history.

For $G$ uniform intervals the radius is at most $(M-1)^2/(8MG^2)$ nats per target. Negative binary entropy has second derivative $1/[t(1-t)]$, bounded by $(M+1)^2/M$ over the transformed range, and one can round within $1/(2G)$ in $w$. The leading constant is attained asymptotically by the binary construction near the first grid midpoint. The exact finite radius is readily evaluated. For adjacent transformed grid points $l<h$, the divergences cross at $t=\log[(1-l)/(1-h)]/(\log[(1-l)/(1-h)]+\log(h/l))$. Within this cell the nearer endpoint in divergence gives its largest minimum at that crossing. On the fitted text readouts the backoff ratios yield $M=2377/136$, hence a five-level worst-case radius of 0.05260550 nats. This is a uniform guarantee over all admissible pairs and data laws. The observed benefit of the installed fractions is much smaller. [Exact examples and resource comparison](EVIDENCE.md#how-much-extra-prediction-did-fractional-addresses-buy)

A bound independent of $M$ is possible by changing the grid. At any interior optimum the same zero-mean derivative lies in $[-1/(1-w),1/w]$. Its endpoint-chord argument gives $H(w)-H(v)\le D_{\rm KL}({\rm Bern}(w)\,\|\,{\rm Bern}(v))$ for interior $v$. For even $G$, store fractions $\sin^2(\pi j/(2G))$, $j=0,\ldots,G$. Round an interior optimum toward the middle. If $w\le v\le1/2$, then $v-w\le2\sqrt{v(1-v)}(\arcsin\sqrt v-\arcsin\sqrt w)$. The mirrored inequality covers the other half. Bounding binary KL by $(v-w)^2/[v(1-v)]$ gives radius at most $\pi^2/G^2$ with exact endpoints. The cost of representing the selected grid and evaluating its weights is additional. The text experiment used the five uniform, exactly represented fractions.

**What samples identify.** The fraction cannot generally be learned from the binary sign statistic $E\log r$. Take $d_0=(.2,.4,.4)$ and $d_1=(.4,.2,.4)$. True rows $(.15,.05,.80)$ and $(.35,.25,.40)$ give the same $E\log r=.10\log2$. The first chooses readout one, and the second has a strict interior optimum. For independent observations and bounded ratios, statistical estimation need not pay $\log |J|$ for the grid. At fixed $u$ and default $a$, the function $\log[(1+v(r-1))/(1+a(r-1))]$ is monotone in $r$ for every $v$ and has variation at most $2\log M$. Thus a uniform CDF error $\epsilon$ at this address bounds every conditional mean-gain error by $2\epsilon\log M$, by integration against the empirical CDF. Occupancy estimation, selection of exceptions and execution still have costs. This independence argument is separate from the chronological language measurements.

## Learning a new output direction from prediction error

Let $p_x(y)>0$ be the current causal forecast at a complete readout context $x$. A maintainable earlier history type $U=u$ is available to the learner and the eventual decoder. For each occupied type set $\pi_u=P(U=u)$ and $r_{uy}=P(Y=y\mid u)-E[p_X(y)\mid u]$. Each residual row sums to zero. All quantities also make sense under the empirical distribution of ordinary labeled observations paired with the forecasts made before seeing their targets. A correction with output values $b_y$ and history values $h_u$ publishes
$$p'(y\mid x,u)=\frac{p_x(y)e^{h_u b_y}}{\sum_z p_x(z)e^{h_u b_z}}.$$
This changes relative odds between target types and can leave the convex hull of the old readouts. Feature induction in exponential models and functional gradient boosting provide precedents for adding a correction against the present error. The calculation here asks which output direction is identifiable, what a constructive update guarantees and how its normalization can be executed. [Della Pietra, Della Pietra and Lafferty](https://arxiv.org/abs/cmp-lg/9506014) · [Mason et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/1999/hash/96a93ba89a5b5c6c226e49b88973f46e-Abstract.html)

**PROVED.** If $|b_y|\le1$, put $s_u=\sum_y r_{uy}b_y$. The improvement in expected log loss is at least $\sum_u\pi_u(h_us_u-h_u^2/2)$. Along a fixed history row the derivative of the log normalizer at zero is $E_{p_x}b$ and its second derivative at any tilt is the variance of $b$ under that tilted forecast. This variance is at most one. Integrate the second derivative bound and average across contexts. In particular, $h_u=s_u$ gives gain at least $\tfrac12\sum_u\pi_u s_u^2$. If all $r_{uy}=0$, concavity of log likelihood in the entire free table of additive log-odds corrections gives a global maximum at the current forecast. Conversely any nonzero row permits a strictly improving coordinate. This exact test concerns corrections indexed by $u$. Query-specific errors can cancel when averaged within $u$.

There is a constructive choice of signs without supplying the target group. Start $s_u=0$. For each output $y$ in fixed order set $b_y$ to the sign of $\sum_u\pi_u s_u r_{uy}$ (choose $+1$ at zero), then add $b_y r_{uy}$ to every $s_u$. The weighted square of $s$ increases by $\sum_u\pi_u r_{uy}^2+2|\sum_u\pi_u s_u r_{uy}|$. Hence one sweep gives
$$L(p)-L(p')\ \ge\ \tfrac12\sum_{u,y}\pi_u r_{uy}^2.$$
The sweep takes $O(|U||Y|)$ arithmetic on a supplied residual matrix. Acquiring the matrix includes expected counts under the complete causal forecasts. Evaluating a learned sign group requires its total base probability for each query. If $h_u$ is installed as $\tilde h_u$, the certificate is $\tfrac12\sum_u\pi_u[s_u^2-(\tilde h_u-s_u)^2]$ before storage and computation charges. Rounding to a fixed grid covering $[-2,2]$ of spacing $\Delta$ loses at most $\Delta^2/8$ from the certificate. The exact optimum over signs is computationally hard even on strictly positive data. [Reduction and null cases](OBSTRUCTIONS.md#choosing-the-strongest-output-direction-can-encode-maximum-cut)

For a **single raw output** $j$ the indicator $b_y=1_{\{y=j\}}$ has tilted variance at most $1/4$. Taking $h_u=4(r_{uj}-\bar r_j)$, $\bar r_j=\sum_u\pi_u r_{uj}$, guarantees gain of at least $2\operatorname{Var}_u(r_{uj})$ before costs. This follows from $\sum_u\pi_u[h_u r_{uj}-h_u^2/8]$. Maximizing this certificate across raw output IDs is a column scan. The actual paid learner can refit the multipliers $\rho_u$ on a finite grid by ordinary log loss, including a global multiplier and no correction as alternatives. The exact deployed normalizer is $1+(\rho_u-1)p_x(j)$. Only the selected token's base probability and the ordinary observed-token probability need to be evaluated when scoring one outcome. On natural text this procedure selected the token “ of” and learned how to discount or increase its odds for different earlier histories. [Prospective matched-budget test](EVIDENCE.md#learning-which-output-probability-to-change)

**What repeated acquisition can represent.** Suppose there is one deterministic base row per occupied history type, with a finite label alphabet. Apply the certified sign sweep at each round using fresh residuals against the current predictions, exact coefficients and the same fixed target law. Writing $R_j=\sum_u\pi_u\|P(\cdot\mid u)-p_j(\cdot\mid u)\|_2^2$, the loss decrease bounds $\sum_{j\ge0}R_j\le2[L(p_0)-H(Y\mid U)]$. Consequently some iterate in the first $T$ has $R_j\le2[L(p_0)-H(Y\mid U)]/T$, and $R_j\to0$ for the whole sequence. Every occupied probability row converges to its target. With empirical rows this is a training-table result. Each round generally needs its own history coefficients and output group. Fixed quantization, a positive rent, a limited state and new deployment data can change the conclusion.

For strictly positive target and base rows, the exact minimum number of unrestricted real rank-one logit corrections is the rank of the matrix of log ratios $\log[P(y\mid u)/p_0(y\mid u)]$ centered within each row. Each correction adds one outer product after centering, since row normalizers disappear. Conversely a rank factorization of the centered matrix provides that many corrections, whose final normalizations recover the target. This counts real-valued factors given the rows. Requiring sign groups, finite precision, affordable acquisition or causal generation of the history index changes the problem.

## A sharp memory and prediction tradeoff for a delayed stream

**PROVED.** Let $X_{t+d}=X_t\oplus E_t$ for independent Bernoulli$(\eta)$ innovations, $0<\eta<1/2$, with an iid fair initial $d$-block. The stationary law interleaves $d$ independent two-state chains. Score ordinary causal log loss on a single forward stream. There is no uncharged access to past bits. Fixed fit parameters and public randomness are independent of the test trajectory. Write $h$ for binary entropy in nats, $h_2=h/\log2$, $s=1-2\eta$, and $h_2^{-1}$ for the inverse on $[0,1/2]$. At history-dependent memory budget $b\le d$ bits, every predictor, including a stochastic update, has expected per-token loss averaged over each stationary $d$-block at least
$$F_\eta(b/d)=h\!\left(\eta+s\,h_2^{-1}(1-b/d)\right).$$
As $d$ grows with $b/d\to R$, this is the attainable limiting **online** loss. A construction can include its code tables, ring controls, buffers and finite-probability precision within $Rd+o(d)$ bits. Training scratch and per-token work have independent accounts below. Wyner and Ziv's binary entropy inequality supplies the information bound. Its binary information-bottleneck curve was already known. Here the additional requirements are a continuing update that replaces the old representation while forecasting, feasible construction, a causal acquisition rule, and a comparison with all binary-linear state summaries. [Wyner and Ziv](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1973.1055107) · [Asoodeh and Calmon, Lemma 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7712227/)

Let $U$ be the preceding uniform block and $V=U\oplus E$ the next block. At the boundary all retained information is $S$, so $H(U\mid S)\ge(d-b)\log2$. A predictor given $S$ and all subsequently observed bits still pays at least $H(V\mid S)$. To apply the binary entropy inequality conditionally, condition each output further on the corresponding clean prefix $U_{<i}$. Given $S,U_{<i}$, the earlier output noise is independent of $U_i$. Each Bernoulli input entropy $u$ then gives output entropy $f(u)=h(\eta+s h^{-1}(u))$. The function $f$ is increasing and convex. One direct check writes its derivative as $s\,\mathrm{atanh}(sr)/\mathrm{atanh}(r)$, where $r=1-2h^{-1}(u)$. As $r$ increases, the positive series for this ratio gives increasing weight to higher powers with smaller ratios $s^{2j+1}$. Thus the derivative increases with $u$. Jensen on coordinates and contexts gives $H(V\mid S)\ge d f(H(U\mid S)/d)$. The bound concerns block averages. At finite $0<b<d$ equality would require $S$ to be a deterministic uniform partition of $U$ and every supported conditional input bias to have the same nondegenerate magnitude. The latter gives positive probability to every word inside every cell, contradicting the former. Finite experiments should not be equated to the limiting optimum.

**Online attainment.** For $0<R<1$ pick $D>h_2^{-1}(1-R)$ with $D<1/2$. Split the $d$ lanes into groups of $\ell$ and at most $\ell-1$ raw leftovers, using the same boundaries each period. A Hamming ball of radius $\lfloor D\ell\rfloor$ has size $W$ with $\log_2 W/\ell\to h_2(D)$. While words remain uncovered, some center covers at least a $W/2^\ell$ fraction of them by averaging over all centers. After at most $\lceil(2^\ell/W)(1+\ell\log2)\rceil$ centers the cube is covered. Encode each observed group by its center and keep the codes in a ring. Forecast its next group from the old center, buffer arriving bits, then overwrite that slot. The expected mismatch with the noisy next block is at most $p=\eta+sD$ on average. Announcing error probability $p<1/2$ costs at most $h(p)$ by linearity of cross entropy in the actual error rate. Raw leftover lanes cost $o(d)$. Choosing $\ell=\lfloor\log_2\log_2 d\rfloor$ eventually makes both directions of lookup and the code construction affordable: tables cost $O(2^\ell\ell)$ bits, construction can use $O(\ell 2^{3\ell})$ bit operations, and buffers and ring addresses need $O(\ell+\log d)$ bits. Deployment uses one code lookup per group in each direction, besides reading and predicting its bits. Addresses, bandwidth and hardware latency still have physical cost. Quantizing $p$ to vanishing error costs $o(d)$ further bits. The payload rate approaches at most $1-h_2(D)<R$, absorbing all this overhead. Let $D$ decrease to the stated inverse. At $R=0$ predict fair; at $R=1$ the limit follows from the raw shift or the interior limit. A cold start pays for its initial observations separately.

**When formation pays.** If a unit of stored bits per delay lane costs $\lambda$ nats per output and other costs are amortized, minimize $F_\eta(R)+\lambda R$. The slope at $R=0$ is $-(\log2)s^2$, and it approaches zero at $R=1$. Strict convexity gives zero optimal rate for $\lambda\ge(\log2)s^2$ and a positive interior rate for $0<\lambda<(\log2)s^2$. This threshold is specific to the source and price.

The best binary-linear sketches have a different frontier. If $A$ is a rank-$r$ binary matrix, $I(AU;V)=r\log2-H(AE)$ because $V$ is uniform and independent of $E$. Conditioning on all nonpivot noise bits leaves an invertible transform of $r$ independent noise bits. Thus $H(AE)\ge r h(\eta)$. Even with an arbitrary decoder and the full observed prefix of the future block, such a sketch pays at least $\log2-(r/d)(\log2-h(\eta))$ per token. Storing raw coordinates attains this chord at the block interface. An affine offset has the same information. A fixed linear sketch of earlier stationary blocks can be reduced to a linear sketch of $U$ plus independent backward noise, since each fair bit chain is reversible. The restriction is binary linearity of the *stored function*, not of the output readout or the name of the algorithm.

There is a finite, physically executable separation. For each $\ell=2^m-1$ raw bits send the index of the nearest Hamming codeword, using $\ell-m$ bits. Compute the syndrome, flip its indicated bit if it is nonzero, and retain the systematic part. Radius-one balls partition the cube. Under the uniform source, the correction is uniformly distributed over zero and the $\ell$ singleton errors, independently of the codeword. Each prototype bit differs from its raw bit with probability $2^{-m}$. Its noisy prediction error is therefore $\eta+s2^{-m}$. The code is linear, but nearest-codeword storage is nonlinear. At $m=6,\eta=1/8,\lambda=0.30$, the frozen construction's population score including its declared controls is at most 0.671581220. Even a zero-overhead binary-linear competitor's best population score is at least $h(1/8)+0.30=0.676770161$. Charging additional native code and CPU time can reverse this comparison. ### When ordinary prediction constructs a perfect code

**PROVED for a common-confidence binary readout.** Let $n=2^m-1$, $m\ge2$, and let a uniformly random clean group $U$ be followed by $V=U\oplus Z$ with independent Bernoulli$(\eta)$ noise, $0<\eta<1/2$. The predictor publishes a binary guess and the same probability of correctness $q>1/2$ at each coordinate. Minimizing ordinary log loss is then equivalent to minimizing mismatch count. Start with a systematic $m\times n$ check matrix $H=[I_m\mid0]$. For a group with syndrome $HU$, flip a minimum-weight vector with that syndrome and retain the $n-m$ nonpivot bits of the resulting kernel word. The predicted group is the recovered kernel word. A deterministic shortest-path tree in the syndrome graph implements this for any setting of the free columns. The learner receives this representation and nearest-word rule. Only the column values depend on the fitted data.

Population coordinate descent, assigning one previously zero free column at a time, **must produce a perfect code**. Let $S$ be the set of nonzero column values and $\ell_S(a)$ the fewest values of $S$ whose sum is $a$. The identity pivots make every syndrome reachable. Uniform $U$ gives each syndrome mass $2^{-m}$ and mean noisy mistakes per group
$$n\eta+(1-2\eta)2^{-m}\sum_a\ell_S(a).$$
A repeated or zero value leaves this sum fixed. A missing value reduces its own distance from at least two to one and cannot increase any distance. Every population minimizer thus takes a missing value. After $n-m$ steps the columns take every nonzero value exactly once. Every word is within distance one of a unique kernel word. This is the classical Hamming code up to coordinate order. The proof concerns its acquisition by prediction and the cost of the supplied parameterization. [Hamming's construction and packing argument, Sections 3 and 8](https://zoo.cs.yale.edu/classes/cs323/doc/Hamming.pdf)

**Optimality even with causal feedback.** Give an arbitrary encoder $M$ possible messages about the group. After receiving the message, an arbitrary decoder may also observe the earlier *noisy* targets before issuing its next guess. Put $V_n(j)=\sum_{r=0}^j\binom nr$. Expected mistakes relative to the *clean* group are at least
$$\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left[1-M V_n(j)/2^n\right]_+.$$
Fix private and shared independent random coins and one entire noise word. From a fixed message the map from a clean word to its sequence of clean prediction mistakes is bijective. The first mistake and first guess determine the first clean bit. This reconstructs the received bit, and iteration reconstructs the whole word. Hence exactly $V_n(j)$ words can give at most $j$ clean mistakes from that message. Take the union over messages and sum tail probabilities. Fresh crossover noise is independent of each causal guess, converting a mean of $D$ clean mistakes into $n\eta+(1-2\eta)D$ noisy mistakes. Random or history-generated messages independent of future noise conditional on $U$ can be represented by an encoder with independent coins. [Lean proof of the length-preserving bijection](CausalState.lean)

At $M=2^n/(n+1)$ the bound is $n/(n+1)$ clean mistakes per group, attained by the learned perfect code. Equality also characterizes the deterministic optimal predictions. Every encoded input must have at most one clean mistake under every possible received word, because the noise has full support. Fix any received word $y$. For each message, all inputs in its cell belong to the radius-one ball about the complete vector of guesses along $y$. There are $M$ cells of size at most $n+1$ covering the whole cube, so each cell is an entire ball. Its center is unique for $n\ge2$. The guesses in that cell therefore give the same center for every received word. Equality for a randomized scheme requires equality for almost every fixed coin realization. This certifies the hard forecasts under one common confidence. Allowing the reported probability itself to vary with the observed prefix changes the log-loss optimization problem.

**Acquisition from a reused training panel.** For $G$ independent group pairs, subtract the raw predictor's errors on each example. A fixed matrix with deterministic tie breaking changes at most $m$ forecast bits, so the per-group residual lies in $[-m,m]$. There are at most $2^{m(n-m)}$ entire systematic matrices. Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound give probability at most $\delta$ of any wrong greedy step when
$$G>\frac{8m^2\,2^{2m}}{(1-2\eta)^2}\left[m(n-m)\log2+\log(2/\delta)\right].$$
Indeed simultaneous errors of less than half the missing-column margin $(1-2\eta)/2^m$ preserve strict preference against every repeat, regardless of which earlier choices were made on those same data. For $m=6,\eta=1/8,\delta=0.05$ this conservative sufficient threshold exceeds 504 million groups. The actual registered run reached the optimum with 262,144 groups. Once the distribution and sign of the crossover are supplied, a conventional designer can fill all missing values without training any individual column. The learned arrangement has the same population error as that designer. [Registered comparison and physical measurements](EVIDENCE.md#learning-the-packed-update-from-next-bit-errors)

### Why an optimal stored computation need not be identifiable

The stationary delayed law is invariant under complementing any fixed subset of its $d$ lanes in every period. For a deterministic boundary encoder $f(U)$, require that each translation of $\mathbb F_2^d$ permute its unlabeled cells. Then $f(u)=f(v)$ iff $f(0)=f(u+v)$, by translating both arguments by $u$. Its zero cell is closed under addition and contains zero, so it is a subspace and every other cell is a coset. Decoding from an arbitrary renamed cell index consequently has exactly the binary-linear-sketch lower bound. If a candidate family and its price respect the translations, a unique optimum partition would have to respect them as well. An optimum beating the chord at its effective payload must therefore have distinct, equally good translated partitions. This falsifies a demand for a unique latent computation recovered from population prediction loss in this symmetric setting. A learner can select a representative by deterministic tie breaking, finite training data or a priced random frame. The statement concerns deterministic functions of the last block and assumes translation-invariant prices; older history can itself act as a frame. Lean checked the two algebraic identities on Boolean words of arbitrary coordinate type. [Checked coset and closure statements](CausalState.lean)

### What ordinary data identifies

With unknown lag in $(M/2,M]$, independent stationary length-$M+1$ windows expose ordinary raw tokens. In the registered last-token fit each wrong antecedent is fair and independent of the true antecedent and label noise. Against any one wrong lag the per-window match advantage is $+1$ with probability $(1-\eta)/2$, $-1$ with probability $\eta/2$, and zero otherwise. A Chernoff comparison gives failure probability at most $(\lceil M/2\rceil-1)(1/2+\sqrt{\eta(1-\eta)})^n$ for $n$ windows. At the registered values this is below $3.15\cdot10^{-16}$. This procedure inspects $O(nM)$ raw inputs and candidate matches. The source grammar and compression algorithm are supplied. Ordinary data identifies the lag, calibrates the decoder and selects a deployment under the price.

A stronger likelihood comparison uses the **entire** window. Put $L=M+1$, $t=[(\sqrt{1+s}+\sqrt{1-s})/2]^n$. At lag $k$, the $L-k$ related pairs form a matching. For distinct candidate lags the union of their matchings is a forest. All edges run from the left half to the right; an alternating two-edge walk translates by the nonzero difference of lags so cannot close. Under a uniform reference word, edge XORs on a forest are independent fair bits. The likelihood ratio for a matching is the product of factors $1+s\sigma_i\sigma_{i+k}$ in sign notation. Hence the $n$-window Bhattacharyya affinity of two candidates is $t^{(L-k)+(L-j)}$. A likelihood union bound gives worst-case error at most $t^3/(1-t)$, independent of the size of the lag range. The two hardest adjacent large lags have affinity $t^3$, giving equal-prior error at least $t^6/4$ by the standard total-variation/affinity bound. These bounds assume known noise and at least two candidates. Reading $nL$ symbols still costs $nL$ symbol accesses on a full-window replay. A naive computation of all autocorrelations costs $O(nM^2)$, or $O(nM\log M)$ arithmetic via convolution with adequate precision. ## A native linear update learned from scalar labels

Let $A$ be a finite input alphabet. Each episode resets, reveals a word $w$, obtains a probability forecast, and finally reveals $Y\in\{0,1\}$. Suppose

$$P(Y=1\mid w)=(1+\rho f(w))/2,\qquad f:A^*\to\{-1,1\},\quad 0<\rho\le1.$$

The hidden function, noise level and transitions are unknown. Conditional labels in the sampling theorem are independent. Use Brier loss $(Y-p)^2$. For $Z=2Y-1$ the squared loss is $(Z-\rho f)^2/4$. At fixed positive amplitude each word's empirical minimizer is the sign of its accumulated $Z$. With those signs fixed, the minimizer in $[0,1]$ is $\hat\rho=\sum_w|\sum Z_w|/\sum_w n_w$. This homogeneous two-level assumption makes exact recovery from noisy labels possible. Continuous conditional probabilities need a different stability argument.

Write $g_s(u)=f(us)$ for the forecast sign after a continuation $s$, and $H_{u,s}=g_s(u)$. Let $r$ be the real rank of this Hankel matrix. The classical finite-rank realization theorem relates this rank to minimal real linear state dimension. Spectral learning already recovers transition operators from empirical Hankel blocks under specified coverage and conditioning. The deductions here concern the signed observer, a bounded integer execution, the exact competing copy width, and its passive acquisition cost. [Carlyle and Paz](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-0000(71)80005-3) · [Balle and Mohri](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/hash/700fdb2ba62d4554dc268c65add4b16e-Abstract.html)

**PROVED — constructive recovery.** Declare a capacity ceiling $R\ge1$, chosen before the data. Set $U=A^{\le R-1}$ and $W=A^{\le 2R-1}$, including the empty word. At a predeclared checkpoint, use only previously revealed observations. Maintain two integer counts per observed word in $W$. Wait on an absent cell or tied sign. Form the short Hankel block on $U\times U$. Row elimination in length then alphabet order supplies independent rows $P$ and pivot columns $S$, giving a nonsingular $B=H_{P,S}$. Refuse a rank above $R$. The first pivot column is the empty suffix since its entries are signs. Define

$$x(u)=H_{u,S},\qquad B_a=H_{Pa,S},\qquad T_a=B^{-1}B_a.$$

For each letter compile each column of $T_a$ into coprime integer coefficients with a positive common scale. At inference initialize at $x(\epsilon)$, accumulate signed integer coefficients from the previous bits, and take their signs simultaneously. The readout is coordinate zero. Use the empirical amplitude for the final probability. A one-term column is one signed read. For an ambiguous or inconsistent empirical table the learner can keep its incumbent. An explicit short-word replay checks a constructed candidate before using it.

To prove recovery, consider the span of Hankel rows of length at most $j$. If it ceases growing, appending any letter maps each spanning row back into this span: on a row as a function of $s$, the corresponding linear operation sends $h(s)$ to $h(as)$. It is then closed at every later depth. Starting with a nonzero empty row, the whole row space is spanned by depth $r-1$. The same argument on columns prepends letters. The finite block therefore has rank $r$ when $r\le R$. Each full row expressed in basis rows $P$ has coefficients $x(u)B^{-1}$. Applying the shift to those rows proves $x(ua)=x(u)T_a$. Induction proves correct signs for **every word length**, even when the training words were short. Conversely any $k$-dimensional homogeneous linear realization factors $H$ into prefix and suffix matrices of inner dimension $k$, hence $k\ge r$. The guarantee is conditional on the true rank bound and correct observed signs.

Every coordinate is a prediction about a possible continuation. Its meaning is learned from outcomes even when that continuation is never executed at inference. Cramer's rule bounds every numerator and denominator before cancellation by $D=\lfloor r^{r/2}\rfloor$ by Hadamard's inequality on sign matrices. With column-wise positive denominators, a reachable dot product is exactly the denominator times the next sign. Integer threshold execution is exact with accumulator magnitude at most $rD$; the state stays at $r$ bits at every step. Multiple coordinates can read the same old bit. A two-buffer implementation needs $2r$ state bits during a transition. No growing rational hidden state is required for this observer.

**Statistical cost.** If each word has at least $k$ visits at a checkpoint determined independently of the labels, Hoeffding's one-sided bound and a union bound give probability at most $|W|\exp(-k\rho^2/2)$ of any wrong or tied sign. For $n$ iid word draws with probabilities $d_w$, add $\sum_{w\in W}P(\mathrm{Bin}(n,d_w)<k)$ for coverage. With the correct signs, fresh excess Brier loss is $(\hat\rho-\rho)^2/4$. On that event $\hat\rho$ is the ordinary sample mean of $Zf(w)$ over retained examples. A two-sided error bound at threshold $t$ adds $2\exp(-nt^2/2)$ when all $n$ examples are retained. A positive lower bound on $\rho$ is only needed to set a prospective sample budget.

## How much computation do the learned bits require?

Let $N$ be the number of distinct full Hankel rows and let $C$ be the number of distinct columns up to a global sign. For finite signed rank, the basis observations distinguish all rows. Evaluating columns on $r$ independent rows distinguishes all columns. Hence $N\le2^r$ and $r\le C\le2^{r-1}$.

**PROVED — exact signed-copy width.** A signed-copy machine of width $m$ can read only one of its $m$ current bits, up to sign, after any fixed suffix. Consequently $C\le m$. Conversely store one $\pm1$ value per projective column class $[g_s]$. After letter $a$ its new value is $g_s(ua)=g_{as}(u)$, represented by one old bit and a fixed sign. Initialize each bit at its empty-prefix value. This constructs a width-$C$ machine. Equality concerns parallel read-and-sign instructions and a single-bit readout. A deterministic indexed transition table may use a different encoding.

**PROVED — sharp density boundary.** If $N>3\cdot2^r/4$, every column of every $T_a$ in any basis of suffix bits is a signed coordinate. Indeed, any real linear form that is binary on this many vertices cannot have two nonzero coefficients $b_i,b_j$. Condition on the other $r-2$ cube bits. For $L=t+b_i x_i+b_j x_j$, the alternating sum of $L^2$ over the four corners is $8b_i b_j$. If every corner had $L^2=1$ the sum would vanish. At most three corners work for each conditioning. A one-term form taking sign values must have coefficient $\pm1$. The same argument applies to every suffix readout. Therefore $C=r$ in this regime. The proof concerns the count of distinct reachable sign states, independently of their sampling frequencies.

At equality take the six cube vertices excluding $(1,1,1)$ and $(-1,-1,-1)$. Start at $(1,1,-1)$ and output the first coordinate. Three letters act by cyclic rotation, global negation and $(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,y,-x-y-z)$. With $x=y$ the last operation fixes the third coordinate, and with $x=-y$ it flips it. Rotation and negation reach all six vertices. Suffix readouts include $x,y,z$ and $x+y+z$ up to sign. The latter is exchanged with $z$ up to sign by the third letter, and the four lines are closed under all three operations. They are distinct on the six vertices, which span three dimensions (the three rotations of the initial vector have nonzero determinant). Hence $N=6,r=3,C=4$. A width-three exact signed-copy program with a signed-copy readout is impossible **in any encoding**. A learned three-bit linear update can execute it. Width four trades the addition for a cached extra value. The input fixes the letter, while the third transformation changes meaning according to distinctions already formed by the learner.

This derives a limited attention-like regime: under an explicit state-density condition the learned value update is hard addressing with optional polarity. It gives no dot-product, softmax or general optimality theorem for attention. Fields of continuous-valued coordinates permit other linear maps. The classical Boolean Fourier rigidity theorem concerns approximation under a specified cube measure; the four-corner argument above is an exact support statement. [Friedgut, Kalai and Naor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03246)

## Pricing the change

A checkpoint uses previously observed counts to fit a challenger, with a hard capacity $R$. Freeze it on a subsequent validation block. For $n_v$ events let $G$ be the sum of the fair predictor's squared losses minus the candidate's, and let $J$ count stored-bit taps on those words. For a declared deployment horizon $h$, price $\pi$ per tap and fixed remaining charge $c$, publish the candidate iff $h(G-\pi J)>n_v c$. Otherwise use the fair forecast. This is an empirical decision on a specified resource axis. A realized fit always costs training work even when declined. Costs common to the two deployments cancel in the comparison; the remaining unequal costs can be included in $c$ or charged separately. Repeat the count, fit and validation updates at declared checkpoints for continued learning. The fair initialization, exact update and tie convention require no latent-state labels, target graph or supplied sign products.

For independent validation words and labels with deterministic per-word tap bound $J_{\max}$, each per-example net gain lies in $[-3/4-\pi J_{\max},1/4]$. Conditional on a frozen candidate, the probability of an empirical net gain exceeding its expectation by $t>0$ is at most $\exp[-2n_vt^2/(1+\pi J_{\max})^2]$. This fixed-block statement cannot certify a sequence of adaptive reuses of one validation set.

## Acquisition without visiting every short word

**PROVED — rank one.** In a nonzero signed rank-one realization the empty readout can serve as the sole state bit. Each symbol acts by multiplication by an unknown sign. Write the sign bits as $\theta\in\mathbb F_2^{|A|+1}$ including the unknown initial sign. Any raw word gives a public design vector $v(w)=(1,(\#_a(w)\bmod2)_{a\in A})$. Its terminal output sign is $(-1)^{v(w)\cdot\theta}$. These features follow from the rank-one restriction. Supplying them to an unrestricted learner would be a substantive inductive bias.

Start with no equations. After labels arrive, group input words by their observed mask; noiseless signs give linear equations directly. Under independent common flip noise, a fixed minimum number of visits per retained mask and its strict majority supply an estimated equation. Insert by binary elimination, skipping ties and reporting inconsistent rows. Maintain a calibration count over the retained cells. For exact recovered signs, a test sign is determined **iff** $v(w)$ belongs to the observed row span. In the span, elimination gives the sign; outside it, there is a null vector $h$ with $v(w)\cdot h=1$, so the two parameters $\theta,\theta+h$ give identical evidence and opposite predictions. With a uniform prior and noiseless equations this pairing gives the exact fair forecast off the span. At known $\rho$ the exact excess Brier against a clairvoyant sign predictor is $\rho^2 P(v(w)\notin\mathrm{span})/4$. Majority reduction with noisy data is a frequentist recovery procedure; the uniform posterior formula applies to the noiseless observation experiment.

A spanning set of at most $|A|+1$ reliable masks suffices for all words. For $b$ prespecified distinct masks with $k$ independent labels each, the preceding bound $b\exp(-k\rho^2/2)$ applies. Counting one word toggles one bit per symbol. Elimination stores at most $(|A|+1)(|A|+2)$ bits of rows including right sides, apart from indexing. Each inserted or queried row takes at most $|A|+1$ row XORs of that width. Training also pays for two integer counts and a mask for each retained input class. A fully identified deployment stores $|A|+1$ sign bits and calibration. A partially identified deployment needs the span/mask calculation to know when to abstain. General noisy parity with sparsely repeated, high-dimensional designs poses a different estimation problem. [Blum, Kalai and Wasserman](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/noise-tolerant-learning-parity-problem-statistical-query-model/)

In particular, every two-symbol word supplies an even design. Using all ordered pairs on $b\ge2$ symbols gives a space of dimension $b$: a repeated symbol supplies the intercept and distinct pairs generate the even-parity subspace. A single observed odd design raises this to $b+1$. Even an unlimited sample from the even-only law cannot fix the action on odd words. This is a limit on extrapolation with a fully usable trained computation on the observed support.

## A genuinely different optimizer

For an independently sampled **pair** of categorical raw inputs, fit a signed scalar outcome by a dot product of learned lookup rows. Neither lookup contains a supplied interaction. Assume equal observed counts in every cell of an $m\times n$ Cartesian design. Let $M$ be the matrix of empirical signed means. Start with $U=aI_m$, $a>0$, and $V=0_{n\times m}$. After each complete balanced training block, an ordinary squared-error objective with a quadratic parameter price is

$$J=\tfrac12\|M-UV^\top\|_F^2+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(\|U\|_F^2+\|V\|_F^2),\qquad\lambda>0.$$

The empirical squared loss on individual outcomes differs only by scaling and a constant in this design. Use a specified number of alternating ridge updates. First solve $V=M^\top U(U^\top U+\lambda I)^{-1}$, then $U=MV(V^\top V+\lambda I)^{-1}$ using ordinary row elimination. Scalar regression scores may be clipped to $[-1,1]$ when published as probabilities. The update minimizes the displayed unprojected regression objective. One-sided identity initialization is independent of the labels. Both-zero initialization is exactly stationary.

**PROVED — reachable limiting shrinkage for this initialization.** In the singular bases of $M$, rotate the arbitrary hidden coordinates by its left singular vectors. The isotropic seed and both updates then separate into modes. In a mode of singular value $s$ the updates are $v=su/(u^2+\lambda)$ and $u'=sv/(v^2+\lambda)$. Zero singular directions contribute zero. All other modes remain positive. Each block is $\lambda$-strongly convex, so each sweep decreases the objective by at least $\lambda/2$ times the sum of the two squared increments. Coercivity bounds the iterates and the increments tend to zero. Every cluster point is stationary. Subtracting the stationary equations after multiplying respectively by $u,v$ gives $u^2=v^2$. The only nonnegative stationary points are zero and, for $s>\lambda$, $u=v=\sqrt{s-\lambda}$. A bounded sequence with increments tending to zero and only finitely many cluster points converges: traveling repeatedly between disjoint small neighborhoods would create another cluster point outside them. At $s>\lambda$ the derivative of the full scalar sweep at zero is $(s/\lambda)^2>1$, so a positive orbit cannot converge to zero. The fitted singular value therefore tends to $(s-\lambda)_+$. Equal singular values leave basis orientation unidentified. The proof concerns this alternating update; deep linear gradient-flow results use other updates and initialization hypotheses. [Saxe, McClelland and Ganguli](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6120)

The factor learner can fit continuously varying conditional values. Its pair architecture imposes a fixed cut, and finite nonzero small modes usually still incur computation. Norm shrinkage is a price on parameters, not a measurement of bytes or execution time. It earns a physical width reduction only after paying to discover and execute a smaller factorization. The signed sequence learner has exact finite gates but pays for evidence about continuations. Neither implication extends to general sequences by taking a limit.


## Ordinary next tokens on one stream

The terminal-word experiment can be removed. Declare an alphabet of size $K$, a finite context ceiling $L\ge1$ and the initial raw context. At time $t$, forecast $X_t$ using only $c=X_{t-L:t-1}$ and previously revealed symbols. Then update the count of $(c,X_t)$ and shift the raw context. At a training checkpoint a finite table gives a causal forecast for each context. The following construction learns a smaller recurrent realization from that table. A full conventional context table remains available on identical training information.

**PROVED — continuous conditional values on a finite physical grid.** Let $Q\ge K$ be an integer mass budget. In each context fit $k_y\ge1$, $\sum_y k_y=Q$. The empirical multiclass Brier objective is minimized by the Euclidean-nearest such probability vector to the empirical frequencies. Start each $k_y$ at one. At each of the remaining $Q-K$ increments select the smallest $(2k_y+1)n-2Qn_y$, breaking ties by symbol order. These are the marginal increases of $n\sum k_y^2-2Q\sum k_y n_y$; within each coordinate they increase. Selecting the smallest available marginals gives the global optimum by exchange. For an unseen cell any allocation minimizes the empty objective; use the closest feasible vector to uniform. A binary cell has the equivalent nearest-integer formula with endpoint clamping.

Let $v_y(c)=k_y(c)$ for the fitted table and let $D_a v(c)=v(\operatorname{shift}(c,a))$. Start with the head columns and repeatedly apply each $D_a$ to each newly independent column. Retain it if ordinary elimination yields a nonzero residual. Once the queue is empty its span is invariant under every shift, so by induction it contains every head after every continuation. Each retained coordinate is itself a fitted prediction at an actual continuation. With $r$ independent columns choose $r$ independent context rows; solve on that square submatrix for every shifted column and every output head. These coefficients reproduce the columns on all contexts. Conversely every homogeneous linear state producing these heads after all continuations must span them. Thus $r$ is the minimum homogeneous linear width for this **fitted** observable, and the learner attains it after finite, paid work. Different coordinate scalings, redundant label coordinates and allowing affine bias change the width convention. A constant can be included as a coordinate.

Each integer minor used in Cramer's rule is bounded by $H=r^{r/2}Q^r$ in magnitude. A transition uses integer coefficients, sums at most $r$ products of magnitude at most $QH$, and divides by its positive denominator. The division is exact on reachable states. The result always lies in $[1,Q]$. The same holds for the heads before the final normalization by $Q$. This gives bounded execution over arbitrarily long continuations without accumulating rounding error. Parameters, output writes, acquisition and the $K^L$ possible raw contexts are separate costs. An arbitrary fitted table can make acquisition impractical.

For stationary order-at-most-$L$ conditionals, suppose every symbol has conditional probability at least $\epsilon>0$. Collect the first $k$ next tokens at each context. One can generate the stream with independent stacks of draws for the contexts, consuming an entry at each visit. The first $k$ entries of each stack have the specified categorical law. This argument avoids conditioning a binomial estimate on its trajectory-dependent final visit count. For $m$ disjoint length-$L$ blocks, each specified context occurs at the end of a block with conditional probability at least $\epsilon^L$ given preceding blocks. Coupling the block indicator by a uniform draw to a Bernoulli$(\epsilon^L)$ trial gives the lower binomial domination, allowing dependence in subsequent blocks. By time $mL+1$ the probability that any context still lacks $k$ labels is at most $K^L P(\mathrm{Bin}(m,\epsilon^L)<k)$.

With probability at least $1-2K^{L+1}\exp(-2kt^2)$ all completed empirical conditional coordinates differ from their true values by at most $t$ (or add the preceding coverage failure at a fixed time). For any simplex point $p$, round $1+(Q-K)p_y$ to integers by floor and largest remainders. Its distance after rescaling from $p$ is at most $e_Q=(\sqrt{K(K-1)}+\sqrt K)/Q$: the shift vector is $(\mathbf1-Kp)/Q$, maximized in norm at a vertex, and rounding changes each mass by less than one. The fitted grid optimum is at least as close to its empirical table. Hence its conditional excess multiclass Brier is at most $(e_Q+\sqrt K\,t)^2$ uniformly. The normalized log-loss excess is at most $Q$ times this quantity by $\log x\le x-1$ and $q_y\ge1/Q$. Neither error bound assumes recovery of a particular basis or equality of empirical and true ranks.

For the earlier binary homogeneous observer, fit each context's strict majority and a pooled amplitude. Use the same closure with sign columns and the signed integer executor above. The failure probability for the true signs after $k$ visits per context is at most $2^L\exp(-k\rho^2/2)$. Because every next symbol has positive probability, equality of future single-token forecasts after every possible suffix is equivalent to equality of the full future word laws. Multiply successive conditionals in one direction; in the other, divide positive prefix probabilities. In this finite-context realization the learned maps update after each **actual scored token**. Predictive-state and observable-operator models also use future predictions as state. Their general stochastic realizations and spectral HMM learning theorems have different hypotheses. Here the input shift is observed, the output grid is paid, and elimination preserves an empirically fitted conditional table exactly. ## When does the learner materialize an extra value?

A prediction basis requires three stored bits in the six-state example. A fourth could cache a sum that would otherwise be recomputed. Its maintenance creates new dependencies on later steps. We can derive the choice from the same learned future predictions and an observed workload.

Fix the fitted sign law and a mandatory independent suffix basis $B$. Compute its finite orbit of suffix columns modulo sign, $\mathcal C$, by starting from the basis and prepending every letter to each new column. Signs of a column on independent rows identify its orientation. A direct implementation may enumerate the $N$ reachable state rows and compare their columns there. Each letter $a$ gives a learned demand $i=d(a,j)$ for each next register $j$, together with its polarity. An absent $i$ can always be synthesized from $B$ using its exact learned integer form. These columns and dependencies are calculated from the fitted predictions.

Declare a machine with simultaneous updates and a transient scratch value shared by all consumers of the same missing column on one letter. Let $z_j$ indicate a resident register, with $z_j=1$ on $B$. Let $u_j\ge0$ price retaining, initializing and transferring that register for the chosen horizon. Let $s_i\ge0$ price a single transient synthesis, and $n_a$ be recorded or expected letter counts for that horizon. All resident designs give exactly the same forecasts. Their variable physical cost is

$$E(z)=\sum_j u_jz_j+\sum_{a,i} n_a s_i(1-z_i)\mathbf1\{\exists j:\ z_j=1,\ d(a,j)=i\}.$$

**PROVED — joint selection, sharing and removal.** An ordinary minimum cut gives the global minimum of this cost, including updating the newly materialized values. Make one node for each column and a directed arc of capacity $u_j$ to the sink. Force the basis to the source side. For each letter and demanded input make an auxiliary node $h_{ai}$. Add a forcing arc $j\to h_{ai}$ for each consumer and a finite arc $h_{ai}\to i$ of capacity $n_as_i$. A forcing capacity of one plus the total finite capacities suffices for integer prices. For any choice of source-side columns, place an auxiliary on the source side iff a selected consumer needs it. The resulting cut costs exactly $E$. In the other direction any minimum cut respects the forcing arcs, so its cost is at least $E$ for its selected columns. This proves equality and handles simultaneous users of the same scratch computation.

The implementation augments residual flow along shortest paths by breadth-first search until the sink is unreachable. Each augmentation preserves flow feasibility and conservation. At termination residual reachability gives a cut whose forward arcs are saturated and whose backward arcs carry no flow, so its value equals the feasible flow. Every cut bounds every feasible flow, proving optimality. The reachable side is contained in every minimum source side and breaks ties by omitting optional registers. There are $O(dC)$ vertices and arcs for $C=|\mathcal C|$ and $d=|A|$, and a shortest-augmenting-path implementation has $O(VE^2)$ arithmetic work. The orbit can itself be exponentially large in $r$. This specializes established graph-cut methods to dependencies created by a prediction fit. [Kolmogorov and Zabih](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~rdz/Papers/KZ-PAMI04.pdf) · [Edmonds and Karp](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~pettie/matching/Edmonds-Karp-network-flow.pdf)

Use three units per destination bit for an addressed bit read, polarity operation and write. In this abstract machine a three-input signed form costs seven extra units for three coefficient-weighted bit reads, two additions, sign testing and one scratch write. On the six-state law only letter $v$ asks the three-bit basis for its missing fourth column. Let $H=\sum n_a$. The two costs at zero rental are $9H+7n_v$ and $12H$. The learned design adds the fourth bit iff $7n_v>3H$. A subsequent workload can reverse this decision. A positive fixed charge for initially creating the bit raises the threshold by that charge. Both designs have the same inferential loss and the same acquired mathematical law. Each predicted bit has a definition, and ordinary observations determine both its meaning and, via the empirical workload, whether to retain its result.

A fixed mandatory basis, synthesis from that basis, one-step scratch lifetime, simultaneous update and additive resource prices are essential hypotheses. A hard bound on peak bytes, alternative arithmetic factorizations or incidental sharing between different synthesized expressions define a different optimization problem.


**PROVED — basis dependence can reverse materialization.** For a finite orbit, enumerate independent $r$-subsets containing the published head, run the preceding cut for each and minimize over the returned costs. This is an explicit finite optimizer over such bases, with up to $\binom{C-1}{r-1}$ cuts. On the six-state law the four lines $x,y,z,s=x+y+z$ have every triple independent. The letter $q$ cycles $x,y,z$ and fixes $s$. The letter $v$ exchanges $z,s$ up to sign and fixes $x,y$. The letter $m$ fixes every line up to sign. Omitting $s$, $y$ or $z$ therefore requires one three-input synthesis respectively on $v$, $q$, or both. Once the head $x$ is held resident, these exhaust the three-register choices. Any synthesis of the missing line in that basis requires all three coefficients by independence of every triple.

The minimum cost over both bases and caches is thus $\min(12H,9H+7\min(n_q,n_v))$ at zero rental. For $n=(2,1,4)$ the fixed basis would add a fourth register while the best basis keeps three. At $(48,4,48)$ even the best three-register basis loses and the fourth value is materialized. At $(48,48,4)$ the three-register design wins again. These are identifiable changes in computational organization within the specified machine interface. A lookup-table state machine can still implement all the same forecasts.


**PROVED — a certificate before full discovery.** The cost of the complete-orbit optimizer can be avoided on some workloads. Start with just the mandatory basis columns $B$ and their fitted short moment blocks. If the signature of a prospective column on the independent rows is $b_i\in\{\pm1\}^r$, its expansion is $B_{\rm mat}^{-1}b_i$ where $B_{\rm mat}$ is the square observation matrix. Prepending $a$ has signature $B_aB_{\rm mat}^{-1}b_i$. Normalize its first sign to identify it up to polarity. This computes a successor on demand using $r$ observed probe rows, without traversing all reachable states. It assumes valid signed moments for the fitted law.

Expand the outgoing demands of a set $K$ of known columns containing the basis. Its immediate boundary $D$ consists of revealed successor columns outside $K$. The **upper** cut permits only $K$ to be resident. It charges each demanded missing input once and synthesizes it from the known basis, including inputs on $D$. It produces an executable feasible program for every letter. The **lower** cut permits $K\cup D$ to be resident and charges their full per-register fees, while dropping dependencies *issued by* $D$ and by unrevealed columns. For any full solution, restricting its selection to $K\cup D$ preserves all known requests and can only remove nonnegative costs. Thus the lower optimum is at most the global optimum. Expanding $K$ cannot decrease the lower optimum by the same restriction argument; the previous upper solution stays feasible so the upper optimum cannot increase.

If the bounds differ, the minimizing lower layout must contain a boundary column. Otherwise it already pays the exact feasible cost. Expand one such column, breaking ties by discovery order, and repeat. Each positive-gap step reveals a new member of the finite orbit. Equality certifies exact global optimality for this basis. If a further expansion costs at least the entire gap in the same horizon and currency, even perfect subsequent optimization cannot repay that first charge. This early exit still returns the executable upper layout and its numerical gap. Discovery work already paid is additional to both deployment bounds.

A simple family shows how sharp the distinction can be. Use the three-letter full-transformation-monoid construction with $r\ge3$ states, a head positive at one state only, and the $r$ singleton sign functions as basis. Their sign matrix $2I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top$ is nonsingular for $r\ne2$. The cycle and adjacent swap permute the basis; the single collapse exposes at most a constant and one doubleton as new projective columns. If the synthesis savings from every observed collapse request for either boundary column are smaller than its unavoidable full register fee, the lower cut excludes both. The upper and lower cuts then coincide after expanding only $r$ columns, although the complete orbit has $2^{r-1}$. A full future workload with different letter frequencies can invalidate that optimality decision. The forecast calculation remains exact.


## Exact finite grids and the continuous limit

**PROVED — sharp finite-grid restriction.** Let $G_m=\{0,\dots,m-1\}$, $m\ge2$. Fix an input symbol and an affine coordinate $F(x)=b+\sum_j a_jx_j$. If at least two coefficients are nonzero, the fraction of the full register grid $G_m^r$ for which $F(x)\in G_m$ is at most

$$\theta_m=1-\frac{\lfloor m^2/4\rfloor}{m^2}.$$

Thus a learned affine update that stays exactly on-grid on more than $\theta_m$ of the input states is constant or reads one coordinate with slope $+1$ or $-1$ and an integral offset. If that input coordinate reaches both endpoints of its grid, the only nonconstant possibilities are $x_j$ and $m-1-x_j$. If the law preserves every constant input ($F(t,\ldots,t)=t$), only $x_j$ is possible in the dense regime. The occupancy hypothesis concerns the actual reachable states in these physical coordinates.

To prove the bound, if some $a_j$ is nonintegral, two consecutive positions on a line in direction $j$ cannot both land on integers. The successful fraction is at most $\lceil m/2\rceil/m\le\theta_m$. Otherwise choose two nonzero integer coefficients and fix the remaining coordinates. Reflect either selected axis to make both slopes positive. Partition the $m$-by-$m$ square into increasing chains of lengths $2m-1,2m-3,\dots,1$: traverse one boundary row and then its final column, remove them and repeat on the remaining square. Each step raises $F$ by at least one, so a chain of length $k$ contributes at most $\min(k,m)$ successes. Summing gives $m^2-\lfloor m^2/4\rfloor$. The central $m$ consecutive sums of $x_1+x_2$ contain exactly that many pairs by the triangular sum counts. Subtracting the smallest of those sums gives an attaining affine map. In the one-coordinate case an integral slope of magnitude at least two gives at most $\lceil m/2\rceil$ successful points per line. An integral offset is necessary for any success when the slope is integral. This proves the conclusions including $m=2$. The bound is sharp over affine maps; the attaining shift need not preserve constant inputs.

For real coordinates in $[0,1]^r$, an affine map preserving constants and staying in $[0,1]$ on the entire cube is precisely a convex mixture. Constant preservation gives $b=0$ and $\sum a_j=1$. Evaluating at each unit vector gives $a_j\ge0$; conversely these conditions suffice. Conditional on the observed symbol this permits different mixing weights. The result identifies a hard addressing regime for exact finite values and a continuous mixing regime from the same restriction on value semantics. It derives neither query/key dot products nor a softmax training rule.

The distinction is testable in the limit. Exact midpoint mixing succeeds on just $(\lceil m/2\rceil^2+\lfloor m/2\rfloor^2)/m^2$ of ordered grid pairs. On the grid rescaled to $[0,1]$, rounding that same mean to its nearest level costs at most $1/(2(m-1))$ pointwise. For an honest Bernoulli mean the excess Brier cost is the square of that error. For a composition of maps with sup-norm Lipschitz constant $\gamma$, per-step rounding at most $\varepsilon$ yields $e_t\le\gamma^t e_0+\varepsilon\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\gamma^j$ by induction. An infinite-horizon bound therefore needs an appropriate stability argument. Taking finer grids does not turn an exact finite-cube identity into a theorem about an approximate learned machine.


## Learning an action from sparse contexts

Suppose ordinary labels on a familiar subalphabet have identified an $r$-dimensional realization $(\alpha,T,e)$ of a scalar forecast, with row state $\alpha$ and output column $e$. Hold this basis fixed during the following acquisition block. A new symbol $b$ occurs once in each scored word $ubv$, with $u,v$ familiar. If $M$ is its unknown action, the target mean is $\alpha T_u M T_v e$. Define $d_{uv,ij}=(\alpha T_u)_i(T_v e)_j$. Each observation thus scores a linear functional of the $r^2$ entries of $M$. No transition entry or product of the correct hidden signs is supplied to the learner.

Initialize the new action as unidentified. After forecasting and receiving ordinary labels, accumulate $G=\sum_\ell n_\ell d_\ell d_\ell^\top$ and $h=\sum_\ell n_\ell d_\ell\bar Y_\ell$. Solve $Gm=h$ by elimination on independent coordinates, setting free coordinates to zero. These are the normal equations for squared prediction loss. They are consistent because the nullspace of $G$ is the orthogonal complement of the design rows and $h$ lies in their span. Forecast a query in the span by its fitted functional and clip it to $[0,1]$. Outside the span the declared learner uses its fair fallback. After a full-rank fit reshape $m$ as the new transition and compose it in the ordinary way. The fallback and free-coordinate convention do not give unknown functionals an identification guarantee.

**PROVED — sparse observability.** For unrestricted real actions with exactly specified observed means, the mean at a new one-occurrence context is identified iff its design vector $d$ lies in the observed span. Sufficiency follows by linear combination. Otherwise a null vector $z$ has $d^\top z\ne0$, and $m+tz$ matches every observed mean while changing this query. Over a finite probability experiment whose admissible predictions all lie strictly inside $(0,1)$, small $t$ preserves those constraints. Further sign, global realization or boundary constraints can identify more. Full rank identifies $M$ in the chosen coordinates, including its repeated compositions under the declared exact realization. No complete rectangular probe block around the rare symbol is required.

For fixed designs and independent binary outcomes, any $w$ with $d=\sum_\ell w_\ell d_\ell$ gives an unbiased estimator $\sum w_\ell\bar Y_\ell$. Hoeffding bounds its absolute error above $t$ by $2\exp(-2t^2/\sum_\ell w_\ell^2/n_\ell)$. The least-squares query attains the smallest such denominator: take $z$ solving $Gz=d$ and $w_\ell=n_\ell d_\ell^\top z$. Any other feasible weights differ by $v$ with $\sum v_\ell d_\ell=0$, so their extra weighted squared norm is $\sum v_\ell^2/n_\ell$. Thus a direction in the span can still be expensive to acquire. These sampling bounds condition on accurate, frozen anchor operators and a design independent of the rare labels. Uncertainty in the anchors and extrapolation through repeated noisy fits require additional bounds.

Acquiring each design costs $O((|u|+|v|)r^2+r^2)$ scalar operations, plus one $O(r^4)$ Gram update per distinct aggregated observation type or unaggregated event. A dense implementation stores $r^4+r^2$ sufficient-statistic entries per unknown symbol and solves a system of order $r^2$ in $O(r^6)$ operations. Once identified the transition has $r^2$ entries and uses $O(r^2)$ work per rare-token occurrence. A conventional linear regression on identical observable features gives the same estimates and uncertainty. The contribution is identifying those features as predictions obtained from a previously acquired computation and accounting for acquiring the next transformation.

The effect of approximate anchors is explicit at one occurrence. Work in a common coordinate system. Let $m$ be the true action with $\|m\|_1\le S$, let each estimated row $\tilde d_i$ have sup-norm error at most $\epsilon_i$, and let $|\bar Y_i-d_i^\top m|\le b_i$. For a query with row estimate $\tilde d$ and error $\epsilon$, any weights $w$ give a clipped forecast with absolute error at most

$$S\left(\left\|\tilde d-\sum_i w_i\tilde d_i\right\|_\infty+\epsilon+\sum_i|w_i|\epsilon_i\right)+\sum_i|w_i|b_i.$$

Subtract $d^\top m$, insert the estimated rows, and apply the triangle inequality and dual norms. Clipping cannot increase distance to a probability. The bound requires justified error radii in the same basis and a bound on action magnitude. Large extrapolation weights or a nearly singular moment matrix can erase the value of full rank.

## When does the current probability suffice for composition?

**PROVED.** For a nonconstant terminal-label law $p(w)$ form the rows $R_w=(p(wv))_{v\in A^*}$. Their affine hull is a line if and only if each input letter has an affine map $F_a$ with $p(wa)=F_a(p(w))$. On a line, evaluation at the empty suffix is nonconstant because $p$ is nonconstant, so this evaluation is an injective affine coordinate. Reading the coordinate for suffix $a$ gives $F_a$. Conversely, composing the $F_a$ gives an affine expression in $p(w)$ for every suffix coordinate. This confines every row to the same line. Equivalently the Hankel matrix with an additional constant-one column has rank two. A constant law has affine dimension zero. These statements concern terminal Bernoulli forecasts on externally read words. Jaeger's observable-operator framework treats states defined by future observations, including the distinct problem of normalizing states after observing a symbol generated by the model. [Jaeger, *Observable Operator Models for Discrete Stochastic Time Series*](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976600300015411)

Let $I=[\inf_w p(w),\sup_w p(w)]$. Every $F_a$ maps the reachable values into $I$, hence by affinity and continuity maps $I$ into itself. On rescaling $I$ to the unit interval, its endpoint images $u_a,v_a$ are probabilities. A two-state categorical machine with transition rows $(1-u_a,u_a),(1-v_a,v_a)$ advances its second-state probability by $(1-z)u_a+zv_a$. Reading out the original interval endpoints realizes exactly the given law. The converse follows from the same affine recursion for the second-state probability. This proves equivalence for this family and identifies one sufficient predictive value. In the original probability units, requiring $F_a([0,1])\subseteq[0,1]$ is an additional hypothesis. The law alternating between $3/4$ and $1$ has unique update $7/4-z$ whose value at zero is $7/4$.

**Learning from adjacent observed words.** Use the full-unit-interval subclass in the original coordinates. Initialize all forecasts at $1/2$. At a checkpoint keep the word observation counts and means $m_w$. For each observed pair $w,wa$ fit the endpoints in $[0,1]^2$ to minimize $\sum n_{wa}((1-m_w)u_a+m_wv_a-m_{wa})^2$. This is ordinary child-label Brier loss with empirical prefix features. The counts for both sides have an acquisition cost. With distinct inputs solve scalar weighted regression. If its unconstrained optimum falls outside the square, convexity puts a minimum on an edge. Solve its four one-dimensional quadratics and keep the cheapest. With constant input the constant weighted mean minimizes the loss. A letter without pairs retains the fair fallback. Install $m_\epsilon$ as the initial forecast where available. Each installed token advances the scalar by one multiply and one addition using a stored intercept and slope. An equivalent positive two-state stochastic calculation gives identical predictions. If a queried letter has $u_a=v_a$, it erases the old distinction.

For a deployment distribution over an accurate scalar law, write $Z$ for the old forecast and $A$ for the current input. The best predictor restricted to $A$ alone is $E[F_A(Z)\mid A]$. Its excess Brier over the composed law is exactly $E[(v_A-u_A)^2\operatorname{Var}(Z\mid A)]$ by conditional variance. Positive slopes, negative slopes and complete overwrite follow from the same update. At a training checkpoint the empirical Brier gain over the best constant for that letter can likewise be compared directly to a price for the extra read and operation. This local calculation holds the observed input features fixed. A change in a deployed recurrent transition can affect later letters too.

**A simultaneous certificate.** From raw counts obtain intervals $I_w$ for all measured true means. For $K$ fixed words with $n_w$ independent binary labels each, the failure probability is at most $\sum_w 2\exp(-2n_w\delta_w^2)$ for radii $\delta_w$. For each sign of $v_a-u_a$, intersect the endpoint square and corresponding half-plane with the conditions $F_a(I_w)\cap I_{wa}\ne\varnothing$ for every observed pair. On one sign each intersection test is two linear inequalities evaluated at opposite interval endpoints. Polygon clipping therefore gives both feasible regions exactly. On the simultaneous event, a true map in the declared subclass belongs to one of them. Starting from $I_\epsilon$, bound a word by propagating the smallest and largest values over both signs, their polygon vertices and the two endpoints of the current interval. For fixed input the expression is linear in the map, and for fixed map it is linear in the input. Induction encloses every word at every length on the same event. Reusing a map across steps can make these propagated intervals conservative. An empty feasible set rejects the assumed model or intervals. Coverage never certifies the scalar-family assumption itself.

For $K$ word types, prefix lookup from a trie costs linear work in their total symbol length. Point fitting costs linear work in the number of adjacent pairs after aggregation. With $d_a$ pairs for a letter, clipping its polygons uses $O((1+d_a)^2)$ rational operations and $O(1+d_a)$ temporary vertices. At inference, the point model has $2|A|+1$ fitted scalars and one live scalar per stream. Interval propagation additionally scans the feasible vertices for the current letter. These are arithmetic counts. Rational denominators can grow with depth. If installed slopes have magnitude at most $\gamma<1$, rounding the state at each step within $\eta$ adds at most $\eta/(1-\gamma)$ to the absolute error from an exact initial state. This follows by iterating $e_{t+1}\le\gamma e_t+\eta$. A fixed-width implementation must account for coefficient error as well.

## What can a learned state change on the original vocabulary?

Let $q_x(y)>0$ be a trained full-vocabulary successor row and let $g(y)$ be a fixed group assignment learned from the fit data. If the only change a recurrent state makes is to predict group masses $\pi_h$ at accessible history $h$ ending in $x$, its token probability is
$$p_h(y)=q_x(y\mid g(y))\,\pi_h(g(y)).$$
This is normalized because each within-group distribution sums to one. On any fixed scored tape its log loss is exactly the sum of the fixed within-group loss and the group log loss. **PROVED.** Even revealing the true next group can remove only the second term. With one group vector per last token, the empirical minimizer sets it to the observed group frequencies for that token. The resulting hindsight lower bound follows from the nonnegativity of categorical KL divergence, allowing zero probabilities on absent outcomes. At the population level, if all group predictors are allowed, the gain from full history over the last token alone is $H(g(Y)\mid X)-H(g(Y)\mid H)=I(g(Y);H\mid X)$. This bound is about which output distinctions the decoder can express. It applies to arbitrary state dimension and any learning algorithm behind this fixed interface.

For a physical test, fit $q_x$ and a binary partition on one training block, then calibrate each row's group mass on a second block. The third block evaluates 25 token-conditional transformations from raw next-token log loss. Each is the preceding scalar endpoint update restricted to one of five slopes per input group, with an integer state of denominator 4,096. Selection minimizes training log loss plus a declared storage price by enumerating the 25 fixed choices. The overwrite control has the same slopes, group access and decoder, with its incoming scalar reset to the global training group mass each step. The zero slopes erase history and eliminate the live scalar. A conventional token-conditioned exponential smoother with the same signed slopes, rounding and decoder is the identical computation. This finite search tests whether the acquisition objective actually chooses and benefits from memory. ## Learning the output distinction

Let $B$ be a binary distinction computed from accessible past tokens and $Y$ the next token. From prior training pairs count $n_y$ and $h_y$ with $B=1$. For a group assignment $g$, compare the pooled maximum-likelihood unigram with a head predicting $g(Y)$ from $B$ and using the pooled empirical distribution inside each group. The reduction in fitted log loss per label is exactly $I_{\rm emp}(B;g(Y))$ by the entropy chain rule. Maximizing it minimizes the empirical conditional entropy of $B$ given the assigned group. The constraint on the output decoder matters here. The comparison assumes pooled within-group distributions.

The binary-input channel quantization theorem of Kurkoski and Yagi gives an optimal partition into at most $k$ contiguous intervals of the sorted posterior $r_y=h_y/n_y$. A direct proof uses the Bernoulli cross entropy. With group means $p_j$ held fixed, assigning token $y$ to group $j$ costs $n_y[-r_y\log p_j-(1-r_y)\log(1-p_j)]$. Two such costs cross at most once as functions of $r_y$, after dividing out the positive weight $n_y$. The lower envelope therefore has contiguous intervals of minimizers, with ordered tie breaking. A group mean of zero or one can accept only compatible endpoints at finite cost. Reassigning according to this envelope and then refitting the means cannot raise loss. Applied to an optimum, this produces a contiguous optimum. Groups with zero token count or equal posterior require a convention for deployment. [Kurkoski and Yagi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.5637)

A contiguous segment with count $n$ and $h$ positives costs $n\log n-h\log h-(n-h)\log(n-h)$ with $0\log0=0$. Prefix sums and the recurrence $D(j,i)=\min_{s<i}[D(j-1,s)+C(s+1,i)]$ specify the optimizer. The binary split scans one boundary after sorting. Charge $O(V_{\rm seen}\log V_{\rm seen})$ comparisons and linear storage for this binary case, plus one observation update per training target. Rational sorting and bounded logarithms can certify the optimum. This builds a Boolean function of raw token identity from ordinary next-token examples. Its eventual benefit as a state variable requires a separate causal deployment test. Class-based language models that learn token groupings from corpus statistics are an established conventional comparison. [Brown et al.](https://aclanthology.org/J92-4003/)

**PROVED — side information changes the optimization problem.** Condition the empirical log-loss gain on an accessible context $Z$ and allow one shared output bit $g$. Suppose each context $z$ has two distinct observed successor types $u_z,v_z$. If their assigned bits agree, the group gives zero information about $B$ within that context. If the bits differ, the group identifies the successor there and recovers all $I(B;Y\mid Z=z)$. Give the edge $(u_z,v_z)$ weight $n_z I(B;Y\mid Z=z)$ and sum weights for repeated pairs. The total fitted gain in nats is exactly the weight crossing the cut defined by $g$. Any simple graph occurs by giving each edge its own context and recording one example at each endpoint with opposite bits. Each edge then weighs $2\log2$. Consequently a polynomial-time exact optimizer on all such finite observation tables would solve unweighted Max-Cut in polynomial time. This is a worst-case acquisition result about a shared bit, including cases with just two outcomes per context. [Garey, Johnson and Stockmeyer](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304397576900591)

A concrete learner assigns tokens in ID order to the side opposite the larger total weight of their already assigned neighbors. At each assignment it cuts at least half the newly decided weight, and every edge is decided exactly once. Its fitted gain is at least half the total edge weight and hence at least half the optimum. With count tables the graph costs one pass over the eligible observations and $O(V+E)$ additional graph storage and assignment work. With bounded numerical logs, applying the rule to nonnegative lower edge weights gives a certified lower bound on actual fitted gain. For contexts with three or more successors, the contribution need not be an ordinary graph cut. This learner has no generalization guarantee from the empirical edge weights.

For the full-alphabet comparison, a stored bit from the previous token and the current raw token $x$ address two calibrated group rates. The objective factorizes by current token except for a shared one-bit register. All rows with positive net gain maximize their sum. ## Certifying a learned computation on a continuing stream

Let two predictors publish positive distributions $p_t,q_t$ from data available before each target. Define the observed full-token gain $X_t=\log(p_t(Y_t)/q_t(Y_t))$, its conditional mean $\mu_t=E[X_t\mid{\cal F}_{t-1}]$, and a predictable upper bound $w_t$ on the range of possible $X_t$. For the same within-group decoder and binary group rates $p,q$, the range width is exactly $|\operatorname{logit}p-\operatorname{logit}q|$ if both groups are possible. It remains an upper bound on restricted supports. This calculation includes outcomes missing from the training counts. Equal predictions have width zero. The bound can be restricted to eligible contexts using an indicator known before the target.

**PROVED.** Fix $\lambda>0$. With probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for every time $s$,
$$\sum_{t\le s}\mu_t\ \ge\ \sum_{t\le s} X_t-\frac{\lambda}{8}\sum_{t\le s}w_t^2-\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\lambda}.$$
Condition on the past. The log moment-generating function of $X_t-\mu_t$ has value and derivative zero at zero. Its second derivative is the variance under exponential tilting, bounded by $w_t^2/4$ since every tilted outcome stays in the same interval. Integration gives the conditional bound $\lambda^2w_t^2/8$. Exponentiating the cumulative centered gains minus these bounds gives a nonnegative supermartingale starting at one. Stopping at the first crossing of $1/\delta$ up to any finite horizon bounds crossing probability by $\delta$. Let the horizon grow. A finite union bound permits predeclared comparisons and selection of the best reported $\lambda$. This is the bounded-increment exponential supermartingale argument. [Howard et al., time-uniform Chernoff bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.03204)

Any predictable difference in deployment price can be subtracted from both sides. A new computation may spend at most the remaining certified gain on acquisition at explicitly stated exchange rates. Counts of fit operations and physical units must be supplied separately. The conclusion concerns the cumulative conditional means along the observed histories of a sequential sampling law. It permits arbitrary temporal dependence. It does not project a fixed corpus gain onto another source, refund model selection on the audit block, or certify a GPU speedup.

The continuing-stream learner fits the past-token bit for the current-token-conditioned loss. Let $A_{wx}=(n,h)$ count occurrences of past token $w$, current token $x$ and $h$ positive next-group outcomes among $n$ observations. For a bit assignment $c(w)$, pool these counts into $A_{xb}$. At maximum-likelihood group rates the varying part of total next-token loss is $\sum_{x,b}C(A_{xb})$, where $C(n,h)=n\log n-h\log h-(n-h)\log(n-h)$. The within-group probabilities are fixed. Toggling token $w$ moves $A_{wx}$ between only two bins per observed $x$. The exact difference of these terms decides whether to flip. Start with every bit zero and complete the two registered sweeps in opposite token-ID orders. An ambiguous log interval is resolved by canceling integer log coefficients and comparing the two resulting integer products. Strict moves lower this empirical ordinary prediction loss. Adjacent observations need no supplied token class or hidden state labels. Each sweep inspects one record for each occupied $(w,x)$ and uses at most four entropy terms per record. The full rate calibration and row gate still consume their separate training blocks. This is a constrained instance of word-class exchange. ## Learning a synchronizing bit from the token stream

**PROVED.** Consider a stationary word-probability law of known global Hankel rank two on a finite alphabet. The target class has two communicating states, positive emission $e_i(a)$ in each state for each symbol, a deterministic destination $f_a(i)$ after each emission, and a synchronizing word that determines the next state from either start. Set $B=(P(uv))$ for $u\in\{\epsilon,p\},v\in\{\epsilon,s\}$ and $C_a=(P(uav))$ on the same indices. Choose $p,s$ with $\det B>0$. Such a pair exists. At rank two the empty and one-symbol prefixes span the row space, since a one-dimensional span stable under all symbol extensions could never grow. The same argument gives the column span. Thus some adjacent covariance is nonzero. The covariances sum to zero, giving a positive entry.

In a minimal realization $P(w)=\alpha M_w\mathbf1$, write $B=RS,C_a=RM_aS$ using the prefix rows and suffix columns. Both factors are invertible. Hence $T_a=B^{-1}C_a=S^{-1}M_aS$ and $r_a=\det C_a/\det B=\det M_a$. Within the target class, row $i$ of $M_a$ has its sole positive entry $e_i(a)$ at $f_a(i)$. A reset has $r_a=0$, identity has $r_a=e_0(a)e_1(a)$ and reversal has $r_a=-e_0(a)e_1(a)$. Any synchronizing word contains a singular letter by multiplicativity of the determinant.

There is an exact membership test under the rank hypothesis. Use normalized coordinates $(1,z)$ where $z=P(s\mid h)$. For each letter compute $(m,j)=(1,z)T_a$. Its probability is $m$ and the updated coordinate is $j/m$. Start immediately after a singular letter and close under every letter with positive mass. Require exactly two distinct coordinates with normalized positive emission rows and communicating destinations. Also require the initial coordinate $P(s)$ to be a convex mixture of those coordinates whose weights are stationary for the recovered chain. Necessity follows because after a reset both hidden states give the same destination, and both distinct pure predictive rows are then reachable. For sufficiency, use the two coordinates as states and the tested masses and destinations as their emission and transition tables. The identities $(1,z_i)T_a=m_i(a)(1,z_{f_a(i)})$ imply equality of every word probability by induction and initial mixing. One live bit suffices after synchronization. Before that point an observer starting from a stationary mixture can carry a posterior probability. For the sampled rule, suppose the class assumption holds and $e_i(a)\ge m>0$. Counts from ordinary adjacent windows estimate the fitted probability blocks. If all required word probabilities differ from their estimates by at most $\varepsilon$, determinant errors are at most $4\varepsilon$. At a selected true anchor determinant $d>4\varepsilon$ each ratio error is at most $8\varepsilon/(d-4\varepsilon)$. Error below $m^2/2$ separates all three action types. After any reset $z$, the histories $z$ and $za$ across letters attain the two pure anchor forecasts by communication. ## Identifying a binary operation from noisy task responses

Let $d\ge2$ and $N=2^d$. The task names are opaque. Pilot input $j$ gives each task one binary response, so a noiseless dictionary with $m$ pilots associates a row in $\{0,1\}^m$ to each name. Consider a fixed binary operation $g$ applied pointwise to any two rows. Say it preserves the observations if the result is always another row. Require both arguments to affect its truth table. This requirement excludes operations that can only return an existing operand or a constant. It does not specify an operation.

**PROVED.** At least $d+1$ binary pilot columns are necessary to identify **both** all $2^d$ rows and a unique such operation by preservation. Fewer than $d$ columns cannot distinguish the rows. At exactly $d$, every possible row must occur, so every one of the 16 binary operations preserves the observations. The bound is sharp in two different families. Take all $d$-bit signatures and add the single column $h(s_1,s_2)$, for $h$ either XOR or OR. Closure now requires
$$g(h(a,b),h(c,e))=h(g(a,c),g(b,e))\quad\text{for all }a,b,c,e.$$
All four bits can vary freely. For XOR this makes $g$ a linear functional on the binary plane, so the preserving gates are zero, the two projections and XOR. For OR it makes $g$ a homomorphism of the join operation. If $g(0,0)=1$ then $g$ is the constant one. Otherwise the values at $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ determine it by joins, giving zero, either projection or OR. Only the true $h$ uses both arguments. This uses the classical connection between commuting operations and homomorphisms. The observation count and forecasting experiment impose additional information and resource constraints. [Tóth and Waldhauser](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04661) · [Lean theorem for every dimension and pair of distinct coordinates](ResponseOperation.lean)

The deployed learner fits by predicting ordinary response labels. For the declared fold grammar the hypotheses with gates XOR and OR give identical predictions on every unit pilot: among the chosen task coordinates, at most one input bit is active. For $d\ge3$, predictions of two-coordinate tasks on these pilots already require $g(0,0)=0$ and $g(0,1)=g(1,0)=1$, leaving $g(1,1)$ unknown. The extra input with two active features resolves that entry. Thus comparing token likelihood and testing closure have distinct ambiguity sets on the first $d$ observations, and identify the same essential gate after the extra condition. If the two teachers are initially equally likely, even perfect knowledge of the new latent input $x$ cannot settle their disagreement using only the unit pilots. A uniform task and a uniform independent $x$ overlap in $J\sim{\rm Bin}(d,1/4)$ active positions. The two operations disagree when $J$ is positive and even. **PROVED for passive full-column observation.** Suppose each new seven-bit input is hidden and independently uniform, and each observation reveals the complete *noiseless* column of responses over all $N$ named tasks. ## When two raw tokens can share one internal symbol

**PROVED for a fixed refinement decoder.** Let $P(w)$ be the cylinder probabilities of a stationary source with a finite alphabet $A$ and positive word probabilities. An internal alphabet is a partition $g:A\to C$. The model uses an arbitrary causal law $Q$ on cell sequences and emits each raw token according to a fixed distribution $r(\cdot\mid c)$ supported on its cell. Its full probability for a word $w$ is $Q(g(w))\prod_i r(w_i\mid g(w_i))$. The coarse state may have arbitrary memory. Brown et al. used the same within-class emission model with a class $n$-gram and derived the adjacent-class mutual information objective for bigrams. Here the question is exact preservation under every continuation and the length of evidence needed to decide a merge. [Brown et al., Sections 3–4](https://aclanthology.org/J92-4003.pdf)

There is a **unique coarsest lossless partition**. Put two tokens $a,b$ in the same cell exactly when, for every prefix $u$ and suffix $v$,
$$\frac{P(uav)}{P(a)}=\frac{P(ubv)}{P(b)}.$$
These are probabilities of the *joint* neighboring context conditional on the central token. In a lossless model the coarse law must be the pushforward of $P$, by summing over each coarse-word fiber. Its one-symbol marginals then force $r(a\mid c)=P(a)/P(c)$. Substitution in the word formula gives the displayed equality. Conversely, equality of signatures is an equivalence relation. Replacing one letter at a time shows that $P(w)/\prod_iP(w_i)$ is constant on each coarse-word fiber. Summation on that fiber gives the required factorization with exactly these $Q,r$. Every lossless partition refines the resulting one. A decoder allowed to condition within-cell odds on history can distinguish a different class. The registered FineWeb-Edu test used such a decoder through the last raw token.

**Finite identification with a sharp horizon.** Write $d$ for the real rank of the Hankel matrix $P(uv)$. In a minimal linear realization $P(w)=\alpha M_w\omega$, a merge is lossless exactly when $M_a/P(a)=M_b/P(b)$. The prefix rows $\alpha M_u$ and suffix columns $M_v\omega$ span both $d$-dimensional spaces. Starting from the empty word, a span that stops increasing is invariant under every extension and cannot increase later. Thus words of length at most $d-1$ on each side span. Testing proportionality on those words suffices; on known row and column bases only $d^2$ entries per letter are required. A minimal realization can operate on $|C|$ action matrices and a per-token refinement table in place of $|A|$ matrices. ## Recovering a continuing update from three-token windows

**PROVED for stationary rank-two permutation sources.** There are two hidden states, both with positive stationary mass. To see this, form a spanning block $B=(P(uv))$ on empty and one-letter prefixes and suffixes as in the [two-state rank argument](#learning-a-synchronizing-bit-from-the-token-stream). Their determinants are positive for a stay and negative for a flip, with absolute value $e_{0a}e_{1a}$. A staying letter with different emissions supplies a diagonal operator with distinct eigenvalues in the hidden basis. If all stays have equal emissions, consider ordered products of two flips. The eigenvalues of such a product are $e_{0a}e_{1b}$ and $e_{1a}e_{0b}$. If every pair had equal eigenvalues, all flips would share the same emission ratio. Summing the two emission rows forces that ratio to equal one whenever flips exist. With no flips and only state-independent stays the same conclusion holds. Both cases would give an iid observable law of rank one. Hence a separating operator of length one or two always exists. Its product uses already fitted operators, so a four-token observation is unnecessary.

Let $\Pi_0,\Pi_1$ be its two spectral projectors. The columns $\Pi_i\beta$ form the hidden coordinate basis with its scaling fixed, since in that basis the terminal vector is $(1,1)^\top$. ## Exact and attainable prediction on a probability clock

Jaeger's probability clock gives a rank-three observable-operator process outside all finite hidden Markov models. His original construction uses two outputs, an irrational rotation and a reset. It also illustrates how unconstrained learned operators can produce negative probabilities. The following three-output variant has constant advance probability, strictly positive event probabilities, and two observed reset marks. This change makes its expected prediction cost reducible to one marked run. [Jaeger, Sections 6 and 9.1.3](https://publica-rest.fraunhofer.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/c5799916-b434-4186-94ab-717cb74db946/content)

At age $j$ emit $a$ with probability $\rho$ and advance. Otherwise emit $b$ with conditional probability $q_j=(1+\kappa\cos j\theta)/2$ or emit $c$ with its complement; either mark resets age to zero. Assume $0<\rho<1$, $0<|\kappa|<1$ and $\sin\theta\ne0$. Stationary ages have probabilities $w_j=(1-\rho)\rho^j$. The span of $1,\cos j\theta,\sin j\theta$ is closed under every shifted word probability. The advance rotates and scales these coordinates, and each reset multiplies evaluation at age zero by a linear combination of them. Three consecutive ages reached after a reset are independent as evaluation rows. The suffixes $\epsilon,b,ab$ distinguish all three coordinates. Thus the stationary word-law Hankel rank is exactly three. For $\rho=2/3$, $\kappa=1/2$, $e^{i\theta}=(3+4i)/5$, the stationary initial phase mean $(1-\rho)/(1-\rho e^{i\theta})$ and every word probability are rational.

**PROVED for all finite positive-state competitors.** Here a $K$-state HMM may use arbitrary nonnegative symbol matrices whose sum is stochastic. Let $R(j,s)=(1-\rho)\rho^j q_j(s)$ be the true joint law of the age and binary mark of a complete run. The smallest asymptotic expected excess log loss among these HMMs equals
$$ (1-\rho)\min_{\beta,A,b,c}\mathrm{KL}(R\Vert R_{\beta,A,b,c}), \qquad R_{\beta,A,b,c}(j,s)=\beta A^j b_s,\quad b_b=b,\quad b_c=c.$$
Here $\beta$ is a distribution on $K$ phases, $A,b,c\ge0$ and $A\mathbf1+b+c=\mathbf1$. Include an atom at infinite run length for a defective candidate. This variational formula permits arbitrary stochastic evolution while observing $a$. It does not impose deterministic ages or a constant candidate advance rate.

For the lower bound, after any observed reset the true run law is always $R$. An arbitrary competitor begins that run at some posterior $\beta$ and uses its own $A$ and exit columns. Score each run only through its first $L$ tokens, including the mark if it appears. The selected token sets are disjoint and selection of each next prediction is predictable. The KL chain rule gives at least the optimal censored-run KL per run. Starts occur with frequency $1-\rho$ and starts too near the end have negligible density. The censored KL objectives increase with $L$ and are lower semicontinuous on the compact set of kernels and initial weights. A convergent subsequence of their minimizers, tested first at each fixed censor length, shows that their minima increase to the minimum uncensored KL. For the upper bound, give each mark the transition matrix $b_s\beta$. This implements independent candidate runs and reaches the run KL per mean true run length. If a minimizing candidate can fail to exit, condition its finite runs on eventual exit. With exit probabilities $h_i$, use $\beta'_i=\beta_i h_i/(\beta h)$, $A'_{ij}=A_{ij}h_j/h_i$, $b'_{s,i}=b_{s,i}/h_i$ on $h_i>0$. Its finite-run probabilities increase by $1/(\beta h)$. A proper finite realization can be initialized stationarily. For a finite-KL competitor each supported run has negative log probability at most linear in its length, by its smallest positive transition and initial weight. The geometric true tail bounds the expected contribution of partial boundary runs. These arguments also cover competitors initialized at arbitrary fixed distributions.

**Deterministic reset machines admit an exact optimizer.** Following $a$ from the reset state gives a transient of length $m$ followed by a cycle of length $\ell$. All $K$ reachable states have $m+\ell=K$; a smaller machine can split off the first visit to its cycle. Optimizing the per-state next-token law gives advance probability $\rho$ everywhere. The $\ell$ repeated cells have weights $(1-\rho)\rho^j/(1-\rho^\ell)$ for $m\le j<K$. Their optimal conditional mark probabilities are
$$\bar q_j=\tfrac12\left[1+\kappa\,\Re\!\left(e^{ij\theta}\frac{1-\rho^\ell}{1-\rho^\ell e^{i\ell\theta}}\right)\right].$$
The transient has the individual probabilities $q_j$. Only the weighted cross entropy within these age groups varies between layouts. Enumerating $\ell=1,\ldots,K$ therefore finds the unrestricted optimum *within deterministic single-reset machines*. A saturated age counter is the case $\ell=1$. Its rate cost is at most $(1-\rho)\rho^{K-1}[\log2-h((1+|\kappa|)/2)]$, even by guessing fair on the tail. Thus an infinite exact-state requirement can coexist with exponentially small attainable finite-state loss.

**Learning and precision.** Runs following observable resets are independent. In the supplied clock grammar, $\rho=\Pr(J\ge1)$, $\kappa=E[Y\mid J=0]$ and $\cos\theta=E[Y\mid J=1]/\kappa$ for marks $Y=\pm1$. The two necessary short-run conditions have probabilities $1-\rho$ and $(1-\rho)\rho$. Ordinary binomial concentration on their counts and empirical mark means gives consistent estimates away from $\kappa=0$. The sign of $\theta$ is observationally irrelevant. Expected prediction loss is stable without certifying an irrational angle. For example, fixing the correct $\rho,\kappa$ and estimating $\theta$ changes each mark probability by at most $|\kappa|j|\widehat\theta-\theta|/2$. The Bernoulli KL upper bound by chi-square, averaged over the geometric age, gives per-token excess at most $(1-\rho)\kappa^2 E[J^2](\widehat\theta-\theta)^2/(1-\kappa^2)$, or $(10/9)(\widehat\theta-\theta)^2$ in the registered source. The stationary infinite-past rate obeys this bound. A common stationary initial age gives the same finite-horizon expected bound by revealing that age. A post-reset start has smaller age moments.

## When does a history statistic transfer to a new prediction?

A source learner observes an earlier raw type $U$, current context $X$ and the single bit $1_{\{Y=s\}}$. It installs a finite code $g(U)$. A destination readout observes the code and a positive base law $p_x$ and redistributes mass inside an output group $T$ disjoint from $s$. Its total probability for $T$ stays $p_x(T)$. Let $d_x(j)=p_x(j)/p_x(T)$ within the group. Multiplying its entries by positive weights and renormalizing gives a full-vocabulary probability law. For several disjoint groups, these operations commute with one another and with a separate global odds multiplier for $s$. Indeed the latter multiplies all nonsource probabilities by the same factor. On each realized target, the joint log improvement is the source log improvement plus the within-group improvement for the unique group containing the target, if any. Thus destination gains measure which group member occurred even when the source indicator and group mass are already accounted for.

**PROVED for additive logit readouts with a fixed base.** Condition on $Y\in T$. Use finite history and context sets with positive probabilities on their support. Write $\pi_u=P(U=u\mid Y\in T)$ and $\mu_u(j)=P(Y=j\mid U=u,Y\in T)>0$. Set the last logit offset to zero. For a free offset vector $c$, let $d_{x,c}(j)\propto d_x(j)e^{c_j}$ and $A_u(c)=E[\log\sum_j d_x(j)e^{c_j}\mid u,T]$. Up to a fixed constant, the conditional risk is $A_u(c)-\mu_u^\top c$. Its Hessian is the mean categorical covariance, positive definite in the reference gauge. A positive lower bound on $d_x$ and interior $\mu_u$ give coercivity since $\max c_j-\sum_j\mu_u(j)c_j$ grows with the offset range. The unique minimizer $c_u^*$ therefore matches $E[d_{X,c_u^*}\mid u,T]=\mu_u$. For arbitrary destination offsets $v_z$, the excess risk over fitting each raw history is exactly
$$
\sum_u\pi_u\,E\!\left[D_{\rm KL}\!\left(d_{X,c_u^*}\,\Vert\,d_{X,v_{g(u)}}\right)\mid u,T\right].
$$
Expanding the KL gives $A_u(v)-A_u(c_u^*)-\nabla A_u(c_u^*)^\top(v-c_u^*)$, equal to the risk difference by moment matching. Minimizing this expression over the $v_z$ prices the best possible use of the frozen code within this decoder. Zero excess requires equal optimal offsets in every occupied cell. For several destination groups with common positive history support, the coarsest lossless code identifies the distinct tuples of optimal offsets. With partial support, join two histories by an edge when some task observes both and their offsets differ. An admissible lossless code is exactly a proper coloring of this graph. This describes the output corrections a learned state must preserve. It grants the encoder no advance knowledge of those corrections.

**Local cost of merging histories.** Start from a calibrated base with $\mu_u^0=E[d_X\mid u,T]$, and perturb the within-group true marginal by $\epsilon r_u$, holding the design distribution fixed. Omit the last coordinate. Let $F_u=E[\operatorname{diag}(d_X)-d_Xd_X^\top\mid u,T]$ on the free coordinates, $h_u=F_u^{-1}r_u$, and $h_z=(\sum_{g(u)=z}\pi_u F_u)^{-1}\sum_{g(u)=z}\pi_u r_u$. On this fixed finite positive support the inverse function theorem applied to the moment equations gives $c_u^*=\epsilon h_u+O(\epsilon^2)$ and $v_z^*=\epsilon h_z+O(\epsilon^2)$. Taylor expansion in the preceding identity gives the tying penalty
$$
\frac{\epsilon^2}{2}\sum_u\pi_u(h_u-h_{g(u)})^\top F_u(h_u-h_{g(u)})+O(\epsilon^3).
$$
For a binary base taking values $0.1,0.5$ in two histories, true values $0.25,0.75$ have different probability residuals and identical odds multiplier three. True values $0.2,0.6$ have equal residuals and require distinct multipliers. Differences in raw residuals alone do not decide whether sharing costs predictive information once a global correction has been fitted.

**One finite-sample route to useful transfer.** Fix a base forecast $p$ and a target-group history feature $\phi\in\mathbb R^r$ before observing independent adaptation labels. Assume $E\phi=0$, $E[\phi\phi^\top]=I$ on the destination population. Put $Z=e_Y-p$, $C=E[\phi Z^\top]$ and $\hat C=n^{-1}\sum_i\phi_i Z_i^\top$. The logit update $2\hat C^\top\phi$ gives population log improvement at least $\|C\|_F^2-\|\hat C-C\|_F^2$ conditional on the fitted update. For a categorical softmax the covariance Hessian has operator norm at most $1/2$ by the variance bound for a unit coordinate vector's range, at most $\sqrt{2}$. Taylor's inequality bounds the gain below by $2\langle C,\hat C\rangle-\|\hat C\|_F^2$. Independence and $\|Z\|^2\le2$ give expected gain at least $\|C\|_F^2-2r/n$. This certifies a specified update relative to its frozen reference and assumes the feature whitening is available. ## When does current predictive state suffice for continued learning?

Consider a finite bank of positive likelihood models $h$, one fixed known positive prior $b_0$, and a stream of ordinary observed outcomes. Each observation supplies the log-likelihood vector $x(h)$ for its actual outcome. Bayes' rule adds $x(h)$ to each log weight, up to a constant independent of $h$. Subtract its value at one reference hypothesis. The linear span $V$ of these centered changes is an additive learning state. Given an orthonormal row basis $Q$ for $V$, retain coefficients $c$, update $c\leftarrow c+Qx$, and recover the centered change to the original log prior as $Q^\top c$. Orthogonalizing observed changes and installing a direction on the first linearly independent change learns this basis from an initially empty state. In exact arithmetic it preserves the originating belief at every prefix when all previous changes lie in its span. A frozen basis generalizes exactly to the extent that future updates also lie there. The bank, a way to evaluate it, and the acquired decoding matrix all need storage. This learner can share computed coordinates between streams. Its supplied bank still defines the hypothesis class.

**A sufficient condition for the readout itself to identify the learning state.** Suppose all log-likelihood changes belong, modulo constants, to the span of functions $T(h)$ whose expectations can be obtained from the current allowed predictions. Every reachable belief is $b_\theta(h)\propto b_0(h)\exp(\theta^\top T(h))$. For two such beliefs,
$$
D(b_\theta\Vert b_\phi)+D(b_\phi\Vert b_\theta)
=(\theta-\phi)^\top(E_\theta T-E_\phi T).
$$
This follows by expanding the log ratios and canceling the normalizers. Equal observable expectations force equal beliefs and hence identical responses to every common future update. This is an exact observer equivalence for a fixed prior and likelihood generator. Its exponential-family foundation is classical. Computing the belief or the next prediction from its means can be expensive. [Wainwright and Jordan, Proposition 3.2](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~jordan/sail/readings/wainwright-jordan-fnt.pdf)

**Application to learning action meanings.** Let $h=\pi$ be a permutation of $K$ ticket names to $K$ word identities. After offering name $i$, a purchased bit gives a strictly positive likelihood $\ell(\pi(i))$. A pass contributes a constant. The belief has weights $b_0(\pi)\prod_i W_{i,\pi(i)}$. Write $D=\log W$. Adding any row or column offsets to $D$ changes every permutation log weight by the same constant. Conversely, if an assignment sum is constant over all permutations, exchanging two assigned columns shows every two-by-two alternating difference vanishes. Subtracting the last row and column therefore gives all identifiable log coordinates
$$
\eta_{ij}=D_{ij}-D_{iK}-D_{Kj}+D_{KK}, \qquad i,j<K.
$$
When name $i<K$ is bought, add $\log(\ell(j)/\ell(K))$ to its row. Buying name $K$ subtracts this same vector from every row. Thus the persistent learning state uses $(K-1)^2$ coordinates for every positive known prior. The matrix of single-name marginals $M_{ij}=P(\pi(i)=j)$ supplies all the needed expectations and has the same number of free entries. Reading one name in a context with word law $p$ gives $(Mp)_i$. If all clipped simplex contexts are available, they separate distinct matrices. Equality for one word law alone supplies fewer observations.

This dimensional bound has a resource interpretation. With freely controllable clipped contexts and positive-probability purchased hits, any desired finite log table $\eta$ is reachable from the prior: for each nonreference row choose $n$ identical successful queries with word law proportional to $(e^{\eta_{i1}/n},\ldots,e^{\eta_{i,K-1}/n},1)$. For large enough $n$ every component exceeds the floor. Row $K$ is unchanged. Distinct tables give distinct readouts by the preceding identity and the gauge characterization. Placing $s$ distinct values in each coordinate requires at least $\lceil (K-1)^2\log_2s\rceil$ bits for exact forecasts across this grid. This does not assume a real number occupies one bit, or that a fixed text tape realizes every possible table. The role of probabilities is twofold here. A current marginal can suffice mathematically, while an additive natural parameter gives a cheaper and more stable learning update.

**Execution for a uniform prior.** The normalizer of $W$ is its permanent. For a column subset $S$, a forward dynamic program sums the positive weights of assignments of the first $|S|$ names onto $S$. Append each unused column to advance one row. A reverse program over the same subsets supplies the continuation weights. Multiplying each forward value, one edge and its reverse value and dividing by the normalizer gives that edge's posterior contribution. Summing yields all first marginals in $O(K2^K)$ arithmetic operations and $O(2^K+K^2)$ working entries. Log-space addition handles wide dynamic ranges. An arbitrary correlated prior generally needs another factorization for this runtime. Expected permutation matrices and permanent-based inference are established methods. [Powell and Smith, sections 2–3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00180-019-00901-2)

A small persistent state can still have an expensive exact decoder. Given a Boolean $K$-square matrix $A$, put $N=K!+1$ and $W_{ij}=N+A_{ij}$. Starting from the uniform prior, present successively one purchased hit on each name $i$ with rational word law $p_{ij}=W_{ij}/s_i$, $s_i=\sum_jW_{ij}$. These probabilities exceed $1/(4K)$. Let $h_i=\sum_jM^{(i-1)}_{ij}p_{ij}$ be its forecast before the hit. The chain rule, or summing the likelihood of all successes over the uniform prior, gives the integer
$$K!\prod_i s_i h_i=\operatorname{per}(W).$$
Reducing modulo $N$ yields $\operatorname{per}(A)$ because it is an integer polynomial and $0\le\operatorname{per}(A)<N$. All input and exact intermediate rational bit lengths are polynomial in $K$. Thus exact next-hit probabilities on these reachable positive clipped histories are #P-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions, even though a polynomial-size rational weight table represents the posterior. This uses Valiant's established counting theorem. The statement concerns variable $K$, exact probabilities, a succinctly specified permutation bank and hypothetical feasible observation histories. It establishes no comparable hardness of approximate decisions, the four-ticket instance or an explicitly enumerated input table of $K!$ entries. ## Which earlier distinctions survive current-token calibration?

Let $U,Q,Y$ be the earlier token, current token and next token. Fix a nonempty proper output set $B$ and write $C=1_{\{Y\in B\}}$. A positive reference row $p_q$ supplies both within-group distributions and the event mass $b_q=p_q(B)$. Multiplying odds for $B$ by $e^x$ sets its mass to $\sigma(\operatorname{logit} b_q+x)$, with $\sigma$ the logistic function. Full-token loss is then the sum of event log loss and the unchanged loss within the observed group. Assume finite positive support edges $e=(u,q)$ with weights $w_e$ summing to one, and interior true event rates $r_e$. Put $\bar r_q=P(C=1\mid q)$. The statements below are population results with unrestricted real offsets. The text experiment uses sparse quantized offsets.

**PROVED.** The optimal loss reduction from revealing $U$ above the best current-only event forecast is exactly $I(C;U\mid Q)$. For a specified current forecast $s_q$ the reduction to the same oracle adds $\sum_q w_q\,\mathrm{kl}(\bar r_q\Vert s_q)$. Both identities follow by conditioning binary log loss on the observed inputs. Remaining within-group information can equal $I(Y;U\mid Q,C)$ even when this event has exhausted its gain. The usual log-loss value of side information applies here with a binary event. [Jiao, Courtade, Venkat and Weissman](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.4679)

Fit $\operatorname{logit}m_e=\operatorname{logit} b_q+\alpha_u+\beta_q$. The fitted edge predictions $m_e$ are unique. The space of attained edge logits is closed and linear after absorbing the fixed base into $\beta_q$. The strictly convex edge risk diverges as any edge logit diverges. Differentiating at its minimum gives zero sums of $w_e(r_e-m_e)$ at every row and column. As a result,
$$
I(C;U\mid Q)=\sum_e w_e\,\mathrm{kl}(m_e\Vert\bar r_q)+
                    \sum_e w_e\,\mathrm{kl}(r_e\Vert m_e).
$$
Expanding the three binary divergences leaves a cross term proportional on each edge to $(r_e-m_e)(\operatorname{logit}m_e-\operatorname{logit}\bar r_q)$. Its weighted sum vanishes by the row and column equations. The first term is the exact gain achieved by additive odds. The remaining information vanishes exactly when the alternating sum of target logits is zero around every support cycle. To see sufficiency, propagate row and column offsets along a spanning forest. Each remaining edge closes a cycle and its alternating sum decides consistency. Each of the $c$ connected components has one freely movable offset. For $|E|$ edges there are $|E|-|U|-|Q|+c$ independent cycle constraints on the logits. Exponentiating gives the classical partial rank-one completion test on positive odds. There is an exact population decision about trying a simpler operation. At the current-only solution set $t_u=\sum_q w_{uq}(r_{uq}-\bar r_q)$. If every $t_u$ vanishes, all derivatives of the joint convex loss vanish and additive odds have already attained their optimum. Otherwise the bit $A=\{u:t_u>0\}$ improves prediction. Profiling out the current-token offsets also measures which distinctions can be estimated. On the componentwise zero-sum subspace $L$ is invertible, so implicit differentiation of the score and a second-order Taylor expansion give best additive gain over the refitted current-only law $\epsilon^2 v^\top L^+v/2+o(\epsilon^2)$. The curvature uses the fitted means at the reference truth. This is a graph Laplacian after eliminating the current vertices, an established Schur complement construction. ## A price for copying an interaction across raw token types

Take any finite table of conditional event rates $r_{ij}$ on supported type pairs with population weights $w_{ij}$. Replace coarse type $i$ by $a_i$ distinct row IDs and $j$ by $b_j$ distinct column IDs. Within that block all $a_i b_j$ pairs share the event rate and have equal probability. On the raw grid choose additive logits for the two IDs, plus arbitrary logit exceptions at individually designated cells. An installed exception costs $c_{ij}\geq0$ per raw cell, constant within its block. Use a separable population loss such as mean Bernoulli KL. The base model may have different row and column parameters for each coarse type.

**PROVED.** The infimum of raw population loss plus exception rent equals the infimum on the coarse grid if each coarse exception costs $a_i b_j c_{ij}$. Independently draw one representative uniformly for each coarse row and column. Restrict a raw fit to those representatives, including its exceptions. This gives a legal coarse fit. Its expected loss is the raw loss and its expected rent is the raw rent, since any raw pair is selected with probability $1/(a_i b_j)$. The coarse infimum lower-bounds this expectation. Conversely, copy any coarse fit to its full blocks. Its loss and rent agree exactly with the coarse objective. The proof applies to other base families closed under these restrictions and copies. It prices cell exceptions. A memory tariff for row tables, shared subroutines or a compressed set of exception locations changes the calculation. For full-token NLL the argument applies to excess over common within-group forecasts. Absolute NLL also requires the same expected within-group loss.

For uniform replication by $n$, let $f(j)$ be minimum template loss with at most $j$ cell exceptions and let $f_{\rm cvx}$ be its lower convex envelope. A raw fit with $k$ exceptions has expected restricted exception count at most $k/n^2$. Jensen's inequality and monotonicity give raw loss at least $f_{\rm cvx}(k/n^2)$. Copying a $j$-exception fit uses $jn^2$ exceptions. More generally the minimum raw exception count for **exact fit** is the minimum coarse repair cost with per-cell charges $a_i b_j$. For any exactly fitting raw model, every restriction fits exactly, giving the lower bound, and copying gives equality. The optimum can be mathematically known without the learner having any way to find the coarse types. ## Learning what to sense from a delayed prediction

Let two independent uniform words $U,V\in\{0,1\}^d$ arrive successively under separate permutations of their names. A controller stores a state before it learns the uniformly requested coordinate $J$. An independent fair target $Y$ will eventually arrive. It may pass or pay $c\ge0$ to query either of two sensors. For the matching sensor $a=X_J$, where $X=U\oplus V$, the answer agrees with $Y$ with probability $1/2+\Delta$ for $0<\Delta\le1/2$. The other sensor gives an independent fair bit. The payoff is correct prediction minus acquisition cost. The ordinary learning observation on a purchase is the emitted bit followed by $Y$. Knowing whether those two bits agree is sufficient for fitting a symmetric binary channel. With $p_{sj}=\max_a\Pr(X_j=a\mid S=s)$, copying a purchased answer is optimal and the exact gain over always passing is
$$
G(S)=\mathbb E_{S,J}[(\Delta p_{SJ}-c)_+] .
$$
In particular a blind purchase has value $\Delta/2-c$ before applying the pass option. This distinction matters even when every individual sensor token has the same marginal distribution.

**PROVED for any $d\ge1$.** If at most one bit survives each of the first two arrivals, the sharp gain is
$$
G_{11}=\frac{(\Delta-c)_+}{d}+\left(1-\frac1d\right)(\Delta/2-c)_+ .
$$
Store one coordinate of $U$ and replace it by its XOR with the corresponding coordinate of $V$. For an upper bound, write the first stored bit as a sign $h(U)$. The final sign can be written $S=a(V)+h(U)b(V)$ with $|b|\le1$, using the half-difference of the second update's two possible signs. For the singleton characters $\chi_j(w)=(-1)^{w_j}$, independence gives $r_j=\mathbb E[S\chi_j(X)]=\widehat h_j\widehat b_j$. Cauchy–Schwarz and Parseval imply $\sum_j|r_j|\le1$. Balance of $\chi_j(X)$ gives $\mathbb E_S|\mathbb E[\chi_j(X)\mid S]|=|r_j|$, including biased binary $S$. Bound the convex purchase gain by its chord on $p\in[1/2,1]$ and average. For $c<\Delta$ the chord has positive slope, so equality forces all the Fourier mass of $h$ onto singletons. In $h^2=1$ each pairwise coefficient is twice the product of its two singleton coefficients. Only one can be nonzero. Equality then forces $b=\pm\chi_j$ at that coordinate and $S=\pm\chi_j(X)$. Conditioning on an independent shared random tape extends the upper bound to randomized updates.

**PROVED for three coordinates.** If the first word is available in full and just one final bit can survive, the sharp gain is
$$
G_{*1}=\max\{(3\Delta/4-c)_+,\,G_{11}\}.
$$
Below $c=5\Delta/8$, the majority of the three route bits attains the optimum. From this price through $\Delta$, a single coordinate attains it. At the switch both are optimal. A map of $(U,V)$ induces a channel from $X$ to the final bit. For any such channel, each fixed downstream policy has linear value. Maximizing these values gives a convex function of the channel, with a maximizing deterministic vertex. It therefore suffices to check the 256 binary functions of $X$. A Lean kernel certificate upper-bounds every function at $c/\Delta=0,1/2,5/8,1$. Each function's optimized gain is convex in $c$. On each interval the claimed bound is a straight line between the certified endpoints, attained by the stated statistic. Above $\Delta$ passing attains the upper bound zero. The zero-price majority rule is classical random access coding. The purchase option and both causal memory budgets determine the price switch here. [Lean certificate](SensorPrice.lean) · [Independent exact enumeration](audit_sensing.py) · [Ambainis et al., optimal classical random access codes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.03045)

The physical first state can cost more than the final answer. For deterministic exact computation of $f(U\oplus V)$ on all pairs, two first words $u,u'$ can share a state precisely when $u\oplus u'$ belongs to $H_f=\{t:f(x\oplus t)=f(x)\ \forall x\}$. Necessity follows by supplying each possible $V$. Encoding the coset of $H_f$ attains sufficiency. Hence the minimum number of first states is $2^d/|H_f|$. Three-bit majority has trivial stabilizer and takes eight first states. A single coordinate takes two. The stabilizer conclusion concerns this exact statistic. The gain bound above separately rules out every possible one-bit first update achieving majority's low-price payoff.

**Finite computation for arbitrary $d$.** With one final bit, a downstream policy is a pair of length-$d$ plans over pass and the two sensor names. For each $X$ the encoder takes the plan of larger total expected payoff. Put $b=\Delta/2-c$. Suppose the plans buy opposite sensors on $o$ coordinates, exactly one plan buys on $t$ others, and the remaining coordinates agree. On agreeing coordinates the best common mean gain is $b_+$. The two plans' mean on the other coordinates is $(o+t/2)b$. Write their maximum as their average plus half their absolute difference. The random part of the difference is $\Delta W_o+(\Delta/2) W_t$ for independent sums $W_k$ of $k$ fair signs. Its deterministic offset is $b$ times the signed count of one-sided purchases. The expected absolute value of a symmetric random variable plus an offset increases with the offset's absolute value, so placing all one-sided purchases in the same plan attains the best offset. Consequently the exact optimum is
$$
\max_{o,t\ge0,\ o+t\le d}
\frac{(d-o-t)b_++(o+t/2)b+
 \tfrac12\mathbb E|\Delta W_o+(\Delta/2)W_t+tb|}{d},
$$
where $o,t$ are integers. Every pair is realizable. The comparison of the two plans yields an optimal threshold statistic with weights of two possible nonzero magnitudes. There are only $(d+1)(d+2)/2$ plan types to evaluate. This reduction uses independent fair coordinates and uniform task weights.

**Asymptotic memory price.** Allow full access to the first word, then carry at most $B$ bits across the delayed task. For $B/d\to R$ the limiting optimum is
$$
\max_{\substack{0\le t\le1,\ 0\le D\le1/2\\t(1-h_2(D))\le R}}
t[\Delta(1-D)-c].
$$
Here $h_2$ is binary entropy in bits, $t$ is the probability of buying, and $D$ is routing error conditional on buying. To prove the converse, independence of the coordinates gives $B/d\ge I(X^d;S)/d\ge I(X_J;S\mid J)$. The purchase and guessed sensor are functions of $(J,S)$. Conditional entropy on a pass is at most one bit. On a purchase the guessed sensor and its binary error determine $X_J$, giving entropy at most $h_2(D)$ by concavity. Together these imply $t(1-h_2(D))\le R$. Bayes routing achieves $D\le1/2$. For attainment, choose a fixed fraction $t$ of coordinates, use a binary Hamming rate-distortion code on them, and pass on the others. Standard limiting codes and continuity cover the endpoints. In particular, for $\Delta/2<c<\Delta$, putting $a=1-c/\Delta$ gives a unique $D_*\in(0,a)$ satisfying $D_*^a(1-D_*)^{1-a}=1/2$. This follows by differentiating $(a-D)/(1-h_2(D))$ and using strict increase of $D^a(1-D)^{1-a}$ on $(0,a)$. Until rate $1-h_2(D_*)$, time sharing only that many coded coordinates in proportion to the available rate is optimal. Above it, all coordinates can be coded at distortion $h_2^{-1}(1-R)$ for $R\le1$. This is an information-rate result. Its proof charges neither a finite codebook's installed bytes nor the work of finding an efficient encoder. ## Forming response classes from incomplete future observations

Let $u$ be an early raw name, $v$ a later context, and $P_{uv}$ a consequent next-token law. Both its membership and its response can change under training. In the controlled binary experiment a response is the vector of target probabilities given a selected sensor and the bit it emitted. Each fitted partition is task-specific. A task arrives after storage, so a deployed early state must support all relevant tasks. Counting classes in one task alone does not price that shared first state.

$$
L(P)=\sum_b\ln\frac{\mathrm B(h_b+1/2,n_b-h_b+1/2)}{\mathrm B(1/2,1/2)}
-\ln \mathcal S(m,|P|),
$$
where $\mathcal S(m,k)$ counts the partitions of $m$ early names into $k$ nonempty classes. This corresponds to uniform prior on allowed sizes and uniform partitions at each size. The learner maximizes $L$ and predicts from the posterior means $(h_b+1/2)/(n_b+1)$. An unexposed block/column has mean $1/2$ under this prior. **What an unlimited training log selects.** Assume a finite list of partitions, independent stationary draws, fixed positive exposure on each eligible context, and interior true Bernoulli parameters. Suppose a partition agrees with all eligible population laws. For each partition let $d(P)$ count its occupied independent Bernoulli cells. A misspecified partition loses linear-in-$N$ log likelihood against a compatible one because its best projected risk has positive weighted Bernoulli KL divergence. For a compatible partition, Stirling's formula in the beta integrals gives

L(P)=\ell_P(\widehat\theta_P)-\tfrac12d(P)\ln N+O_p(1)

$$
Indeed the log-likelihood increase in a true occupied cell is $n_b\,\mathrm{KL}(\widehat p_b\Vert p_b)=O_p(1)$ by Taylor expansion at $0<p_b<1$. There are finitely many cells and partitions. Write $p_{Av}(y)$ for the block's posterior predictive conditional law at a later context $v$. Define $m(A)=\Pr(A\in\pi\mid\text{data})$. Then one-step prediction is exactly
$$
q(y\mid u,v)=\sum_{A\ni u}m(A)p_{Av}(y).
$$
The coefficients for each row sum to one because a partition contains precisely one block covering that row. Even a modal one-block partition can yield distinct averaged predictions for all earlier names. The resulting continuous-valued table can be installed discretely at a chosen precision. Its predictive advantage and continuing learning cost require separate tests.

A subset recursion computes these coefficients. Set $Z_0(\varnothing)=1$ and all incompatible entries to zero. For $S\ne\varnothing$ choose its lowest indexed name $i$. Then
$$
Z_k(S)=\sum_{i\in B\subseteq S} L(B)Z_{k-1}(S\setminus B),\qquad
m(A)=\frac{L(A)\sum_{k\ge1}\lambda_k Z_{k-1}(U\setminus A)}
{\sum_{k\ge1}\lambda_k Z_k(U)}.
$$
The distinguished name counts each unordered partition once. Removing a required block proves the second formula. The direct subset version takes $O(K3^{|U|})$ arithmetic and $O(K2^{|U|})$ table entries for a cap $K$. This is an application of existing exact Bayesian clustering and cluster marginal algorithms. Eight names already allow the direct sum and the subset calculation to check one another. [Kohonen and Corander, exact clustering posteriors](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1034) · [Greenberg et al., §3.2, cluster marginals](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/file/29c4a0e4ef7d1969a94a5f4aadd20690-Paper.pdf) · [Implementation and exact update example](partition_posterior.py)

There is a useful restriction on counterexamples to storing these marginals. For two strictly positive block weight systems $L,M$ with the same prior and support, subtract their log partition probabilities and take expectations under both laws. Constants from the normalizers cancel and the shared size prior cancels pointwise, giving
$$
\mathrm{KL}(P_L\Vert P_M)+\mathrm{KL}(P_M\Vert P_L)
=\sum_A\big[m_L(A)-m_M(A)\big]\ln\frac{L(A)}{M(A)}.
$$
Consequently equal block marginals determine the entire law of the partition within this fixed exponential family. This is an identifiability property. Computing a joint marginal or recovering natural parameters can still require work, and counts controlling the next *emission* update must also be available. For example even a single Bernoulli model with symmetric beta prior has the same current mean after different amounts of balanced evidence but changes by different amounts after one more success.

The standard sequential Bayes mixture identity gives an independent measurement of how much uncertainty can cost. Fix any admitted partition $P$ and condition on the training log. Score every further observation *before* assimilating it, with the same context, mask and per-block update law for the mixture and each component. For the observations actually assimilated, write $\ell_{\rm mix},\ell_P$ for cumulative unrounded log losses. Repeated Bayes updates telescope to
$$
\ell_{\rm mix}-\ell_P=\ln\frac{w_{\rm end}(P)}{w_{\rm start}(P)}
\ \le\ -\ln w_{\rm start}(P).
$$
This bound is pathwise, including when the next context is chosen from past observations. It covers every horizon. A skipped label can still be scored, but the identity for a selected-update run applies to the admitted subsequence. Finite-precision release adds its actual score error and stored posterior weights and updates have physical costs. The natural-text test measured the score error and deployment costs. ## How much memory does continued prediction require for two noisy coins?

**PROVED for issued probabilities of deterministic, time-homogeneous predictors.** A fair source chooses a bias $p$ or $1-p$ once, with $1/2<p<1$, and produces independent draws conditional on that choice. A learner sees only the bits and must issue its next probability before seeing the next bit. It starts in a fixed state independent of the source. At a declared horizon $T$ its transition and readout tables may depend on $T$, but each step uses the same tables. Its risk is forward transcript KL relative to the Bayesian mixture, equivalently its total expected excess next-bit log loss. For **every fixed positive budget** $b$, the minimum number of reachable states needed for risk at most $b$ is $\Theta(\log T)$. Its live state therefore takes $\log_2\log T+O_{p,b}(1)$ bits. At exactly zero budget the minimum is $2T-1$ states. These statements charge all information persisting between steps, including any clock. Fixed program and forecast precision are accounted for separately below.

**Finite-horizon lower bound.** More generally, let finitely many positive-prior distinct biases lie inside $(0,1)$. Put $\alpha=\min_z\{p_z,1-p_z\}$ and $J=h(\sum_z\mu_zp_z)-\sum_z\mu_zh(p_z)>0$. For every $S$-state predictor of this kind,
$$
D_T\ \ge\ (T-S+1)_+\,\alpha^{S-1}J-H(\mu).
$$
From the starting state choose a simple path of length $\ell$ into a reachable closed communicating class of size $k$, so $\ell+k\le S$. Under every source row this particular path has probability at least $\alpha^\ell$. From each state in the class every specified member can be reached in at most $k-1$ transitions. In any complete block of $k$ prediction times in that class its expected number of visits to each member is therefore at least $\alpha^{k-1}$. There are $\lfloor(T-\ell)/k\rfloor$ complete blocks after entry when $T\ge\ell$. At any state with issued probability $q_s$ the sum $\sum_z\mu_z d(p_z\Vert q_s)$ is at least $J$, by minimization at the prior mean. Multiplying the visit bound and summing over the $k$ states gives at least $(T-S+1)_+\alpha^{S-1}J$ expected excess over the source-row oracle. The full Bayesian learner's own excess over that oracle is the mutual information, at most $H(\mu)$. Periodicity, multiple closed classes and transient initial states are allowed. A zero-probability release at a reachable state gives infinite loss as soon as that state can issue it. For fixed $b$, either $S>T/2$ or the bound forces $S\ge 1+[\ln T-O(1)]/\ln(1/\alpha)$ at large $T$.

**Matching construction.** Let $r=p/(1-p)>1$. Keep the signed excess $s$ of ones over zeros until its first hit of $\pm L$, then freeze there. On the interior the posterior odds of the positive bias are $r^s$, giving forecast $b_s=(p r^s+1-p)/(1+r^s)$. After freezing, issue the chosen side's coin bias. For each stopped path ending on the positive boundary its likelihood ratio is exactly $r^L$, independent of its duration. The probability of choosing the wrong bias conditional on either sign and its hitting time is $1/(1+r^L)$. Before hitting, the issued probabilities exactly match Bayes. After hitting, convexity of KL, or conditioning on the latent row, bounds the remaining expected price by $T d(p\Vert1-p)/(1+r^L)$. Hence $L=O_{p,b}(\log T)$ suffices and the counter has $2L+1$ states. Conditioning on the stopping time is important because it determines how many predictions remain. For zero budget every reachable signed count from $-(T-1)$ through $T-1$ must issue its distinct $b_s$ at some time before the last draw. A signed counter stopped at those extrema attains the $2T-1$ count with exact readouts. No fixed finite machine can attain a bounded positive cumulative budget for every horizon.

**Finite arithmetic.** At $p=3/4$ the endpoint biases are exactly dyadic. Keep the exact update in one signed counter. Within $|s|\le K<L$, issue a rounded approximation to $b_s$. Outside this center, issue the bias on the current side, including after freezing. In the unrestricted positive-drift walk, the expected number of visits to $j$ equals $1/(2p-1)$ for $j\ge0$ and $r^{-|j|}/(2p-1)$ for $j<0$. A positive level is hit almost surely, a negative level is hit with probability $r^{-|j|}$ by the one-sided gambler's ruin formula, and the probability of returning after each visit is $2(1-p)$. Reflection handles the other bias. Stopping reduces all pre-freeze visit counts. Put $e_K=\max_{|s|\le K}d(b_s\Vert\operatorname{round}(b_s))$ and $d_*=d(3/4\Vert1/4)$. The expected total transcript loss obeys
$$
D_T\le(2K+3-3^{-K})e_K+d_*[3^{-K}+T/(1+3^L)].
$$
The first term sums visits to the center. For an outer sign before freezing, bound predictive KL by the posterior-weighted source-row loss. Only visits on the wrong side contribute. Their expected number outside the center is at most $3^{-K}$ under the fair mixture. For any given positive budget choose $K$, then the fractional precision, then $L$. The first two choices are independent of $T$. A preassigned grid can impose a floor. For instance two ones have probability $5/16$ and require the next forecast $7/10$, which no fixed dyadic grid represents exactly. The executable $K=1$ version issues only $1/4,3/8,1/2,5/8,3/4$, makes one comparison and integer-counter update up to constant factors, and stores no prediction table. Its threshold and signed representation use $O(\log L)$ program and live bits. Its 25-state instance used one live byte in the native binary. ## A shared residual for discarded Bernoulli hypotheses

A smaller predictor can keep a selected set of latent rows and let a single updating model cover all discarded rows. For each queried arm $a$, keep its counts of successes $k_a$ and trials $n_a$, initially zero. Issue the Krichevsky–Trofimov forecast $(k_a+1/2)/(n_a+1)$ and update only the queried arm. Let $U^\pi$ denote the resulting transcript law. It uses ordinary observations and needs no list of discarded row means. Define $B(n)=4^n/\binom{2n}{n}$, including $B(0)=1$. Then on every positive-policy path, for every fixed response row,
$$
\frac{P_z^\pi(H_T)}{U^\pi(H_T)}\le\prod_a B(n_a).
$$
For one arm with $k$ successes in $n$ trials, the half-count path probability is $(1/2)_k(1/2)_{n-k}/n!$, with $(x)_k$ the rising factorial. Divide the maximum likelihood $(k/n)^k(1-k/n)^{n-k}$ by this mass. As a function of $k$ the ratio is proportional to $F(k)F(n-k)$ for $F(k)=k^k/(1/2)_k$, with $F(0)=1$. The successive ratio $F(k+1)/F(k)$ increases because the derivative of its log at positive real $x$ is $\ln(1+1/x)-1/(x+1/2)>0$, by integrating the strictly convex function $1/u$ over $[x,x+1]$. The expression extends continuously to $x=0$. The product is consequently largest at $k=0,n$, where the likelihood ratio equals $B(n)$. Multiplying the arm contributions cancels the same adaptive policy path factors. Since $B(n+1)/B(n)=2(n+1)/(2n+1)$ decreases, the largest product for $T$ observations on $m$ arms occurs at balanced integer counts. With $u=\lfloor T/m\rfloor$ and $v=T-mu$ it is $B(u)^{m-v}B(u+1)^v$. This is attained when all arms are deterministic and the experiment is balanced. Also $B(n)\le2\sqrt n$ for $n\ge1$ by induction. [Original half-count universal code](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1981.1056331) · [Exact finite maxima](coin_memory.py)

If discarded rows have total prior mass $\eta$, write $P=(1-\eta)P_R+\eta P_D$ and deploy $Q=(1-\epsilon)P_R+\epsilon U$, mixing the two *whole processes* with posterior updating. Here $P_R,P_D$ use their conditional priors. Including and then marginalizing the branch label gives
$$
D(P^\pi\Vert Q^\pi)
\le d(\eta\Vert\epsilon)+\eta E_{P_D^\pi}\sum_a\ln B(N_a).
$$
At $\epsilon=\eta$ the first term vanishes. The residual budget is at most $\eta m[\ln2+\tfrac12\ln(1+T/m)]$ for every adaptive policy, including one that first probes a new arm late. This reduces a possible linear loss from a memoryless merge to a logarithmic upper bound without access to the omitted prototypes. The full hybrid also pays for its branch odds, the retained model, and both component updates. The residual uses at most $2m\lceil\log_2(T+1)\rceil$ persistent counter bits and one increment of its trial count and an optional increment of its success count per label. Exact output is a rational ratio of two horizon-sized integers. Arbitrary rounding and overflow need separate treatment. On the known symmetric two-coin family the small stopped counter has a tighter horizon-specific guarantee. A source with changing biases or unobserved dependence across trials conditional on its latent row requires a different residual law.

## What does it cost to learn the prototype that a small state uses?

**PROVED for an ordinary paired-observation experiment.** An unknown amplitude $\theta\in[a,b]\subset[1/2,1)$ is common to all episodes, with $a<b$. Each episode independently chooses a fair orientation and draws conditionally independent bits at bias $\theta$ or $1-\theta$. A learner observes $N$ independent two-draw episodes, transmits a model packet $M$ of at most $B$ bits, then predicts a fresh episode of $T$ draws. All training information available at deployment passes through this packet. Program code, any public randomness and the interval are independent of $\theta$. Let $P_\theta^T$ be the fresh law with orientation hidden. Allow the decoder any future updates and take the minimax expected excess $\mathcal R=\inf_{\text{encoder,decoder}}\sup_\theta E_\theta D(P_\theta^T\Vert Q_M^T)$. For $T\ge1$ and $N,B\ge0$,
$$
\left|\mathcal R-\ln^+\!\left(\sqrt T\,\max\{(N+1)^{-\beta},2^{-B}\}\right)\right|\le C_{a,b},
\qquad
\beta=\begin{cases}1/2,&a>1/2,\\1/4,&a=1/2.\end{cases}
$$
The constant is independent of the three budgets and $\ln^+ x=\max(0,\ln x)$. Thus to keep a fixed positive cumulative loss allowance, training needs order $T$ independent pairs if $a>1/2$, and order $T^2$ if the interval reaches $1/2$. Both regimes require at least $\tfrac12\log_2 T-O(1)$ bits of installed information, even with unrestricted adaptation during the fresh episode. The constants in the corresponding sufficient budgets can be chosen for any fixed positive allowance. Independently re-sampling the orientation for each *single* training bit makes its law fair at every amplitude and gives zero calibration information at any sample size.

**Lower bounds.** For any prior on $\Theta$, conditional independence of the fresh path and the packet given the amplitude gives
$$
E D(P_\Theta^T\Vert Q_M^T)\ge I(\Theta;Y^T\mid M)
=I(\Theta;Y^T)-I(M;Y^T).
$$
The difference between the left side and the conditional information is the average KL of the conditional mixture to $Q_M^T$. Write $\tilde\theta=1/2+|\bar Y-1/2|$. Folding the empirical mean around $1/2$ is 1-Lipschitz, and maps either orientation's true bias to $\theta$. Hence $E_\theta(\tilde\theta-\theta)^2\le1/(4T)$. The Gaussian maximum-entropy inequality for the estimation error gives $I(\Theta;Y^T)\ge h_{\rm diff}(\Theta)+\tfrac12\ln T-\tfrac12\ln(\pi e/2)$. Here $h_{\rm diff}$ denotes differential entropy; the uniform prior on an interval of width $w$ contributes $\ln w$.

The first bit of a training pair is fair and the probability its second bit agrees is $v(\theta)=\theta^2+(1-\theta)^2$. The two patterns for each agreement status are equiprobable. All calibration information is therefore in $N$ independent Bernoulli agreement indicators. For the full interval prior, $I(M;Y^T)\le\min\{B\ln2,I(\Theta;\text{training})\}$. The latter information is at most $\ln[4^N/\binom{2N}{N}]\le\tfrac12\ln(N+1)+\ln2$, by the half-count path envelope and the KL centroid identity. This proves both the sample and bit converses on an interval above $1/2$. Private encoding randomness cannot increase these bounds. Independent shared randomness can be conditioned on throughout. A data-dependent code, timing side channel or source-correlated deployment seed must be included in the packet.

At $a=1/2$ also choose a prior on $[1/2,1/2+w]$ with $w=c(N+1)^{-1/4}$ and $0<c\le b-1/2$. Since $v=1/2+2(\theta-1/2)^2$, the training KL to $N$ independent fair agreements is at most $16Nw^4\le16c^4$, using Bernoulli KL bounded by chi-square divergence. Thus $I(\Theta;\text{training})$ is bounded under this localized prior. The fresh entropy bound now yields $\tfrac12\ln T-\tfrac14\ln(N+1)-O_b(1)$. Use the original full-width prior for the independent packet-size lower bound. Taking the larger bound and nonnegativity gives the stated converse.

**Attainment with an updating decoder.** For $N>0$, maximize ordinary conditional likelihood of each pair's second bit given its first, equivalently estimate $v$ by the empirical agreement fraction, clamp to $[v(a),v(b)]$ and invert. The inverse is Lipschitz on the separated interval. At $a=1/2$ its square-root modulus gives mean-square error at most $1/(4\sqrt N)$ from the Bernoulli variance bound. Quantize the estimated amplitude to an interval midpoint using $2^B$ bins, or choose a fixed center when $N=0$. With $s=\max\{(N+1)^{-\beta},2^{-B}\}$ the transmitted center $C$ satisfies $\sup_\theta E(C-\theta)^2\le C_0s^2$.

Decode the center as a distribution on amplitudes with density proportional to $\exp[-(u-C)^2/(2s^2)]$ on $[a,b]$, and update its mixture on the fresh stream. Put $\delta=\min\{s,T^{-1/2},b-a\}$. The interval within $\delta$ of the true amplitude has intersection length at least $\delta$ with $[a,b]$. Its assigned mass is at least $\delta(\sqrt{2\pi}s)^{-1}\exp[-(|C-\theta|+\delta)^2/(2s^2)]$. Conditional on the same latent orientation, $D(P_\theta^T\Vert P_u^T)\le T d(\theta\Vert u)\le T(\theta-u)^2/[a(1-b)]$. Restrict the mixture to this interval, pay minus the log of its mass, then apply convexity in the second argument. Averaging over the training packet proves the upper bound $\ln^+(s\sqrt T)+O_{a,b}(1)$. A finite grid with spacing comparable to $T^{-1/2}$ also suffices: assign each grid point the truncated distribution's mass in its Voronoi cell. Every cell touching the chosen interval has a representative within $\delta+O(T^{-1/2})$ of the true amplitude, so its component divergence stays bounded. This decoder can use $O(\sqrt T)$ amplitude hypotheses. The bound charges information transmitted in $M$ and allows additional computation to issue its forecasts. [Classical cumulative Bayes risk and prior-mass bounds](https://tr.soe.ucsc.edu/sites/default/files/technical-reports/UCSC-CRL-96-27.pdf) · [Related memory-assisted universal coding with shared source observations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.4338)

**A small program when enough data are available.** Install a single quantized estimate $\hat\theta$. The unrestricted fitted mixture has excess at most $T E d(\theta\Vert\hat\theta)$ by coupling the episode orientations. This already gives any fixed positive allowance with sufficiently large constant factors in the sample and bit budgets in both regimes. It can track a full signed count in $O(\log T)$ live bits. On a separated interval the small stopped counter achieves the same acquisition orders with just $\log_2\log T+O(1)$ live bits. Set $r_a=a/(1-a)>1$. Until first hitting $\pm L$, issue the posterior forecast for $\hat\theta$ given the signed count. The KL of this stopped prefix is at most $E_\theta[\min(\sigma,T)]\,d(\theta\Vert\hat\theta)$ by including the latent orientation on both sides. After a boundary hit, issuing the fitted bias of the indicated sign costs at most $d(\theta\Vert\hat\theta)$ per remaining draw when correct, and at most a fixed $D_{a,b}$ otherwise. Under the true amplitude the conditional probability of choosing the wrong orientation given the sign and hitting time is $1/[1+(\theta/(1-\theta))^L]$. Together the bounds give
$$
D(P_\theta^T\Vert Q_{\hat\theta,L}^T)\le T d(\theta\Vert\hat\theta)+T D_{a,b}/(1+r_a^L).
$$
Thus $L=O_{a,b,\epsilon}(\log T)$ suffices for any fixed $\epsilon>0$ after calibrating and pricing $\hat\theta$. The known-amplitude lower bound applies even with perfect training and any source-independent starting state or randomized choice of trained machine. The compact live state and the learned constant have different costs. Endpoints approaching fair are the source of the quadratic acquisition requirement. The live-state upper bound using $r_a>1$ has only been established on a separated interval.

One finite-precision realization uses the fitted mixture only for a fixed central band $|s|\le K$. Its stopped-prefix KL on the histories before the boundary is bounded as above for the full fitted mixture; for central steps, approximate the positive conditional forecast by a grid value within $\delta_q$. On an interval bounded away from zero and one the change in expected log loss there is at most a constant times $\delta_q$ per central visit. The expected number of such visits is bounded in $T$ uniformly over the interval by the biased-walk return formula. For outer steps before freezing, compare the issued side's bias directly with the true row. Correct-sign cost is at most $d(\theta\Vert\hat\theta)$ per draw, and expected wrong-sign visits there are $O_a(r_a^{-K})$. Dropping outer terms from the full fitted prefix KL can only decrease that nonnegative conditional-KL sum, so the joint bound may pay at most an extra $T d(\theta\Vert\hat\theta)$ this way. Choose $K$ and central precision for the fixed allowance. The fitted endpoint nevertheless needs precision of order $T^{-1/2}$ across the continuous family, already present in the packet. The measured implementation decodes its dyadic endpoints exactly and its central probabilities on a Q12 grid at the registered horizons. ## What can short episodes teach about an unrestricted future mixing law?

**PROVED for ordinary grouped Bernoulli observations.** Each new episode draws a fresh bias $p$ from the same arbitrary probability law $G$ on $[0,1]$, then emits iid bits conditional on $p$. Let $P_G^T$ be the ordered law of one fresh $T$-draw episode. The prediction cost is total expected excess causal log loss above an oracle knowing $G$ but facing a new latent bias. It equals $D(P_G^T\Vert Q)$ for a proper causal forecaster. In the population experiment the learner knows the distribution of an ordinary group of length $m$, equivalently the first $m$ moments of $G$. Any shorter groups, including prefixes selected from observations, can be produced from independent full groups. Define $R_{m,T}$ by minimizing the worst cost over all $G$ using a decoder of those moments. The worst case includes the choice of moment vector. Some particular vectors, such as mean zero, already fix every future law.

With natural logarithms, $n=m+1$, and absolute constants,
$$
R_{m,T}=\ln^+(\sqrt T/n)+O(1).
$$
It is zero exactly for $T\le m$. Through the entire remaining small-loss range $\sqrt T\le n\le T$,
$$
c\exp(-C n^2/T)\le R_{m,T}\le C'\exp(-c'n^2/T).
$$
Thus groups much shorter than $T$ can suffice for a small predictive penalty even when the mixing law itself remains unidentified. Every population statement below grants exact real moments. The finite-data result later prices their acquisition.

**A predictor using only the supplied moments.** Write $p=\cos^2(\theta/2)$ and reflect the angle law evenly around the circle. Its cosine moments $c_j=E_G\cos(j\theta)$ for $j\le m$ are known polynomial functions of the group law. Convolve the angle law with the Fejér kernel $F_n(x)=n^{-1}|\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}e^{irx}|^2$, using uniform probability measure on the circle. Its density is $1+2\sum_{j=1}^{m}(1-j/n)c_j\cos(j\theta)$, everywhere nonnegative with integral one. Mix the future Bernoulli processes once using that density. Its one-step probabilities continue to update on the fresh bits.

For any ordered path with $k$ successes let $L(\theta)=p^k(1-p)^{T-k}$. On $[0,\pi]$ it increases toward, then decreases away from, its maximum at $\cos^2(\theta_*/2)=k/T$. Put $h=\tfrac14\min(T^{-1/2},n^{-1})$. If $|\theta-\theta_*|\ge h$, integrate along the segment of length $h$ toward the maximum. Otherwise choose a length-$h$ segment from the maximum into the interval. On that segment $L\ge e^{-2}L(\theta_*)$. Indeed within $T^{-1/2}$ of the maximum every positive fitted half-angle sine or cosine is at least half its value at the maximum, since that value is at least $T^{-1/2}$ and each is $1/2$-Lipschitz. Consequently $|(\ln L)''|\le 4T$, with zero-count terms omitted and the endpoint maxima having zero derivative. On both chosen segments the kernel is at least $c n$ by the elementary sine formula for $F_n$, as the displacement from $\theta$ is at most $2h$. The resulting path likelihood is at least $c' n h L(\theta)$. Averaging over $G$ gives the claimed upper order even for the worst individual path. When $T\le m$, integrate the actual degree-$T$ likelihood polynomial against the known moments to predict exactly.

**What remains hidden.** Put equal masses at angles $U+2\pi j/n$, $j=0,\ldots,n-1$, fold to $p$, and let $U$ be uniform on $[0,\pi/n]$. All these laws have identical first $m$ moments, those of the arcsine distribution. From a fresh episode estimate $U$ by the distance of $2\arccos\sqrt{\bar Y}$ to $(2\pi/n)\mathbb Z$. The distance is 1-Lipschitz and exact at every true angle after folding. Under a single coin, the expected squared chord between $(\sqrt{\bar Y},\sqrt{1-\bar Y})$ and $(\sqrt p,\sqrt{1-p})$ is at most $1/T$, using each binomial variance divided by the corresponding true probability, with deterministic endpoints treated directly. Squared angular error is at most $\pi^2$ times squared chord. The Gaussian maximum-entropy bound applied to the estimation error then gives $I(U;Y^T)\ge\ln(\sqrt T/n)-\tfrac12\ln(2\pi e)$. The common training moments carry no information about the phase. Mutual information lower-bounds the average excess of every forecast. If only $K$ possible mixing laws are entertained, averaging the corresponding transcript laws caps the pathwise excess at $\ln K$. A continuum matters in the diverging worst case.

**Positive lower bound beyond the training depth.** Under the arcsine reference $H$, write $v_j(p)=\cos(j\theta)$ and $h_{t,j}(k)=E_H[v_j(p)\mid K_t=k]$ for the success count. Sampling $K_t$ from $p$ and then a new bias from its beta posterior defines a self-adjoint operator on $L^2(H)$ because its quadratic form is the product of the two conditional expectations. On degree $j$ it preserves the polynomial space and multiplies the leading coefficient by
$$
\lambda_{t,j}=\prod_{r=0}^{j-1}\frac{t-r}{t+1+r}
$$
for $j\le t$, and is zero on the higher orthogonal modes. This follows from the ordinary binomial factorial moments and the beta posterior moment $(k+1/2)^{\overline j}/(t+1)^{\overline j}$. Orthogonality makes $v_j$ an eigenfunction. Hence $E_H h_{t,j}^2=\lambda_{t,j}/2$ for positive $j$. This is the arcsine specialization of the beta-binomial Gibbs spectrum. The mixing laws $(1\pm v_n)dH$ have identical moments through $m$. Their count likelihood ratios to the reference are $1\pm h_{T,n}$. Their binary-prior information radius is at least $E_H h_{T,n}^2/2=\lambda_{T,n}/4$ by the scalar binary entropy inequality. For $n\le T/2$, summing the logarithms gives $\lambda_{T,n}^{-1}\le\exp(2n^2/T)$. For $n>T/2$, $\lambda_{T,n}\ge1/\binom{2T}{T}\ge4^{-T}$. This proves the positive exponential lower bound without requiring atomic alternatives. [Diaconis, Khare and Saloff-Coste, Proposition 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0808.3852#page=14)

**Exponential small-loss upper bound.** For a function $|g(k)|\le1$ of the future count, its expected value $f(\theta)$ under one bias is an entire trigonometric polynomial. The absolute sum of its binomial probability terms at complex angle $\theta+iy$ is $(\cosh y)^T$, giving $|f(\theta+iy)|\le e^{Ty^2/2}$. Cauchy's estimate at radius $\sqrt{j/T}$ gives $\|f^{(j)}\|_\infty\le(\sqrt{e jT})^j$. Let $A$ convolve on the circle with a normalized squared Fejér kernel $F_{\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1}^2$, of degree at most $m$. Its mean absolute angular displacement is at most $C/n$: the normalizing integral is comparable to $n$, and $F_d(x)\le C\min(d,1/(dx^2))$ for principal displacements. Thus $\|(I-A)u\|_\infty\le(C/n)\|u'\|_\infty$. Derivatives commute with convolution. The function $[I-(I-A)^j]f$ has degree at most $m$ and is even. Its error is at most $(C\sqrt{jT}/n)^j$. Taking $j$ a sufficiently small constant times $n^2/T$, rounded down, bounds the TV distance between the count laws of any two compatible mixing measures by $\delta=\min(1,C_0 e^{-c_0 n^2/T})$. The bounded range where $j<1$ is covered by the preceding constant upper bound.

Choose a compatible reference and mix its transcript law with mass $\delta$ of the safe Fejér predictor. For $n\ge\sqrt T$ the latter dominates every compatible path up to a constant. The new law $Q$ is within $2\delta$ in TV of every compatible $P$, with $P/Q\le C/\delta$. For $1\le x\le M$, $x\ln x\le (x-1)\ln M/(1-1/M)$. Summing positive likelihood-ratio contributions bounds $D(P\Vert Q)$ by a constant times $\delta\ln(C/\delta)$, yielding the upper exponential order. Sufficiency of the count preserves both KL and TV for the full ordered paths. This existence proof permits continuous moments and the choice of a compatible reference. The simple Fejér decoder by itself is only claimed to attain the preceding additive-constant bound.

**Finite ordinary groups.** With $N\ge(m+1)^2$ independent length-$m$ training groups, the minimax expected total fresh loss is
$$
\ln^+\!\frac{\sqrt T}{\min\{m+1,\sqrt{m\ln(1+N)}\}}+O(1).
$$
The associated lower bound holds for every $N\ge1$, $m\ge1$. To see it, use the shifted angular grid with $\ell$ equally spaced points instead of $n$. Relative to the arcsine count law its density at length $m$ has cosine contributions only at multiples of $\ell$. Expanding the count likelihood polynomial in the orthogonal $v_j$ basis gives $1+2\sum_{s\ge1:s\ell\le m}\cos(s\ell U)h_{m,s\ell}$. The $h_{m,j}$ are orthogonal under the reference count law by self-adjointness. Since $\lambda_{m,j}\le e^{-j^2/(2m)}$ (sum $\ln[(m+1+r)/(m-r)]\ge(1+2r)/(2m)$), the one-group chi-square divergence is at most $2/[\exp(\ell^2/(2m))-1]$. Take $\ell=\min\{m+1,\lceil\sqrt{2m\ln(1+2N)}\rceil\}$. In the first case the training laws agree exactly. Otherwise all $N$ groups reveal at most one nat about the phase by KL to the product reference. Subtracting that information from the fresh-phase lower bound proves the claim, even for adaptively shortened prefixes of at most $N$ such groups.

For an attainable upper bound, estimate $c_j$ by averaging $h_{m,j}(K)/\lambda_{m,j}$ over the ordinary training counts, clipping to $[-1,1]$. The spectral identity proves unbiasedness before clipping, and clipping cannot increase error. With $d\le m/2$, the uniform Fejér density error at degree $d$ has expectation at most $2d\exp(2d^2/m)/\sqrt N$. More precisely this upper-bounds $E e$ for $e=2\sum_{j\le d}|\hat c_j-c_j|$. The estimated Toeplitz matrix with entry $\hat c_{|i-j|}$ and diagonal one may fail positivity. The true matrix is positive semidefinite because it is a trigonometric Gram matrix. Adding $s=\max(0,-\lambda_{\min})$ to the diagonal suffices and $s\le e$ by the row-sum norm. The resulting normalized Fejér form is $(\hat f+s)/(1+s)$. Give half its mass to the uniform density to obtain a safe reported law $g$. Since $\hat f+s\ge0$ and $f\le\hat f+s+e$, $f/g\le 2(1+s)\max(1,e)$. Thus the expected extra total path price is at most $\ln2+2\ln(1+Ee)$ independently of $T$. Fixed uniform floors in $(0,1)$ change constants. Rounding each input cosine moment to $O(1/d)$ accuracy only adds $O(1)$ to $e$. Select $d$ comparable, with a small constant, to $\min(m/2,\sqrt{m\ln(1+N)})$ (use the uniform density for bounded cases). For $N\ge(m+1)^2$, $d\le C N^{1/4}\sqrt{\ln(1+N)}$ and the expected error bound is uniformly bounded. This proves the finite-sample rate in the stated regime. It does not assert optimality below that sample threshold. For $\sqrt T\gg m$, matching the exact-moment price to within a fixed additive constant can require exponentially many groups in $m$, and an exponential number suffices. Transport-distance and smooth-density estimation use related polynomial tools under different losses. **Causal implementation.** For fitted coefficients $b_j$ in density $1+2\sum b_j\cos(j\theta)$ put $F(t,k)=1+2\sum b_j h_{t,j}(k)$. The next success probability after $k$ ones in $t$ draws is $(k+1/2)F(t+1,k+1)/[(t+1)F(t,k)]$. Compute the modes with $h_0=1$, $h_1=(2k-t)/(t+1)$, and
$$
(t+1+j)h_{j+1}=2(2k-t)h_j-(t+1-j)h_{j-1}.
$$
Integrate the derivative of $\sin\theta\cos(j\theta)L(\theta)$ with the uniform angular reference. The endpoint terms vanish and the cosine product formulas give this recurrence. Modes above $t$ vanish. The trained density, two integer counts, $O(d)$ operations per query and a constant number of temporary scalar registers suffice. Training uses an $m+1$-bin histogram. ## When does an ordinary bit justify buying a reset?

**PROVED for the following two environments.** A session starts with equal probabilities for $E=0,1$. Each new coin is independent conditional on $E$. Its bias law is $(7/12)\delta_{1/8}+(5/12)\delta_{7/8}$ for $E=0$, and $(7/15)\delta_{1/2}+(8/15)\delta_{7/8}$ for $E=1$. After observing $X$, keep the coin or buy an independent coin at cost $c$ nats. Observe its bit $B$, then forecast all $T\ge1$ bits $Y$ of another independent coin. The oracle knows the environments' laws, observes $X,B$ and must infer $E$. The benchmark for excess report loss knows $E$ and faces the same fresh coin.

Before a purchase, both actions have the same law of $Y$ conditional on $X=x$. The reduction in expected optimal report loss from seeing $B$, under action $a$, is $I(B;Y\mid X=x,a)$. The optimal policy buys if the difference of these reductions (reset minus keep) exceeds $c$. Exchangeability makes the fresh count sufficient for $Y$. The same criterion applied to two separately fitted action laws is an available decision rule. Fitting each action independently can leave incompatible report marginals. Evaluation in that case needs the actual deployed loss for the chosen action as well as the decision's value under a correct readout.

In the two environments the probabilities of success on a new coin are $7/16$ and $7/10$. On the original coin, after a one the success probabilities are both $3/4$. The kept bit then carries zero further information about $E$ or $Y$. The bought bit has positive information for all $T\ge1$. Its covariance with the first report bit is the posterior variance of the two distinct means. After a zero, the kept bit's success probabilities are $7/36$ and $7/12$. Send that bit through a channel which outputs a one with probability $49/160$ on zero and $157/160$ on one. Under either environment the output has exactly the reset law. The channel has full support. The kept bit is correlated with the first fresh bit since the respective success probabilities are strictly increasing in $E$. Equality in data processing would require its two different conditional report laws to coincide whenever both input bits can produce the same output. Thus the keep decision strictly dominates a free reset after zero. For $0\le c<I(B_{\rm reset};Y\mid X=1)$ the unique optimal policy is to keep after zero and buy after one. Exchanging the two bit labels exchanges the decisions. A law with one fixed bias per environment makes both experiments equivalent, so any positive rent favors keeping. ## What does it cost to learn and store unknown persistent prototypes?

**PROVED for an upper bound on the number of interior bias values.** Fix $K\ge1$, a compact interval $0<a<b<1$ and a group length $m$. The environment is any law $G$ supported on at most $K$ points of $[a,b]$. Neither locations nor probabilities are given. Calibration consists of $N\ge1$ independent groups of $m$ ordinary bits, each using its own latent bias from $G$. Calibration reaches the forecaster only through a packet $M$ with at most $2^B$ possible values, $B$ a nonnegative integer. It forecasts all $T\ge1$ bits from another independent bias. Price the expected total causal log loss in excess of an observer who knows $G$ and must infer that new bias. Thus the risk is $E_{M\mid G}D(P_G^T\Vert Q_M)$, scoring the normalized prediction made by the actual recipient. The decoder can condition on every new observation and knows $K,m,N,T,a,b$. Supremize over $G$ after choosing the encoder and decoder. Write $R$ for the resulting minimax risk.

For $m\ge2K-1$,
$$
R=\ln^+\!\left(\sqrt T\max\{N^{-1/(4K-2)},2^{-B/K}\}\right)+O_{K,m,a,b}(1).
$$
For $m<2K-1$, $R=\tfrac12\ln T+O_{K,m,a,b}(1)$ even if the exact ordinary training distribution is supplied. The constants are independent of $T,N,B$. For any fixed small positive loss allowance and sufficient group length, necessary budgets have orders $N=\Omega(T^{2K-1})$ and $B\ge(K/2)\log_2T-O(1)$. Suitable constant factors in the sample budget and additive constants in the packet budget suffice at that allowance. The logarithmic formula alone addresses additive accuracy. The last assertion about arbitrarily small allowances uses the refinement below.

**Turning short counts into a reusable packet.** For each $j=1,\ldots,2K-1$, average $(k)_j/(m)_j$ over the observed counts $k$, with falling factorials. The expectations are the power moments of $G$ and every summand lies in $[0,1]$. Let $\widehat G$ minimize the maximum deviation from the estimated moments among laws with at most $K$ atoms. A minimizer exists on the compact parameter domain, allowing repeated locations and zero weights. Its maximum true moment discrepancy $\epsilon$ has expectation at most $C_K/\sqrt N$ by comparison with $G$. This compact estimator is an existence construction for general $K$.

Here is a short form of the sparse moment comparison. For two candidate laws let $D$ be the difference of their cumulative distribution functions and $W=\int|D|$. This is the one-dimensional Wasserstein distance. At most $2K-1$ gaps between consecutive support values have nonzero imbalance. Place one root at a support point between successive opposite-sign portions. A polynomial $q$ with these $r\le2K-2$ simple roots and a suitable overall sign has $qD\ge0$. With $A'=q$, integration by parts bounds $\int qD$ by $C_K\epsilon$ because $A$ has degree at most $2K-1$ and bounded coefficients. Outside radius $W/(4r)$ of the roots at least $W/2$ of the integral of $|D|$ survives, using $|D|\le1$. Thus $\int qD\ge c_K W^{r+1}\ge c_KW^{2K-1}$. For $r=0$ use $|q|=1$. Consequently $E W_1(G,\widehat G)\le C_K N^{-1/(4K-2)}$. The inverse-moment exponent and the finite-mixture estimation rates have established precedents. Here the target includes a charged packet and the ordered likelihood of one future group. [Wu and Yang, Proposition 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.07237) · [Heinrich and Kahn](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.04313) · [Rabani, Schulman and Swamy on finite snapshots](https://users.cms.caltech.edu/~schulman/Papers/rabaniSchulmanSwamy14.pdf)

Give each of $K$ slots $\lfloor B/K\rfloor$ bits. Pad missing locations by duplication. Round the fitted locations in $[a,b]$ to this resolution, using the interval midpoint for zero bits. Their distances move by at most $C2^{-B/K}$. For a fresh true bias $p$ write $d(p)$ for distance to the nearest transmitted location. Coupling $G$ and $\widehat G$ gives $E_{\rm train,p}d(p)\le C_K(N^{-1/(4K-2)}+2^{-B/K})$. Let $r=\max\{N^{-1/(4K-2)},2^{-B/K},T^{-1/2}\}$. Place a fixed regular prediction grid on $[a,b]$ with $\lceil(b-a)\sqrt T\rceil$ equal subintervals. Round the packet locations to grid indices $j_1,\ldots,j_K$. At grid index $\ell$ assign mass proportional to
$$
\sum_{i=1}^K 2^{-\lfloor|\ell-j_i|/s\rfloor},\qquad s=\lceil r\sqrt T\rceil.
$$
Normalize and mix the resulting Bernoulli processes once per reporting group. Each center contributes at most $4s+1$ before normalization. The grid point nearest $p$ differs by at most $T^{-1/2}$, so predicting from it costs at most $C_{a,b}$ nats against that component, by $T\,{\rm kl}(p\Vert p')\le T(p-p')^2/[p'(1-p')]$. The negative log of its mixture mass is at most $\ln(K(4s+1))+C_{a,b}(1+d(p)/r)$. Averaging proves the stated risk upper bound. It works with just the locations in the learned packet. The deterministic decoder expands them to $O(\sqrt T)$ shared masses for this known horizon. Two counters identify the complete history likelihood; a direct grid update takes $O(\sqrt T)$ arithmetic per draw. Smoothing costs log mass near each true point and protects a slightly misplaced centroid from loss linear in $T$.

**A continuum hidden at an atom collision.** Take a symmetric $K$-by-$K$ tridiagonal matrix $J(z)$ with unit off-diagonals, zero diagonal except for the last entry $z$, and spectral measure at the first coordinate. For $K=1$ this is the single point $z$. In general every eigenvalue is simple, and both endpoint coordinates of every eigenvector are nonzero by the tridiagonal recurrence. Eigenvalue derivatives in $z$ are the squared last coordinates. On a small fixed interval of $z$ all derivatives are bounded below and the ranges of the different eigenvalue branches are disjoint. The spectral weights are the squared first coordinates. Its $j$th moment is $(J(z)^j)_{11}$. A closed walk from the first index using the last diagonal needs at least $2K-1$ steps. At exactly that degree the coefficient of $z$ is one. Consequently all these laws share their first $2K-2$ moments and every support branch moves.

Push each spectral value $x$ to $p=(a+b)/2+\delta x$ with a sufficiently small scale. Each length-$m$ path probability is a polynomial in $p$ of degree at most $m$. Its difference from the law at a fixed reference $z$ is $O_{K,m}(\delta^{2K-1})$ uniformly in the path, by Taylor expansion and the common moments. Path probabilities are bounded below by $\min(a,1-b)^m$. Hence one-group KL to the reference is $O_{K,m,a,b}(\delta^{4K-2})$. For $z$ uniform on its interval and $\delta=cN^{-1/(4K-2)}$, all the training groups reveal at most a constant number of nats about $z$, using KL to the independent reference product. If $m<2K-1$, the laws agree exactly and $\delta$ can be constant.

There is a function recovering $z$ from any of that law's true support values, with Lipschitz constant $C_K/\delta$. Invert each branch, interpolate across their gaps and extend at the ends. Applying that function to the fresh empirical mean estimates $z$ with mean squared error at most $C_K/(T\delta^2)$. Gaussian maximum entropy for the estimation error gives $I(z;Y^T)\ge\ln(\sqrt T\delta)-C_{K,a,b}$. Conditional information lower-bounds the average prediction risk with a packet. Because training and report are independent given $z$, the loss is at least $I(z;Y^T)-I(z;\text{training})$. This proves the sample converse and the entire below-threshold order. The half-count rule gives the matching universal upper order there.

**How many locations can one packet carry?** Independently choose $K$ biases $U_i$ uniformly in fixed disjoint subintervals of $[a,b]$ and use equal mixture weights. Let $J$ be the uniform fresh component, independent of the packet. Revealing $J$ to the lower-bound calculation changes information by at most $\ln K$. Conditional on $J=i$, the sample mean estimates $U_i$ with squared error at most $1/(4T)$, so its report carries at least $\tfrac12\ln T-C$ nats before seeing the packet. The part predictable from the packet is at most $I(U_i;M)$ by data processing. Independence of the $U_i$ gives $\sum_i I(U_i;M)\le I((U_i)_i;M)\le B\ln2$. The resulting lower bound is $\tfrac12\ln T-(B/K)\ln2-O_{K,a,b}(1)$. These are independent sample and bit converses on potentially different hard environments. Taking their maximum is valid for minimax risk. Hiding an encoder's random choice from the evaluator and scoring the marginal prediction over all possible packets would define a different experiment.

**Small absolute allowances.** The same sign-polynomial comparison bounds the squared transport error. On a support gap of length $g$ with absolute CDF imbalance $u$, its middle half is at least $g/4$ from every root. It follows that $ug^{2K-1}\le C_K\epsilon$. Under the monotone coupling, mass $u$ crosses that gap. Squaring a displacement crossing several gaps costs at most $(2K-1)$ times the sum of the squared crossed gaps. For $K\ge2$ this yields $W_2^2\le(2K-1)\sum ug^2\le C_K\epsilon^{2/(2K-1)}$. For $K=1$ the empirical mean directly has mean squared error $O(1/N)$. Thus $E W_2^2\le C_KN^{-1/(2K-1)}$ in either case. For $0<\eta<1$, code each fitted location to precision $O(\sqrt{\eta/T})$. Code the vector of square roots of the fitted masses with an $O(\sqrt\eta)$-net on their positive unit sphere. Its size is at most $C_K\eta^{-(K-1)/2}$. Add $\sqrt\eta$ to each rounded coordinate, renormalize in Euclidean norm and square. This keeps each fitted-to-rounded root ratio bounded and their squared distance $O_K(\eta)$. The chi-square bound on KL then gives $D(\widehat w\Vert w')\le C_K\eta$. Pair each true bias to a fitted index using the transport coupling. Conditional KL to its rounded location is at most $C_{a,b}T$ times squared distance. Marginalizing that index yields risk at most $C_{K,m,a,b}(TN^{-1/(2K-1)}+\eta)$. There are at most $C_{K,a,b}T^{K/2}\eta^{-(2K-1)/2}$ distinct packets. This proves sufficiency at any fixed positive allowance, and vanishing loss when $N/T^{2K-1}\to\infty$ and $B-(K/2)\log_2 T\to\infty$. It leaves open the exact minimax price once the loss tends to zero.

## How much prediction accuracy is lost by rounding a learned persistence state?

**PROVED conditional on the learned emissions and refresh law.** Let $Z$ be binary. Before the next observable word its probability of being one is $w$. Each regime issues a positive normalized raw-word law $q_i(\cdot\mid x)$ for the actual observed history $x$. Both filters under comparison use those same functions on that history. After observing $y$, update the log odds by $\ln(q_1(y\mid x)/q_0(y\mid x))$, producing posterior $u$. Then the regime persists with probability $1-h$ and refreshes to Bernoulli $\pi$ with probability $0<h\le1$, $0<\pi<1$. The new prior is $g(u)=(1-h)u+h\pi$. A rounded filter stores log odds on a regular grid of spacing $\Delta$ from $\operatorname{logit}(h\pi)$ to $\operatorname{logit}(1-h(1-\pi))$. Start with $\pi$, rounding it on the same grid. For $h=1$ the exact prior is constant and needs no carry.

Set $a=g(1)$ and $b=g(0)$. In log odds the transition is $\ln[(a e^s+b)/((1-a)e^s+1-b)]$. Maximizing its derivative gives the Lipschitz constant
$$
\tau=\frac{\sqrt{a(1-b)}-\sqrt{b(1-a)}}{\sqrt{a(1-b)}+\sqrt{b(1-a)}}<1.
$$
Indeed the denominator of the derivative divided by $e^s$ is minimized at $e^{2s}=b(1-b)/(a(1-a))$. Observing the same word adds the same log likelihood ratio to both filters. Thus rounding to the nearest grid point gives prior log-odds discrepancy at most $\Delta/[2(1-\tau)]$ at every release. The derivative of $\ln((1-w)q_0(y)+wq_1(y))$ with respect to prior log odds is the posterior of regime one minus $w$, of absolute value at most one. On **any** length-$T$ transcript the sum of absolute conditional log-score discrepancies is therefore at most $T\Delta/[2(1-\tau)]$. Positive HMM contraction and its role in predictive memory are established techniques. [Atar and Zeitouni on filter stability](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~zeitouni/pdf/diffusionsrev.pdf) · [Feng and Meng, 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13760v1#S3)

A sharper expected-loss guarantee accounts for information spent on the actual words. Work under the exact learned source. Let $V$ be Bernoulli KL from the exact prior to the rounded filter's prior. The joint KL chain rule for regime and word gives $V=d+E D$, where $d$ is the KL of the issued word laws and $D$ is the Bernoulli KL of the two posteriors after that word. This holds for arbitrary positive raw-word emissions. After applying the same refresh, convexity bounds the new prior KL by $(1-h)D$. Let $v=g(u)$ be the exact new prior and $\hat v$ be the new approximate prior before rounding. Perturbing its natural logit by $\epsilon$ adds at most $(\hat v-v)\epsilon+\epsilon^2/8$ to KL, using the Bernoulli log-partition Hessian bound $1/4$. Pinsker gives $|\hat v-v|\le(1-h)\sqrt{D/2}$. With $|\epsilon|\le\Delta/2$, Young's inequality absorbs $hD$, leaving
$$
V_{\rm next}\le D+\frac{\Delta^2}{32}\left(1+\frac{(1-h)^2}{h}\right).
$$
The initial KL is at most $\Delta^2/32$. Telescoping the chain rules bounds total expected transcript KL by $T\Delta^2[1+(1-h)^2/h]/32\le T\Delta^2/(32h)$. If each update independently rounds to adjacent grid points with conditional mean error zero, the first-order term averages to zero and the conditional variance is at most $\Delta^2/4$. The resulting expected operational KL is at most $T\Delta^2/32$, including the initial rounding. This version consumes randomness. It scores the predictor using its realized carry, with observations generated independently by the exact source. Actual held-out language can favor a coarse rounding or penalize it. The expectation bound requires the specified source; the absolute pathwise bound holds on the observed language tape.

For the word-class implementation, let $r$ be the base mass of a learned set $S$. State $i$ multiplies the odds of $S$ by $e^{\beta_i}$, keeping the conditional word distributions within and outside $S$. On target word $y$ its raw-word likelihood ratio to the base is $e^{\beta_i 1_S(y)}/[1+(e^{\beta_i}-1)r]$. All filter parameters can therefore be fitted using ordinary next-word log loss, the base mass and the membership of the word actually observed. A single probability carries the full posterior between releases. On the same output set a conventional competitor can carry an exponentially averaged signed error $1_S(y)-r$. This is exactly the negative derivative of the conditional log loss with respect to an odds shift at zero. The held-out comparison determines which state better predicts subsequent words. ## Which prediction errors justify constructing a recurrent direction?

**PROVED for a frozen positive base law and predictable linear features.** Partition the raw vocabulary into $d$ classes, and let $q_t$ be the base model's class probabilities given the actual past. At target time $t$ let $e_t=\mathbf{1}_{c(y_t)}-q_t$ denote the class one-hot error. Take any square-integrable feature vector $u_t$ computed before $y_t$ from the actual observed history and the frozen model. For a matrix $W$ apply log-odds shifts $z_t=Wu_t$ to the classes, multiplying every raw-word probability by $\exp(z_{t,c})/\sum_jq_{t,j}\exp(z_{t,j})$. The resulting normalized law preserves the full distribution inside each class. Expectations below may mean either the empirical average on named training positions or a specified sequence distribution. Those choices give different statements.

Let $C=E[e_tu_t^\top]$ and $S=E[u_tu_t^\top]+\lambda I$ with $\lambda>0$. For every $W$, its mean raw-word log-score gain $G(W)$ satisfies
$$
G(W)-\frac{\lambda}{4}\|W\|_F^2\ \ge\
\langle W,C\rangle-\frac14\operatorname{tr}(WSW^\top).
$$
Indeed the exact gain per word is $z_{t,c(y_t)}-\ln\sum_jq_{t,j}e^{z_{t,j}}$. For the random variable with probabilities $q_{t,j}$ and values $z_{t,j}$, the log moment-generating function above its mean is at most its squared range divided by eight, by integrating the tilted variance bound. The squared range is at most $2\|z_t\|_2^2$. The coefficient $1/4$ cannot be reduced uniformly, since a fair binary base with shifts $(v,-v)$ gives $\ln\cosh v\sim v^2/2$. The multinomial log-likelihood quadratic bound is classical. [Böhning, multinomial logistic regression](https://www.ism.ac.jp/editsec/aism/pdf/044_1_0197.pdf)

Write $A=CS^{-1/2}$. Completing the square gives the certificate $\|A\|_F^2-\|WS^{1/2}-2A\|_F^2/4$. Its maximum among matrices of rank at most $k$ is $\sum_{j\le k}\sigma_j(A)^2$, attained at $W=2A_kS^{-1/2}$ for a best rank-$k$ singular truncation $A_k$. For a singular unregularized $S$ the same statement uses the inverse square root on its range, since $Cv=0$ for $v$ in its nullspace. The class-constant component has zero contrast because $\mathbf{1}^\top C=0$. Ties at the cutoff can leave multiple equally valid directions. Jensen's inequality also gives $G(W)\le\langle W,C\rangle$, so $C=0$ is necessary and sufficient for absence of *any* strict gain in this linear-tilt family on the declared distribution. Any nonzero $C$ already permits a rank-one improvement.

For the prior history of errors $u_{t+1}=(1-\rho)u_t+\rho e_t$, a rank-one fit $W=b a^\top$ actually deploys with just one dynamic value. Its update is
$$
s_{t+1}=(1-\rho)s_t+\rho[a_{c(y_t)}-a^\top q_t],\qquad z_t=b\,s_t.
$$
Thus ordinary errors can specify both which scalar to remember and which outputs to modify. The supplied class partition, common scalar decay family, base normalization and matrix rank remain explicit inductive choices. The deployed predictor must have access to $q_t$ to compute $a^\top q_t$ and the new normalizer. An arbitrary vector transition does not admit this scalar reduction unless the fitted input direction closes under that transition on reachable errors. Using the corrected predictor's own errors would also change the training objective. Neural language caches offer a separate history-dependent conventional construction. Positive training gain can occur under pure noise. One fair binary target, a constant feature one and no regularizer give fitted shifts $(1,-1)$ oriented toward that observation. The observed gain is $1-\ln\cosh1$, and the next independent fair target has expected gain $-\ln\cosh1$. Consequently an empirically certified direction must still earn its price on future raw words. For held-out sequence evaluation, the product of its conditional likelihood ratios to the base has mean one if the base generates the sequence and the frozen correction is causal. This supplies a null test against that particular base model. Interpreting an improvement on one chronological tape as a population claim requires further assumptions.
